# Evaluator Guide: Hands-On Skills Assessment

Hands-on verification is what makes a grade mean something. This guide explains how to run a
fair, consistent and safe skills assessment.

## Who can evaluate

- **LT1–LT3 skills:** an evaluator at **LT4 or higher**, a lead technician, or a supervisor designated by the company.
- **LT4–LT5 skills:** a supervisor, a CALT-certified lead, or a licensed electrician.
- **Electrical track skills (EA1–JW):** a **licensed journeyman or master electrician**. Most state apprenticeship rules require this for the hours and skills to count.
- Evaluators may not assess themselves. Companies should avoid having someone assess a close relative.

## Before the assessment

1. Open the technician's record (**Team → name**) and the skill. Read the description, equipment list and every criterion.
2. Choose a real job or a shop mock-up that lets the technician show **every** criterion.
3. Hold a job briefing: hazards, energy sources, PPE, the LOTO plan, and the rule that you will stop the task immediately if anything becomes unsafe.

## During the assessment

- **Observe; don't coach.** Let the technician do the task their own way. You may ask "why" questions afterward.
- **Stop the work** for any imminent danger. That is an automatic NOT YET.
- Check a criterion only if you **saw** it done correctly. "They probably know that" does not count.
- **Critical skills (⚠):** any safety deviation fails the assessment, even if every other criterion was met. Examples: skipping verification of absence of voltage, missing PPE, defeating a lockout, or standing on the top step of a ladder.

## Recording the result

- **PASS** requires every criterion to be checked.
- **NOT YET** requires coaching notes: what was missed and what to practice. The technician can see these notes.
- Your name and the date are recorded permanently. By signing off, you attest that you personally observed the demonstration.
- A technician can be re-assessed as many times as needed. The full history is kept.

## Promotion

When all four requirements are met (courses, final exam, hours, skills), the technician shows
as **Ready** on the team page. Before approving the promotion, ask yourself: *"Would I send this
person alone to this level of work tomorrow?"* If not, re-assess the skills you have doubts
about first.

## Approving on-the-job hours

Approve only hours you can verify from schedules, timesheets or direct knowledge. Hours must be
real work at the technician's current level. Reject entries that are vague or duplicated, and
add a note explaining why.
