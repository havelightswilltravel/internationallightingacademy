# Master Syllabus

Grades, courses, and hands-on skill areas for the full program. The authoritative content
lives in `curriculum/`; this document is the map. Typical durations are guidance only —
**technicians advance when they demonstrate competence, not when time passes.**

| Track | Level | Title | Typical timing | Min. verified OJT hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lighting | LT1 | Lighting Technician I (Entry Level) | Months 0–3 | 400 |
| Lighting | LT2 | Lighting Technician II | Months 3–9 | 900 |
| Lighting | LT3 | Lighting Technician III | Months 9–15 | 1,000 |
| Lighting | LT4 | Lighting Technician IV (Senior) | Months 15–21 | 1,000 |
| Lighting | LT5 | Certified Advanced Lighting Technician | Months 21–24 | 500 |
| Electrical | EA1 | Electrical Apprentice I | Year 3 | 1,600 |
| Electrical | EA2 | Electrical Apprentice II | Year 4 | 1,600 |
| Electrical | EA3 | Electrical Apprentice III | Year 5 | 1,600 |
| Electrical | EA4 | Electrical Apprentice IV | Year 6 | 1,600 |
| Electrical | JW | Journeyman Exam Preparation | Final months | 0 |

Passing a level requires **all** of:
1. Every course quiz passed (≥ 80%).
2. The level final exam passed (≥ 80%, randomized, timed).
3. Minimum verified on-the-job hours for that level, approved by a supervisor.
4. Every hands-on skill signed off by a qualified evaluator (critical safety skills with zero deviations).
5. Final promotion approval by an evaluator or company admin, which records the new grade.

## Lighting Technician Track (~24 months)

### LT1 — Lighting Technician I (Entry Level)
- **LT1-C01 Orientation, Safety Culture & PPE** — safety culture, hazard recognition, OSHA basics, PPE selection/inspection, electrical shock & arc-flash awareness (NFPA 70E awareness level), incident/near-miss reporting.
- **LT1-C02 Ladders & Fall Protection** — ladder types/ratings, inspection, 4:1 setup, three points of contact, working near energized parts, fall protection basics, intro to MEWPs (awareness only).
- **LT1-C03 Tools & Basic Meter Use** — hand/power tools, non-contact voltage testers and their limits, digital multimeter basics, CAT ratings, live-dead-live verification.
- **LT1-C04 Electrical Fundamentals I** — voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm's law & Watt's law, AC vs DC, series/parallel, common building voltages (120, 208, 240, 277, 480).
- **LT1-C05 Light Sources & Fixture Basics** — incandescent/halogen, fluorescent (T12/T8/T5, CFL), HID (MH, HPS, MV), LED; lamp bases; ballast/driver role; fixture types; lumens, watts, efficacy, CCT, CRI.
- **LT1-C06 Lockout/Tagout & Safe Relamping** — LOTO procedure, de-energize and verify, group lockout, relamping and ballast replacement procedures, lamp breakage, universal waste & mercury, PCB ballast identification.
- **LT1-C07 Professionalism, Customer Service & Work Orders** — job-site conduct, communicating with customers, work orders, documentation, photos, reporting problems.

Hands-on skills: PPE inspection & donning; ladder inspection/setup; DMM live-dead-live verification; LOTO application; fluorescent relamp; ballast replacement (supervised); universal waste handling; completing a work order.

### LT2 — Lighting Technician II
- **LT2-C01 Electrical Fundamentals II** — AC waveforms, single- vs three-phase, 120/208V and 277/480V wye systems, neutral vs equipment grounding conductor, power factor intro, voltage drop basics.
- **LT2-C02 Branch Circuits, Wiring Methods & Panelboards** — breakers, panel schedules, conductor colors, sizing basics, connectors/splices, boxes, NEC structure intro (Art. 110, 210, 240, 300, 310 basics).
- **LT2-C03 Test Instruments & Measurement** — DMM in depth, clamp meters, continuity/resistance, circuit tracers, meter safety and selection.
- **LT2-C04 Fluorescent & HID Systems** — ballast types (instant/rapid/programmed start), HID ballasts, ignitors, capacitors, restrike, end-of-life symptoms, troubleshooting.
- **LT2-C05 LED Retrofit Fundamentals** — TLED types (UL Type A/B/C/dual-mode), retrofit kits (UL 1598C), labeling requirements, ballast-bypass wiring, shunted vs non-shunted sockets.
- **LT2-C06 Troubleshooting Methodology** — systematic approach, symptom → cause, half-splitting, verifying the fix, documentation.
- **LT2-C07 Print Reading I** — lighting plans, symbols, fixture schedules, panel schedules, specifications.

Hands-on skills: safe voltage measurement on a three-phase panel; identifying/tracing a circuit; Type B TLED bypass conversion; HID ballast/ignitor replacement; systematic troubleshooting of a non-working fixture; locating fixtures/circuits from a lighting plan; installing a fixture whip and splices.

### LT3 — Lighting Technician III
- **LT3-C01 LED Technology & Drivers** — LED basics, thermal management, constant-current vs constant-voltage drivers, driver matching, flicker, L70/lumen maintenance, failure modes.
- **LT3-C02 Dimming Systems** — forward/reverse phase, 0–10V, DALI, DMX intro, compatibility, low-end trim.
- **LT3-C03 Lighting Controls I** — occupancy/vacancy sensors (PIR, ultrasonic, dual-tech), photocells, time clocks, relays, contactors, low-voltage control wiring.
- **LT3-C04 Emergency & Egress Lighting** — life-safety purpose, NFPA 101 testing (monthly 30-second, annual 90-minute), NEC 700 overview, battery packs, inverters, UL 924 transfer devices, exit signs.
- **LT3-C05 Exterior, Site & Pole Lighting** — area/pole lights, wall packs, photocontrols, pole and base inspection, underground circuits, wet locations, ground faults.
- **LT3-C06 Aerial Work Platforms** — MEWP types, ANSI A92.22/A92.24 concepts, pre-use and workplace inspection, fall protection, power-line clearances, ground conditions.

Hands-on skills: driver replacement and matching; 0–10V dimming troubleshooting; occupancy sensor install and adjustment; emergency driver install and test; photocell/contactor troubleshooting; MEWP pre-use inspection and operation (after authorized training).

### LT4 — Lighting Technician IV (Senior)
- **LT4-C01 Networked Lighting Controls** — architectures (wired, wireless mesh), gateways, zoning, commissioning, BMS integration, BACnet intro, luminaire-level lighting control.
- **LT4-C02 Energy Codes & Standards** — ASHRAE 90.1, IECC, Title 24 overview, lighting power density, mandatory controls, DLC qualified products, utility incentive programs.
- **LT4-C03 Photometry & Lighting Quality** — footcandles/lux, IES recommended illuminance, light meter surveys, uniformity, glare, CCT/CRI/TM-30.
- **LT4-C04 Power Quality & Advanced Electrical** — harmonics, LED inrush & nuisance tripping, voltage drop calculations, multiwire branch circuits & shared neutrals, transients & surge protection.
- **LT4-C05 Advanced Troubleshooting** — intermittent faults, system-level and controls interactions, root-cause analysis, case studies.
- **LT4-C06 The NEC for Lighting Work** — Art. 410 (luminaires), 411, 404, 225, 250 basics for lighting, 600, 700; navigating the code book.

Hands-on skills: commissioning an NLC zone; light-level survey and report; diagnosing nuisance tripping; troubleshooting a multiwire branch circuit; leading a retrofit audit walk-through.

### LT5 — Certified Advanced Lighting Technician
- **LT5-C01 Lighting Audits, Project Planning & Estimating** — audits, retrofit scopes, takeoffs, labor estimates, simple payback/ROI.
- **LT5-C02 Commissioning & Functional Testing** — Cx process, functional performance tests, documentation, owner training.
- **LT5-C03 Leadership, Mentoring & Crew Safety** — leading crews, job hazard analysis, toolbox talks, mentoring, conflict resolution.
- **LT5-C04 Emerging Lighting Technology** — PoE lighting, tunable white/circadian, UV-C safety, IoT sensors, solar/off-grid, smart-city, Li-Fi.
- **LT5-C05 Capstone & Comprehensive Review** — integrated review of LT1–LT4 and capstone project requirements.

Hands-on skills: capstone project; leading a job end-to-end; delivering a toolbox talk; mentoring sign-off of an LT1; full-system troubleshooting practical.

**Completing LT5 awards LT5 status and eligibility to enter the Electrical Development Track.**

## Electrical Development Track

> Journeyman licensing is regulated by each state (and sometimes city). Most require roughly
> 8,000 hours of supervised work under a licensed electrician plus classroom hours, and a
> passing score on a licensing exam. To make hours count, run this track as (or alongside)
> a **registered apprenticeship** approved by the U.S. DOL or your State Apprenticeship
> Agency, and confirm what lighting-track hours can be credited.

### EA1 — Electrical Apprentice I
- **EA1-C01 DC Theory & Magnetism** — atomic theory, DC circuits, Kirchhoff's laws, magnetism, electromagnetism.
- **EA1-C02 Introduction to the NEC** — code structure, definitions, Chapters 1–4 navigation, AHJ & adoption.
- **EA1-C03 Conduit Bending & Raceways** — EMT bending (90s, offsets, saddles, kicks), multipliers, shrink, take-up, raceway types.
- **EA1-C04 Wiring Devices & Residential Circuits** — receptacles, 3-way/4-way switching, GFCI/AFCI, dwelling branch circuits.
- **EA1-C05 Construction Safety** — OSHA construction topics, trenching, confined space awareness, hazard communication, scaffolds.

### EA2 — Electrical Apprentice II
- **EA2-C01 AC Theory** — inductance, capacitance, reactance, impedance, power triangle, power factor correction.
- **EA2-C02 Grounding & Bonding** — Article 250 concepts, grounding electrode system, EGC sizing, bonding, fault paths.
- **EA2-C03 Conductor Sizing & Overcurrent Protection** — ampacity tables, temperature correction, adjustment factors, OCPD selection.
- **EA2-C04 Commercial Wiring Methods** — EMT/MC/RMC/PVC, box fill, conduit fill, support and securing.
- **EA2-C05 Blueprint Reading II** — commercial electrical drawings, one-lines, riser diagrams, specifications.

### EA3 — Electrical Apprentice III
- **EA3-C01 Transformers** — principles, single-/three-phase connections (delta/wye), sizing, overcurrent protection, Article 450 concepts.
- **EA3-C02 Motors & Motor Controls** — motor principles, Article 430 concepts, starters, overloads, ladder diagrams, VFDs.
- **EA3-C03 Services & Feeders** — service components, feeders, introduction to load calculations.
- **EA3-C04 Three-Phase Distribution** — switchboards, panelboards, distribution systems, metering, high-leg delta.
- **EA3-C05 NFPA 70E & Qualified Worker Safety** — qualified person, risk assessment, approach and arc-flash boundaries, energized work permits.

### EA4 — Electrical Apprentice IV
- **EA4-C01 Advanced Load Calculations** — dwelling standard/optional methods, commercial calculations.
- **EA4-C02 Special Occupancies & Equipment** — hazardous locations, healthcare facilities, pools/spas.
- **EA4-C03 Fire Alarm & Low-Voltage Systems** — fire alarm circuits, Class 1/2/3 circuits, communications cabling.
- **EA4-C04 PLCs & Building Automation** — PLC fundamentals, I/O, ladder logic, BAS integration.
- **EA4-C05 Renewable Energy, Storage & EV Charging** — PV systems, energy storage, EV supply equipment.

### JW — Journeyman Exam Preparation
- **JW-C01 Exam Strategy & Code Navigation** — exam formats, tabbing the code book, time management.
- **JW-C02 Calculations Review** — Ohm's law, voltage drop, box/conduit fill, ampacity, motors, transformers, load calcs.
- **JW-C03 Comprehensive Code Review** — chapter-by-chapter review.
- **JW-C04 Practice Exams** — full-length timed practice exams.

Each electrical level includes hands-on skills appropriate to the content (e.g., conduit bending to tolerance, 3-way/4-way switching, motor control wiring, service installation, transformer connections).
