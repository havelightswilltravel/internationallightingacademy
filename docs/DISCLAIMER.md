# Important: Local Codes, Licensing and Scope of Work

This training program covers the **full range** of lighting technician work, from an
entry-level lighting technician to Senior and Master Lighting Technician. It is general
training. It does not replace the laws, codes or licensing rules that apply where you work.

**Before any skill in this program is performed in the field, the company and the technician are
responsible for confirming that it is allowed where the work takes place.**

- **Local codes vary.** The National Electrical Code (NEC), the Life Safety Code (NFPA 101), energy
  codes and building codes are adopted, amended and enforced differently by each state, county and
  city (the Authority Having Jurisdiction, or AHJ). The edition and amendments your AHJ has adopted
  always govern, not the edition cited in a lesson.
- **Licensing and scope of work vary.** Some jurisdictions allow only a licensed electrician, a
  registered apprentice under supervision, or a licensed sign or low-voltage contractor to perform
  certain tasks this program teaches. That can include work inside panels, branch-circuit work,
  sign and neon work, low-voltage and data cabling, and energized testing. A technician may only be
  allowed to do a **subset** of what this training covers.
- **Safety rules still apply in full.** OSHA, state OSHA plans, NFPA 70E, the employer's written
  safety programs and the manufacturer's instructions always apply. Where this training and one of
  those rules differ, follow the stricter requirement and tell your supervisor.
- **This program does not lead to an electrician's license.** Becoming a licensed (journeyman)
  electrician requires a state-approved or accredited apprenticeship or school, which this
  training is not.
- **Completing a course is not a license.** Passing a quiz, exam or skill sign-off shows training
  completion within this program. It does not qualify anyone to perform work that local law reserves
  for licensed or certified persons, and it does not by itself make someone a "qualified person"
  under OSHA or NFPA 70E. The employer makes that determination.
- **Content is reviewed but not guaranteed.** Lessons are written and reviewed in good faith and
  updated over time, but codes and products change. Report anything that looks wrong or out of date
  to your supervisor or training administrator.

By using this program you acknowledge that it is your responsibility, and your employer's, to
check local codes, licensing requirements and company policy, and to work only within the scope
you are licensed, trained and authorized for.
