# Discussion 006: Twelve grades from Day One to Master Lighting Technician

## Why the program has five grades today
- **The original build had ten grades.** When the program was first built, the request was about
  two years of lighting training followed by an electrical track. I built:
  - five lighting levels (LT1–LT5, about 24 months)
  - five electrical levels after them (EA1–EA4 and journeyman exam prep)
- **The cut to five was a side effect.** When we decided the program would stop at Senior/Master
  Lighting Technician instead of journeyman, I archived the five electrical levels. I didn't
  replace them with more lighting grades, so the ladder dropped to five. I should have flagged
  that at the time.
- **Five big grades are a poor fit for pay-by-grade.** Each one is 3–6 months and 4–12 courses
  long, so a tech can go half a year without a promotion. That's too long for the program's
  "advance by demonstrated skill" goal.

## The fix: twelve smaller grades
The fix is to split the existing lighting courses into **twelve grades**. The best of the archived
electrical courses come back, reworded as senior topics: seven courses, per Discussion 004's
recommendation. Each grade keeps the same gate as today:
- pass the courses
- pass a grade exam
- log the field hours
- prove the hands-on skills to an evaluator

The five titles stay as bands, so the job titles techs and customers know don't change.

"understand, inspect, recognize and escalate," not installation reserved for licensed electricians.

### How the numbers were set
- **Coursework hours** are the courses' own estimates. They include reading, video, quizzes and
  practice, not just lesson reading time.
- **Field hours** assume about 160 work hours a month, so they match the typical time in grade.
  They are minimums. **Advancement is by demonstrated skill:** a strong tech who passes everything
  can be promoted when the hours are met, and a tech who can't yet show the skills waits.
- **Weekly study load:** about 2–3 hours of coursework a week throughout.
- **Grade 1 is deliberately short and all safety.** A day-one hire finishes it in the first month,
  before working at height or on a circuit without close supervision.
- **Grade 9 is the heaviest**, at 69 hours, because it adds the two electrical-theory courses. One
  of them could move to Grade 10 if that's a better balance.

### If four years feels too long
Shorten Grades 9–12 to 3–4 months each. That gives about 3 years in total, with the same courses
and fewer field hours.

## What changes in the program
| Item | Change |
|---|---|
| Levels in the app | 12 instead of 5. Each grade gets its own exam, hours minimum and skill list. |
| Course codes | Can stay as they are (LT1-C04 lives in Grade 2) or be renumbered by grade. **Keeping them is less work and keeps quiz history.** |
| Hands-on skills | The 84 lighting skills are split across the grade where each skill's course is taught, plus a few new ones for the seven returning courses |
| Grade exams | 12 smaller exams (about 30–40 questions) instead of 5 large ones |
| Pay by grade | 12 steps instead of 5, so smaller and more frequent raises (example figures only, set by each company) |
| Current techs | Placed at a grade by assessment, as today |

## My recommendation
Go to twelve grades as laid out above:
- Keep the course codes.
- Keep the five titles as bands.
- Bring back the seven reworded electrical courses inside Grades 7–12.

It gives a new hire a first promotion within about a month, shows steady progress, and still ends
at Master Lighting Technician without promising a journeyman license.
