# Discussion 005: Who presents which videos

## Background
Four people will record videos:

| Presenter | Role | Strength on camera |
|---|---|---|
| **Kyle** | President | Why we do things: company values, safety culture, the grade ladder, stop-work authority |
| **Danny** | Operations Manager | How we do things: field procedures, safety programs, lifts, rigging, LOTO, projects, scheduling |
| **Chris Munford** | VP of Sales | The customer side: customer service, audits, proposals, retrofit-or-replace conversations, incentives |
| **You** | Program lead | How lighting systems work and how to troubleshoot them, plus the Skills Matrix and evaluations |

A fifth "presenter" is recommended: **a senior field tech** on camera doing the hands-on work
(meter use, ballast and driver swaps, ladder setup, LOTO) while one of the four introduces or
narrates. Viewers trust watching a working tech, and it keeps the four of you from having to
perform every task.

## Ground rules (recommended)
1. **Kyle bookends; he doesn't carry whole courses.** A 2–3 minute welcome or "why this
   matters" at the start of key courses has more impact than Kyle teaching technical detail.
2. **Company-policy lessons go to the person who owns the policy.** Danny owns most field
   policies, so he presents the "Our Company's…" lessons.
3. **Technical theory goes to one voice (you)**, so terms and teaching style stay consistent
   from LT1 to LT5.
4. **Customer-facing topics go to Chris**, so techs hear how sales sees the job and what the
   customer was promised.
5. **Nobody reads the lesson text.** Use the lesson as an outline; keep videos 5–12 minutes.
6. **No customer names, sites or logos on camera**, and no real rates or prices (example
   figures only), because the program is licensed to other companies.
7. **Swap-ready segments.** Record company-specific segments (policy, "how we do it here")
   separately from general teaching segments, so a licensee can replace only the company part.

## What this means for each person
- **Kyle:** about 12 short segments (2–5 minutes). Could be recorded in one session.
- **Chris:** 3 lead courses (LT1-C07, LT5-C01, LT5-C06) plus about 5 single lessons.
- **Danny:** about 16 lead courses plus the toolbox talks; the heaviest field-safety load.
- **You:** about 35 lead courses (most technical content) plus app and evaluator videos.

Danny's and your loads are large. Options: the senior tech presents some technical lessons
(LT2 track, low-voltage, data cabling), and toolbox talks rotate among crew leads.

## Recording order (suggested)
1. Kyle's welcome and LT1-C01 L1, so the program has a face from day one.
2. LT1 safety courses (C01, C02, C06, C11) with Danny.
3. The rest of LT1, then LT2, following the order techs will take them.
4. Senior and Master courses last; they're needed later and change most as decisions land.

## My recommendation
Use the assignments above, with Kyle as the bookend voice, Danny for safety and field
policy, Chris for the customer side and the master-level business courses, and you as the
main technical instructor. Add a senior tech for the hands-on demos.

4. Should licensees get your videos with company segments removed, or record their own?
