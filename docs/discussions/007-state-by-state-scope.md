# Discussion 007: What a lighting tech may do differs by state

## The question
Some states and cities let an unlicensed lighting technician do more electrical work than others.
The program is licensed to companies across the country, so it must not teach a task as if every
tech everywhere may do it. This builds on Discussion 001, which added the sign-in notice.

## How the rules commonly differ (general patterns; verify for each location)
I've deliberately named no states here. These rules change, and each licensee must confirm its own.
- **Who must be licensed.** Some states license electrical work statewide. Others leave it to
  cities or counties, and a few license little outside certain cities.
- **Maintenance exemptions.** Some areas exempt maintenance or "like-for-like" replacement:
  - lamps
  - ballasts and drivers
  - sometimes switches, receptacles or whole fixtures

  Others treat anything past relamping as electrical work.
- **Retrofits and new wiring.** Ballast-bypass (Type B) conversions, retrofit kits, new fixture
  whips and new circuits often need a licensed electrician, a permit and an inspection.
- **Low-voltage work.** Some states license low-voltage (limited-energy) work separately:
  - controls
  - data cabling
  - PoE lighting
- **Signs.** Sign work can need a sign contractor or sign electrician license.
- **Breakers and panels.** Site rules, a company's insurance and NFPA 70E qualification can limit
  who may operate breakers or open panels, even where the law doesn't.
- **Other state rules beyond licensing:**
  - lamp, battery and ballast disposal
  - heat-illness rules
  - in-state commercial driving limits

## Where the program now flags it
Every lesson that teaches one of these tasks now carries the same notice. They're in:
- ballast and driver replacement and LED retrofit kits
- switches and receptacles
- fixture replacement and whips
- breakers and panels
- energized testing
- low-voltage controls and data cabling
- neon and LED signs
- exterior and underground wiring
- pole removal
- emergency lighting testing
- refrigerated cases
- 3-way, track and low-voltage transformer work

The lessons on lamp, battery and ballast disposal carry a shorter disposal version.

> **Scope varies by state and city.** In some areas a lighting technician may [do this task] under
> the company's license; in others it must be done by, or under the supervision of, a licensed
> electrician, and some jobs need a permit and inspection. Before you do this on a job, confirm
> with your supervisor that it is allowed in that location and that you are authorized for it.
> When in doubt, stop and ask.

The full notice that every user accepts (`docs/DISCLAIMER.md`) now has a matching "Scope varies by
location" section. Because its wording changed, every user will be asked to accept it again.

## Suggested wording for the license agreement (for your attorney)
> Licensee is solely responsible for determining which tasks its personnel may lawfully perform in
> each jurisdiction where it works, including licensing, permit, inspection and supervision
> requirements, and for configuring the program's scope settings accordingly. The training content
> describes tasks a lighting technician may perform in some jurisdictions; it does not authorize
> any person to perform work, does not confer a license, and does not replace the requirements of
> the authority having jurisdiction.
