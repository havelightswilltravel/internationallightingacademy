# Discussion 002: LED service, repair the driver or replace the fixture?

## The question
When an LED fixture fails, do we troubleshoot and replace the failed driver, or replace the whole
fixture? Replacement fixture prices keep coming down while driver prices have gone up. If trucks
carried a few **standard** wall packs, pole heads and cans instead of a bin of assorted drivers,
the total cost to the customer could be lower and we would avoid many return trips. The trade-off
is that properties end up with fixtures that don't all match.

## What drives the decision
| Factor | Favors repairing the driver | Favors replacing the fixture |
|---|---|---|
| Parts cost | Driver costs a small fraction of a new fixture | Driver costs a large share of a new fixture (increasingly common) |
| Return trips | The exact driver is on the truck | The driver must be ordered; a standard fixture on the truck finishes the job in one visit |
| Labor | Quick driver swap in an accessible fixture | Driver is hard to reach, potted or integral; or the LED board is also failing |
| Fixture age and condition | Newer fixture, LEDs healthy, housing sound | Older fixture, lumen loss, yellowed lens, corrosion, more failures coming |
| Warranty | Fixture under manufacturer warranty (driver or fixture may be free) | Out of warranty |
| Appearance and light quality | Property has a uniform look (franchise or brand standard, storefront, lobby) | Back-of-house, alley or parking areas where matching matters less |
| Light output and color | Matching an existing CCT, output and distribution exactly | A field-selectable fixture can be set to match closely anyway |
| Controls | Driver must match the existing dimming or sensor setup | New fixture includes its own sensor or photocell |
| Waste and sustainability | Less material discarded | More e-waste, partly offset by fewer trips |

## The matching problem, and how to shrink it
- **Field-selectable fixtures.** Many current wall packs, area lights and downlights let the tech
  set wattage or lumens and color temperature (for example 3000/4000/5000 K) with switches. One
  stocked model can match most existing fixtures closely in light, even if not in shape.
- **A "property standard" per customer.** Record in the asset inventory (LT5-C06) the fixture the
  customer has approved as the replacement for each fixture type. Techs install the approved model
  without a phone call, and over time the property becomes consistent instead of random.
- **Replace by zone.** When one fixture on a façade or row fails, offer to convert that façade or
  row on the same visit, or plan it, so mismatches don't scatter across the property.

## A sample decision rule (for discussion, not policy)
1. **Under warranty?** Follow the warranty process (LT5-C06 lesson 3).
2. **Does the property have an approved standard fixture for this type?** Install it if the driver
   is not on the truck or the fixture is past mid-life.
3. **Driver on the truck, fixture in good condition, and the look must match?** Replace the driver.
4. **Driver cost above roughly half the cost of a standard replacement fixture, or the driver needs
   ordering?** Replace the fixture with the standard model, set to match output and color, and
   note it in the inventory.
5. **When in doubt, give the customer the choice on the spot**, with both prices and the
   return-trip implication, and record their answer as the property's standing preference.

(The "roughly half" threshold is a starting point to test against real job data, not a company
figure.)

## Truck stock idea
A small standard kit with three or four models instead of dozens of drivers:
- One or two **field-selectable wall packs** (small and medium).
- One **field-selectable area or pole head** with a universal mounting kit (arm, slip-fitter and
  bolt patterns; see the bolt-pattern measurement lesson in LT3-C12).
- **4-inch and 6-inch can retrofit modules** with selectable CCT.
- A short list of the **most common universal drivers** for indoor fixtures that will still be
  repaired.

## What this means for training
- Techs still need to **diagnose** the failure (driver vs. LED board vs. supply vs. controls).
  You can't recommend the right fix without knowing what failed, and the troubleshooting courses
  stay.
- Add a **"repair or replace" decision lesson** with this rule, how to present the choice to a
  customer, and how to record the property standard. LT5-C06 already covers retrofit vs.
  replace vs. maintain for whole systems; this would extend it to single-fixture service calls,
  at LT3 level, where techs make the call.
- Add **parts knowledge** for selectable fixtures and mounting adapters (LT1-C10).

## My recommendation
Adopt a **hybrid policy**:
- Diagnose every failure.
- Replace drivers when the fixture is healthy, the driver is on hand and matching matters.
- Otherwise replace with a **customer-approved, field-selectable standard fixture** carried on
  the truck.
- Record each property's standard so future calls need no discussion.

Pilot it for 60–90 days on a few service customers. Track return trips avoided, total cost per
repair, and customer feedback before making it policy.

## Questions for you
1. Which wall pack, pole head and can models do you already like? A standard works best if it's a
   product line you trust and can get quickly.
2. Do key customers have brand standards that would rule out mismatched fixtures?
3. Should the tech be allowed to make the swap decision on site, or does it need customer or
   office approval above a certain amount?
4. Do you want the "repair or replace" lesson written now as draft policy, or after the pilot?
