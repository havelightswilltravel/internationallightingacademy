## The DesignLights Consortium (DLC)

The **DesignLights Consortium (DLC)** is a non-profit that sets performance requirements for
commercial lighting products and publishes **Qualified Products Lists (QPLs)**. Utilities across
North America use the DLC lists to decide which products are eligible for incentives.

DLC maintains two main lists you will use:

| List | Covers | What qualification means |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid-State Lighting (SSL) QPL** | LED luminaires and retrofit kits, TLEDs (some categories), high bays, area lights, etc. | Meets DLC minimums for efficacy (lumens per watt), lumen maintenance, color quality, power factor, THD, warranty and other technical requirements, based on approved test reports (e.g., IES LM-79, LM-80, TM-21) |
| **Networked Lighting Controls (NLC) QPL** | NLC systems | Meets DLC required capabilities (networking, occupancy sensing, daylight harvesting, high-end trim, zoning, etc.); LLLC capability is identified separately |

DLC periodically updates its technical requirements (for example, the SSL V5.x series). When
requirements change, some previously listed products are delisted after a transition period, and
utility programs may set their own cutoff dates. Always check the list **at the time of purchase**
and against the specific program's rules.

## Checking a Product on the QPL

1. Get the **full catalog/model number** from the spec sheet or carton – including every option code.
2. Search the QPL by manufacturer and model number.
3. Confirm the **exact configuration** is listed. A troffer listed at 4000K/3,500 lm may not be
   listed at 5000K/5,000 lm. Wildcards in DLC model numbers have specific meanings – read the listing.
4. Note the **DLC product ID**, listed wattage and the category (Standard or Premium where applicable).
5. Save a screenshot or PDF of the listing with the date for the project file.

> **Note:** DLC listing does not replace safety certification. A product still needs to be
> **listed by a Nationally Recognized Testing Laboratory** (e.g., to UL 1598 for luminaires,
> UL 8750 for LED equipment, UL 1598C for retrofit kits) as required by NEC 110.3 and the AHJ. DLC
> is about performance; NRTL listing is about safety.

## Utility Incentive Programs

Utilities and energy-efficiency program administrators offer incentives to reduce energy use and
peak demand. Common program types:

| Program type | How it works | Typical documentation |
|---|---|---|
| **Prescriptive / deemed** | Fixed dollar amount per qualifying product (per fixture, per lamp, per kW reduced) | Invoices, spec sheets, DLC listings, before/after counts |
| **Custom / calculated** | Incentive based on calculated or measured savings | Detailed audit, hours of operation, wattages, sometimes metering |
| **Midstream / instant** | Discount applied at the distributor counter | Distributor handles paperwork; installation address required |
| **Controls incentives** | Extra incentive for NLC, LLLC or sensors | NLC QPL listing, controlled wattage, commissioning proof |

Most programs share rules that directly affect field work:

- **Pre-approval** is often required before equipment is purchased or installed. Installing first can
  forfeit the incentive.
- **Pre- and post-inspections** may be performed. Inspectors count fixtures and compare to the
  application.
- **Old equipment must be removed** (and disposed of properly – universal waste and PCB ballast rules
  from LT1-C06 still apply).
- **Quantities and models must match** the approved application exactly.

## What the Field Tech Contributes

During the audit walk-through (skill LT4-S06) and installation:

1. **Accurate existing counts and wattages.** Record lamp type, lamps per fixture, ballast type and
   ballast factor where possible, and operating hours. Savings and incentive are calculated from these.
2. **Photos.** Take representative "before" photos of each fixture type, ballast labels, and control
   devices, and "after" photos of installed products with labels visible.
3. **Model number control.** Confirm received products match the approved model numbers before
   installing. Report any distributor substitution immediately.
4. **Disposal records.** Keep recycling receipts and PCB ballast manifests where applicable.
5. **Controls proof.** Keep commissioning records and screenshots showing NLC programming when
   controls incentives are claimed.

> **Safety:** Incentive deadlines never justify shortcuts. Removing existing fixtures and ballasts is
> line-voltage work: de-energize, apply LOTO and verify absence of voltage (live-dead-live) before
> disconnecting anything. Treat ballasts without a "No PCBs" label as PCB-containing, handle leaking
> ballasts with gloves and spill procedures, and keep fluorescent lamps intact for universal-waste
> recycling.

## Simple Savings Math

Annual kWh saved = (existing W − new W) × quantity × annual hours ÷ 1,000

Example: replace 100 fixtures at 112 W with 45 W LED troffers, operating 4,000 h/yr:
(112 − 45) × 100 × 4,000 ÷ 1,000 = **26,800 kWh/yr**. At $0.14/kWh that is about **$3,752 per year**
before controls savings. Payback and ROI calculations are covered in LT5-C01.

## Key Takeaways
- DLC publishes the SSL and NLC Qualified Products Lists used by most utility incentive programs.
- Verify the exact model and configuration on the QPL at time of purchase and save the listing.
- DLC qualification is about performance; NRTL listing (UL 1598, UL 8750, UL 1598C) is about safety and is still required.
- Incentive programs commonly require pre-approval, matching quantities/models, removal and proper disposal of old equipment, and inspection.
- Accurate audit data, photos and model-number control protect the customer's incentive.
