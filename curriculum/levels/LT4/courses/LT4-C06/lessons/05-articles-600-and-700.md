## Article 600 – Electric Signs and Outline Lighting

Many lighting companies service and retrofit signs – converting neon or fluorescent sign lighting to
LED. Article 600 governs this work (it is a Chapter 6 "special equipment" article, so it supplements
Chapters 1–4).

### Key requirements

| Topic | Requirement (2023 NEC – AHJ edition governs) |
|---|---|
| **Listing (600.3)** | Signs, outline lighting, skeleton tubing systems and retrofit kits must be listed and installed in conformance with the listing |
| **Markings (600.4)** | Signs must be marked with manufacturer and input rating. A sign **retrofitted with a field-installed retrofit kit** must be marked to show the kit's manufacturer and that the sign has been modified, per the kit instructions |
| **Branch circuit for sign outlet (600.5)** | Commercial buildings and occupancies accessible to pedestrians must have at least one outlet for sign or outline lighting at each entrance to each tenant space, supplied by a branch circuit rated at least 20 A that supplies no other load (read the section for details and exceptions) |
| **Disconnecting means (600.6)** | Each sign and outline lighting system (and each feeder or branch circuit supplying it) must be controlled by an externally operable switch or circuit breaker that opens all ungrounded conductors. The disconnect must be **within sight** of the sign or outline lighting it controls, or be capable of being **locked in the open position** where it is out of sight (with the locking provision remaining in place) |
| **Class 2 sign illumination** | Signs using Class 2 LED power supplies have their own wiring rules in Article 600 (and Article 725/722 references) for secondary wiring |

### Sign retrofit best practice
1. Verify the retrofit kit is **listed** for the sign type and follow the instructions exactly.
2. Remove the old ballasts/neon transformers per the instructions; dispose of fluorescent lamps and
   ballasts per universal waste and PCB rules (LT1-C06).
3. Apply the **retrofit label(s)** supplied with the kit.
4. Confirm the **sign disconnect** is present and within sight, or lockable.

> **Safety:** Neon sign transformers produce **thousands of volts** on the secondary, and their output
> can remain hazardous. Sign bodies may also contain capacitors that store charge. De-energize at the
> sign disconnect and the branch circuit, apply LOTO, verify absence of voltage on the primary, and
> follow manufacturer guidance for secondary circuits before touching tubing, GTO wire or electrodes.
> Use MEWP and fall-protection rules for elevated signs.

## Article 700 – Emergency Systems

**Article 700** covers emergency systems legally required by codes or government agencies to supply
illumination and power automatically when normal power fails – including **egress and exit lighting**
required by NFPA 101 (LT3-C04 covered the NFPA 101 illumination and testing requirements). Article 700
is a Chapter 7 article and modifies the general rules.

### Rules lighting techs must protect

| Topic | What it means in the field |
|---|---|
| **Testing and records** | The system must be witness-tested on installation and tested periodically, with written records kept (Article 700, Part I) |
| **Wiring separation (700.10)** | Emergency circuit wiring must be kept **entirely independent** of all other wiring – not in the same raceway, cable, box or cabinet as normal wiring, except where specifically permitted (such as in transfer equipment, or in emergency luminaires supplied from two sources) |
| **Identification** | Emergency boxes, enclosures and circuit equipment are required to be marked so they are readily identified as emergency system components |
| **Sources of power (700.12)** | Storage batteries, generators, UPS, unit equipment (battery packs), and other permitted sources – each with specific requirements |
| **Emergency illumination design (700.16)** | The system must be arranged so that **failure of any single lighting element (such as a burned-out lamp) cannot leave a space requiring emergency illumination in total darkness** |
| **Control of emergency luminaires** | Where emergency luminaires are switched or dimmed in normal operation (e.g., by NLC), listed devices must ensure they operate at the required level when normal power fails – for example, UL 924 emergency lighting control devices or listed branch-circuit transfer equipment |
| **Unit equipment** | Battery units and their supply circuits have specific rules – e.g., the unit must be connected to the same branch circuit as the normal lighting in the area, ahead of any local switches, so it activates when that circuit fails |
| **Selective coordination** | Overcurrent devices in emergency systems must be selectively coordinated – a fault should open only the nearest device |

### Common violations found during lighting work
1. Normal-power conductors pulled into emergency raceways or boxes during a retrofit.
2. Emergency luminaires connected to normal circuits after a fixture replacement.
3. Emergency luminaires added to NLC dimming zones without a UL 924 device.
4. Battery units plugged into or wired to a circuit other than the area's normal lighting circuit,
   or connected after the local switch so they turn on every time the lights are switched off.
5. No test records.

### Related articles
- **Article 701 – Legally Required Standby** – required by codes but not life-safety emergency (e.g.,
  some smoke control, sewage systems).
- **Article 702 – Optional Standby** – owner-chosen backup power.

These have different rules; emergency (700) is the most stringent. Know which system a circuit belongs
to by its panel labeling and identification.

> **Safety:** Emergency circuits can be fed from generators, UPS or batteries – they may be energized
> even when normal power is off. Treat every emergency luminaire and circuit as having a second source.
> Identify all sources, lock out each (including battery units where applicable), and verify absence of
> voltage before work.

## Key Takeaways
- Article 600 requires listed signs and retrofit kits, retrofit marking, a sign outlet circuit at commercial entrances, and a disconnect within sight or lockable.
- Neon secondaries are high voltage – LOTO and verify before touching sign components.
- Article 700 emergency wiring must be kept independent of normal wiring and clearly identified.
- No single lamp failure may leave an emergency-illuminated space in darkness (700.16).
- Emergency luminaires controlled by switches or dimmers need listed devices (e.g., UL 924) to force them on during power loss.
- Unit equipment must be on the area's normal lighting branch circuit ahead of local switches.
- Emergency circuits may have multiple sources – lock out all of them.
