## Scope

**Article 410** covers luminaires, portable luminaires, lampholders, pendants, incandescent filament
lamps, arc lamps, electric-discharge and LED lamps, retrofit kits, and the wiring and equipment
forming part of them. It is the article you will use most as a lighting technician. This lesson
highlights the rules you meet daily. Section numbers below are from the 2023 NEC.

## Listing (410.6)

Luminaires, lampholders and **retrofit kits** must be **listed**. Combined with **110.3(B)** (install
listed equipment according to its instructions), this means:

- A retrofit kit must be listed (UL 1598C for luminaire retrofit kits) and installed **exactly per its
  instructions**, including applying the required retrofit labels (LT2-C05).
- Field modifications not covered by a listed kit's instructions can void the listing.

## Locations (410.10)

| Location | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Damp locations** | Luminaires must be marked suitable for damp or wet locations |
| **Wet locations** | Luminaires must be marked suitable for wet locations, installed so water cannot enter or accumulate in wiring compartments, lampholders or other electrical parts |
| **Bathtub and shower zone** – 3 ft (900 mm) horizontally and 8 ft (2.5 m) vertically from the top of the tub rim or shower threshold | No cord-connected luminaires, chain-, cable- or cord-suspended luminaires, lighting track, pendants or ceiling-suspended (paddle) fans in the zone. Luminaires in the zone must be listed for damp locations, or wet locations where subject to shower spray |

## Luminaires in Clothes Closets (410.16)

Closet lighting has specific rules because stored clothing can contact hot lamps:

- Certain luminaire types are **not permitted** in closets – notably incandescent luminaires with open
  or partially enclosed lamps, and pendant luminaires or lampholders.
- Permitted types (such as surface or recessed LED with a completely enclosed light source, and
  fluorescent) must maintain **minimum clearances** from the defined "storage space." Typical values
  are 12 in for surface incandescent/LED and 6 in for surface fluorescent and recessed types; certain
  surface fluorescent or LED luminaires identified for use within the storage area are permitted there.

Read 410.16 and the Article 100 definition of storage space before approving any closet installation.

## Support (410.30 and 410.36)

- Luminaires must be **securely supported**. A luminaire weighing more than 6 lb or larger than 16 in
  in any dimension must not be supported by the screw shell of a lampholder.
- Outlet boxes used to support luminaires must be designed for the purpose (Article 314); boxes are
  generally rated for luminaires up to 50 lb unless marked for more.
- **Suspended ceilings (410.36(B)):** framing members used to support luminaires must be securely
  fastened to each other and to the building structure, and luminaires must be **securely fastened to
  the ceiling framing members by mechanical means** such as bolts, screws, rivets, or **listed clips**
  identified for the framing and luminaire. Laying a troffer loose in the grid does not comply.
- **Poles (410.30(B))** – rules for metal and nonmetallic poles supporting luminaires, including
  handholes and grounding terminals (covered in lesson 4).

## Recessed Luminaires (410.110–410.122)

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Type IC** | Identified for contact with insulation – may be in direct contact with combustible materials and insulation |
| **Non-Type IC clearances (410.116)** | At least **1/2 in (13 mm)** from combustible materials (except at points of support), and at least **3 in (75 mm)** from thermal insulation; insulation must not be above the luminaire or within 3 in |
| **Temperature (410.117)** | Branch-circuit conductors must have insulation suitable for the temperature encountered; tap conductors ("fixture whips") with suitable insulation are permitted, run in a raceway or cable at least 18 in (450 mm) and not more than 6 ft (1.8 m) long |
| **Thermal protection** | Most recessed incandescent luminaires require integral thermal protection |

When an insulation contractor blows insulation over non-IC cans, the installation becomes a fire
hazard and a violation. Report it.

## Grounding (Part V)

Exposed metal parts of luminaires must be connected to an **equipment grounding conductor**. In
existing installations without an EGC, replacement luminaires have specific rules – check the section
and ask before installing a metal luminaire on an ungrounded outlet.

## Luminaires as Raceways (410.64)

Luminaires generally must not be used as a raceway for circuit conductors unless **listed and marked**
for that use, or identified for through-wiring – and through-wiring is limited to conductors of the
circuit supplying the luminaire unless the luminaire is listed for more. Continuous rows of
luminaires that are designed and listed to be connected end-to-end may carry the branch circuit.

## Disconnecting Means for Ballasted Luminaires (410.130(G))

In **indoor locations other than dwellings**, fluorescent luminaires that use double-ended lamps and
contain ballasts that can be serviced in place must have a **disconnecting means** – either internal
or external, so the luminaire can be serviced de-energized. Where the luminaire is supplied by a
**multiwire branch circuit**, the disconnecting means must open **all supply conductors, including the
grounded conductor**, simultaneously. Listed retrofit kits and Type B TLED instructions address
disconnects for converted luminaires – follow them.

> **Safety:** A luminaire disconnect lets you isolate one fixture, but it is not a replacement for your
> company's LOTO program and verification. Before touching conductors, verify absence of voltage at the
> point of work with a tested meter (live-dead-live). On MWBCs, confirm the neutral is also open or
> lock out every circuit sharing it.

## Key Takeaways
- Luminaires, lampholders and retrofit kits must be listed (410.6) and installed per instructions (110.3(B)).
- Wet/damp locations require marked luminaires; the bathtub/shower zone (3 ft horizontal, 8 ft vertical) prohibits pendants, track and cord-connected luminaires.
- Closet luminaires have type restrictions and clearances to storage space (410.16).
- Luminaires in suspended ceilings must be mechanically fastened to framing members (410.36(B)).
- Non-IC recessed luminaires need 1/2 in from combustibles and 3 in from insulation (410.116).
- Ballasted fluorescent luminaires indoors (non-dwelling) need a disconnect; on MWBCs it must open the neutral too (410.130(G)).
