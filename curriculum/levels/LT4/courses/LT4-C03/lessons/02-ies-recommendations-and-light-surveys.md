## Where Light-Level Targets Come From

The **Illuminating Engineering Society (IES)** publishes recommended illuminance for tasks and
spaces in its Lighting Handbook and Recommended Practices (ANSI/IES RP series, for example for
offices, education, industrial facilities, healthcare, roadways and parking). Current IES guidance
selects targets based on the visual task, the age of the occupants and the importance of the task.
Targets are expressed as **maintained average illuminance** on a specified plane, often with
uniformity criteria.

Typical **orders of magnitude** (always use the project specification or the current IES document
for the actual target):

| Space / task | Typical horizontal target range |
|---|---|
| Corridors, general circulation | ~5–10 fc |
| Open office, reading/computer work | ~20–40 fc |
| Classrooms | ~30–50 fc |
| Warehouse, bulk storage aisles | ~5–20 fc |
| Detailed assembly / inspection | 50–100+ fc |
| Parking lots | well under 5 fc average, with uniformity criteria |

Some spaces are more about **vertical** illuminance (faces, shelves, signage). A warehouse rack aisle
may need vertical readings on rack faces, not just the floor.

## When You Do a Survey

- After a retrofit, to verify the design target was met (often a contract requirement)
- During an audit, to document existing conditions (skill LT4-S06)
- In response to a complaint ("it's too dark")
- To set high-end trim during commissioning (LT4-C01)

## Equipment

- **Illuminance meter** that is cosine-corrected and color-corrected (photopic response), with a
  **current calibration** (commonly annual – follow the manufacturer and company policy).
  Inexpensive meters and phone apps can be badly wrong under LED spectra.
- Tape measure or laser measure, a grid sheet or plan, a small stand or tripod to hold the sensor at
  work-plane height, camera, and a report template.

## Step-by-Step Survey Procedure

### 1. Prepare conditions
- **Exclude or document daylight.** Survey at night, or with shades closed. If daylight cannot be
  excluded, take one set of readings with lights on and one with lights off and subtract to get the
  electric-light contribution.
- **Stabilize the lighting.** Let LED systems run for a period (often 15–30 minutes or more)
  before reading; fluorescent and HID need longer. Confirm controls are set to full output (override
  dimming, trim and daylight harvesting unless you are measuring the as-controlled condition – state
  which in your report).
- Note lamp outages, dirty lenses and anything blocking light.

### 2. Lay out the grid
- Choose a grid that captures variation – for a regular troffer layout, include points under
  luminaires, between them and at walls. A common practice in open areas is a 2–5 ft grid in small
  rooms and wider in large spaces, consistent across the area.
- Keep points away from walls by about 2 ft unless wall areas are part of the task.
- Mark the grid on the plan so the survey can be repeated.

### 3. Set the measurement plane
- **Work plane** for desks and tables: typically 30 in (2.5 ft) above floor.
- **Floor** for corridors, warehouses and parking (or as the specification states).
- **Vertical** at the specified height for rack faces, whiteboards, signage.

### 4. Take readings
- Hold the sensor **level** (or on a stand), not tilted.
- Stand to the side and keep your body and shadow out of the light path. Wear dark clothing if
  possible – light-colored clothing reflects light onto the sensor.
- Wait for the reading to settle; record it against the grid point immediately.

### 5. Calculate and compare
- Average = sum of readings ÷ number of readings
- Maximum, minimum, and uniformity ratios (next lesson)
- Compare to target and identify areas above or below

## Writing the Report

A good light-level report includes: project and space, date and time, surveyor, meter make/model/
serial and calibration date, daylight condition, control state, lamp condition, grid layout, data
table, calculations, comparison to target, photos and recommendations. The report should allow
someone else to **repeat the survey** and get comparable results.

> **Safety:** Surveys in occupied warehouses and parking lots expose you to forklift and vehicle
> traffic. Wear high-visibility clothing, coordinate with the facility, and cone off areas as needed.
> Never stand on equipment or racking to reach a vertical measurement point – use a proper ladder or
> MEWP under the rules you learned in LT1 and LT3.

## Key Takeaways
- IES Recommended Practices set illuminance targets by task, occupant age and importance; the project specification governs the contract target.
- Use a calibrated, cosine- and color-corrected meter.
- Exclude or subtract daylight, stabilize the lighting and document control state.
- Lay out a repeatable grid at the correct measurement plane and keep the sensor level and unshadowed.
- Report method, conditions, data, calculations and recommendations so the survey can be repeated.
