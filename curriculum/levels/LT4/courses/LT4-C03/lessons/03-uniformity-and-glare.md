## Why Average Isn't Enough

A space can meet its average illuminance target and still be poorly lit. Bright spots and dark
patches make eyes constantly re-adapt, hide hazards in shadows and generate complaints. **Uniformity**
describes how even the light is; **glare** describes light that causes discomfort or reduces
visibility.

## Uniformity Ratios

| Ratio | Formula | Lower is… |
|---|---|---|
| **Average-to-minimum (avg:min)** | Average ÷ minimum | More uniform |
| **Maximum-to-minimum (max:min)** | Maximum ÷ minimum | More uniform |
| Minimum-to-average (min/avg, "U0") | Minimum ÷ average | (Higher is more uniform – common in international practice) |

Using the sample survey from the previous lesson (avg 37.3, max 47, min 29):

- avg:min = 37.3 ÷ 29 = **1.29:1**
- max:min = 47 ÷ 29 = **1.62:1**

That is a very uniform interior. Typical guidance (always confirm in the applicable IES document or
specification):

| Application | Typical uniformity criteria |
|---|---|
| Offices and classrooms | avg:min around 1.5:1 to 3:1 |
| Warehouses | avg:min around 2:1 to 4:1 |
| Parking lots | avg:min around 3:1 to 4:1 and max:min around 10:1 to 20:1 |

When reporting uniformity, always state which ratio you used. "Uniformity of 3" is meaningless
without saying avg:min or max:min.

## Common Causes of Poor Uniformity

1. **Spacing too wide** for the luminaire's distribution (exceeding the spacing criterion).
2. **Wrong optics** – narrow beams in a low ceiling create pools of light; wide beams in tall racks
   waste light on the rack tops.
3. **One-for-one retrofits** where new luminaires have a different distribution than the old ones.
4. **Outages** – one dead fixture in a parking lot can wreck max:min.
5. **Mixed sources or wattages** after piecemeal maintenance.
6. **Obstructions** – new racks, ductwork or partitions installed after the lighting design.

## Glare

**Discomfort glare** causes annoyance or squinting without necessarily reducing visibility.
**Disability glare** scatters light in the eye and actually reduces the ability to see – like
oncoming headlights at night. **Reflected glare** (veiling reflections) is bright images reflected in
screens, glossy paper or polished floors.

### Interior glare tools
- **Luminance limits** – designers limit how bright luminaires appear at high viewing angles.
- **Unified Glare Rating (UGR)** – a calculated index widely used internationally (lower is better;
  about 19 is a common target for offices). It is calculated by designers, not measured with a
  footcandle meter.
- **Shielding** – lenses, louvers, baffles, regressed optics and indirect components.

### Exterior glare: BUG ratings
For outdoor luminaires, IES **TM-15** defines **BUG ratings** – **Backlight, Uplight and Glare** –
each rated from 0 (least) upward (B0–B5, U0–U5, G0–G5). They appear on many area-light spec sheets
and are used in some outdoor lighting ordinances and the model lighting ordinance approach. A
full-cutoff area light typically has U0 – no light emitted above horizontal.

## Field Fixes for Glare and Uniformity Complaints

| Complaint | Likely fix (with designer/customer approval) |
|---|---|
| "Lights are blinding" in a low-ceiling room | Diffuse lens, lower output (high-end trim), wider/indirect distribution |
| Glare in computer screens | Reposition desks, add louvers, avoid luminaires directly behind/above viewers at reflective angles |
| Light trespass into neighbor's windows | House-side shields, change to lower backlight (B) rating optics, re-aim |
| Dark spots between poles | Repair outages, correct optic type (Type III/IV/V distribution), add poles if needed |
| Wall pack glare for drivers | Replace unshielded wall packs with full-cutoff models; lower output |

> **Safety:** Disability glare is a safety issue, not just comfort. Glare at loading docks,
> parking entrances and stairways can hide pedestrians, steps and hazards. When adjusting aiming or
> installing shields on energized exterior fixtures, de-energize and apply LOTO first, and follow MEWP
> procedures for pole-mounted work.

## Key Takeaways
- Average illuminance alone does not describe lighting quality – also report uniformity.
- avg:min and max:min (lower = more uniform); always state which ratio you used.
- Poor uniformity usually comes from wide spacing, wrong optics, outages or new obstructions.
- Discomfort, disability and reflected glare are different problems with different fixes.
- UGR is a calculated interior glare index; BUG ratings (IES TM-15) describe outdoor backlight, uplight and glare.
- Glare can be a safety hazard – treat complaints seriously.
