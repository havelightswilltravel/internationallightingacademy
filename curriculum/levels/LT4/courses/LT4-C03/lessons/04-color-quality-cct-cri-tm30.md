## Color Has Two Separate Questions

1. **What color is the light itself?** – warm or cool appearance → **CCT**.
2. **How well does the light reveal object colors?** → **color rendition** (CRI, TM-30).

They are independent. A 4000K lamp can render colors well or poorly. LT1-C05 introduced these terms;
this lesson goes deeper so you can match products, diagnose color complaints and explain
specifications.

## Correlated Color Temperature (CCT)

CCT is expressed in kelvin (K). Lower = warmer (yellow-orange), higher = cooler (blue-white).

| CCT | Appearance | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 2200–2700K | Very warm | Hospitality, residential, some exterior "dark sky" applications |
| 3000K | Warm white | Retail, hospitality, many offices |
| 3500K | Neutral-warm | Offices, schools |
| 4000K | Neutral/cool white | Offices, schools, healthcare, industrial |
| 5000K+ | Cool, bluish | Industrial, warehouses, some exterior, inspection |

ANSI C78.377 defines standard **nominal CCT bins** and tolerances for solid-state lighting. Two
products both labeled "4000K" can still look different side by side.

### Color consistency: MacAdam ellipses / SDCM
Spec sheets often list color consistency in **MacAdam ellipse steps** or **SDCM** (standard deviation
of color matching). A **2- or 3-step** product looks consistent between fixtures; 5–7 step products
can show visible differences, especially on walls lit by multiple fixtures. When replacing a few
fixtures in an existing room, matching manufacturer, CCT **and** consistency avoids a "patchwork"
ceiling.

### Duv
Duv describes whether a light is slightly greenish (positive) or pinkish (negative) relative to the
blackbody line at the same CCT. Two lights with the same CCT but different Duv look different.
If a customer says new fixtures "look green," Duv is a suspect.

## Color Rendering Index (CRI)

**CRI (Ra)** compares how a source renders eight standard pastel color samples (R1–R8) compared with
a reference source of the same CCT. Maximum is 100.

| CRI Ra | General guidance |
|---|---|
| 90+ | High-fidelity – retail, healthcare exam, museums, high-end spaces |
| 80–89 | Typical commercial standard for offices and schools |
| 70–79 | Industrial and exterior, where color matters less |

**R9** (saturated red) is not part of Ra. Many LEDs score poorly on R9, making skin, meat, wood
and red objects look dull. Specifications for retail and healthcare often require a minimum R9.
Red is also used on safety signage and equipment – poor red rendering can matter for visibility of
fire equipment and warning colors.

## IES TM-30

**ANSI/IES TM-30** is a newer, more detailed method that evaluates 99 color samples:

- **Rf (fidelity index, 0–100)** – how closely colors match the reference. Similar in concept to
  CRI but more accurate.
- **Rg (gamut index, around 100 = neutral)** – whether colors appear more saturated (>100) or less
  saturated (<100) on average.
- **Color vector graphic** – a circular chart showing which hues are shifted and in which direction.

Example: Rf 88, Rg 104 means good fidelity with slightly increased saturation – often preferred in
retail. Rf 78, Rg 92 means lower fidelity and duller colors.

## Field Applications

| Situation | What to check |
|---|---|
| Spot-replacing fixtures | Same CCT, same consistency (SDCM), ideally same manufacturer/series |
| "The new lights look blue/harsh" | CCT higher than original; glare; high-end trim at 100% |
| "Food/skin looks gray" | Low R9 or low Rf; suggest higher-CRI product |
| Mixed fixtures look different | Different CCT bins, different Duv or consistency |
| TLED retrofit in a gallery or clinic | Confirm CRI/TM-30 meets spec, not just lumens |

Color complaints are often blamed on "bad LEDs" when the real issue is a specification mismatch. A
senior tech who can read the spec sheet and explain the difference saves callbacks.

> **Safety:** Do not judge color under a fixture by staring into it. High-luminance LED sources can
> cause discomfort and temporary visual impairment. Evaluate color by looking at illuminated objects
> and surfaces, and never look directly into lasers or high-intensity sources on a lift.

## Key Takeaways
- CCT describes the color of the light; CRI and TM-30 describe how well it reveals object colors.
- ANSI C78.377 defines CCT bins; MacAdam/SDCM steps describe fixture-to-fixture consistency (2–3 step is tight).
- CRI Ra uses eight pastel samples; check R9 for saturated red performance.
- TM-30 uses Rf (fidelity) and Rg (gamut) with a color vector graphic for a fuller picture.
- When replacing fixtures, match CCT, consistency and color rendering, not just lumens.
