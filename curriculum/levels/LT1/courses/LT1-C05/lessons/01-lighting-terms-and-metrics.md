## Speaking the Language of Light

Customers, supervisors, and product labels use a handful of terms constantly. If you know
them, you can read a lamp box, match a replacement correctly, and answer customer questions
with confidence. Getting them wrong leads to mismatched lamps, unhappy customers, and return
trips.

## Watts Are Not Brightness

**Watts (W)** measure the **power the lamp uses**, not how much light it makes. For decades
people bought "a 60-watt bulb" expecting a certain brightness because nearly every household
lamp was incandescent. Now a 9 W LED can produce the same light as a 60 W incandescent.

**Lumens (lm)** measure the **total visible light output** of a lamp or fixture. When replacing a
lamp with a different technology, match lumens (and the other properties below), not watts.

| Incandescent watts (old reference) | Approximate lumens | Typical LED replacement |
|---|---|---|
| 40 W | 450 lm | 5–6 W |
| 60 W | 800 lm | 8–10 W |
| 75 W | 1,100 lm | 11–13 W |
| 100 W | 1,600 lm | 14–17 W |

## Efficacy: Lumens per Watt

**Efficacy** tells you how efficiently a light source converts electricity into light:

> **Efficacy = lumens ÷ watts** (lm/W)

| Source (typical ranges) | Efficacy |
|---|---|
| Incandescent | 10–17 lm/W |
| Halogen | 15–25 lm/W |
| Compact fluorescent (CFL) | 50–70 lm/W |
| Linear fluorescent (T8/T5 with electronic ballast) | 80–100 lm/W |
| Metal halide (MH) | 65–115 lm/W |
| High-pressure sodium (HPS) | 80–140 lm/W |
| LED (lamps and fixtures) | 80–180+ lm/W |

Efficacy figures depend on whether they are for the lamp alone or the whole system (lamp plus
ballast or fixture losses). **System efficacy** is what matters for energy savings.

The **DesignLights Consortium (DLC)** publishes lists of qualified commercial LED products that
meet efficacy and quality requirements. Many utility rebate programs require DLC-listed products.
LT4-C02 covers this in detail.

## Color Temperature (CCT)

**Correlated color temperature (CCT)** describes whether white light looks warm (yellowish) or
cool (bluish). It is measured in **kelvin (K)**. Counterintuitively, **lower kelvin looks warmer.**

| CCT | Appearance | Common uses |
|---|---|---|
| 2700 K | Warm white, like incandescent | Homes, hospitality, restaurants |
| 3000 K | Warm/soft white | Retail, hotels, offices wanting a warmer feel |
| 3500 K | Neutral | Offices, schools |
| 4000 K | Cool white / neutral | Offices, schools, warehouses, healthcare |
| 5000 K | Daylight / crisp | Warehouses, manufacturing, parking, display areas |
| ~2000–2100 K | Orange-gold | High-pressure sodium street and parking lights |

> **Field rule:** never mix CCTs in the same space or fixture unless the customer asks for it.
> A single 5000 K lamp in a row of 3500 K fixtures is immediately obvious and looks like a
> mistake. Read the CCT on the existing lamp before you order or install replacements.

## Color Rendering (CRI)

**Color rendering index (CRI)**, shown as Ra, measures how accurately a light source shows the
colors of objects compared with a reference source, on a scale up to 100.

| CRI | Quality | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 90+ | Excellent | Museums, retail, healthcare exam rooms, high-end LED |
| 80–89 | Good | Most offices and schools; standard LED and quality T8 |
| 70–79 | Fair | Older fluorescent, low-cost LED, warehouses |
| Below 70 | Poor | Mercury vapor, HPS (about 20–25) |

CRI and CCT are separate: a 4000 K lamp can have a CRI of 70 or 95. A newer metric, **TM-30**,
gives a more detailed picture of color quality (LT4-C03).

## Lamp Life and Lumen Maintenance

- **Rated life** for traditional lamps is usually the hours at which half of a large test group
  has failed. Life depends on burn hours, starts, and operating conditions.
- **LEDs** rarely "burn out"; they slowly dim. LED life is often given as **L70**, the hours until
  output falls to 70% of its initial lumens (LT3-C01).
- All light sources lose output over time and as fixtures get dirty. That is why group relamping
  and cleaning restore light levels.

## Reading a Lamp Label or Box

Typical information you will find: lamp type and shape, wattage, lumens, CCT, CRI, base type,
voltage, rated life, ballast compatibility (for fluorescent and LED tubes), dimmability,
and environmental notes (mercury content, "Hg" symbol, or recycling instructions).

## Key Takeaways
- Watts measure power used; lumens measure light produced.
- Efficacy (lm/W) tells you how efficient a source is; LEDs lead, incandescent trails.
- Lower kelvin looks warmer; match the CCT of existing lamps.
- CRI measures color accuracy; 80+ is typical for offices, 90+ for color-critical spaces.
- LED life is usually rated as L70, the point where output has dropped to 70%.
