## Lamp Bases

The **base** connects the lamp to the socket. Matching the base is the first step in ordering
the right lamp.

### Screw Bases (Edison)
The "E" number is the diameter in millimeters.

| Base | Name | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| E12 | Candelabra | Decorative and chandelier lamps, some exit signs |
| E17 | Intermediate | Appliance and some decorative lamps |
| **E26** | **Medium** | Standard household and commercial A-lamps, PARs, many LED lamps |
| **E39** | **Mogul** | HID lamps and large LED HID replacements |

### Pin and Twist-Lock Bases

| Base | Description | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **G13** | Medium bi-pin, 13 mm spacing | T8 and T12 fluorescent and many TLEDs |
| **G5** | Miniature bi-pin, 5 mm spacing | T5 fluorescent |
| **Fa8** | Single pin | 8-ft slimline T8/T12 |
| **R17d** | Recessed double contact | 8-ft HO T8/T12 |
| G23, G24d | 2-pin plug-in | Compact fluorescent with magnetic ballast |
| G24q, GX24q, 2G11 | 4-pin plug-in | Compact fluorescent with electronic ballast |
| **GU24** | Twist-lock, two pins | Energy-efficient fixtures; prevents installing incandescent screw-in lamps |
| GU10 | Twist-lock, line voltage | Line-voltage halogen and LED spot lamps |
| GU5.3 | Bi-pin | 12 V MR16 lamps |

## Ballasts and Drivers: What They Do

Gas discharge lamps (fluorescent and HID) have a property called **negative resistance**: once the
arc starts, the lamp would draw more and more current until it destroyed itself. A **ballast**:

1. Provides a **high voltage to start** the arc.
2. **Limits the current** once the lamp is running.
3. In many cases, conditions the power (corrects power factor, reduces flicker).

| Device | Used with | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluorescent ballast** (electronic or magnetic) | Linear and compact fluorescent | Start and regulate lamps |
| **HID ballast** (with ignitor and capacitor as needed) | MH, HPS, MV | Start and regulate HID lamps |
| **LED driver** | LED modules and fixtures | Convert AC to regulated DC |
| **Transformer** | Low-voltage halogen and some LED tape | Step down voltage (e.g., 120 V to 12 V) |

### Reading a Ballast or Driver Label
Every ballast and driver has a label with critical information:

- **Input voltage** (120, 277, 120–277 V UNV, 347–480 V)
- **Lamp type and quantity** (e.g., "(2) F32T8") or LED output (current/voltage ranges)
- **Input current and watts**
- **Wiring diagram** with wire colors
- Start method (instant, rapid, programmed; covered in LT2-C04)
- Listing mark (UL, ETL, CSA), class P thermal protection, sound rating, and date code
- For older ballasts: whether it contains **PCBs** (LT1-C06)

When you replace a ballast or driver, the new one must match the lamps, the number of lamps, and
the supply voltage. **Photograph the old label and wiring** before removing it.

## Common Fixture (Luminaire) Types

The NEC and industry use the word **luminaire** for a complete lighting unit: the housing,
optics, sockets, ballast or driver, and lamps or LEDs.

| Fixture | Where found | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Troffer** (2x4, 2x2, 1x4) | Offices, schools, healthcare | Recessed into lay-in ceiling grid; lens or louver door |
| **Strip / channel** | Back rooms, stockrooms, garages | Exposed lamps on a wiring channel |
| **Wraparound** | Corridors, stairwells | Surface-mounted with acrylic lens |
| **Recessed downlight** ("can") | Lobbies, corridors, homes | Must be rated for the ceiling (IC vs. non-IC) |
| **High-bay / low-bay** | Warehouses, gyms, manufacturing | High-bay generally for mounting heights over about 20 ft |
| **Vapor-tight** | Car washes, parking garages, cold storage | Sealed and gasketed for wet or dusty locations |
| **Wall pack** | Building exteriors | Mounted on walls for perimeter lighting |
| **Area / pole ("shoebox")** | Parking lots | On poles; often photocell controlled (LT3-C05) |
| **Flood** | Building facades, signs, sports | Aimable |
| **Track** | Retail, galleries | Adjustable heads on a powered track |
| **Exit sign / emergency unit** | Egress paths | Life-safety equipment; tested on schedule (LT3-C04) |

### Ratings to Look For
- **Listing mark** (UL, ETL, CSA): the fixture was tested to the applicable safety standard
  (UL 1598 is the main standard for luminaires).
- **Damp location** vs. **wet location**: a damp-rated fixture (covered porches, some bathrooms)
  is not suitable where water can directly hit it; wet locations require a wet-rated fixture.
- **IC-rated** recessed fixtures may contact building insulation. **Non-IC** fixtures need clearance
  from insulation to avoid overheating and fire (see NEC Article 410).
- **Maximum lamp wattage** label: never exceed it.

> **Safety:** Never install a lamp with higher wattage than the fixture label allows, a lamp type the
> fixture is not designed for, or a fixture in a location it is not rated for. Overheated fixtures
> are a fire hazard.

## Key Takeaways
- Match the lamp base first: E26 medium, E39 mogul, G13 for T8/T12, G5 for T5.
- Ballasts start gas discharge lamps and limit their current; LED drivers convert AC to DC.
- Read and photograph ballast and driver labels before replacing them.
- Know common fixture types and where they are used.
- Respect fixture ratings: listing, damp/wet, IC/non-IC, and maximum wattage.
