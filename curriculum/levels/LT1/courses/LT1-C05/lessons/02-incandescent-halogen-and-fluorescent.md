## Incandescent Lamps

An **incandescent** lamp passes current through a thin tungsten filament until it glows white
hot. About 90% of the energy becomes heat, not light.

- Very low efficacy (10–17 lm/W) and short life (about 750–2,000 hours).
- Excellent color rendering (CRI ~100), warm color (~2700 K), and simple dimming.
- Federal efficiency standards have removed most general-service incandescent lamps from the
  market. You will still find them in older buildings and specialty uses.

## Halogen Lamps

A **halogen** lamp is an incandescent lamp with a small quartz capsule filled with halogen gas,
which lets the filament run hotter and last longer. Types you will see: PAR and MR16 reflector
lamps, T3 double-ended lamps in floodlights and torchieres, and bi-pin capsules (G4, G9).

> **Safety:** Halogen capsules run **extremely hot** and can burn skin and ignite nearby
> materials. Let them cool fully before relamping. **Do not touch the quartz with bare fingers**;
> skin oils create hot spots that can make the lamp fail early or even shatter. Use gloves or the
> packaging sleeve. Many halogen fixtures require a protective lens; never operate them with a
> missing or broken lens.

Low-voltage halogen (for example, 12 V MR16) uses a transformer in the fixture or remotely.

## Fluorescent Lamps

A **fluorescent** lamp is a glass tube filled with low-pressure gas and a small amount of
**mercury**. An electric arc through the mercury vapor produces ultraviolet light, which makes the
phosphor coating inside the tube glow. Fluorescent lamps **cannot** connect directly to line
voltage; they need a **ballast** to start the arc and limit current (Lesson 4).

### Linear Fluorescent Tubes

The "T" number is the tube diameter in **eighths of an inch**.

| Type | Diameter | Common lengths | Base | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T12** | 12/8" = 1.5" | 2, 4, 8 ft | Medium bi-pin (G13); 8-ft slimline single pin (Fa8) or HO recessed double contact (R17d) | Older; most T12 lamps and ballasts have been phased out by federal efficiency standards |
| **T8** | 8/8" = 1" | 2, 3, 4, 8 ft | Medium bi-pin (G13); 8-ft single pin or R17d | The most common commercial fluorescent lamp |
| **T5 / T5HO** | 5/8" | Metric lengths (roughly 22", 34", 46", 58") | Miniature bi-pin (G5) | High-bay and slim fixtures; **not interchangeable** with T8 |

T8 and T12 lamps share the G13 base and 4-ft length, but they generally need **different
ballasts**. Installing the wrong lamp on a ballast can cause poor starting, early failure, or
overheating. Always match the lamp to the ballast label.

### Decoding a Lamp Code
Example: **F32T8/841**
- **F** = fluorescent
- **32** = 32 watts (nominal)
- **T8** = 1-inch diameter
- **8** = CRI in the 80s
- **41** = 4100 K color temperature

Other common suffixes: **/ES** or **/SS** (energy-saving reduced wattage, such as 28 W or 25 W
T8), **/ALTO** or **/ECO** (reduced mercury), and **HO** (high output). Codes vary by
manufacturer, so check the catalog when in doubt.

### Compact Fluorescent Lamps (CFL)

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Screw-in (self-ballasted) | Ballast built into the base; fits standard sockets |
| Pin-based, 2-pin (e.g., G23, G24d) | Starter built into lamp base; used with magnetic ballasts |
| Pin-based, 4-pin (e.g., G24q, GX24q, 2G11) | Used with electronic ballasts; often dimmable |

Pin-based CFLs must match the socket and ballast exactly; the base keying prevents many but not
all mix-ups.

## End-of-Life Signs (Fluorescent)

- **Blackened ends:** normal aging; the lamp is near the end of life.
- **Flickering, slow starting, or "swirling":** lamp aging, a failing ballast, cold temperatures, or
  a bad socket connection.
- **Ends glowing, middle dark:** usually a ballast or lamp issue; report it.
- **Pink or dim output:** often an end-of-life lamp.

At LT1, you replace lamps; if new lamps do not fix the problem, report it for ballast or
socket troubleshooting (LT2-C04).

> **Safety:** Fluorescent and CFL lamps contain mercury. Handle them so they do not break,
> and recycle them as universal waste (LT1-C06).

## Key Takeaways
- Incandescent and halogen lamps are inefficient and hot; most general-service incandescent lamps are no longer sold.
- Never touch halogen quartz with bare fingers, and let lamps cool before handling.
- Fluorescent lamps need a ballast and contain mercury.
- T-numbers are diameter in eighths of an inch; T5 lamps are not interchangeable with T8.
- Read lamp codes (like F32T8/841) to match wattage, diameter, CRI, and CCT.
