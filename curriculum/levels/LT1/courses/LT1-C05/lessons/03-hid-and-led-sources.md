## High-Intensity Discharge (HID) Lamps

**HID** lamps create light with an electric arc inside a small, high-pressure **arc tube**, which
sits inside an outer glass bulb. They produce a lot of light from a compact source, which made
them the standard for warehouses, gymnasiums, parking lots, and streets for decades. Like
fluorescents, HID lamps need a **ballast** (and often an **ignitor** and **capacitor**), and they
contain **mercury**.

| Type | Light color | Typical CRI | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mercury vapor (MV)** | Bluish-white | ~15–50 | Oldest and least efficient; manufacture and import of MV ballasts was prohibited in the U.S. starting in 2008. Still found in old installations |
| **Metal halide (MH)** | White | ~65–90 | Common in high-bays, sports lighting, parking lots |
| **High-pressure sodium (HPS)** | Orange-gold (~2000–2100 K) | ~20–25 | Very efficient; streetlights and parking lots |
| Low-pressure sodium (LPS) | Monochromatic yellow | Near 0 | Rare today; some observatory-area street lighting |

### HID Behavior You Must Know

- **Warm-up:** HID lamps take several minutes to reach full brightness after striking.
- **Restrike delay:** after a power interruption, a hot HID lamp cannot restart until it cools.
  HPS typically restrikes within about a minute or two; metal halide can take **10–20 minutes**.
  Customers often call this a "failed light." Wait before you troubleshoot.
- **End of life (HPS):** **cycling** on and off is the classic sign of an aging HPS lamp.
- **End of life (MH):** color shift (pink, green, or blue tint), dimming, and slow starting.
- **Matching:** each HID lamp must be used with a ballast designed for that lamp type and wattage.
  An MH lamp on an HPS ballast (or vice versa) will not work properly and can be dangerous.

> **Safety: Metal halide rupture.** Metal halide arc tubes can rupture at end of life, and if the
> outer bulb breaks, hot glass fragments can fall and start fires. Follow the lamp's markings:
> lamps rated for **open fixtures** (often marked "O" or "protected") are designed to contain a
> rupture; others must be used **only in enclosed fixtures** with an intact lens. Follow the
> manufacturer's guidance for continuously operated MH lamps (for example, turning them off for a
> period each week) and group-relamp near rated life.

> **Safety: UV exposure.** If the outer bulb of an MH or MV lamp is broken while the lamp still
> operates, it can emit harmful ultraviolet radiation that burns skin and eyes. Turn it off and
> replace it immediately. Lamps marked "T" are designed to self-extinguish when the outer bulb breaks.

HID fixtures run **very hot**. Allow time to cool before servicing, and note that HID ballast
capacitors can store a charge after power is removed.

## LED Light Sources

**Light-emitting diodes (LEDs)** are semiconductors that produce light when DC current flows
through them. A white LED is usually a blue LED chip with a phosphor coating that converts some
blue light to other colors.

### LED Advantages
- High efficacy (often 100+ lm/W for fixtures).
- Long life; output fades slowly rather than failing suddenly (rated as L70).
- **Instant on**, with no warm-up or restrike delay.
- Directional light, which means less is wasted inside the fixture.
- No mercury; many LED products are easier to dispose of than lamps containing mercury.
- Good dimming and control capability (LT3).

### LED Components You Will Hear About

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| LED chips / module / board | Produce the light |
| **Driver** | Converts AC line voltage to the regulated DC the LEDs need |
| Heat sink | Draws heat away from the LEDs; **heat is the main enemy of LED life** |
| Optics / lens | Shape and spread the light, reduce glare |

### LED Forms

| Form | Description |
|---|---|
| Screw-in and pin-based LED lamps | Replace incandescent, halogen, and CFL lamps; driver built in |
| **Tubular LED (TLED)** | Replace fluorescent tubes; several types with different wiring (LT2-C05) |
| Retrofit kits | Replace the guts of an existing fixture (LT2-C05) |
| Integrated LED fixtures | Entire new fixture with LEDs and driver built in |
| Corn-cob / HID replacement lamps | Replace HID lamps in existing fixtures; usually require ballast bypass |

> **Safety:** Some LED tubes and HID replacement lamps require **rewiring the fixture to bypass the
> ballast**. This changes the fixture and must follow the product instructions and labeling
> requirements. At LT1, **never install a TLED or LED retrofit lamp without your supervisor's
> direction**; installing the wrong type in a fixture can create shock and fire hazards.

## Quick Comparison

| | Incandescent | Fluorescent | Metal halide | HPS | LED |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Efficacy | Very low | Good | Good | Very good | Excellent |
| Warm-up | Instant | Near instant | Minutes | Minutes | Instant |
| Restrike | Instant | Instant | 10–20 min | ~1–2 min | Instant |
| Mercury | No | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Needs | Nothing | Ballast | Ballast (+ ignitor/capacitor) | Ballast + ignitor | Driver |

## Key Takeaways
- HID types include MV, MH, and HPS; all need matched ballasts and contain mercury.
- Hot HID lamps need time to restrike: about 1–2 minutes for HPS and 10–20 minutes for MH.
- Use MH lamps only in fixtures that match their rating (open vs. enclosed), and replace broken-outer-bulb lamps immediately.
- LEDs use drivers to convert AC to DC; heat management drives LED life.
- Install TLEDs or LED retrofit lamps only under supervisor direction.
