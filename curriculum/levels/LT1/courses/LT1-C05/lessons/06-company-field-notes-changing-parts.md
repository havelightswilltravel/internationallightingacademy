## Why This Lesson Exists

The light sources you learned in Lessons 1 to 4 are not standing still. At our company's
monthly meetings, a regular agenda item is **"what changed in our stock this month"** - which
lamps and ballasts are gone, what replaces them, and what the office needs from you to order
the right thing. This lesson collects those field notes. At LT1 your job is mostly to
**recognize** these parts and **write them up well**; the conversion work itself is taught in
LT2-C05.

## HID Is Being Phased Out

Metal halide (MH) lamps and ballasts are being discontinued by manufacturers. Common MH ballast
wattages are already gone from our stock, and the last 400 W MH lamp types are going away too.
Our company is working through its remaining HID ballasts and will not restock them. Going
forward, a failed HID system is fixed with:

1. **An LED HID-replacement lamp** - usually a ballast-bypass lamp - when the fixture can take it
   and the customer allows it (LT2-C05), or
2. **A new LED fixture.**

What this means for you on an HID call:

- **Record the existing lamp exactly** - wattage, type and **shape/size**. HID lamps come in
  different envelope sizes (for example a larger BT-shape and a smaller ED-shape). LED
  replacement lamps also come in different body sizes, and the one we choose has to fit the
  fixture.
- **Photograph the fixture**, inside and out, and measure the room around the lamp.
- **Some LED replacement lamps are color-selectable** (a switch sets 3000 K, 4000 K or 5000 K, for
  example). Write the color you need on your pull sheet so the right lamp comes out with you.
- **Some customers do not allow ballast-bypass lamps** in their exterior fixtures yet. Learn each
  customer's standard from your supervisor; when in doubt, write it up rather than convert.

> **Safety:** Converting a fixture to a ballast-bypass lamp means rewiring the fixture to line
> voltage. That is LT2 work, done locked out, and the fixture must be labeled as converted.

## When a Product Line Disappears

Sometimes an entire product line is discontinued - one manufacturer's whole line of
refrigerated-case ballasts, for example. Do not be the last contractor hunting for an obsolete
part. Bring back what the office needs to find an LED option or another ballast:

- **Photos of the case**, the plug and receptacle, and the ballast label
- **Measurements** (lamp length, case length)
- **Color temperature** of the existing lamps
- **Lamp type and quantity per ballast**, and the number of cases

## Plug-In Drivers and Power Supplies

LED fixtures and display lights increasingly use plug-in drivers. The same wattage can come with
many different connector tips and cord caps.

- **Always photograph the secondary (output) cord cap or tip** - and the label.
- **Best practice: bring back a labeled sample.**
- For low-voltage (12 V or 24 V) supplies, record the output voltage, wattage and the connector.

## Lenses

- **Flat prismatic lenses:** our vendor cuts them to size for only a little more than an uncut
  lens. That means **always take a precise measurement along with your count.** If you already
  have a lens on the truck and can cut it on site to finish the job, do that.
- **Wrap lenses are not always available.** Trace the lens and bring back a sample, **photograph
  the fixture, and count how many matching fixtures are in the area.** If the lens cannot be
  found, the fix may be new fixtures - and one new fixture in a row of old ones can look wrong, so
  the office may quote the whole area.

## Clean Fixtures Are Part of the Service

Our company still cleans fixtures as part of service, and our customers notice when we don't.
Customers have told us that bugs "sure do love" the fixtures we maintain.

- **Bugs, cobwebs and dust bunnies** are the giveaway that a fixture needs cleaning - indoors and
  outdoors, including lenses and sign faces.
- Every truck carries rags and cleaner on the pull sheet. Clean the fixture while it is open.
- **Write it in your notes** when you clean fixtures, so the customer knows you did.

> **Safety:** Clean inside a fixture only with it locked out and verified dead, and let lamps
> cool first. Use the cleaner the company provides - its Safety Data Sheet is in the shop.

## Key Takeaways

- Metal halide is being phased out; failed HID systems are moving to LED replacement lamps or new LED fixtures.
- Record HID lamp wattage, type and size, and photograph the fixture so the right replacement fits.
- Note the color you need for color-selectable lamps, and follow each customer's standard on ballast-bypass lamps.
- For discontinued parts, bring back photos, measurements, color temperature and lamp counts per ballast.
- Photograph the output tip or cord cap of plug-in drivers; bring a labeled sample when you can.
- Measure flat lenses precisely; trace wrap lenses, photograph the fixture and count the area.
- Clean bugs and dust out of fixtures and note it on the work order.
