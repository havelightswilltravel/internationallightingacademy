## Your Job on a Fluorescent Call

At LT1 you relamp, inspect and report. Deeper testing (ballast diagnosis, energized
measurements) belongs to a qualified technician (LT2-C04). But the company's fluorescent
procedure starts with steps you can do on day one, and doing them in the right order solves
most calls before anyone opens a ballast channel.

## The Parts of a Fluorescent / CFL System

| Component | What it does | How it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Fixture (luminaire) | Holds everything; channel cover hides wiring | Damaged lens, loose channel, water damage |
| Lamp | Makes the light | Blackened ends, flicker, dim/pink, won't start |
| Ballast | Starts the lamp and limits its current | Lamps dark, glowing ends only, hum, heat, burning smell |
| Igniter / starter | Helps the lamp strike (on 2-pin CFLs it is built into the lamp base) | Lamp blinks but never lights |
| Sockets (lamp holders) | Hold the lamp and connect its pins | Cracked, burnt, loose; one lamp out |
| Wire | Connects ballast, sockets and supply | Burnt insulation, loose push-in or splice |
| Switch / control | Turns the fixture on and off | Whole fixture or group dark |

## The Company Sequence, Step by Step

1. **Check what else is out.** One fixture, a row, or a whole room? A whole room or circuit
   dark points to the switch, control or breaker — report it before you climb.
2. **Look at the lamp ends.** Blackened ends mean the lamp is worn out. **If the ends are
   black, change the lamps.** Replace with the exact type on the ballast label (for example
   F32T8/841 or a 4-pin 26 W CFL with the same base).
3. **If the ends are not black,** the lamps may still be bad, but the next question is
   whether power is reaching the fixture. A **voltage check with a meter** is required. At
   LT1, that reading is taken by a qualified technician unless your employer has trained and
   authorized you; you can assist and record it.
4. **Power present and all lamps out:** the ballast (or on some CFL and HID fixtures, the
   igniter) is the likely cause. Write it up for ballast replacement, or do it supervised if
   you are signed off on LT1-S06.
5. **Power present and some lamps working:** check the **sockets and wiring** for the dark
   lamps. A cracked socket or a pin not seated is common, especially after a careless relamp.
6. **No power:** check for a **switch** — wall switch, occupancy sensor, or a switch on the
   fixture itself. If the switch is on and there is still no power, the problem is between the
   fixture and the **first junction box** upstream. That tracing is LT2 work; report what you
   found.
7. **T5 fixtures relamped but still dark:** many T5 ballasts shut themselves off when a lamp
   fails or is removed, and they won't restart until their power is turned off and back on.
   Turn the fixture off at its switch or disconnect, wait as the ballast label says, and turn it
   back on. Ask your lead before turning off any breaker that feeds other areas.

> **Safety:** Relamp with the fixture switched off and locked out per company relamping
> procedure (LT1-C06). Wear eye protection — lamps can break. Never touch socket contacts or
> open a ballast channel on a fixture you have not locked out and verified dead with a tested
> meter (live-dead-live, LT1-C03).

## Compact Fluorescent Notes

- **Screw-in CFL:** the ballast is in the lamp base. A dark screw-in CFL is a lamp problem
  until a known-good lamp proves otherwise.
- **2-pin CFL:** the starter (igniter) is inside the lamp base, so a new lamp is also a new
  starter. If a new 2-pin lamp won't light, report a possible ballast or socket problem.
- **4-pin CFL:** works with an electronic ballast in the fixture. A new lamp that won't light
  points to the ballast, socket or power.
- CFL sockets get brittle from heat. Push lamps straight in and pull straight out.

## Parts Identification for the Work Order

Write down what the next tech needs to bring:

| Item | What to record | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Lamp | Full code (e.g., F28T5/835, CFT26W/GX24q/841) and quantity | Printed on the lamp |
| Ballast | Brand, model, input voltage, lamp type/quantity, start method | Ballast label (photograph it) |
| Socket | Type (shunted/non-shunted, G5, G13, G24q-3) | Old socket or fixture catalog |
| Fixture | Type, size, location number | Fixture label, plan |

## When to Stop and Report

- Whole circuit or room dark
- Burning smell, scorched wiring, leaking or very hot ballast
- Lamps still dark after a known-good lamp and a T5 power reset
- Any repair that requires energized testing or opening junction boxes you are not trained for

## Key Takeaways
- Check what else is out first, then look at the lamp ends — blackened ends mean change the lamps.
- Power present with all lamps out points to the ballast or igniter; some lamps out points to sockets or wiring.
- No power means check the switch, then the circuit back to the first junction box (report it at LT1).
- T5 ballasts often need their power cycled after relamping.
- Record lamp codes, ballast label details and socket types so the right parts arrive.
