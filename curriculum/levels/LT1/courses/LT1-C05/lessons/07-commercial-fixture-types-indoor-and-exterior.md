## Why Fixture Names Matter

On every service call you will be asked "what kind of light is it?" Dispatch, the supply house,
the office and the customer all use the same short list of **common fixture names**: troffer,
wraparound, wallpack, area light, canopy. If you write "light out by the door," the office cannot
tell whether to send a ladder or a lift, which parts to order, or what a replacement fixture
should look like. If you write "wallpack, 12 ft, north wall by dock door 3," everyone knows.

This lesson covers the 24 commercial fixture types you will see most. Lesson 4 introduced
several of them; here you learn to recognize all of them on sight and know what to expect when
you service each one.

## Indoor Fixtures

"Legacy" sources are what you may find in older fixtures; "LED" is what is installed today.

| Fixture | What it looks like | Where you find it | Common sources | On a service call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Strip light** | Narrow metal channel with 1–2 bare lamps or an LED board; no lens or a small snap-on lens | Back rooms, stockrooms, mechanical rooms, coves; surface or chain/cable hung | Legacy: T8/T12 fluorescent. LED: TLEDs or integrated LED strip | Ladder. Lamps, ballast or driver, sockets. Note length (4 or 8 ft), lamp count, mounting |
| **Wraparound** | Linear fixture with a curved acrylic lens that wraps over the bottom and sides | Corridors, stairwells, back offices, garages | Legacy: T8/T12 fluorescent. LED: TLEDs or integrated LED | Ladder. Lamps, ballast, cracked or yellowed lens (lenses may be discontinued). Note length, lamp count, lens condition |
| **Troffer** | Rectangular box recessed in a lay-in grid (or surface mounted) with a lens, louver or center basket | Offices, schools, healthcare | Legacy: T8/T12 or T5 fluorescent. LED: TLEDs, LED retrofit kits or integrated LED | Ladder; lift ceiling tiles carefully. Lamps, ballast or driver, sockets. Note size (1x4, 2x2, 2x4), door type, lamp count |
| **Linear high bay** | Wide, long open fixture with several parallel lamps or LED bars, hung by chains or hooks | Warehouses, gyms, manufacturing, big-box stores | Legacy: T5HO or T8 fluorescent. LED: integrated LED bars | Lift. Lamps (often 4–6 T5HO), ballasts, wiring at the cord and plug. Note mounting height, lamp count, cord-and-plug or hardwired |
| **HID high bay** | Round metal or prismatic glass dome reflector under a finned ballast housing | Older warehouses, industrial plants, gyms | Legacy: metal halide (sometimes HPS or mercury vapor) | Lift. Lamp, ballast, ignitor, capacitor; slow start and restrike delay. Note lamp wattage and shape; often written up for LED replacement |
| **LED high bay** | Round, flat "UFO" disk with cooling fins on top, hung from a hook | Warehouses, big-box retail, gyms | LED (integrated) | Lift. Driver or whole-fixture failure, flicker, bad sensor. Note wattage, catalog number, driver label, any sensor |
| **Recessed can** (downlight) | Round opening in a finished ceiling with a trim ring or baffle; housing hidden above | Offices, lobbies, retail, restaurants, corridors | Legacy: incandescent/halogen BR, PAR or A-lamps, pin-based CFL. LED: LED lamps, retrofit modules or integrated LED | Ladder. Lamp, thermal protector tripping, CFL ballast, socket. Note aperture size (4, 5, 6 in), lamp shape and base, IC or non-IC |
| **Pendant** | Fixture hung below the ceiling on a cord, chain or rod; decorative glass or metal shade, or an industrial dome | Restaurants, lobbies, retail, open offices, shops | Legacy: incandescent, CFL, metal halide (industrial). LED: LED lamps or integrated LED | Ladder or lift by height. Lamp, socket, cord. Note hanging height, shade type and how it detaches |
| **Surface mount** | Fixture mounted flat to the ceiling; often a round or square "drum" or low-profile panel | Hallways, stairwells, restrooms, utility and storage areas | Legacy: incandescent, CFL, circline or short fluorescent. LED: LED lamps or integrated LED | Ladder. Lamp or LED board, driver, lens latch. Note size, shape and lens type; stairwell units may be on an emergency circuit |
| **Track lighting** | Adjustable heads clipped onto a powered track | Retail sales floors, displays, galleries, restaurants | Legacy: halogen PAR, MR16 or metal halide heads. LED: LED lamps or LED heads | Ladder. Lamp, head adapter, track connector. Note track type (the adapter must match the track brand system), head count, aiming |
| **Linear slot** | Very narrow (often 2–4 in) recessed line of light set into the ceiling, sometimes running continuously | Modern offices, retail, lobbies | Legacy: T5 fluorescent. LED: integrated LED | Ladder. Driver or LED board; access through the slot. Note run length, section count, driver location |
| **Wall sconce** | Decorative fixture mounted on the wall, often lighting up and down | Corridors, lobbies, stairwells, restrooms | Legacy: incandescent, CFL. LED: LED lamps or integrated LED | Ladder or none. Lamp, socket, glass. Note mounting height and shade/glass type; some are emergency units |

## Exterior Fixtures

| Fixture | What it looks like | Where you find it | Common sources | On a service call |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wallpack** | Box mounted on a building wall with a glass or refractor lens angled forward and down | Building perimeters, dock doors, back doors, security lighting | Legacy: HPS, metal halide, CFL. LED: integrated LED | Ladder or lift by height. Lamp, ballast, photocell, failed gaskets. Note height, wattage, photocell, emergency (battery) option |
| **Flood light** | Square or rectangular aimable head on a knuckle or yoke | Building facades, signs, sports fields, large open areas | Legacy: metal halide, halogen T3. LED: integrated LED | Lift (often). Lamp, ballast, aiming. Note mounting (yoke, knuckle, slipfitter), aiming before removal, wattage |
| **Area light** ("shoebox") | Flat rectangular head on an arm at the top of a pole, sometimes on a wall | Parking lots, large outdoor areas | Legacy: metal halide, HPS. LED: integrated LED | Bucket truck. Lamp, ballast, photocell, circuit or pole base problems. Note pole number, head count per pole, arm type, pole height |
| **Canopy light** | Square or round low-profile fixture under a canopy ceiling | Gas stations, store entrances, loading areas, drive-throughs | Legacy: metal halide. LED: integrated LED | Lift or tall ladder; traffic control. Lamp, ballast, lens. Note canopy deck height, cutout size and shape (replacement kits must match) |
| **Bollard** | Short post (about 3–4 ft) with a light head at the top | Walkways, entrances, landscaped areas | Legacy: CFL, HID, incandescent. LED: LED lamp or integrated LED | No ladder. Lamp, water inside, damaged post. Note diameter, height, head style, damage (often hit by vehicles or mowers) |
| **Building mount decorative** | Architectural wall fixture such as a cylinder or lantern | Building entrances, walkways, storefronts | Legacy: CFL, metal halide, incandescent. LED: LED lamp or integrated LED | Ladder. Lamp, lens, water entry. Note style and finish so a match can be ordered |
| **Recessed can, exterior** | Downlight recessed into an overhang or soffit, usually with a wet-rated trim and lens | Overhangs, soffits, covered entries, walkways | Legacy: incandescent, CFL. LED: LED lamps or integrated LED | Ladder. Lamp, corroded socket, water in the housing. Note aperture size and that it is wet or damp rated |
| **Step light** | Small rectangular or round fixture set into a wall, with a louvered or shielded face | Stairs, ramps, pathways | Legacy: incandescent, CFL. LED: integrated LED | Kneel or no ladder. Lamp, water damage, faceplate. Note size, faceplate style, how many on the run |
| **In-ground / uplight** | Round lens set flush in the ground or pavement, aimed up | Facades, trees, flagpoles, architectural features | Legacy: halogen PAR, metal halide. LED: integrated LED | No ladder. Water intrusion, burned or cracked lens, damaged wiring. Note diameter, aiming, standing water or damage |
| **Street light** ("cobra head") | Elongated head on a curved arm, high on a pole | Roads, large parking lots, campuses, large sites | Legacy: HPS, mercury vapor, metal halide. LED: integrated LED | Bucket truck; traffic control. Lamp, ballast, photocell. Note pole number and owner (some are utility-owned and not ours to service) |
| **Pole top decorative** | Lantern, globe or acorn-style head on top of a short decorative pole | Campuses, malls, downtown walkways, pedestrian areas | Legacy: metal halide, HPS, CFL. LED: LED lamp or integrated LED | Ladder or lift by height. Lamp, ballast, globe or panels, base access door. Note style, globe condition, pole number |
| **Sign light** | Small flood or gooseneck head aimed at a sign | Building signs, monument signs, building identifiers | Legacy: halogen PAR, metal halide, CFL. LED: LED lamp or integrated LED | Ladder or lift. Lamp, aiming, time clock or photocell. Note mounting and aiming; check sign circuit and controls |

> **Safety:** Fixture type tells you access before you arrive. High bays, area lights, street
> lights and many floods need a lift or bucket truck and a lift-trained tech; canopy and parking
> lot work needs traffic control. Every fixture is serviced locked out and verified dead
> (LT1-C01), and HID fixtures need time to cool.

## How to Tell Similar Fixtures Apart

- **Strip vs. wraparound vs. linear high bay.** A **strip** is narrow with bare lamps or a small
  lens and holds one or two lamps. A **wraparound** is wider and its lens curves around the bottom
  *and* sides. A **linear high bay** is much wider, holds four to six lamps or LED bars with a
  reflector between them, and hangs high (usually over about 20 ft).
- **Wallpack vs. flood vs. area light.** A **wallpack** is a box fixed flat to the wall and
  aimed down and out; you cannot tilt it. A **flood** is a head on a knuckle or yoke that can be
  aimed. An **area light** is on a pole arm (or occasionally a wall arm) and throws a wide pattern
  down onto a parking lot.
- **Recessed can vs. exterior recessed can.** Both look like a round hole with a trim. An
  **exterior** can is in a soffit or overhang, has a wet- or damp-rated label and usually a
  gasketed lens. Never put an interior trim or lamp that is not rated for damp or wet locations
  into an exterior can.
- **Bollard vs. step light.** A **bollard** is a freestanding post you can walk around. A **step
  light** is set *into* a wall or stair riser and sits flush or nearly flush.

## Writing It on the Work Order

Write the common name first, then the details. Good examples:

- "Troffer, 2x4, 3-lamp T8, lens door, office 214."
- "Wallpack, about 14 ft, east wall at dock door 2, LED, no photocell visible."
- "Area light, pole P7, 2 heads, about 25 ft, both out."
- "Canopy, 6 of 12 out, square cutout about 16 in, deck about 15 ft."

When you are not sure of the name, describe it ("round flush light in the walkway, aimed up")
and attach a photo. The office would rather have a photo than a guess.

## Key Takeaways
- Learn the 24 common fixture names and use them on every work order.
- Fixture type tells the office the access (ladder, lift, bucket truck), the likely parts and the right replacement.
- Many fixtures came with fluorescent or HID sources and now use LED; note which one you find.
- Tell similar fixtures apart by width and lens (strip, wrap, linear high bay), mounting and aiming (wallpack, flood, area light), location rating (interior vs exterior can) and mounting (bollard vs step light).
- Record size, count, height, location and a photo with the fixture name.
