## Why This Lesson Exists

Lessons 1 to 4 taught professionalism and documentation in general terms. This lesson is
**our company's version**: the habits our monthly meetings keep coming back to, because each one
has cost us a payment, a callback or a customer's trust. The common thread: **we need to be a
cut above our competition.**

## Rule 1: Finish Everything Before You Drive

Our company rule is that **everything about the service gets done while you are on site and
still clocked in to that job.** Put the truck in drive only when:

- The **work order is complete** - everything except time and pre-pulled parts.
- **Exact part codes** are written for every part used. "24 V driver" is not a part code.
- **Photos are uploaded** to the company photo folder for that service order (not some other
  folder, where nobody will find them) **and** to the customer's work-order portal if they use
  one. In portals, set notes and photos so **the customer can see them** - internal-only notes
  never reach the customer, and some customers deny payment when completion photos are missing.
- The **return sheet** is written.
- **Signatures** are collected on all paperwork, **with the signer's printed name**. Some
  customers question every signature they cannot read.
- The customer's **check-in/check-out** (phone IVR or app) is done. If their system fails, call
  the dispatcher or the customer's service desk and **write it in your notes** - customers can
  charge penalties for missed check-ins.
- Receipts are submitted, equipment is stored and the van is organized.

## Rule 2: Work Order = Done, Return Sheet = Needed

- The **work order** describes **only what you did**. If more work is needed, end with **"see
  return."**
- The **return sheet** lists **only what is still needed**: parts with codes and quantities,
  equipment, number of people, time estimate, access notes and photos.

The office turns your return sheet into a quote. The clearer it is, the faster the customer
gets an answer.

**Record what costs us money.** Write down any equipment you used beyond your standard ladders
and tools (lifts, large ladders, circuit tracers, generator, torches) and job supplies such as
rags, caulk, spray paint, wasp spray, parking fees and batteries. Write the type and weight of
batteries and other spent material you bring back for recycling. If it is not on the work
order, the customer never sees it.

## Rule 3: Respect the NTE and Try to Finish the First Trip

- **Know the not-to-exceed (NTE) amount** before you start. **Once an NTE is blown, we can never
  bill it afterward.**
- A **quote** is for work that needs a return trip. An **NTE increase** is what you request **from
  the site** so you can finish now with the parts and equipment you have.
- **Call before you leave the job.** Customers respond to a tech on site faster than to the
  office calling later.
- Approved or denied, **get the person's name and ask them to email the approval.** Note it on
  the work order.
- Before you write a return, ask yourself: could one phone call let me finish today?

> **Example figures only:** If a job has a $400 NTE and you are at $350 with another hour of
> work, you call for an increase *before* doing that hour - not after.

## Rule 4: Leave It Cleaner Than You Found It

**Fingerprints.** Ceiling tiles show every fingerprint and do not clean well. Company procedure:

1. Photograph any tiles or surfaces that are already dirty or damaged, and note it.
2. **Lift all the tiles** in your work area **with clean hands.**
3. Do the dirty work - remove the old fixtures and install the new ones.
4. **Wipe down the new fixtures, wash your hands, then put the tiles back.**

**Clean the fixtures.** Bugs, cobwebs and dust in fixtures, lenses and sign faces tell the
customer we did not care. Clean them and note it.

**Doors and floors.** Before you bring a lift through a door, inspect the door and frame,
photograph any existing damage and tell the manager - otherwise we will be blamed for it.

> **Safety:** Clean fixtures only when they are locked out and verified dead, and go slow with a
> spotter whenever you move a lift through a doorway.

## Rule 5: Conduct and Appointments

- **Watch your language.** Swearing carries in public places and is noticed.
- **Appointments are not suggestions.** The office will contact you the day before so you can
  review the paperwork and ask questions. Arrive **before** the customer; our company should
  never be associated with being late.
- **Install it right, and question it when it looks wrong.** Center fixtures unless you have
  approval otherwise. If sales wrote "one for one" but the old layout is crooked and a straight
  line is easy, ask before you install.
- **Report what you see.** Damage you find (a cracked pole base, a broken lens elsewhere) gets
  reported to the customer and the office **before** you start - it protects everyone. Lighting
  or electrical problems the customer may want fixed are leads; pass them to the office.

## Key Takeaways

- Finish the paperwork, photos, return sheet, signatures and check-out before you drive away.
- Use exact part codes and put photos where the office and customer can see them.
- Work order = what you did; return sheet = what is still needed.
- Never exceed an NTE; request increases from the site and get the approval by name and email.
- Lift tiles with clean hands, wipe fixtures, wash up, then replace tiles. Clean fixtures and note it.
- Photograph existing damage before you start; report problems you find.
- Arrive early, watch your language, and question layouts that look wrong.
