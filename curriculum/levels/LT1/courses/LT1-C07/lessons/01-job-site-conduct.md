## You Are the Company

For most customers, **you are the only person from our company they ever meet.** The way you
dress, talk, drive, and leave their building is what they remember. Good technical work done
with poor conduct still loses customers. Professional conduct wins repeat work and referrals.

## Before You Arrive

- **Be on time.** If you will be late, call the site contact (or have dispatch call) **before**
  the appointment time with a realistic arrival estimate.
- **Prepare.** Review the work order, site notes, access instructions, and parts list. Confirm
  you have the right lamps, ballasts, ladders, and PPE. A second trip for forgotten parts costs
  money and credibility.
- **Look the part.** Clean company uniform or shirt, ID badge visible, PPE in good condition.
- **Vehicle.** Keep the truck clean and organized. You are driving a billboard with our name on
  it; drive courteously and obey traffic laws.

## Arriving On Site

1. **Park legally.** Never in fire lanes, accessible parking spaces, or loading docks without
   permission.
2. **Check in** with the site contact, front desk, or security. Follow sign-in, badge, and escort
   rules. Many sites (schools, hospitals, secure facilities) have strict access requirements.
3. **Confirm the scope**: "I'm here to replace lamps in the second-floor conference rooms and check
   the corridor fixture that's flickering. Is that right?"
4. **Ask about site rules**: hours when noisy work is allowed, areas that are off-limits, hot work
   permits, safety orientation, restroom location, and where to stage materials.

## While You Work

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Protect floors and furniture with drop cloths | Set ladders or tools on desks or equipment |
| Keep walkways, exits, and fire equipment clear | Block exits, extinguishers, or electrical panels |
| Keep materials staged neatly in one area | Spread boxes and debris across the building |
| Ask before moving customer property | Move computers, files, or merchandise without asking |
| Keep conversation professional | Use profanity, make jokes about the site, or discuss politics |
| Limit personal phone use to breaks | Take personal calls in front of customers |
| Respect privacy and confidentiality | Read documents, screens, or discuss what you see on site |
| Follow the customer's safety rules **and** ours | Assume our rules are the only ones that apply |

> **Safety:** Never let customer pressure, schedules, or politeness push you into an unsafe act.
> "I need to lock out this circuit before I can work on it" is a professional statement. If a
> customer objects, call your supervisor.

## Working in Occupied Spaces

Lighting work often happens around people: offices, classrooms, stores, and hospitals.

- **Barricade** under your ladder and warn people before turning off lights.
- Schedule disruptive work (shutting off a whole area) with the site contact.
- In healthcare, follow infection-control rules (for example, ceiling-access permits and dust
  containment) and patient-privacy rules.
- In schools, follow background-check and escort requirements.
- Be patient with interruptions and questions. A friendly "Give me just a moment to get down from
  the ladder" goes a long way.

## Integrity and Company Property

- Record your time and materials honestly.
- Company tools, materials, and vehicles are for company work only.
- Leftover customer materials (spare lamps the customer paid for) stay with the customer or go back
  as directed; never take them.
- If you damage something, **report it immediately** to the customer and your supervisor. Hiding
  damage is far worse than the damage itself.
- Never accept cash or side jobs from customers; refer requests to the office.

## Leaving the Site

1. Clean up completely: wipe fingerprints off lenses, pick up packaging, sweep up dust.
2. Remove your waste (lamps, ballasts, boxes) unless arrangements were made otherwise.
3. Walk the area to make sure everything works and nothing was left behind.
4. Review the work with the site contact (Lesson 3).
5. Check out per site rules.

## Key Takeaways
- To the customer, you are the company; conduct matters as much as technical skill.
- Be on time, prepared, in uniform, and call ahead if you will be late.
- Check in, confirm scope, and learn site rules before starting.
- Protect the customer's space, keep exits clear, and leave the area cleaner than you found it.
- Be honest about time, materials, and any damage, and never let pressure override safety.
