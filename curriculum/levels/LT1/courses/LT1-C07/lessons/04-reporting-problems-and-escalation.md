## Your Job Includes Seeing Problems

Lighting technicians go into ceilings, electrical rooms, and building corners that few other people
see. You will regularly find problems that are **outside the scope** of your work order. Reporting
them correctly protects people, helps the customer, and creates legitimate follow-up work for the
company.

## What to Report

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Immediate hazards** | Exposed energized wiring, open panels, scorched or melted wiring, burning smell, water near electrical equipment, sparking |
| **Life safety** | Dark exit signs, failed emergency lights, blocked exits, missing ceiling tiles near fire-rated assemblies, damaged sprinkler heads |
| **Code or installation concerns** | Open junction boxes, missing covers, fixtures hanging by their wires, unsupported cables, improper splices, non-IC fixtures buried in insulation |
| **Equipment condition** | Failing ballasts, discolored lenses, corroded fixtures, broken sockets, obsolete lamp types |
| **Environmental** | Leaking ballasts (possible PCBs), broken lamps, suspected asbestos-containing material |
| **Scope changes** | More fixtures out than reported, wrong parts ordered, access problems |
| **Customer requests** | Requests for additional work, pricing questions, complaints |

## How to Escalate: Three Levels

### Level 1: Stop Now
**For immediate danger to people.**
1. Stop work and keep people away. Barricade or post someone if needed.
2. Do not touch exposed energized parts or attempt repairs beyond your training.
3. Call your supervisor **immediately by phone.**
4. Inform the site contact so they can protect their people.
5. Document it with written notes and, if it is safe, photos.

> **Safety:** Do not try to fix a hazard you are not qualified to fix, even if it seems simple. For
> example, do not push exposed wires back into an open box or re-terminate a burned connection. An
> LT1's job is to make the area safe from a distance, report, and wait for a qualified person.

### Level 2: Report Today
**For conditions that are not immediately dangerous but need attention**, such as a failing
emergency light, a damaged socket in another fixture, or an obsolete fixture type.
1. Note the location and condition on the work order with photos.
2. Tell your supervisor before leaving the site.
3. Tell the site contact what you found and that the office will follow up.

### Level 3: Recommend
**For improvement opportunities**, such as fixtures that could be upgraded to LED, areas that seem
under-lit, or controls that do not seem to work as intended.
1. Note them in the recommendations section of the work order.
2. Mention them to the customer without pressure: "I noted a few things that may be worth a look.
   Our office will reach out."

## Who to Call

| Issue | First contact |
|---|---|
| Injury, shock, fire, or immediate danger | 911 (if needed), then your supervisor |
| Unsafe condition, unexpected voltage, LOTO problem | Your supervisor |
| Wrong or missing parts | Supervisor or dispatch |
| Customer complaint or extra-work request | Supervisor or office |
| Environmental spill or leaking ballast | Supervisor (who contacts the environmental/safety lead) |
| Life-safety equipment out of service | Supervisor and the site contact |

Keep your supervisor's number, dispatch, and the safety hotline saved in your phone.

## Good Problem Reports

A good report answers **who, what, where, when, and what you did**:

> "Mon 10/6, 2:15 pm, Building A, 3rd floor, room 312. While relamping, found an open junction box
> above ceiling tile at grid D-2 with three wire nuts and exposed copper on one splice. Did not touch.
> Left the box undisturbed and placed a cone below the opening. Called
> supervisor (R. Patel) at 2:20; he will send an electrician tomorrow. Informed site contact (A. Kim).
> Photos attached."

## Common Mistakes

- **Not reporting because it "isn't my job."** Seeing a hazard and saying nothing can get someone hurt.
- **Reporting only verbally.** Conversations get forgotten; write it down.
- **Fixing it yourself** beyond your training level.
- **Alarming the customer** with guesses. State what you saw and that a qualified person will evaluate it.
- **Waiting until the end of the week** to report.

## Learning From Problems

Every problem you report is a learning opportunity. Ask your supervisor what caused it and how it
was fixed. This builds the troubleshooting judgment you will need in LT2 and beyond.

## Key Takeaways
- You will find problems outside your work order; reporting them is part of the job.
- Immediate hazards: stop, protect people, do not attempt repairs, and call your supervisor by phone.
- Non-urgent issues: document with photos and report the same day.
- Improvement opportunities go in the recommendations section without pressuring the customer.
- Good reports answer who, what, where, when, and what you did.
