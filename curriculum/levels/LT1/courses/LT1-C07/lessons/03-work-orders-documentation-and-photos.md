## Why Documentation Matters

If it is not written down, it did not happen. Work orders are used to:

- **Bill the customer** correctly for labor and materials.
- **Prove what was done** if there is a question or warranty claim later.
- **Track recurring problems**, such as the same fixture failing every few months.
- **Order follow-up work** and parts.
- **Protect you and the company** if an incident or complaint occurs.
- **Log your OJT hours** toward advancement in this program.

## Parts of a Work Order

| Section | What to enter |
|---|---|
| Customer and site | Verify the name, address, building, and contact are correct |
| Arrival / departure times | Actual times, recorded as they happen |
| Requested work | The problem as reported by the customer |
| Location | Building, floor, room number, fixture ID or grid location |
| Findings | What you observed (facts) |
| Work performed | What you did, step by step in summary |
| Materials used | Quantity, description, and part number for each item |
| Waste removed | Lamps, ballasts (PCB / non-PCB), batteries, and quantities |
| Photos | Before, during (if useful), and after |
| Recommendations / follow-up | Additional work needed, parts to order, safety concerns |
| Customer acknowledgment | Signature or approval per company policy |

## Writing Good Findings and Work Descriptions

Write so that someone who was not there can understand exactly what happened.

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "Fixed lights." | "Rm 214: replaced (3) F32T8/835 lamps in 2x4 troffer at grid C-4. All lamps operating." |
| "Ballast bad." | "Rm 214, troffer C-6: lamps would not start with new lamps installed. Supervisor confirmed failed ballast. Replaced with (1) 2-lamp T8 instant-start 120–277 V ballast, P/N XXXX. Fixture operating." |
| "Hallway light needs work." | "2nd floor east corridor, wraparound near stair B: lens cracked, socket on left end burned. Fixture left off; supervisor notified. Needs socket replacement. Recommend quoting." |
| "Customer upset." | "Site contact (M. Lee) reported warehouse aisle 6 lights out since 9/30. Replaced (4) lamps; (2) fixtures still out, suspected ballast. Ordered parts; office to schedule return." |

Tips:

- Use **numbers and units**: quantities, wattages, voltages, room numbers.
- Use **standard abbreviations** your company uses (Rm, qty, P/N), but avoid private shorthand.
- Record **facts**, not guesses or blame.
- Note anything you **could not** complete and why.

## Taking Useful Photos

Photos are evidence. A good photo answers "where, what, and what condition."

1. **Before photos:** take a wide shot showing the location (room number sign, column number, or
   recognizable feature), then a close-up of the problem.
2. **Labels:** photograph ballast, driver, and fixture labels; they are needed for ordering and warranty.
3. **Wiring:** photograph existing connections before disconnecting anything.
4. **Problems found:** burned sockets, damaged wiring, water leaks, unsafe conditions.
5. **After photos:** the completed work, fixture lit, area clean.

Photo quality:

- Use light (flashlight or phone light) and hold steady.
- Make sure the subject is in focus and fills the frame.
- Do not capture people, computer screens, documents, or security-sensitive areas. Follow site photo
  rules; some facilities prohibit photography or require permission.
- Upload photos only to the company system, not personal social media or messaging apps.

> **Safety:** Do not take photos inside energized, open panels or reach toward exposed parts to get a
> better angle. If a picture requires getting close to energized equipment, describe it in writing
> instead and let a qualified person document it.

## Timing and Accuracy

- Complete the work order **as you go**, not from memory at the end of the week.
- **Submit the same day.**
- Double-check part numbers and quantities against what you actually used; these drive billing
  and inventory.
- If you made an error, correct it promptly and tell the office. Never alter records to hide a mistake.

## Closing Out With the Customer

1. Walk the site contact through what was done and what was not.
2. Point out any recommendations and explain that the office will follow up.
3. Obtain the signature or acknowledgment required by policy.
4. Thank them and confirm how to reach the company if anything changes.

## Key Takeaways
- Work orders drive billing, warranty, follow-up, and your OJT hours. If it is not documented, it did not happen.
- Record exact locations, quantities, part numbers, and facts.
- Take before, label, wiring, and after photos that show where and what, following site photo rules.
- Complete work orders as you go and submit them the same day.
- Close out with the customer and note all recommendations for follow-up.
