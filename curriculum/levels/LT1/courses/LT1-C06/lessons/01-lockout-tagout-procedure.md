## Why Lockout/Tagout Exists

**Lockout/tagout (LOTO)** is the process of isolating energy sources and physically locking them
so equipment cannot be energized while someone is working on it. It is the single most
important procedure in this program. Most electrical injuries to maintenance workers happen
because a circuit was assumed dead, someone turned it back on, or the wrong breaker was opened.

**Rules that apply:**

| Standard | Covers |
|---|---|
| OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 | The control of hazardous energy (lockout/tagout) for servicing and maintenance |
| OSHA 29 CFR 1910.333(b) | Electrical lockout and tagging; includes verification of absence of voltage |
| OSHA 29 CFR 1926.417 | Lockout and tagging of circuits in construction |
| NFPA 70E Article 120 | Establishing an electrically safe work condition |

Our company has a **written energy control procedure**. This lesson teaches its general steps,
but you must follow the written procedure for your site.

## Key Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Authorized employee** | A trained person who locks out equipment to service it (you, once trained) |
| **Affected employee** | Someone who uses or works near the equipment but does not service it (the customer's staff) |
| **Energy-isolating device** | A device that physically blocks energy: circuit breaker, disconnect switch, fused switch |
| **Lockout device** | A device that holds an isolating device in the safe position so it cannot be operated (breaker lockout, hasp) |
| **Tag** | A warning tag identifying who applied the lock and why; it is a warning, not a physical block |

> **Safety:** A **wall switch, occupancy sensor, relay, or lighting control panel is NOT an
> energy-isolating device.** These are control devices. They may switch only one conductor, can be
> turned on by someone else or by automatic controls, and often leave conductors in the fixture
> energized. Always lock out at the breaker or disconnect.

## Locks and Tags

- Each authorized employee has their **own personal lock** with **one key** that they keep.
- Locks are **standardized** (same color/shape), **durable**, and used **only for LOTO**.
- Each lock gets a **tag** showing your name, company, date, the reason, and how to reach you.
- Tags are used alone **only** where a lock truly cannot be applied, and then only with an
  additional safety measure as required by the written procedure. On lighting circuits, a lock
  can almost always be applied with the right breaker lockout device.

## The LOTO Sequence

### 1. Prepare
- Identify the fixtures and **every circuit** that feeds them. Use the panel schedule, drawings,
  and labels, but remember that schedules are often wrong.
- Identify other energy sources: emergency battery packs, generator-fed circuits, and stored
  energy in capacitors.
- Get the right lockout devices for the breaker type.

### 2. Notify
Tell the affected employees (site contact, building engineer, occupants) **what** will be shut off,
**why**, and **for how long**. Lights going out unexpectedly can cause falls and alarm.

### 3. Shut Down
Turn the fixtures **off at the normal control** (wall switch or control system) first. This
reduces load on the breaker when it is opened.

### 4. Isolate
Open the breaker or disconnect. Stand to the side, use your left hand if practical, and look away
from the panel as the breaker is operated. Opening breakers in a panel must be done in accordance
with the company electrical safety program; at LT1 this is done by, or under the direct supervision
of, a qualified person.

### 5. Apply Locks and Tags
Install the breaker lockout device so the handle **cannot** be moved to ON. Attach your personal
lock and completed tag. If more than one person is working, use a hasp (Lesson 2).

### 6. Release Stored Energy
Wait for capacitors to discharge as labels direct, and disconnect or isolate emergency battery
packs if they affect the work (follow manufacturer instructions).

### 7. Verify
- **Try:** operate the wall switch or control to confirm the fixtures do not come on. Then return
  the switch to OFF.
- **Test:** verify absence of voltage at the work location using live-dead-live (LT1-C03), testing
  every conductor.

Only after verification is the equipment in an **electrically safe work condition.**

## Restoring Power

1. Make sure all tools and materials are removed and the fixture is fully reassembled.
2. Confirm all workers are clear and accounted for.
3. Notify affected employees that power is about to be restored.
4. Each person removes **only their own** lock and tag.
5. Remove the lockout device, close the breaker (qualified person or under supervision), and test
   the fixtures.
6. Notify the site contact that the work is complete.

> **Safety:** Never remove someone else's lock. Never ask someone to remove yours for you. If a
> lock is left on after a worker leaves, follow the supervised lock-removal procedure in Lesson 2.

## Common LOTO Mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Trusting the panel schedule without verifying | Wrong breaker locked; technician works on a live circuit |
| Using the wall switch as isolation | Hot conductors or a second circuit still energized |
| Skipping the "try" step | A mislabeled breaker goes unnoticed |
| Skipping live-dead-live | A failed meter shows "dead" on a live circuit |
| Forgetting the emergency battery pack | Shock from a fixture that should be dead |
| Lending your key or lock | Someone else can energize your circuit |

## Key Takeaways
- LOTO isolates energy at a breaker or disconnect, never at a switch or control device.
- Sequence: prepare, notify, shut down, isolate, lock and tag, release stored energy, verify.
- Verify with "try" at the control and "test" with live-dead-live at the work location.
- Each authorized employee uses their own lock and keeps the only key.
- Only the person who applied a lock removes it.
