## Building on Lesson 6

Lesson 6 covered how one technician applies our company's lockout. This lesson covers the
company rules for crews, for getting locks off safely, and for the question every technician
gets asked sooner or later: "Can't you just do it live?"

## More Than One Person or Crew

- **Every worker places their own lock and tag** before starting work, on a hasp or group lockbox
  (Lesson 2). Our handbook says "each employee or crew"; in this program a crew lock never
  replaces a personal lock for each person exposed.
- When your part of the work is done, **remove your own lock immediately.** Do not leave it for
  someone else to deal with.
- The **last person** to remove a lock tells the supervisor (or the contractor in charge of the
  site) that the work has ended. The supervisor makes sure all work really is finished before the
  equipment is released for normal use.

## The Forgotten-Lock Rule

If someone leaves a lock on, the supervisor must make **every attempt to contact that person**
to arrange removal. If they cannot be reached, the lock may be removed only after an **authorized
person physically inspects** the work and confirms it is complete and safe, and the worker must be
told before they return to work (29 CFR 1910.147(e)(3)).

**Unauthorized removal of another person's lock or tag is not permitted. Under company policy it
results in severe disciplinary action and probable dismissal.**

## Plug-In Equipment

For a tool or piece of equipment that runs on a cord and plug, **unplug it and keep the plug
under your control** (in your sight, or with a plug lockout cover), and hang a **DANGER** tag on
the plug so no one plugs it back in while you work on it.

## Removing LOTO: Our Sequence

1. **Clear personnel and tools.** Everyone is out of the fixtures; nothing is left inside.
2. **Check for hazards.** Covers and lenses on, no conductors pinched, no short circuits.
3. **Notify** the site contact and affected employees that power is coming back.
4. **Remove your lock and tag** (only yours), then restore power and test the fixtures.

## What About Working Live?

Our handbook is clear: **whenever working on a lighting system, the power source is disconnected
first.** Only trained, qualified and authorized personnel may work on energized systems, and only
when de-energizing is truly not feasible or would create a greater hazard, such as darkening an
occupied area during business hours where the lights are needed for safety. Even then, the first
answers are temporary portable lighting, splitting the work by circuit, or scheduling after hours.

Our handbook also describes an older method of cutting the hot and neutral leads inside an
energized fixture and fitting an in-line disconnect. That is **energized work**. An LT1 never
does it. It is allowed only for a qualified person under our **Energized Electrical Work Permit**
and written safety plan approved by a supervisor (LT4-C07). For you, the answer is simple:
**if you cannot lock it out, you do not work on it. Call your supervisor.**

## Key Takeaways
- Every worker locks on with a personal lock; a crew lock never replaces it.
- Remove your own lock as soon as your part is done; the last one off notifies the supervisor.
- Removing someone else's lock without the formal procedure means severe discipline and
  probable dismissal.
- Plug-in equipment: unplug, keep the plug under your control, DANGER tag on the plug.
- Removal: clear people and tools, check for hazards, notify, remove only your own lock.
- If it cannot be locked out, an LT1 does not work on it. Energized work requires a qualified
  person and the Energized Electrical Work Permit.
