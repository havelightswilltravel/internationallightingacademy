## Your Role at LT1

At LT1 you replace ballasts **only under direct supervision**. Your supervisor or a qualified
technician inspects your work before power is restored. In LT2-C04 you will learn ballast types and
troubleshooting in depth. Here the focus is doing the replacement **safely and correctly** once a
failed ballast has been identified.

## What You Need

- Replacement ballast that matches (see below)
- LOTO kit, DMM, and proving unit
- Screwdrivers and nut drivers, wire strippers, lineman's pliers
- **Listed wire connectors** sized for the conductors (twist-on or push-in)
- Phone or camera for photos
- Nitrile gloves and a sealable bag (in case of a leaking ballast)
- Stepladder and standard PPE

## Choosing the Right Replacement

The new ballast must match:

| Item | Check |
|---|---|
| Lamp type | e.g., F32T8, F54T5HO; check the existing lamps and fixture |
| Number of lamps | 1, 2, 3, or 4 lamps |
| Input voltage | 120 V, 277 V, or 120–277 V UNV; confirm the circuit voltage |
| Start type | Instant start, rapid start, or programmed start, as specified (LT2-C04) |
| Physical size and mounting | Fits the channel and mounting holes |
| Special features | Dimming, emergency compatibility, low-temperature rating |

If anything is unclear, **ask before installing.** Do not "make it work" with a different ballast.

## Step-by-Step Replacement

### 1. Lock Out and Verify
Apply LOTO (Lesson 1). Remove the lamps and place them safely aside. Verify absence of voltage at
the ballast's supply leads using live-dead-live, testing every conductor.

### 2. Open the Wiring Channel
Remove the channel cover or reflector. Keep track of screws.

### 3. Check the Old Ballast for PCBs and Leaks
- Look for a **"No PCBs"** statement on the label.
- If the ballast **has no "No PCBs" label** (common on ballasts made before 1979), assume it
  contains PCBs.
- If the ballast is **leaking** (oily liquid or black tar-like material), **stop.** Do not touch the
  material with bare hands. Notify your supervisor and follow Lesson 5.

### 4. Document the Existing Wiring
Take clear photos of the old ballast label and the wire connections. Note which colored leads go to
which sockets.

### 5. Disconnect and Remove
- Disconnect the supply connections (hot and neutral) from the old ballast.
- Cut the ballast leads **close to the old ballast** so the socket wires stay as long as possible.
- Remove the mounting nuts or screws and lift out the old ballast. Ballasts are heavier than they
  look; keep a firm grip.

### 6. Mount the New Ballast
Secure it with **all** its mounting hardware, flat against the fixture metal. Solid metal-to-metal
mounting helps the ballast shed heat and keeps the ballast case grounded through the fixture.

### 7. Connect Following the New Ballast's Diagram
- Follow the **diagram printed on the new ballast.** Do not copy the old wiring blindly; different
  ballasts can use different color codes and socket connections.
- Strip each conductor to the length the connector manufacturer specifies; no bare copper should show
  outside the connector.
- For twist-on connectors, twist until snug and tug-test each conductor. For push-in connectors, push
  conductors in fully and tug-test.
- Connect the supply: hot to the ballast line lead (often black) and neutral to the common lead
  (often white), **as the label shows.** Cap any unused leads individually as the label directs.
- Confirm the fixture's equipment grounding conductor is still connected to the fixture.

### 8. Route and Secure Wires
Keep conductors away from sharp edges and the ballast's hot surfaces. Do not pinch wires when
reinstalling the channel cover.

### 9. Supervisor Inspection
Have your supervisor inspect the wiring **before** the cover goes on and before power is restored.

### 10. Reassemble, Restore, Test
Install lamps, close the fixture, remove LOTO following the restoration steps, and test. All lamps
should start promptly and burn evenly.

> **Safety:** Never test a ballast by energizing it with the wiring channel open and your hands in the
> fixture. If the fixture does not work after replacement, lock out again before investigating.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Result |
|---|---|
| Wrong input voltage (120 V ballast on 277 V) | Ballast fails immediately |
| Wrong lamp type or count | Poor starting, short lamp life, overheating |
| Loose splice or nicked conductor | Intermittent operation, heat, fire risk |
| Unused leads not capped | Shock or short circuit |
| Ballast not fully secured | Overheating and premature failure |
| Skipping the PCB check | Improper disposal of regulated waste |

## Key Takeaways
- At LT1, ballast replacement is supervised and inspected before power is restored.
- Match lamp type, lamp count, input voltage, and start type.
- Check every removed ballast for a "No PCBs" label and for leaks.
- Wire to the new ballast's label diagram with listed connectors and correct strip length.
- If it does not work, lock out again before you investigate.
