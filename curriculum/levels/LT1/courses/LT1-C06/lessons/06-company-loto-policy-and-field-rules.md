## Why Our Company Has Its Own Rules

Lessons 1 and 2 taught lockout/tagout (LOTO) as OSHA and NFPA 70E describe it. This lesson
teaches **our company's policy**, taken from the employee handbook and our electrical safety
training. These are the rules you will be held to on every job. They sit on top of OSHA
29 CFR 1910.147 (control of hazardous energy), 1910.333(b) (electrical lockout and tagging),
1926.417 (construction) and NFPA 70E Article 120, and where they are stricter, they win.

Our handbook starts from one idea: **always handle all wiring as if it were energized, even if
you turned it off yourself.** Lighting fixtures surprise people. Our technicians have found live
wiring in "dead" fixtures because of:

- other circuits passing through the fixture or its wiring channel,
- incorrect wiring during the original installation,
- DC battery backup (emergency) packs,
- 24-hour night-light or emergency circuits, and
- a back-fed neutral shared with another circuit.

That is why our procedure ends with **verification**, every time.

## Our Company's LOTO Steps

Our written method for locking and tagging equipment follows these steps.

1. **Identify the equipment.** Determine exactly which fixtures, circuits and controls are
   involved in the work.
2. **Determine availability.** When needed, the project supervisor works with the customer's
   representative to make the equipment available, and makes sure normal operation of other
   equipment (time clocks, lighting control panels, building automation, contactors) will not
   switch the circuit back on while you work.
3. **De-energize and tag.** Turn the fixtures off at the normal control, then place the
   disconnect (breaker) in the off position and tag it.
4. **Lock out.** The person who will do the work places a lockout device and personal lock so
   the breaker cannot be operated.
5. **Safety review.** Before work starts, inspect to make sure the breaker is physically
   inoperative and stored energy (capacitors, battery packs) is discharged or isolated.
6. **Verify.** Try the switch, return it to off, then test for absence of voltage at the work
   location with a meter, live-dead-live (LT1-C03).
7. **Keep your lock and tag on** until your work is finished, then follow the removal sequence
   in Lesson 7.

### Where to Lock Out: the Breaker

Our handbook describes lockout at the **circuit breaker** (Part I) and older versions also allowed
lockout at the **local light switch** with a wall-switch lock (Part II). In this program the rule
is: **the isolation point is the breaker or disconnect.** A wall-switch lock may be added as an
extra warning so occupants do not flip the switch, but it never replaces the breaker lock.
A switch can be miswired, can control only part of the fixture, and does nothing about other
circuits in the fixture (Lesson 1). If the breaker cannot be locked (no device fits, the panel is
locked and no one has the key), **stop and call your supervisor.**

### Our Locks and Tags

- The company issues each technician a **personal lock** and **tags**. Locks and tags must
  **identify the technician who placed them**, and that technician is the only person
  authorized to remove them.
- Each tag must show your **name, date, time, the company responsible, and a description of
  the work** (for example, "Replacing drivers, fixtures 12 to 18, east office").
- If the circuit has more than one place it can be turned on, a lock and tag go on **every**
  point.

> **Safety:** Tags are warnings only. A tag does not stop anyone from closing a breaker. That
> is why our rule is a lock **and** a tag, never a tag alone, for LT1 work.

## Verification: Our Company's Checklist

Our LOTO inspection guide asks four questions before you call anything dead:

- **Are you wearing gloves for verification?** Testing for absence of voltage is treated as
  work on a live circuit, because it is live until proven otherwise. Use the rubber insulating
  gloves with leather protectors and the eye protection our electrical safety program requires,
  and do it under the direct supervision of a qualified person until you are signed off.
- **Are you using the correct meter for the job?** A meter rated for the voltage and category
  (CAT III or higher for building circuits), with good leads, proved on a known source before and
  after the test. A non-contact tester is a screening tool, not verification.
- **Is PPE in place?** If the work turns out to require energized work, it stops here (see below).
- **Is there an equipment-specific lock and tag procedure, and do you need one?** Simple lighting
  branch circuits are covered by our general procedure. Equipment with several energy sources
  (central inverters, generator transfer switches, large lighting control panels) may have its own
  written procedure; ask your supervisor.

## Key Takeaways
- Treat all wiring as live until you have locked it out and verified it yourself.
- Our steps: identify, determine availability, de-energize and tag, lock out, safety review, verify.
- Lock out at the breaker with your own company lock and a tag showing name, date, time,
  company and work description; a switch lock is only an extra warning.
- Verify with rubber gloves, the correct meter and live-dead-live before calling it dead.
- If the breaker cannot be locked, stop and call your supervisor.
