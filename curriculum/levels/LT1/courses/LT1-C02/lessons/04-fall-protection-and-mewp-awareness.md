## When Fall Protection Is Required

OSHA sets **trigger heights** at which fall protection is required when working near an
unprotected side or edge:

| Setting | Trigger height | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| General industry (maintenance) | **4 ft** | 29 CFR 1910.28 |
| Construction | **6 ft** | 29 CFR 1926.501 |

Portable ladders are covered by their own ladder rules, which is why Lessons 1–3 focus on
using ladders correctly. But lighting technicians also work on **mezzanines, roofs, catwalks,
open-sided platforms, and in aerial lifts**, where fall protection is required.

## The Three Main Types of Fall Protection

1. **Fall prevention (passive):** guardrails, covers over holes, and parapets. These protect
   everyone without any action by the worker. Preferred whenever possible.
2. **Fall restraint:** a harness and lanyard that are short enough to **stop you from
   reaching the edge.** You cannot fall.
3. **Fall arrest:** a personal fall arrest system (PFAS) that **stops you after you fall.**

## Personal Fall Arrest System: A-B-C-D

| Part | What it is | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **A – Anchorage** | The structure you tie off to | Must support 5,000 lb per worker or be designed by a qualified person with a safety factor of two (1926.502(d)(15)). Never use conduit, sprinkler pipe, light fixtures, or ductwork. |
| **B – Body support** | Full-body harness | Body belts are not allowed for fall arrest. |
| **C – Connector** | Shock-absorbing lanyard or self-retracting lifeline (SRL) | Locking snap hooks and carabiners only. |
| **D – Deceleration / Descent & Rescue** | Shock absorber; rescue plan | A suspended worker needs prompt rescue. |

OSHA limits free fall to **6 ft** for a PFAS and requires that the worker not contact a lower
level. Remember to add up the total fall clearance: lanyard length, shock absorber deployment,
your height below the D-ring, and a safety margin. A 6-ft lanyard usually needs about 18 ft of
clearance below the anchor. Close to the floor, an SRL is often the better choice.

### Harness Inspection and Fit
1. Check webbing for cuts, burns, frayed edges, chemical damage, and broken stitching.
2. Check D-rings, buckles, and grommets for cracks, distortion, and corrosion.
3. Check the impact indicator. If it is deployed, the harness has arrested a fall; remove it
   from service.
4. Put on: back D-ring between the shoulder blades, chest strap at mid-chest, leg straps snug
   (a flat hand fits under them), no twisted webbing.

> **Safety:** Any harness, lanyard, or SRL that has arrested a fall, or fails inspection, must
> be removed from service immediately.

### Rescue and Suspension Trauma
A worker hanging in a harness can lose consciousness from restricted blood flow in a matter
of minutes. Every job with fall arrest must have a **rescue plan** that does not depend only
on calling 911.

## Introduction to MEWPs (Awareness Only)

**Mobile elevating work platforms (MEWPs)**, commonly called aerial lifts, are used for high
fixtures in warehouses, gymnasiums, parking structures, and site lighting. ANSI standards
(A92.20 design, A92.22 safe use, A92.24 training) group them as follows:

| Group | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Group A** | Platform stays inside the tipping lines (moves straight up) | Scissor lifts, vertical mast lifts |
| **Group B** | Platform can extend beyond the tipping lines | Boom lifts (telescopic, articulating), bucket trucks |

General rules every LT1 should know:

- **You may not operate a MEWP until you have completed operator training, familiarization on
  the specific model, and been authorized by the company.** This is covered in LT3-C06.
- Occupants of **boom lifts** wear a full-body harness and lanyard attached to the **platform's
  designated anchor point**, never to a nearby structure.
- On **scissor lifts**, the guardrails are the primary fall protection; keep the gate or chain
  closed and stand on the platform floor. Follow the manufacturer's and company's rules on
  whether a harness is also required.
- Never climb on guardrails or use a ladder on a platform.
- Pre-use inspection, a workplace (site) inspection for holes, slopes, overhead hazards, and
  power lines, and function tests are required before every use.
- Keep MEWPs and occupants well away from overhead power lines; minimum clearances depend on
  line voltage, and contact is often fatal.

As a ground worker, you can help by keeping the area around a lift clear, acting as a spotter
when asked, and never walking under a raised platform.

## Key Takeaways
- Fall protection is required at 4 ft in general industry and 6 ft in construction at unprotected edges.
- Guardrails and covers come first; restraint prevents a fall; arrest stops one.
- PFAS anchors must support 5,000 lb per worker. Never tie off to conduit, pipe, or fixtures.
- Inspect your harness before each use; remove any equipment that has arrested a fall.
- Do not operate a MEWP until trained and authorized. On boom lifts, tie off to the platform anchor.
