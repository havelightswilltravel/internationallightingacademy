## When Fall Protection Is Required

OSHA sets **trigger heights** for fall protection at unprotected sides and edges:

| Setting | Trigger height | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| General industry (most maintenance and service work) | **4 ft** | 29 CFR 1910.28(b)(1) |
| Construction (new installation, renovation) | **6 ft** | 29 CFR 1926.501(b)(1) |
| Scaffolds (construction) | 10 ft | 29 CFR 1926.451(g)(1) |

Our company handbook says it plainly: **any time a potential fall of more than four feet
exists, a guardrail, or a harness and lanyard, must be used.** Our training decks say "fall
protection is a must any time you are 6 ft or higher." Treat the **4-ft rule** as the one
that applies on service calls; the 6-ft figure is the construction trigger.

Portable ladders are covered by their own rules, which is why Lessons 1–3 focus on using
ladders correctly. But you will also work on **mezzanines, roofs, catwalks, open-sided
platforms, and in aerial lifts**, where fall protection is required.

## Three Ways to Protect Against a Fall

The order matters. First choice is to **eliminate** the hazard (do the work from the
ground, lower the fixture, use a lift with guardrails). If you cannot eliminate it:

1. **Fall prevention (passive):** guardrails, covers over holes. Protects everyone with no
   action by the worker.
2. **Fall restraint:** a harness and a lanyard rigged short enough that you **cannot reach
   the edge.** You cannot fall. Our company policy calls for restraint at unprotected
   platform edges wherever it can be rigged.
3. **Fall arrest:** a personal fall arrest system (PFAS) that **stops you after you fall.**

> **Safety:** A self-retracting lifeline is **not** a restraint system unless you still
> cannot reach the edge with it fully extended. Our handbook calls this out specifically.

## Personal Fall Arrest System: A-B-C-D

| Part | What it is | Our company's rules |
|---|---|---|
| **A – Anchorage** | What you tie off to | Must hold **5,000 lb** per worker, or be designed by a qualified person with a safety factor of two (1926.502(d)(15)). Engineered anchors are **tagged**. Never conduit, sprinkler pipe, fixtures, or ductwork. |
| **B – Body support** | Full-body harness | Company-provided, fitted to you, with **relief steps (trauma straps)** installed. Body belts are never used for arrest. |
| **C – Connector** | Lanyard or self-retracting lifeline (SRL), snap hooks, carabiners | Every arrest lanyard has a **deceleration device** (shock absorber). Locking hooks only. |
| **D – Deceleration, Descent & Rescue** | Shock absorber, rescue plan | Rescue must come quickly (Lesson 6). |

A PFAS must limit free fall to **6 ft** and keep you from hitting the lower level
(1926.502(d)(16); 1910.140(d)).

### The 18½-Foot Rule

Our company rule: **with a 6-ft lanyard, your tie-off point must be at least 18½ ft above
the level below.** Here is where the number comes from:

| Item | Distance |
|---|---|
| Lanyard length (free fall) | 6 ft |
| Shock absorber stretch | 3½ ft |
| D-ring to bottom of your feet (average worker) | 6 ft |
| Safety margin | 3 ft |
| **Total required below the anchor** | **18½ ft** |

So ask yourself before you clip in: **if the level below is around 18½ ft or less, will
this lanyard actually save me?** Between about 6 ft and 18½ ft, you may need a different
method: a restraint setup, an SRL rated for short clearance, a higher anchor, or a lift with
guardrails. Always check the manufacturer's clearance chart for your exact equipment.

### Harness Basics

- **Inspect before every use** (step-by-step in Lesson 5 and skill LT1-S21).
- **Fit:** back D-ring centered between the shoulder blades, chest strap at mid-chest, leg
  straps **snug** (a flat hand, about three fingers, fits under them), no twisted webbing.
- **Use the back (dorsal) D-ring** for fall arrest. Our company recommends it in almost all
  cases because it best supports your spine if you fall. Front D-rings are only for the uses
  the manufacturer lists, such as a ladder climbing system.
- Any harness, lanyard, or SRL that has **arrested a fall, shows a deployed impact
  indicator, or fails inspection** is removed from service immediately.

## Introduction to MEWPs (Awareness Only)

**Mobile elevating work platforms (MEWPs)**, often called aerial lifts, reach high
fixtures in warehouses, gymnasiums, parking structures, and site lighting. ANSI A92.20
(design), A92.22 (safe use), and A92.24 (training) group them:

| Group | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Group A** | Platform stays inside the tipping lines | Scissor lifts, vertical mast lifts |
| **Group B** | Platform can extend beyond the tipping lines | Boom lifts, bucket trucks |

Our company's rules every LT1 should know:

- **You may not operate a lift until you are trained, familiarized on that model, and
  authorized.** Knowledge of the specific lift is a must before operating (LT3-C06).
- **Boom lifts and bucket trucks: wear a harness at all times**, with the lanyard on the
  platform's designated anchor point, never on a nearby structure.
- **Scissor lifts:** the guardrails are the fall protection; keep the gate closed. A harness
  is worn when the site's policy or the manufacturer requires it.
- **Inspect every lift before each use**; red-tagged "Out of Service" equipment is never used.
- **Never use a ladder on a lift platform** and never stand on the guardrails.
- **A spotter is required** for lift work on most interior jobs, especially during the
  customer's business hours.
- Stay **at least 10 ft** from power lines up to 50 kV. For higher-voltage lines, our
  policy is that the utility must shield the lines before any work under them.

As a ground worker, keep the area around a lift clear, act as a spotter when asked, and
never stand or reach under a raised platform.

## Key Takeaways
- Protect against falls at 4 ft on service work (6 ft in construction); eliminate, then guard, restrain, or arrest.
- Anchors hold 5,000 lb per worker and engineered anchors are tagged; never tie off to conduit, pipe, or fixtures.
- With a 6-ft lanyard you need 18½ ft below the anchor; closer to the floor, choose another method.
- Use the back D-ring, keep relief steps installed, and remove equipment that has arrested a fall.
- No lift operation until trained and authorized; harness at all times in boom lifts; spotter for interior lift work.
