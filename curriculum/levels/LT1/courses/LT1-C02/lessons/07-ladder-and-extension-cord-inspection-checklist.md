## Why We Use a Written Form

Every technician inspects a ladder before using it (Lesson 2). On top of that, our company
uses a **Ladder and Extension Cord Inspection Form** for each **vehicle or location.** The
form makes the inspection consistent, puts it on paper, and catches the slow damage that a
quick look can miss: worn feet, a loose rivet, a cord jacket rubbed through on the rack.

OSHA supports both habits. Ladders must be inspected before first use in each shift and
defective ones tagged and removed (29 CFR 1910.23(b)(9)–(10)). Portable cord-and-plug
equipment, including extension cords, must be **visually inspected before use on any
shift,** and a defective cord must be removed from service until repaired or replaced
(1910.334(a)(2)).

The checklists in this lesson are also in the Library under **Inspection Checklists**, so
you can open them on your phone on the job.

## Filling Out the Form

1. **Date and vehicle or location.**
2. **Both technicians sign** when two techs share a van. Both of you are vouching for the
   equipment you will climb and plug in.
3. **One column per ladder,** headed with the **ladder size** (for example, 6-ft step,
   8-ft step, 24-ft extension, multi-position).
4. Go down **every line for every ladder.** Mark each item pass or fail. Do not mark a whole
   column "OK" without looking.
5. **Inspect the ladder rack** for damage and note it on the form.
6. **Extension cords:** list the number of each cord on the van and **initial** that it was
   inspected.
7. Anything that fails is handled the same day (see below), and **reported to your
   supervisor** and **written on your pull sheet** so the replacement is in your next order.

## The Ladder Lines

1. Non-slip material on the bottoms of all four rails
2. All surfaces free of oil, grease, or other slippery materials (clean it on the spot;
   keep cleaning materials on the van)
3. Joints between steps and rails tight
4. All hardware and fittings securely attached (no loose rivets, bolts, or nuts)
5. Moving parts operate without binding or too much play
6. Spreader bars straight and lock securely
7. Rails and steps without cracks, crushing, or other damage
8. Metal ladders have fully intact insulator pads on the bottoms of the rails
9. All parts free of sharp edges
10. All stickers in place and legible
11. All steps have non-skid surfaces

**Then the ladder rack:** mounting bolts tight, no cracked welds or bent arms, rollers and
locks working, and **straps or tie-downs in good condition.** A ladder that bounces loose on
the highway is damaged before you ever climb it, and it can kill another driver.

## The Extension Cord Lines

Our form's rule is short: **inspect extension cord insulation for breaks, burns, cuts, and
cracks, and check the condition of the plug.** In practice, uncoil the cord and run it
through your hands from end to end:

| Check | Fail if you find |
|---|---|
| **Outer jacket** | Cuts, nicks through to the inner wires, burns, melted spots, cracks, crushing, or bulges |
| **Taped or spliced sections** | Any tape or splice; a repaired cord is not a safe cord (1926.416(e)(1): worn or frayed cords are not used) |
| **Plug (male end)** | Missing or bent **ground pin**, loose or bent blades, cracked body, burn marks |
| **Connector (female end)** | Cracked housing, loose grip on the plug, burn marks |
| **Strain relief** | Jacket pulled out of the plug or connector so inner wires show |
| **Label** | Rating and type readable; hard or extra-hard usage cord for job-site work; outdoor rated ("W") for outdoor use |

> **Safety:** Never remove or bend back the ground pin to fit an old two-prong outlet. The
> ground pin is what clears a fault through the breaker instead of through you (OSHA
> 1910.334(a)(3)). Use GFCI protection for cords and tools on construction and outdoor work
> (1926.404(b)(1)), and test the GFCI before use.

### Using Cords Safely on the Job

- Use a cord **rated for the load** and as short as practical; do not daisy-chain cords.
- **Route cords away from walkways** or tape and cover them; a cord across an aisle is a
  trip hazard for shoppers and coworkers.
- Do not run cords through doorways, windows, or ceilings where they can be pinched.
- Keep connections out of water and snow.
- **Unplug by the plug,** not by yanking the cord.
- Coil cords loosely and keep them off the ladder rack edges, where they get cut.

## When an Item Fails

| Equipment | What you do |
|---|---|
| **Ladder** with a structural defect | **Red tag it "DO NOT USE,"** take it off the truck or set it aside where nobody will grab it, report it to your supervisor, and write it on your pull sheet. It is repaired the manufacturer's way or replaced. |
| **Ladder** that is only dirty or slippery | Clean it before use, then re-inspect. |
| **Extension cord** | Tag it "DO NOT USE" and remove it from service. If your supervisor directs, **cut off the plug** so nobody uses it by mistake. Report it and write it on your pull sheet. |
| **Ladder rack** | Do not load a ladder onto a damaged rack. Report it; the vehicle may need to be taken out of service. |

Never leave a defective ladder or cord on the van "for the next guy to decide." The next guy
might be you, in the dark, in a hurry.

## Key Takeaways
- Inspect every ladder and every cord before use on every shift; the form records it per vehicle.
- Both techs sign; one column per ladder size; check the ladder rack; number and initial each cord.
- Cords fail for any cut, burn, crack, tape, splice, missing ground pin, or damaged plug.
- Red tag "DO NOT USE" and remove failed equipment from service; report it and write it on your pull sheet.
- Never remove a ground pin; use GFCI protection and keep cords out of walkways.
