## Three Points of Contact

When climbing up or down, always keep **three points of contact** with the ladder: two hands
and one foot, or two feet and one hand. This means:

- **Face the ladder** when climbing up or down.
- **Do not carry anything in your hands** while climbing. Use a tool pouch, a tool belt, a
  hand line, or have a coworker hand items up to you.
- Grip the **rungs or side rails**, not the items you are carrying.
- Climb **one step at a time**; do not slide down or jump off the last step.
- Wear clean, dry, slip-resistant footwear.

OSHA requires that a worker on a ladder not carry any object or load that could cause loss of
balance (see 29 CFR 1926.1053(b)(22)).

## Body Position While Working

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Keep your **belt buckle between the side rails** | Lean or reach so your body moves outside the rails |
| Move the ladder when the work is out of reach | "Walk" or shift the ladder while standing on it |
| Stand at or below the highest standing level | Stand on the top cap, the step below it, or the pail shelf |
| Keep one hand on the ladder whenever practical | Use both hands for heavy lifting at height for long periods |
| Let a coworker steady the ladder in busy areas | Allow people to walk under the ladder |
| Work facing the ladder | Stand on the back (non-step) side of a stepladder |

> **Safety:** Only **one person** on a ladder at a time, unless the ladder is designed for two
> (some stepladders are rated for one person on each side; check the label).

## Handling Lamps, Lenses, and Fixtures at Height

Lighting work involves awkward loads: 4-ft and 8-ft lamps, lens doors, and fixtures.

1. **Plan the sequence.** Open the fixture, remove old lamps, come down, then go up with new
   lamps. Do not juggle old and new lamps at the same time.
2. **Use a helper** for 8-ft lamps and fixtures. Hand items up and down; never toss them.
3. **Control the lens door.** Many troffer doors swing down on hinges or hang on safety
   chains. Support the door as you release the latches.
4. **Barricade below.** A dropped lamp shatters glass and releases mercury vapor.
5. **Lift heavy fixtures with a lift or a second person**, not alone from a ladder.

## Working Near Energized Parts From a Ladder

At LT1, the fixture you are working on will be **locked out and verified dead** before you
touch it (LT1-C06). But other energized parts may be near your ladder:

- Adjacent fixtures on a different, still-energized circuit.
- Open junction boxes or damaged wiring in the ceiling.
- Busway or plug-in duct in warehouses.
- Overhead service drops and power lines outdoors.

Rules:

1. Use a **fiberglass ladder** only and keep it clean.
2. **Remove conductive items**: jewelry, metal watch bands, and keep metal tape measures and
   long metal tools away from exposed conductors.
3. Before reaching into a ceiling space, **look first** with a flashlight. Do not reach
   blindly above a ceiling tile.
4. If you find exposed energized parts within reach (damaged wiring, an open box), **stop and
   call your supervisor.** Do not touch them, even with "insulated" tools.
5. Treat every unidentified wire as energized.

> **Safety:** A small shock on a ladder can cause a deadly fall. The startle reaction alone
> can throw you off balance. This is one more reason we lock out and test before touch.

## Weather and Outdoor Work

- Do not use ladders outdoors in high wind, lightning, or icy conditions.
- On soft ground, use ladder levelers or a base pad designed for the ladder, never bricks or
  scrap wood.
- Wet fiberglass is more conductive; dry it and keep greater distance from energized parts.

## Getting Off the Ladder Safely

When stepping onto a roof or platform from an extension ladder, the rails must extend at
least 3 ft above the landing so you have a handhold. Step **sideways** off the ladder at the
top, not over the top. Reverse the process when returning.

## Key Takeaways
- Face the ladder and keep three points of contact while climbing; carry tools in a pouch.
- Keep your belt buckle between the rails and move the ladder instead of overreaching.
- Barricade the area below and use a helper for long lamps and heavy fixtures.
- Stop and call your supervisor if exposed energized parts are within reach.
- A small shock can cause a fatal fall. Lockout and testing protect you on the ladder too.
