## Inspect Before Every Use

OSHA requires that ladders be inspected by a competent person for visible defects
periodically and after any event that could damage them, and that defective ladders be
tagged and removed from service (see 29 CFR 1926.1053(b)(15)–(16) and 1910.23). In our
company, **you inspect the ladder every time you use it.** It takes two minutes.

### Stepladder Checklist

| Check | Look for |
|---|---|
| Labels | Present and readable: type, duty rating, warnings |
| Side rails | Cracks, bends, splits, fiberglass "blooming" (exposed fibers), burn marks |
| Steps and top cap | Loose, bent, cracked, missing; slippery from oil or paint |
| Spreaders | Bent, broken, do not lock fully |
| Feet | Missing, worn-through, or loose shoes/pads |
| Hardware | Loose rivets, bolts, missing parts |
| Cleanliness | Mud, grease, paint, or moisture on rails and steps |

### Extension Ladder: Add These Checks

- **Rung locks** (pawls/dogs) engage fully and are not cracked.
- **Rope and pulley** are not frayed or damaged; rope runs freely.
- **Guide brackets** hold the fly section straight.
- **Swivel feet** move freely and the pads are not worn.

> **Safety:** If you find a defect, do not use the ladder. Tag it **"DANGER – DO NOT USE"**,
> remove it from service, and tell your supervisor. Never try to repair a ladder in the field.

## Setting Up a Stepladder

1. Inspect the ground. It must be **firm, level, dry, and clear.**
2. Open the ladder **fully** and push the spreaders down until they lock.
3. Check that all four feet sit flat with no rocking.
4. Position the ladder **close to the work** so you do not need to lean or overreach. Face the
   work with the front steps toward it, unless the manufacturer allows side loading.
5. In a doorway, lock or block the door and post a person or a sign. In aisles, set cones or
   barricade tape around the base.
6. Never use a stepladder **closed and leaned against a wall**; it can kick out. Use an
   extension ladder or a multi-position ladder in lean mode instead.

## Setting Up an Extension Ladder

### The 4:1 Rule
Set the base **1 ft away from the wall for every 4 ft of working length** (measured from the
base to where the ladder touches the support). That is about a 75-degree angle.

| Height to support point | Base distance from wall |
|---|---|
| 12 ft | 3 ft |
| 16 ft | 4 ft |
| 20 ft | 5 ft |
| 24 ft | 6 ft |

**Quick field check (elbow test):** stand with your toes at the base of the rails and stretch
your arms straight out. Your palms should just reach the rung at shoulder height. Many ladders
also have an angle indicator sticker on the rail.

### Raising and Securing
1. With the fly section retracted, place the base against the wall (or have a coworker foot
   it), then "walk" the ladder up hand over hand.
2. Move the base out to the 4:1 position.
3. Extend the fly section with the rope, keeping the ladder upright, and confirm **both rung
   locks** are engaged.
4. Maintain the minimum section overlap shown on the label.
5. For roof or landing access, extend the side rails **at least 3 ft above the landing**.
6. **Secure** the ladder: tie off the top to a solid point and/or secure the base, or have a
   coworker hold it while you climb. Secure it against slipping on smooth floors.
7. Never lean the top against a window, an unstable surface, or a pole without a pole strap.

## Overhead and Area Checks

Before raising any ladder, **look up.** Overhead power lines are a deadly hazard when raising
or carrying extension ladders outdoors. Keep yourself and the ladder **at least 10 ft** away from
overhead lines rated up to 50 kV (OSHA 1910.333(c)(3) for unqualified persons); keep farther away
from higher-voltage lines, and assume every line is energized.

Inside, look for sprinkler heads, open junction boxes, ductwork, and other obstacles.

## Key Takeaways
- Inspect the ladder every time; tag and remove any defective ladder.
- Stepladders must be fully open with spreaders locked on firm, level ground.
- Extension ladders go at a 4:1 angle and extend 3 ft above any landing used for access.
- Secure extension ladders and control the area around the base.
- Look up for power lines before raising a ladder; stay at least 10 ft away.
