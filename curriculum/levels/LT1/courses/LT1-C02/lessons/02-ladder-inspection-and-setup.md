## Inspect Before Every Use

OSHA's general industry rules require ladders to be inspected before first use in each work
shift and defective ladders to be tagged and removed from service until repaired (29 CFR
1910.23(b)(9)–(10)). The construction rules add periodic inspection by a competent person
and after any event that could damage the ladder (1926.1053(b)(15)–(16)). Our company goes further:

- **You inspect every ladder every time you use it.** It takes two minutes.
- A **competent person** also inspects the whole ladder fleet periodically. Our company does
  a **quarterly** ladder inspection and retires ladders there regularly, but **you can
  retire a ladder at any time you feel it is no longer safe.**
- Ladders with structural defects are **red tagged "DO NOT USE,"** removed from service,
  and repaired by the manufacturer's method or replaced. Never repair a ladder in the field.

### Our Pre-Use Checklist

These are the items on our company's ladder inspection form. Learn them in this order;
Lesson 7 shows how the form is filled out for your vehicle.

| # | Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Non-slip material on the bottoms of all four rails** (feet/shoes present, not worn through) | Worn feet let the ladder skid out |
| 2 | **All surfaces free of oil, grease, or other slippery materials** (clean it before use) | Slippery rungs cause missed steps |
| 3 | **Joints between steps and rails tight** | Loose joints let the ladder rack and twist |
| 4 | **All hardware and fittings securely attached:** no loose rivets, bolts, or nuts | A loose rivet is the first sign of failure |
| 5 | **Moving parts operate without binding or too much play** (hinges, rung locks, pulley) | Binding parts may not lock fully |
| 6 | **Spreader bars are straight and lock securely** | A bent spreader can fold under load |
| 7 | **Rails and steps free of cracks, crushing, bends, or splits** | Any structural damage = red tag |
| 8 | **Metal ladders have fully intact insulator pads on the bottoms of all rails** | Bare metal feet slide on smooth floors and mark them |
| 9 | **All parts free of sharp edges** | Prevents cuts and damage to cords and harness webbing |
| 10 | **All stickers (labels and warnings) in place and legible** | The label carries the duty rating and the rules for that ladder |
| 11 | **All steps have non-skid surfaces** | Footing, especially with wet or snowy boots |

For extension ladders, also check the **rung locks** (they engage fully and are not
cracked), the **rope and pulley** (not frayed, runs freely), the **guide brackets**, and the
**swivel feet and spur spikes.** For fiberglass, look for "blooming" (exposed glass fibers)
and burn marks.

> **Safety:** A ladder that was dropped, fell over, or came off the rack is inspected again
> before anyone climbs it. Even a "slight dent" in a rail can mean the rail will buckle. If in
> doubt, red tag it and tell your supervisor.

## Setting Up a Stepladder

1. Check the floor: **firm, level, dry, and clear.**
2. Open the ladder **fully** and push the spreaders down until they lock.
3. Check that all four feet sit flat with no rocking.
4. Position the ladder **close to the work** so your torso stays between the rails.
5. **Never set a ladder near a door that could open into it.** Lock or block the door and post
   a person or a sign. In aisles, cone off or barricade the base.
6. Keep the areas around the **top and bottom of the ladder clear** of clutter.
7. Never use a stepladder **closed and leaned against a wall**; the feet are not designed for
   that angle and can kick out.

## Setting Up an Extension Ladder

### The 4:1 Rule
When leaning a ladder against a structure, set the base **one-fourth of the working length**
away from the point of contact (29 CFR 1926.1053(b)(5)(i)). Our company example: a **12-ft
contact point = base 3 ft out.** That is about a 75-degree angle.

| Height to point of contact | Base distance out |
|---|---|
| 12 ft | 3 ft |
| 16 ft | 4 ft |
| 20 ft | 5 ft |
| 24 ft | 6 ft |

### Raising and Securing
1. **Look up first** for power lines, then raise with the fly section retracted. Long ladders
   (32 ft and over) take **two people**.
2. Move the base out to the 4:1 position; on soft ground, **use the spur spikes**.
3. Extend the fly with the rope and confirm **both rung locks** are engaged and the overlap on
   the label is met.
4. **Used for access** to a roof or platform: the side rails extend **at least 3 ft above the
   landing** (1926.1053(b)(1)).
5. **Used as a work platform** (for example, to reach a wall pack): the ladder does not need
   to extend past the point of contact, and our company allows resting it against a
   structural beam for that purpose.
6. **Secure it.** A straight or extension ladder used on anything other than a stable, level,
   dry surface must be tied off, held, or otherwise secured. **Tie off extension ladders so
   wind cannot blow them down.**
7. Never lean the top against a window, an unstable surface, or a pole without a pole strap.

## Overhead and Area Checks

Before raising any ladder outdoors, **look up.** Keep yourself and the ladder **at least 10
ft** from overhead lines up to 50 kV, and farther from higher voltages (29 CFR
1910.333(c)(3)). Assume every line is energized.

## Put It Away

Our company rule: **put ladders away when not in use.** A ladder left standing is a trip
hazard, an invitation for a customer to climb, and a target for carts and forklifts. Strap
it back on the rack, and **report any damage to the vehicle ladder rack** (Lesson 7).

## Key Takeaways
- Inspect every ladder every time with the company checklist; red tag "DO NOT USE" and remove defective ladders.
- A competent person also inspects the fleet periodically (quarterly in our program); you can retire a ladder any time.
- Set extension ladders at 4:1 (12 ft up = 3 ft out) and use spikes on soft ground.
- Extend 3 ft above a landing used for access; tie off extension ladders against wind.
- Never set up near a door that can open; keep the top and bottom clear; put ladders away.
