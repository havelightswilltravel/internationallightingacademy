## The Basic Lighting Tool Kit

Every LT1 should carry and know how to use the following tools. The company issues the kit;
you are responsible for keeping it clean, complete, and in safe condition.

| Tool | Typical use on lighting jobs |
|---|---|
| Screwdrivers (Phillips #1/#2, flat 1/4", 3/16") | Fixture covers, lens frames, ballast covers, terminal screws |
| Multi-bit / 11-in-1 driver | Quick access to common bits and nut drivers |
| Nut drivers (1/4", 5/16", 3/8") | Ballast mounting nuts, fixture hardware |
| Wire strippers (with AWG markings) | Stripping 10–22 AWG fixture and branch wire |
| Lineman's pliers | Twisting, cutting, gripping |
| Needle-nose pliers | Forming loops, reaching into fixtures |
| Diagonal cutters (dikes) | Cutting wire and cable ties |
| Utility knife (retractable) | Cutting cardboard, tape; not for stripping wire |
| Tape measure | Ladder setup, fixture layout |
| Flashlight / headlamp | Inspecting ceiling spaces and fixtures |
| Non-contact voltage tester | Quick presence check (Lesson 2) |
| Digital multimeter | Voltage verification (Lessons 3–4) |
| Cordless drill/driver with bits | Mounting fixtures and brackets |
| Personal locks, tags, lockout devices | LOTO (LT1-C06) |

## Using Hand Tools Correctly

### Wire Strippers
1. Identify the wire size (printed on the insulation, e.g., "12 AWG THHN").
2. Place the wire in the **matching** AWG notch. A notch that is too small nicks the copper,
   creating a weak spot that can break or overheat.
3. Strip to the length specified by the connector or device (often marked on the package or
   with a strip gauge on the device), typically about 1/2" to 5/8" for twist-on connectors and
   as marked for push-in connectors.
4. Inspect: no nicks, no stray strands, insulation cut cleanly.

### Screwdrivers and Nut Drivers
- Use the correct tip size; a worn or wrong-size tip slips and strips screws.
- Never use a screwdriver as a pry bar or chisel.
- Push while turning, especially on painted fixture screws.

### Utility Knives
Cut **away** from your body, retract the blade after every use, and replace dull blades.
Do not use a knife to strip wire; it damages conductors and causes hand injuries.

> **Safety:** Insulated tools (rated 1,000 V, marked with the double-triangle symbol, meeting
> ASTM F1505 / IEC 60900) are used by qualified persons for energized work. Ordinary plastic
> handles are for comfort, **not** for shock protection. An LT1 never relies on tool handles
> for protection; the circuit is always locked out and verified dead.

## Inspecting Hand Tools

Before use, check for:

- Cracked, chipped, or loose handles.
- Mushroomed or worn tips, worn jaws, loose rivets.
- Insulated tools: cuts, nicks, or exposed metal in the insulation (remove from service).
- Rust or dirt that affects function.

Damaged tools are tagged and turned in for replacement.

## Power Tools

Most lighting work uses **cordless** drills, impact drivers, and occasionally reciprocating or
band saws for strut and threaded rod.

### Safe Use Rules
1. Read and follow the manufacturer's instructions.
2. Wear safety glasses; add hearing protection for saws and hammer drills.
3. Inspect batteries and chargers for cracks, swelling, or damage. Damaged lithium-ion
   batteries can catch fire; remove them from service and store them away from flammables.
4. Use the correct bit and secure the workpiece. Use a clamp, not your hand.
5. Keep cords and loose clothing away from moving parts.
6. Remove the battery or unplug before changing bits or blades.
7. Before drilling into walls or ceilings, check for hidden wiring, pipes, and sprinklers.

### Corded Tools and Extension Cords
- Use only tools that are double-insulated or have a grounding plug with the ground pin intact.
- Use **GFCI protection** for corded tools and temporary power. On construction sites, OSHA
  requires GFCIs or an assured equipment grounding conductor program for temporary
  receptacles (29 CFR 1926.404(b)(1)).
- Inspect cords for cuts, crushed areas, missing ground pins, and taped repairs. Remove damaged
  cords from service.
- Do not run cords through doorways or windows where they can be pinched, or across aisles
  without protection.

## Organizing and Carrying Tools at Height

- Use a tool pouch or belt with holders sized for your tools.
- Use **tool lanyards** when working above people or over equipment that could be damaged.
- Never leave tools on top of a ladder or a ceiling grid.

## Key Takeaways
- Know every tool in your kit and use it only for its intended purpose.
- Match the stripper notch to the wire size; nicked conductors are a failure point.
- Plastic handles are not shock protection. Lock out and verify every time.
- Inspect hand tools, power tools, batteries, and cords before use; tag out anything damaged.
- Use GFCI protection for corded tools and temporary power, and secure tools at height.
