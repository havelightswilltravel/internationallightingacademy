## Why This Lesson Exists

Lessons 1 to 4 taught tools and meters the way any good trade school would. This lesson adds
**our company's own rules** - the ones repeated at our monthly safety meetings because the same
mistakes keep showing up in the field. They sit on top of OSHA 29 CFR 1910.242 (hand and power
tools), 1910.334 (cord-and-plug equipment) and NFPA 70E, and where our rules are stricter, ours
win.

## Tool and PPE Inspections

Several times a year our safety meeting includes a **PPE and hand-tool inspection.** Your
supervisor looks at every tool and every piece of PPE you carry. You are also expected to
inspect your own tools before each use (Lesson 1).

Our company rules:

- **A tool that fails inspection is not used again - period.** It is signed off with your
  supervisor, and you have **30 days to repair or replace it** and show the replacement to your
  supervisor.
- **Failed PPE is replaced immediately.** You do not go to work without it.
- **Tools you bring from home must meet OSHA and ANSI requirements**, the same as company tools.
- **Gloves are not disposable.** Keep them dry and with your tools, not stuffed in a door
  pocket. Use them until they are actually worn out, and turn in the old pair when you get a new
  one. Our company rule is **dry gloves at all times** - wet gloves are an accident waiting to
  happen around electricity.

> **Safety:** An insulated tool with cracked, chipped or cut insulation has failed, even if it
> still turns screws. Do not tape it and keep using it.

## Power Tool Rules From Our Safety Manual

- Turn the tool off and **let it stop** before you set it down. Never leave a running tool.
- **Unplug it or pull the battery** before changing bits or blades, or making any adjustment.
- Guards stay on saws and grinders. Never run a grinder wheel faster than its rated speed.
- **Compressed air** is used for cleaning only when reduced below **30 psi**, with chip guarding
  and eye protection (OSHA 1910.242(b)).
- **Avoid striking metal on metal** - chips and mushroomed heads send fragments flying.
- Use equipment only for its intended purpose, with every guard and safety device in place.
- **No modifications to company equipment** without the manufacturer's written approval. That
  includes drilling, screwing brackets onto lift baskets, or "improving" a tool.
- **Need a tool you don't have? Ask.** The shop keeps a list of the tools it owns in the tech
  cheat sheets. If brush is blocking a wall pack, come back with the right tool to clear it
  instead of fighting the branches from a ladder - and take photos.

## Extension Cord Rules

Our safety manual has a specific list for extension cords:

1. **Inspect before use** - jacket, plug, ground pin, strain relief. A cord that fails is red
   tagged and **the ends are cut off** so nobody uses it again; tell the shop so it is replaced.
2. **Match the cord to the tool.** The tool's rating must not exceed the cord's. Company rule of
   thumb: a 50-ft 14-gauge, three-wire cord handles about **12 A (1,440 W) sustained**, and a
   100-ft cord about 15% less. Use a heavier cord for bigger loads.
3. **Grounded cords only** (three-wire) unless the tool is double-insulated. Never remove or bend
   a prong. Use cords with a listing mark (UL or another NRTL).
4. **Do not plug one extension cord into another.**
5. **Do not run cords through doorways, or holes in walls, ceilings or floors.** A closing metal
   door can crush a cord and **energize the door.** If you must cross a doorway, prop the door
   open yourself and put out cones and caution signs so nobody can close it.
6. Never drive over, drag or set things on a cord. Keep cords out of walkways.
7. **No cords in wet conditions**, and use GFCI protection (or the assured equipment grounding
   program) on job-site power (OSHA 1926.404(b)(1)).
8. **Unplug by the plug**, not the cord. We have ruined lift battery chargers by yanking cords.
9. Cords are **temporary only** - unplug when finished.

Ladders, ladder racks and extension cords on every vehicle also get a **two-person inspection**
with the company form several times a year. Two sets of eyes catch what one person misses.

## The Meter-First Rule

The question our safety meetings ask most often is: **"What is Step 1 in troubleshooting an
LED fixture?"** The answer is: **put your meter across the hot and the neutral.**

- **You cannot troubleshoot with a "sniffer."** A non-contact voltage tester gives a warning, not
  a measurement (Lesson 2). It cannot tell 120 V from 277 V, and it can be fooled both ways.
- **Use your meter every time.** No voltage at the fixture means the problem is upstream, and a
  new driver will not fix it.
- **Measure before you connect a new fixture.** Our company has destroyed many brand-new fixtures
  - mostly on exterior jobs - because someone assumed the supply voltage was compatible. A
  120-277 V fixture connected to 480 V is ruined the instant it is energized. **You are the only
  one who can verify the voltage, and that responsibility lands on your meter.**
- **The meter only works if you turn it on.** Every tech has one on the company tool list.

> **Safety:** Measuring an energized circuit is energized work. At LT1 you take readings only
> under the direct supervision of a qualified person, in the PPE the company requires. Before
> touching conductors, lock out and verify absence of voltage live-dead-live (Lesson 4).

## Key Takeaways

- Failed tools are not used again; repair or replace them within 30 days. Failed PPE is replaced immediately.
- Keep gloves dry and with your tools; use them until they are worn out.
- Power tools: off and stopped before setting down; unplugged before changing bits; guards on; air under 30 psi.
- No modifications to company equipment without the manufacturer's written approval.
- Cords: inspect, red-tag and cut failed cords, no daisy chains, never through doorways unless the door is propped and coned.
- Step 1 on a dark fixture is your meter across hot and neutral - never a sniffer.
- Measure the supply voltage before connecting any new fixture.
