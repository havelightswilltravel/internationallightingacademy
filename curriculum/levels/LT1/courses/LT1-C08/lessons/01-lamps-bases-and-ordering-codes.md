## What This System Is and Where You Find It

An incandescent lamp makes light by heating a thin tungsten filament until it glows. A
**halogen** lamp is an incandescent lamp with a small quartz capsule filled with halogen gas,
which lets the filament run hotter and last a little longer. Both are simple, but they are
inefficient: most of the energy comes out as heat, not light.

Even though new installations are almost all LED, you will still find incandescent and
halogen lamps in:

- Recessed "can" lights in older offices, hotels, restaurants and homes
- Track lighting and display lighting in retail stores
- Decorative fixtures: chandeliers, sconces, pendants (often candelabra-base lamps)
- Work lights, floodlights and older exterior wall packs (quartz halogen)
- Appliance, oven and specialty lamps that have no practical LED replacement yet

Because you will replace these lamps and often convert them to LED, you need to read the
lamp, name it correctly and order the right one.

## Lamp Shapes

Lamp shapes are named with letters for the shape and a number for the **maximum diameter in
eighths of an inch**. A PAR38 is 38/8 = 4.75 in across. An A19 is 19/8 = about 2.4 in.

| Code | Shape | Typical use |
|------|-------|-------------|
| A19, A21 | Standard "household" bulb | Table lamps, utility fixtures, older ceiling fixtures |
| BR30, BR40 | Bulged reflector, soft wide beam | Recessed cans in homes and offices |
| R20, R30 | Reflector, older style | Small cans, display |
| PAR20, PAR30, PAR38 | Parabolic aluminized reflector, tight controlled beam | Track, recessed accent, outdoor floods |
| B, CA, F (B10, CA10, F15) | Bullet, candle, flame tips | Chandeliers and sconces |
| G25, G40 | Globe | Vanity and decorative |
| T3, T4 | Tubular halogen capsules | Floodlights, torchieres, under-cabinet |
| MR16 | Multifaceted reflector, usually 12 V | Covered in LT2 (low-voltage systems) |

PAR lamps also come in **short neck (SN)** and **long neck (LN)** versions, especially PAR30.
Ordering the wrong neck length means the lamp sits too deep or sticks out of the trim.

## Bases

The base must match the socket exactly. "E" bases are Edison screw bases; the number is the
diameter in millimeters.

| Base | Name | Where you see it |
|------|------|------------------|
| E26 | Medium | Most A-lamps, BR and PAR lamps in North America |
| E12 | Candelabra | Chandeliers, sconces, night lights |
| E17 | Intermediate | Appliance and some decorative lamps |
| E39 | Mogul | Large incandescent and HID lamps |
| G9 | Looped two-pin capsule (line voltage) | Small decorative and pendant fixtures |
| R7s | Recessed single contact, double-ended | T3 linear halogen floodlights and torchieres |
| GU10 | Twist-lock two-pin (line voltage) | Track and recessed, mostly European style |
| Bi-pin (G4, GY6.35) | Small straight pins, usually low voltage | Low-voltage capsules (LT2) |

R7s halogen lamps are ordered by **overall length**: the common sizes are 78 mm (about 3 in)
and 118 mm (about 4-5/8 in). Always measure the old lamp or the space between the socket
ends; do not guess.

## Reading an Ordering Code

Manufacturers use different formats, but a full ordering line normally contains these
pieces:

1. **Wattage**: 65, 90, 150, 300
2. **Shape and size**: BR30, PAR38, T3
3. **Beam or finish**: FL (flood), SP (spot), NFL (narrow flood), CL (clear), F (frosted)
4. **Base**: E26, E12, R7s
5. **Voltage**: 120 V or 130 V (130 V lamps are a "long life" version that runs slightly dimmer on 120 V)
6. **Special features**: SN/LN, long life, rough service

Example: `90PAR38/HAL/FL40/120V` reads as a 90 W halogen PAR38 lamp, flood beam with a 40
degree spread, rated 120 V, medium base. Example: `300T3/CL/118mm` is a 300 W clear
double-ended T3 halogen, 118 mm long, R7s base.

> **Safety:** Always check the fixture's maximum-wattage label before ordering. Ordering a
> higher wattage than the label allows overheats the fixture and wiring and is a fire
> hazard, even if the lamp fits.

## The Fixture Label Matters as Much as the Lamp

Most fixtures carry a label with a line similar to "Use maximum 75 W type BR30 or PAR30
lamp." That label limits **wattage** and sometimes **lamp type**. A recessed can listed for
reflector lamps only must not get a bare A19, because an A-lamp throws heat up into the can
and trips the thermal protector (covered in Lesson 2). Write the label information on the
work order along with the lamp you removed.

## Key Takeaways
- Lamp shape numbers are diameter in eighths of an inch (PAR38 = 4.75 in).
- Base codes such as E26, E12, E39, G9 and R7s must match the socket exactly; R7s lamps are ordered by length.
- A complete order includes wattage, shape, beam or finish, base, voltage and features such as neck length.
- The fixture's maximum-wattage and lamp-type label limits what you may install, no matter what fits.
- Record both the removed lamp and the fixture label on the work order.
