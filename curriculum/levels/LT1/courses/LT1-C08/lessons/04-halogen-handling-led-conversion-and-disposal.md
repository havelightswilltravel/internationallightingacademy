## Handling Halogen Lamps Safely

Halogen lamps run much hotter than standard incandescent lamps. The quartz capsule can
reach several hundred degrees, and the capsule is under pressure while operating.

**Rules for handling halogen lamps:**

1. **No bare fingers on the quartz.** Skin oils leave a residue that creates a hot spot on the
   quartz. The capsule can weaken, fail early, or even rupture. Handle the lamp by its sleeve
   or wrapping, or wear clean cotton or nitrile gloves.
2. **If you touch it, clean it.** Wipe the quartz with a clean lint-free cloth and isopropyl
   alcohol before installing, and let it dry.
3. **Let it cool.** A halogen lamp and its fixture can cause serious burns for many minutes
   after being turned off. Lock out, then wait, or use heat-resistant gloves.
4. **Keep the lens and guard in place.** Many halogen fixtures, such as floodlights and
   torchieres, require a glass lens or wire guard to contain the capsule if it ruptures and to
   keep the hot lamp away from combustibles. A fixture marked "Do not operate without lens"
   must never be returned to service without one.
5. **Watch clearances.** Halogen work lights and floodlights can ignite paper, fabric and
   insulation nearby. Report fixtures mounted close to combustibles.
6. **Use the right lamp.** Some halogen lamps are marked for use only in enclosed fixtures.
   Match the replacement to the fixture label and the original lamp.

> **Safety:** Lock out and verify absence of voltage before replacing a halogen lamp. R7s
> lamps are installed by pressing one end into a spring-loaded socket; your fingers are close
> to both contacts. Inspect the sockets for discoloration or cracked ceramic while the power
> is off.

## Phase-Outs and Conversion to LED

Federal efficiency rules that took effect in 2023 have effectively ended the sale of most
general-service incandescent and halogen lamps in the United States. Many reflector and
specialty lamps are also disappearing or getting expensive. As a result, many calls that start
as "replace a burned-out bulb" end as "convert to LED."

There are three common conversions:

| Conversion | What it is | What to check |
|------------|------------|---------------|
| LED replacement lamp | Screw-in LED lamp in the same shape and base (A19, BR30, PAR38) | Lamp is rated for enclosed or damp fixtures if needed; dimmer compatibility |
| LED retrofit module for recessed cans | LED trim/module that plugs into the existing socket with an adapter and clips into the can | The kit is listed for use in that size can; follow the kit instructions exactly |
| New LED fixture | Remove the old fixture, install a new one | Usually needs work order approval; may involve wiring (supervised or electrician) |

**Conversion checklist:**

1. Confirm the work order authorizes conversion, and confirm the color temperature (CCT,
   such as 2700 K or 3000 K) and light output the customer wants.
2. Match the **base**, **size**, and **beam** of the original. An LED PAR38 flood replaces a
   halogen PAR38 flood.
3. Check the **dimmer.** Use a dimmer listed as compatible with the LED product, or note on the
   work order that the dimmer needs replacement.
4. For retrofit kits, follow the instructions and apply any label the kit requires.
5. Note that a retrofit module does not fix a damaged socket, scorched wiring, or a failed
   thermal protector that has been bypassed. Those are written up.
6. Record the old and new part numbers on the work order.

## Parts and Ordering Summary

When ordering parts for an incandescent or halogen fixture, record:

- **Lamp:** wattage, shape/size, beam or finish, base, voltage, length (for R7s), neck (PAR30)
- **Socket:** base type (E26, E12, G9, R7s), mounting style (bracket, threaded, snap-in),
  wire lead length, temperature rating (marked in °C on many sockets)
- **Thermal protector:** usually replaced as part of the housing or a manufacturer kit; record
  the fixture manufacturer and catalog number from the label
- **Fixture:** manufacturer, catalog number, IC or non-IC, can size (4 in, 5 in, 6 in)
- **Dimmer:** wattage rating and lamp-type compatibility

Parts sourcing in detail is taught in LT1-C10.

## Disposal

- **Incandescent and halogen lamps** do not contain mercury and in most areas may be disposed
  of as regular solid waste. Follow company policy and any stricter local or customer rules.
- **Broken glass** is a cut hazard. Wrap broken lamps or place them in a rigid container
  before putting them in the trash. Never leave broken quartz in a customer's wastebasket
  unwrapped.
- **Do not mix lamp types.** Fluorescent, CFL and HID lamps contain mercury and go to
  universal waste (LT1-C06). Keep incandescent and halogen lamps out of the universal waste
  boxes and keep mercury lamps out of the trash.
- **LED lamps and electronic retrofit parts** may be recycled as electronics where programs
  exist; follow company policy.

## Key Takeaways
- Never touch halogen quartz with bare fingers; clean it with alcohol if you do.
- Let halogen lamps cool, keep lenses and guards in place, and watch clearance to combustibles.
- Most general-service incandescent and halogen lamps are phased out; conversions to LED are common.
- Match base, size, beam and dimmer compatibility when converting, and follow retrofit kit instructions.
- Incandescent and halogen lamps are usually regular waste; keep them separate from mercury lamps.
