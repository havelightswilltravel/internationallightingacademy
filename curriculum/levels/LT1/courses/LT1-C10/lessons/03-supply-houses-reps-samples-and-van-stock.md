## Working With Distributors (Supply Houses)

An electrical **distributor**, or supply house, sells parts to contractors. Your company
probably has accounts at one or more distributors with negotiated pricing.

**How to work the counter well:**

1. **Come prepared.** Bring the work order number, the customer or job name, and a written
   parts list with complete part numbers, quantities, and label photos on your phone.
2. **Use the company account** and purchase order (PO) process exactly as your office
   requires. Never pay personally unless your company tells you to.
3. **Ask the counter to confirm crosses** and to show you the spec sheet if you are unsure.
4. **Open the box at the counter** and compare the new part to your photos or sample: lamp
   base and length, ballast lamp count and start type, driver output, case size. It is much
   easier to fix a mistake at the counter than at the customer's site.
5. **Keep the receipt or packing slip** and turn it in with the work order. You will need it
   for billing and for warranty claims.
6. **Be respectful.** Counter staff can save you hours. Treat them as part of your team.

For larger or special-order items (fixtures, special lenses, emergency drivers), the
office or a supervisor usually places the order. Your job is to give them **complete,
correct information.**

## Manufacturer Representatives

A **manufacturer's rep** (or rep agency) represents one or more manufacturers in your region.
Reps can:

- Identify discontinued or unusual products from photos and catalog numbers
- Provide spec sheets, installation instructions, and cross-references
- Arrange **product samples** for customer approval
- Help with warranty claims and return authorizations
- Answer technical questions about compatibility (driver with dimmer, retrofit kit with fixture)

At LT1, contact reps through your supervisor or office unless you have been told otherwise.
Keep notes on who helped and how to reach them.

## Bringing and Requesting Samples

### Bringing the old part as a sample

Bring the removed part (or one from the same fixture type) to the supply house when:

- The label is missing, faded or unreadable
- The part is unusual: an odd socket, a special lamp, a lens clip, a bracket
- Your measurements or photos leave doubt
- The counter cannot find a cross from the information you have

Get approval before taking anything from a customer's site, and note it on the work order
("removed (1) ballast as sample, returned/replaced on [date]"). Bag and label the sample with
the job name and fixture location so it is not lost or mixed up. Do not carry broken lamps
or leaking ballasts as samples; photograph them instead and follow waste procedures. A
ballast that may contain PCBs (older magnetic ballasts without a "No PCBs" label) stays on
site and is handled per LT1-C06.

### Requesting a sample of a new product

When the job involves many fixtures or a product change (for example, converting a whole
floor to LED retrofit kits), the office may **request a sample** from the rep or distributor
before ordering the full quantity. A sample lets you:

- Confirm the fit (lens size, mounting, can size) on one fixture
- Show the customer the color temperature and brightness and get approval
- Check compatibility with the existing dimmers or controls

A wrong product on one fixture is a small problem. A wrong product on 200 fixtures is a
large one.

## Van and Truck Stock

Most companies give each technician a standard **van stock** list: the parts used on most
calls so you can fix common problems on the first visit.

| Typical van stock | Notes |
|-------------------|-------|
| Common lamps (F32T8 in the site's color temperature, A19/BR30 LED, common HID lamps) | Store in protective sleeves or boxes; secure against breakage |
| Common ballasts and universal LED drivers | Note the exact models the company stocks |
| Sockets (shunted and non-shunted tombstones, medium-base) | Keep them labeled; they look alike |
| Wire connectors, wire, tape, cable ties | Listed products only |
| Switches, receptacles, GFCIs, cover plates | Match colors used by regular customers |
| Fuses for fixtures and photocells | Label by size and rating |

**Good van stock habits:**

1. **Record every part** you use from van stock on the work order, so it is billed and
   restocked.
2. **Restock on schedule** using the company list.
3. **Rotate stock** so older items are used first, and keep boxes dry and undamaged.
4. **Keep parts organized** in labeled bins. A part you cannot find is a part you do not have.
5. **Do not let wrong or returned parts pile up** in the van. Return them promptly (Lesson 4).

> **Safety:** Secure lamps, ladders and heavy boxes in the van so they cannot shift during a
> stop. Broken fluorescent and HID lamps in a closed van release mercury vapor and glass.
> If a mercury lamp breaks in the van, ventilate and clean up per company procedure.

## Key Takeaways
- Go to the supply house prepared: work order, complete part numbers, quantities and photos.
- Open the box at the counter and compare it to the old part before you leave.
- Reps help identify products, provide documents, arrange samples and support warranty claims.
- Bring the old part when labels are unreadable; request a sample before large orders or product changes.
- Record van stock usage on the work order, restock on schedule and keep stock organized and secured.
