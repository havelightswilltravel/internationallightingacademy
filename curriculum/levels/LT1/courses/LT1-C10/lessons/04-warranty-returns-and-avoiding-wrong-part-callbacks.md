## Warranties

Most lighting products carry a manufacturer's warranty. LED fixtures, drivers and retrofit
kits commonly carry multi-year warranties; lamps and ballasts often have shorter ones. The
exact terms are in the manufacturer's warranty statement or spec sheet.

A warranty claim usually needs:

- **Part number and catalog number** of the failed product
- **Date code or serial number** from the label
- **Install date** (from the work order) and/or proof of purchase (invoice or packing slip)
- **Description of the failure** and sometimes photos
- **The failed part itself**, returned under an authorization number

This is why documentation matters. If you cannot show when a part was installed, the company
may not be able to claim the warranty. Some companies have techs write the install date on
new ballasts and drivers with a permanent marker. Follow your company's practice.

**Things that can void a warranty:**

- Installing the product in a way the instructions do not allow (wrong voltage, wrong
  location rating, wrong dimmer)
- Using an incompatible part, such as a driver that does not match the board
- Physical damage or modification

## Returns and RMAs

There are two kinds of returns:

| Return type | Example | What to know |
|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Unused / wrong part | Ordered a 2-lamp ballast but needed a 3-lamp | Return promptly in the original, undamaged box with the receipt; restocking fees may apply; opened or special-order items may not be returnable |
| Defective / warranty | New driver failed after a month | Requires a **Return Material Authorization (RMA)** from the distributor or manufacturer before shipping |

### RMA steps

1. **Do not throw away the failed part.** Remove it (de-energized), keep it intact, and bring
   it back unless told otherwise.
2. **Tag it** with the job name, work order number, fixture location, date installed, date
   failed and the symptom ("no output," "flickers at low dim").
3. **Give the office the information** so they can request the RMA number from the
   distributor or manufacturer.
4. **Write the RMA number** on the tag and on the box.
5. **Store it in the designated return bin**, not loose in the van.
6. **Ship or return it** as instructed, and keep the paperwork with the work order.

> **Safety:** Lock out and verify absence of voltage before removing any part for return.
> Do not pack broken lamps with returns; mercury lamps are universal waste. Damaged ballasts
> that are leaking or may contain PCBs are not returned; they are handled per LT1-C06.

## Documenting Part Numbers on the Work Order

Every part that goes in or comes out should be on the work order:

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Location | Bldg A, 2nd floor, Rm 214, fixture tag L-3 |
| Removed | (1) Brand X driver, part no. as labeled, 40 W, 700 mA, 0-10 V, date code from label |
| Installed | (1) Brand Y driver, part no., crossed per spec sheet; programmed to 700 mA |
| Lamps | (3) F32T8/835 |
| Source | Van stock / Supply house invoice no. |
| Warranty | Old part under warranty? RMA requested? |
| Photos | Label before, wiring before, completed fixture |

Why this matters: the next technician on site needs to know what is installed, the office
needs it for billing and restocking, and warranty claims depend on it.

## Avoiding Wrong-Part Callbacks

Most wrong-part problems come from the same few mistakes. Use this checklist **before you
leave the site** with an order and **again at the counter**:

1. **Did I read the whole label?** Not just the wattage. For drivers: output current,
   voltage window, dimming, case size.
2. **Do I have clear photos** of every label, the wiring, and the inside of the fixture?
3. **Did I measure** lamps, lenses, cases and cans where there was any doubt?
4. **Did I count correctly?** How many fixtures, lamps per fixture, and spares requested?
5. **Do lamp and ballast match?** ANSI code (HID), start type and lamp count (fluorescent),
   lamp type and ballast compatibility (reduced-wattage lamps, TLEDs).
6. **Is the replacement allowed by the fixture label?** Maximum wattage, lamp type, enclosed
   or open rating, IC/non-IC.
7. **Did I confirm the cross** against the spec sheet, not just the cross-reference tool?
8. **Did I open the box at the counter** and compare?
9. **Did I write it all on the work order** with the part numbers?

If you are unsure about any item, call your supervisor before ordering. A five-minute call
is cheaper than a second truck roll.

## Key Takeaways
- Warranty claims depend on part numbers, date codes, install dates and proof of purchase.
- Unused returns go back promptly and undamaged; defective parts need an RMA before return.
- Keep, tag and store failed parts properly; never return broken mercury lamps or PCB ballasts.
- Record every removed and installed part with full part numbers, source and photos.
- Use the wrong-part checklist on site and at the counter to prevent callbacks.
