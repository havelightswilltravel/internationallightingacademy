## PPE Is the Last Line of Defense

Personal protective equipment does not remove a hazard. It reduces injury when everything
else has failed. OSHA requires the employer to assess hazards and provide appropriate PPE
(29 CFR 1910.132), and requires you to use it properly. PPE only works if it is the **right
type**, in **good condition**, and **worn correctly.**

## Standard PPE for a Lighting Service Call

| PPE | Standard / marking to look for | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hard hat | ANSI/ISEA Z89.1, **Class E** (electrical) | Falling objects, bumps, limited electrical protection |
| Safety glasses | ANSI/ISEA **Z87.1** ("Z87" or "Z87+" on frame and lens) | Flying debris, lamp glass, dust from ceilings |
| Work gloves | Cut-resistance rating (ANSI/ISEA 105 level A1–A9) | Sheet-metal edges, broken glass |
| Safety footwear | ASTM F2413, often with **EH** (electrical hazard) rating | Crushing, punctures, some protection from step potential |
| High-visibility vest | ANSI/ISEA 107 | Being seen in warehouses, parking lots, roadways |
| Hearing protection | NRR rating on package | Loud plants, power tools |

Your job hazard analysis may add other items: respirator for dusty ceilings (requires a
separate program, fit test, and medical clearance), face shield, or fall protection harness.

### Hard Hat Classes

- **Class E (Electrical):** tested to 20,000 volts. This is the class we use.
- **Class G (General):** tested to 2,200 volts.
- **Class C (Conductive):** no electrical protection. **Never use a Class C hard hat or a
  vented hat for electrical work.**

The voltage rating is about the shell's resistance during a brief contact from above. It does
not make you safe to touch energized equipment.

## Inspecting PPE Before Every Use

### Hard Hat
1. Check the shell for cracks, dents, gouges, holes, and a chalky or faded surface (UV damage).
2. Flex the brim gently; a brittle shell is done.
3. Check the suspension for cracked straps, broken clips, and stretched headbands.
4. Find the date stamp. Follow the manufacturer's replacement interval (commonly the
   suspension every 12 months and the shell every few years, or sooner if damaged).
5. Never drill holes, paint, or apply solvent-based stickers that can weaken the shell.

### Safety Glasses
1. Look for the Z87 mark.
2. Replace if scratched enough to affect vision, cracked, or missing side shields.
3. Clean with lens cleaner, not your shirt.

### Gloves
1. Look for holes, cuts, worn fingertips, and soaked-in oil or chemicals.
2. Choose by task: cut-resistant for lamps and sheet metal; leather for rough handling.
3. Ordinary work gloves provide **no** electrical protection.

### Footwear
Check for worn-through soles, punctures, separated soles, and exposed steel. An EH rating
is reduced by wet conditions and worn soles.

> **Safety:** Damaged PPE is removed from service immediately. Tag it, tell your supervisor,
> and get a replacement. Do not "make do."

## Wearing PPE Correctly

- Hard hat: brim forward unless the manufacturer allows reverse wear (the hat will be marked
  with a reverse-donning symbol). Adjust the suspension snug enough to stay on when you bend.
- Glasses: snug against your face; use foam-lined or goggles in dusty ceilings.
- Clothing: long pants and sleeved shirts made of natural fibers (cotton, wool) or arc-rated
  material. **Synthetic fabrics like polyester and nylon can melt onto skin** if exposed to a
  flash or flame.
- **Remove conductive jewelry:** rings, watches with metal bands, bracelets, and necklaces
  can conduct current and cause severe burns.

## Electrical PPE: Awareness Only

You will see qualified electricians use specialized electrical PPE:

- **Rubber insulating gloves** with leather protectors. Classes are based on maximum use
  voltage: Class 00 (500 V AC), Class 0 (1,000 V AC), and higher classes for higher voltages.
  They must be air-tested (inflated and checked for leaks) before each use and electrically
  re-tested at regular intervals (29 CFR 1910.137).
- **Arc-rated (AR) clothing and face shields/hoods** rated in cal/cm².

These are required when someone works on or near exposed parts that are, or might be,
energized, including **testing for absence of voltage**, because a circuit is treated as live
until it is proven dead. An LT1 never performs energized repair work. As part of your LT1
training you will learn to verify absence of voltage on lighting circuits (LT1-C03 and
LT1-C06), and you will do it **only after you have been trained, issued the PPE required by
our electrical safety program, and while working under the direct supervision of a qualified
person.** The full qualified-worker rules come in later levels (EA3-C05). Until then: if a
task calls for rubber gloves or arc-rated PPE, it is not your task to do alone.

## Caring for PPE

- Store PPE out of direct sunlight and away from heat (not on the truck dashboard).
- Keep glasses in a case.
- Wash cut-resistant gloves per the manufacturer.
- Replace rather than repair.

## Key Takeaways
- PPE must be the right type, in good condition, and worn correctly to protect you.
- Use Class E hard hats and Z87 eye protection on every lighting job.
- Inspect every item before each use and remove damaged PPE from service.
- Wear natural-fiber or arc-rated clothing and remove conductive jewelry.
- Rubber insulating gloves and arc-rated PPE are used only by workers trained and authorized for the task; an LT1 uses them for supervised voltage verification, never for energized repair work.
