## Driving Is the Most Dangerous Thing We Do

Counting the hours, **driving is the riskiest part of a lighting technician's day**. Our trucks
are heavy (a loaded bucket truck can weigh around 17,000 lb), many tow lifts, and we drive in
traffic, parking lots and winter weather every week. In less than a year our company had **two
vehicles totaled**. This lesson collects our company's driving rules from the safety manual and
the monthly safety meetings.

## Before You Roll: The Daily DVIR

- **Every vehicle and every trailer gets a pre-trip driver vehicle inspection report (DVIR)
  every shift**, completed in the company app. It takes about five minutes. **Defects are
  reported to your supervisor before the vehicle is used.** Nobody drives a company vehicle until
  they have been trained on the DVIR and the electronic logs.
- **On you or in the truck:** driver's license, medical card if required, registration and
  insurance, inspection paperwork.
- **The walk-around:** horn; windshield (no cracks in the driver's view), wipers and fluid;
  mirrors; **no warning lights on the dash**; service and parking brakes; steering; headlights,
  tail, brake and marker lights and reflectors; seat belts; three reflective triangles; fire
  extinguisher mounted, charged and current; tires, wheels and rims; loads secured or covered;
  **registered weight greater than actual weight**, including any trailer.
- **Going out of town?** Inspect the vehicle **days ahead** so there is time to fix defects.
- **Log in and out** of the electronic log every shift, and set yourself off duty at day's end.

### DOT rules and why we follow them anyway

Federal motor carrier rules generally apply to vehicles (or vehicle-plus-trailer combinations)
rated **over 10,000 lb that cross state lines** (49 CFR 390.5); a commercial driver's license is
required federally at **26,001 lb** (49 CFR 383.5). **In-state thresholds for DOT rules, DOT
numbers and inspections vary by state**, and a heavy trailer can push a combination over a limit.
Our company policy is to **follow the federal standards everywhere**: the rules can change, and a
single trip can cross a state line. Ask your supervisor before towing anything unusually heavy.

## Warning Signs You Act On

- **Battery light at start-up** can mean a broken serpentine belt: no belt, no coolant flow. Shut
  it off and call it in.
- **Check-engine light or any vehicle problem:** tell your supervisor **right away** and note it on
  your pull sheet.
- **Engine dies while moving:** press hard and hold the brake. Some of our trucks have a backup
  electric brake-boost motor.
- **Diesel exhaust filter "regen" request:** accept it and pull over for the few minutes it takes.
- **Check the oil regularly** (a good habit: every fourth fuel stop).
- **Rock chip:** inspect at the next stop, circle it with a marker and note it on the pull sheet.

## On the Road

- **Flip the switch.** During your pre-trip, switch into professional, defensive-driver mode.
- **Phones distract three ways:** eyes off the road, hands off the wheel, mind off driving.
  **Hands-free still distracts the mind.** Let calls go to voicemail or pull over safely. Set
  navigation and audio before you roll. Commercial drivers may not use a hand-held phone at all
  (49 CFR 392.80, 392.82).
- **Keep at least a 3-second following gap**, more for heavy trucks and bad weather; use your
  mirrors constantly.
- **Slow way down when passing a lane of stopped traffic.** Someone may pull or step out.
- **Never be in a hurry.** If you feel pressure, tell your dispatcher. No job is worth a crash.
- **Bucket and lift trucks:** the bucket and step platform **hang off the front** and grab things
  when you turn the wheel and when you back up. One of our lift trucks was totaled this way.
  Watch your swing, and **use an observer to back whenever possible.** Stow the jib before you
  drive (LT3-C06).
- **Parking:** set the parking brake every time; trucks over 15,000 lb GVW are also **chocked**.
  Cone off the area around bucket and lift trucks at the job. Equipment left near a road overnight
  needs lights or reflectors.
- **No off-road driving** in a company vehicle. **Report any moving violation**, on or off the
  clock, to your supervisor right away. Shut the engine off rather than idle, except to cool the
  cab or run the PTO.

## Towing Trailers

- **No brake controller, no heavy braked trailer.** Our company had a near miss when a new
  vehicle went out towing a lift trailer with **no brake controller installed**.
- **Set the controller gain every time you hook up**, per the company setup guide. Some
  controllers have no dial, only plus and minus buttons.
- **Trailer checks:** coupler latched and **pinned** ("pins in holes," every pin, every time);
  **safety chains crossed** under the coupler; breakaway cable attached separately and its
  battery charged; lights working; tires and load secured.
- **No homemade repairs.** A broken spare-tire mount is fixed the manufacturer's way, or the spare
  comes off. After any scrape, check the jack and tongue welds. Report all trailer damage.

## After an Accident

1. **Injuries? Call 911 first.** Then call your supervisor or the office.
2. **Another vehicle or person involved:** call the police, and report to our insurance company
   **no matter how minor**. If the police will not come out, **call both insurance companies from
   the scene.**
3. **Document:** photos of all vehicles, plates, the scene close and far; witness names; a
   diagram; the company accident investigation form (keep blank forms in your paperwork tin).
4. **Never admit fault** or argue at the scene. Answer the police honestly.
5. **Property damage only:** do not decide to settle it yourself; call your supervisor. If the
   owner is not there, leave your name and your supervisor's contact information.

## Breakdowns on the Road

Our trucks have stalled in traffic, broken down out of town and been stuck in mud. A
breakdown becomes an injury when someone stands in a traffic lane, crawls under a truck that can
roll, or makes a roadside repair that should have waited.

1. **Get out of traffic.** Signal and coast as far off the road as you safely can. If the engine
   dies while moving, press hard and hold the brake (see above) and steer for the shoulder.
2. **Hazard flashers on immediately**, then **set out the three reflective triangles within
   10 minutes**: on a two-way road, one about 10 ft behind the truck on the traffic side, one
   about 100 ft behind and one about 100 ft ahead; on a divided or one-way road, at about 10,
   100 and 200 ft behind. Move the rear triangle back as far as 500 ft if a hill or curve hides
   the truck (49 CFR 392.22). Wear a high-visibility vest and walk facing traffic.
3. **Stay clear of traffic while you wait.** On a busy road, the safest place is usually away
   from the vehicle, off the shoulder and behind a barrier if there is one. If you must stay in
   the cab, keep your seat belt on.
4. **Call your supervisor.** The office arranges towing through our fleet service; the contact is
   in the tech cheat sheets. **Do not run an engine with a broken serpentine belt** while you wait.
5. **No roadside repairs unless our mechanic or your supervisor has approved them**, the truck is
   off the road on level ground, and the parking brake holds and the wheels are chocked. Never
   work under a vehicle held up only by a jack.
6. **Stuck in mud or snow:** don't spin the wheels. If light rocking and traction material do not
   free it, call for a proper tow. Do not let a passer-by yank the truck out with a chain or strap
   tied to whatever is handy; a strap or hook that lets go can kill.
7. **Lift truck with the boom stuck up:** stay in the basket and use the manufacturer's emergency
   lowering, then call. Never climb out of an elevated basket (LT3-C06).
8. **Document it:** location, what happened, photos, and the note on your pull sheet.

### Traveling out of town: check in every day
When you're on an out-of-town job, **talk with your supervisor every day**: where you are, how
the work and the vehicle are doing, any problems, and your plan for tomorrow. If something goes
wrong on the road, your supervisor already knows where you are and what you're driving, and can
send help or make decisions quickly.

## Winter Driving

- Slow down and **triple your following distance**. Bridges, overpasses and shaded spots freeze
  first. No cruise control on ice; don't pass snowplows.
- **Canyon and mountain roads with snow:** our company prefers that you **not travel them**. Some
  states require chains or traction tires, and not every company vehicle has them. **Check the
  weather and talk to your supervisor before you go**; dispatchers can misjudge conditions.
- **Diesels: keep at least a half tank in winter**, especially overnight. Fuel lines and filters
  gel when a diesel runs low in the cold, and there is no quick way to restart it.
- **Stranded?** Stay with the vehicle and call for help. Tie bright cloth to the antenna. Run the
  engine about 10 minutes an hour for heat, **with the exhaust pipe clear of snow and a downwind
  window cracked** to prevent carbon monoxide poisoning. Keep moving your arms and legs.

> **Safety:** A vehicle is a tool like any other. If the DVIR finds a defect, if the brake
> controller will not set, or if the road is beyond what the truck is equipped for, stop and
> call your supervisor. You have stop-work authority behind the wheel too.

## Key Takeaways
- DVIR on every vehicle and trailer every shift; report defects before use.
- Federal DOT rules start at 10,001 lb across state lines; in-state thresholds vary by state;
  our company follows the federal standards everywhere.
- Hands-free still distracts. Keep a 3-second gap; slow down passing stopped lanes.
- Watch the front-mounted bucket when turning and backing; use an observer to back.
- Brake controller set, pins in, chains crossed, breakaway attached before towing.
- Accident: 911 if hurt, supervisor, police and both insurers, photos and the form; never admit fault.
- Breakdown: off the road, flashers on, triangles out within 10 minutes, stay clear of traffic,
  call your supervisor; no roadside repairs without approval.
- Winter: no snowy canyons without supervisor approval; diesels at least half full; stay with a
  stranded vehicle and keep the exhaust clear.
