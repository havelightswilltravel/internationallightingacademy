## Why This Lesson Exists

Lessons 1–5 covered how OSHA expects a safe workplace to run. This lesson covers **how our
company runs it**: the rules from our safety manual that are reviewed at the monthly safety
meetings, what happens when a trained rule is broken, and exactly what to do when someone gets
hurt. Every rule here traces back to an injury, a near miss or a damaged truck somewhere in our
history.

## The Ten Commandments of Safety

Our safety manual boils safe work down to ten rules:

1. Learn the safe way to do your job **before** you start.
2. Always think safely and act safely.
3. Obey safety rules and regulations; they are there for your protection.
4. Wear proper clothing and protective equipment.
5. Conduct yourself properly. **Horseplay is prohibited.**
6. Operate equipment correctly, according to your training, and only for its intended use.
7. Inspect tools and equipment **before** starting work.
8. Tell your supervisor **immediately** about unsafe conditions or practices.
9. Report any injury **immediately**.
10. Support the safety program and take an active part.

## Everyday Workplace Rules

- **Come to work fit for duty**, rested and unimpaired, and dressed for the work: work clothes,
  gloves and proper boots. No loose clothing or dangling jewelry near tools and moving parts.
- **Come prepared for any environment.** A job in a walk-in freezer can last all day, even in
  July. Keep a jacket and spare dry gloves on the truck year-round.
- **Hard hat** (Class E, ANSI/ISEA Z89.1) whenever something could fall on you, or you could
  strike your head while being lifted in a bucket truck or lift. Use **insulated wire strippers**
  and **hearing protection** around loud equipment (Lesson 3).
- **Be trained before you operate anything unsupervised**: lifts, power tools, the jib, a
  forklift. Our rule on forklifts is simple: if you have not been trained and authorized, do not
  run one; stay well clear of one that is moving, and ask trained site staff to move material
  for you.
- **Know the emergency exits** at every site before you start work.
- **Housekeeping is the foundation of a safe site.** Stack material so it cannot fall, clear scrap
  often, and keep walkways, exits, **electrical panels and fire equipment** clear.
- **Stay in your scope.** Lighting technicians work **inside the lighting fixture**. Anything
  beyond that (the branch circuit, the panel, the building wiring) needs supervisor or management
  approval and the right qualification (LT4-C07 covers what electricians may do).
- **Steer clear of equipment you do not recognize.** Our crews have come across industrial
  equipment with radiation warning labels on job sites. Do not touch, move or work next to it
  until the site contact tells you it is safe.

## Health Hazards and Fire Prevention

- **Stay reachable.** Keep cell contact with the office at every site so help can be sent.
- **Dust and insulation:** wear a mask above ceilings, in attics and when cutting or drilling.
- **Lead and asbestos:** any demolition or remodel work (drywall, painted surfaces, grinding) is
  assessed **before** work starts. If you suspect asbestos in old ceiling tile or insulation,
  stop and call your supervisor (29 CFR 1926.1101, 1926.62).
- **Chemicals: no SDS, no use.** Label every secondary container (LT1-C11 Lesson 5 covers HazCom).
- **Fire extinguishers** in every vehicle and in the shop are visible, accessible, inspected and
  charged (29 CFR 1910.157). Know where yours is.
- Flammable liquids over one gallon go in **approved safety cans**. Keep combustibles away from
  torches and other heat-producing tools, with an extinguisher at hand.
- **No unvented open-flame heaters ("salamanders")** in enclosed spaces, and **no propane (LPG)
  stored inside buildings**.

## Material Handling and Team Lifts

Material handling is on our safety-meeting calendar several times a year because ladders,
fixtures, lift parts and boxes of material are heavy and awkward, and a back injury can end a
career. Our rules:

- **Size up the load first.** Look for a weight label, sharp corners, nails or other
  projections. **Get help if the load is over 50 lb or the lifting is repetitive**, or use a cart,
  dolly, hoist or lift (only one you have been trained to operate).
- **Team lifts are required** for the large multi-position ladder and for extension ladders over
  30 ft, to carry, raise and position. Agree on the route and the signals (stop, turn, set down)
  before you lift, and keep in step. **This rule applies to everyone, managers included**, and
  anyone may stop a one-person lift.
- **The squat lift:** clear the path, feet firm with one beside the load and one slightly behind,
  get close, grip with your fingers under the load, tighten your stomach muscles and lift
  smoothly with your legs. **Don't twist; step.**
- **Treat ladders as material.** Carry them balanced and watch both ends around people, doors and
  vehicles.
- **Store heavy items low and light items high**, in the warehouse and on the trucks.
- **Warm up.** Strains happen most at the **start of a shift**, when muscles are cold, and
  **right after lunch**. A few minutes of stretching before the first heavy lift helps.
- **Sore or stiff? Tell your supervisor before the shift** so your dispatch can be changed. We
  would rather move a job than send you to a lifting job hurt.
- **Hoists, slings and chains:** use only gear that is maintained and rated for the load, inspect
  it before the lift, and report defects to your supervisor and the maintenance lead (LT3-C12
  covers rigging).

## How Our Rules Are Enforced

Our enforcement policy applies to safety rules **you have been trained on**:

| Violation | Result |
|---|---|
| First | Written warning, a copy to you and your supervisor, refresher training if needed, and a note in your file |
| Second (same or similar) within one year | Suspension |
| Third within one year | Termination, regardless of how serious it was |
| Willful neglect, blatant disregard, or creating a life-threatening condition | Can mean immediate termination without a warning |

The policy is not there to punish honest mistakes or to discourage reporting. It says that a
rule you were trained on is **not optional**. Reporting a near miss, or your own mistake, is
exactly what we want, and OSHA forbids retaliation for reporting (29 CFR 1904.35–.36).

> **Safety:** Discipline targets unsafe acts, never reports. If you are ever told not to report
> an injury or a near miss, tell the safety committee or management.

## When Someone Is Hurt: Our Procedure

1. **Emergency** (serious injury, any shock with symptoms, fall from height, loss of
   consciousness, heat stroke): **call 911** or go to the nearest emergency room. Notify your
   supervisor as soon as you are able.
2. **Non-emergency work injury:** tell your supervisor **first**, then go to the **company's
   designated occupational clinic**.
3. **Anything beyond first aid needs the company incident investigation form**, filled out the
   same day. **Temporary or agency workers** also notify their own employer right away.
4. **Carry the form.** A blank copy belongs in your vehicle's **paperwork tin**, especially if you
   work alone or travel. Incidents while traveling for work are reported too.
5. **Preserve the scene and take photos** when it is safe (Lesson 2).

Vehicle accidents follow their own procedure (Lesson 7).

## The Safety Committee and Root Causes

Our **safety committee** (company leadership plus employee representatives) meets **monthly**.
At least once a year it reviews injury trends and the **root causes** of serious and
near-serious incidents. A root-cause investigation looks past "he slipped" to the methods,
equipment, training, organization and culture behind it, so the fix protects the next person.
The committee also keeps the **preventive maintenance** program for tools, vehicles and lifts
on track, and decides which hazards get a written **job hazard analysis**. If you have an idea
or a concern, take it to your representative or to the monthly safety meeting.

## Key Takeaways
- Know the ten commandments: learn the safe way first, no horseplay, inspect before use, report
  hazards and injuries immediately.
- Come fit for duty and prepared for any environment; be trained before operating anything,
  including forklifts.
- Get help over 50 lb; team-lift the big ladders, whatever your title; warm up, and tell your
  supervisor before the shift if you are sore.
- Lighting technicians work inside the fixture; anything beyond needs approval.
- No SDS, no use. Extinguishers charged; safety cans over one gallon; no salamanders or indoor LPG.
- Enforcement: warning, then suspension, then termination within a year; willful or
  life-threatening violations can mean immediate termination.
- Emergency: 911. Non-emergency: supervisor, then the company clinic. Beyond first aid: the
  incident investigation form, with a blank copy in your paperwork tin.
