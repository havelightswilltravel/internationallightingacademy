## Scope: What an LT1 May Do

Replacing a switch or receptacle is common work, but it is still work on a branch circuit.
At LT1:

- You may replace a switch or receptacle **like-for-like** only when **your company has
  trained and authorized you** for that task, and under supervision until you are signed off
  on skill LT1-S10.
- You **never** work on it energized.
- You **write it up** when anything goes beyond the device itself (see the table in Lesson 2),
  when the power source is not right, or when a different device type is needed.

## The Company Procedure, Expanded

The company guide says: **look for damaged, burned or loose parts; check connections; and
replace if line voltage is good.**

"Replace if line voltage is good" means: before you blame the device, confirm that proper
voltage is **reaching** it. If voltage is good at the incoming (line) conductors, the device is
the problem and can be replaced. If voltage is **not** good, a new device will not fix
anything; the problem is upstream (a tripped breaker, an upstream GFCI, a loose splice in
another box) and is written up.

That voltage check is an **energized** test. It must be performed by a qualified person with
the PPE required by the company's NFPA 70E program, **before** the circuit is locked out.
The replacement itself is always done with the circuit locked out and verified dead.

## Step-by-Step Replacement

### Before you start
1. **Confirm the scope** on the work order and that you are authorized for this task.
2. **Identify the device and the replacement.** Read the old device: type, amps, voltage,
   color, TR/WR/GFCI/IG features. Confirm the replacement is the **same type and rating or an
   approved upgrade** (for example, adding TR where it is now required).
3. **Confirm line voltage** (qualified person only), or confirm that a qualified person has
   done so and recorded it.

### De-energize and verify
4. **Identify the correct breaker.** Use the panel schedule, a circuit tracer, or have a
   qualified person identify it. Do not open the panel dead front.
5. **Lock out and tag** the breaker per company LOTO procedure.
6. **Verify absence of voltage** at the device with a tested meter, **live-dead-live**:
   hot-to-neutral, hot-to-ground and neutral-to-ground. Check every conductor in the box; a box
   can contain more than one circuit.

> **Safety:** A non-contact voltage tester is useful for a quick check, but it is never
> enough to prove a circuit is dead. Use a meter, live-dead-live. If you find voltage on any
> conductor after the breaker is locked out, stop. There is another source in the box. Write
> it up.

### Replace
7. **Photograph the wiring** before disconnecting, so you can put it back exactly.
8. **Remove the old device**, keeping track of which wire went where:
   - Receptacles: hot (black or red) on **brass** screws, neutral (white) on **silver**
     screws, ground (bare or green) on the **green** screw.
   - Switches: the hot and switch leg on the brass screws, ground on the green screw.
   - GFCI: incoming power on **LINE**; downstream wiring on **LOAD** only if the old GFCI was
     protecting downstream outlets (more in Lesson 4).
9. **Inspect the conductors.** Cut back any nicked or heat-darkened copper only if enough
   length remains (generally you need at least about 6 in of free conductor in the box). If not
   enough wire remains, or the insulation damage continues back into the box, write it up.
10. **Connect the new device** on screw terminals (clockwise hook) or back-wire clamps, not
    backstabs. Strip to the strip gauge, torque to the manufacturer's value.
11. **Connect the ground** to the green screw. If several grounds are in the box, they should
    be spliced together with a pigtail to the device.
12. **Note the breaking tabs.** On a duplex receptacle, a brass tab between the two halves may
    have been removed on the old device (split-wired or half-switched receptacle). If so,
    remove the same tab on the new one. If you are unsure, write it up.
13. **Fold the wires** neatly into the box, mount the device straight and flush, and install
    the cover plate. Do not overtighten the plate screws on plastic plates.

### Restore and test
14. **Remove LOTO** per company procedure, after making sure everyone is clear.
15. **Test the device:**
    - Switch: operate it several times; the load turns fully on and off with no flicker.
    - Receptacle: plug in a receptacle tester and check the indication (Lesson 4).
    - GFCI: press TEST (power should cut off), then RESET (power should return).
16. **Document** on the work order: location, device removed (with damage noted), device
    installed (manufacturer and part number), line-voltage reading and who took it, test
    results, and anything written up. Attach before and after photos.

## Like-for-Like Replacement Table

| Removed | Replace with | Notes |
|---------|--------------|-------|
| 15 A duplex, ivory | 15 A duplex, ivory, TR if required | Ask about TR/WR/GFCI requirements in the location |
| 20 A duplex (5-20R) | 20 A duplex | Only if the circuit is 20 A |
| GFCI | GFCI of same rating | Wire LINE/LOAD to match the original protection |
| Single-pole switch, 20 A 120/277 V | Same rating | Check 277 V rating in commercial buildings |
| Dimmer | Dimmer rated for the load type and wattage | Check LED compatibility and derating when ganged |
| 3-way switch | Write it up at LT1 | Company guide allows tracing with a diagram or write-up |

## Key Takeaways
- Replace devices only if you are authorized, and always with the circuit locked out and verified dead.
- "Replace if line voltage is good" is a qualified-person energized check; no voltage means the problem is upstream.
- Photograph wiring, transfer conductors to matching terminals, use screws or back-wire clamps, and torque.
- Watch for removed break-off tabs, multiple circuits in one box and damaged conductors.
- Test the finished device and document part numbers, readings and results.
