## What This System Is and Where You Find It

Every lighting tech works around **switches** (which control fixtures) and **receptacles**
(outlets, which supply plug-in equipment). The company's component list for this system is:
**outlet, switch, wire, fixture and electrical box.** These devices are on almost every job,
and loose or burned devices are a leading cause of flickering lights, dead outlets and
overheating.

| Component | What it does | How it fails |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| Receptacle (outlet) | Lets cord-and-plug equipment connect to the circuit | Worn contacts that no longer grip plugs, loose terminals, cracked face, burned slots |
| Switch | Opens and closes the hot conductor to a fixture | Worn contacts, loose or burned terminals, broken toggle |
| Wire | Carries current to and between devices | Loose connections, heat-damaged insulation, nicked conductors |
| Fixture (load) | Uses the power | Can overload a switch or dimmer if over its rating |
| Electrical box | Encloses connections, supports the device, contains sparks | Broken ears, missing cover plate, overfilled, recessed too deep in the wall |

## Switches

| Device | What it does | How to recognize it |
|--------|--------------|---------------------|
| Single-pole | Controls a light from one location | Two brass terminals plus green ground; ON/OFF printed on toggle |
| 3-way | Used in pairs to control a light from two locations | One dark "common" terminal, two lighter "traveler" terminals, no ON/OFF marking |
| 4-way | Used between two 3-ways for three or more locations | Four terminals plus ground |
| Dimmer | Varies light output | Rated in watts or VA; marked for lamp types (incandescent, LED, ELV/MLV, 0-10 V) |
| Occupancy/vacancy sensor | Turns lights on/off automatically | Covered in later controls courses |

3-way switching is introduced here only so you can recognize it. The company guide says to
use a 3-way diagram to trace power, **or write it up for an electrician.** At LT1, write it up.

## Receptacles

Receptacles are identified by their **NEMA configuration**, which describes the slot pattern.

| Type | Rating | How to recognize it |
|------|--------|---------------------|
| NEMA 5-15R | 15 A, 125 V | Two parallel vertical slots plus round ground hole |
| NEMA 5-20R | 20 A, 125 V | Same, but the neutral (wide) slot has a sideways "T" shape |
| GFCI | 15 or 20 A | TEST and RESET buttons; LINE and LOAD terminals on the back |
| AFCI receptacle | 15 or 20 A | TEST button, often marked "AFCI"; protects against arcing faults |
| Tamper-resistant (TR) | 15 or 20 A | "TR" marked on the face; internal shutters block objects |
| Weather-resistant (WR) | 15 or 20 A | "WR" marked on the face; for damp and wet locations |
| Isolated ground (IG) | 15 or 20 A | Orange triangle on the face (or an orange device); ground is isolated from the strap |
| USB combination | 15 or 20 A | One or two USB ports plus outlets; has a USB output rating |

A 20 A receptacle may be installed only on a 20 A circuit. A 15 A duplex receptacle is
allowed on a 20 A circuit when there is more than one receptacle on that circuit (a duplex
counts as two). Never put a 20 A receptacle on a 15 A circuit; it invites overloads.

### Where special receptacles are required

You do not need to memorize code sections at LT1, but know that the NEC requires:

- **GFCI protection** in places such as bathrooms, kitchens, outdoors, garages, and near
  sinks (2023 NEC 210.8)
- **AFCI protection** for many dwelling-unit circuits (2023 NEC 210.12)
- **Tamper-resistant receptacles** in dwelling units and some other occupancies (2023 NEC 406.12)
- **Weather-resistant receptacles** in damp and wet locations (2023 NEC 406.9)

The rules depend on the occupancy and the edition the local AHJ has adopted. When you
replace a device, a replacement that would now require GFCI, AFCI, TR or WR protection may
have to meet the current rule. If you are not sure what is required, ask your supervisor
before ordering.

## Reading Device Markings

Every device carries markings that tell you what it is and how it may be wired.

| Marking | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| 15A 125V / 20A 125V | Current and voltage rating |
| 15A 120-277V AC (on switches) | Switch rated for 120 V and 277 V lighting circuits |
| CU / CU-CLAD ONLY | Use with copper conductors only |
| CO/ALR | Rated for direct connection of aluminum wire (rare; aluminum wiring is a write-up) |
| Strip gauge | Molded line on the back showing how much insulation to strip |
| "Push-in: 14 AWG solid copper only" | Backstab holes accept 14 AWG only |
| LINE / LOAD | GFCI terminals: incoming power goes to LINE |
| TR, WR, orange triangle | Tamper-resistant, weather-resistant, isolated ground |
| Listing mark (UL, ETL, CSA) | Tested by a recognized lab; do not install unlisted devices |

> **Safety:** Switches used on 277 V lighting circuits must be rated for 277 V. Many cheap
> residential switches are rated 120 V only. Always read the rating before installing a
> device on commercial lighting.

## Key Takeaways
- Components: outlet, switch, wire, fixture and electrical box; know how each fails.
- Identify switches (single-pole, 3-way, 4-way, dimmer) and receptacles by their markings and NEMA configuration.
- 5-15R is 15 A; 5-20R has the T-shaped neutral slot and is 20 A only on a 20 A circuit.
- GFCI, AFCI, TR and WR requirements depend on location and the adopted code; ask before downgrading or changing device type.
- Read every marking: amps, volts, CU/AL, strip gauge, push-in limits, LINE/LOAD and listing.
