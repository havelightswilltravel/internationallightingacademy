## Why the Survey Comes First
Every decision in this course — maintain, retrofit or replace (Lesson 4), what to stock, how to
schedule — rests on what someone actually saw on site. Our company has always used **survey forms
as checklists**: if every tech records the same fields the same way, the office can quote from the
sheet without sending someone back, and the next visit can be compared to this one. As a senior
tech you will run surveys yourself and check the sheets your crew turns in.

Two company forms are covered here, each with a Library guide in the **Inspection Checklists**
category:
- **Exterior Lighting Survey Checklist** — the company Lighting Survey Worksheet, used for exterior
  inspections and retrofit quotes.
- **Emergency Lighting Inspection Checklist** — the company 90-minute inspection spreadsheet
  (taught in LT3-C04, Lesson 6). Add it to any survey that includes egress lighting.

## Planning the Survey
1. **Scope:** inspection only (lights out, damage, controls), a retrofit quote, or baseline data
   for a service agreement? The scope decides whether you need light readings and lift access.
2. **Get a map.** A site plan or aerial printout for outside, a floor plan for inside. Number every
   pole and every area on it; those numbers go in the "Fixture Location" column.
3. **Pick the time.** Exterior outages and controls problems show up at **night**; mounting
   details, pole condition and labels are easier in **daylight**. A dusk start gets both.
4. **Know the panels.** Ask where the lighting panels, time clocks, photocells and contactors are
   so voltage and controls can be confirmed.

> **Safety:** A survey is still field work. Wear high-visibility clothing in parking lots, use
> cones around a parked truck or lift, and keep away from damaged or leaning poles and open hand
> holes until they are made safe. Reading a label inside a fixture or hand hole means **LOTO and
> live-dead-live verification** first; otherwise mark the field "not verified." Lift work only by
> trained, authorized operators with fall protection per company policy and OSHA/ANSI A92.

## The Exterior Survey Worksheet, Field by Field
Work one row per **group** — the same fixture type, source and mounting in the same area.

| Field | How to fill it in well |
|---|---|
| Fixture Location | Area plus pole numbers from your map ("Front lot, poles 1–8") |
| Fixture Type | Area/shoebox, flood, wall pack, canopy, bollard, sign, post top |
| Lamp Type/Watts | Existing source and wattage (e.g., 400 W MH, 150 W LED) — this sizes the replacement |
| Fixture Height | Mounting height; drives the access plan and labor |
| Fixt Qty / Pole QTY | Fixtures and poles; note heads per pole in Comments |
| Fixture Mount Type/Size | Arm type and length, tenon/slip-fitter size, yoke, wall bracket |
| Square or Round Pole | Decides which arm or adapter will fit |
| Tapered Pole Size (base and top) | Both ends; a straight pole has the same number twice |
| Can Size | Canopy or soffit recessed can diameter |
| Voltage | From the label or panel schedule; "not verified" if unconfirmed |
| Photo# | Fixture, mounting, label and any damage |
| Comments | Outages, day-burners, cycling, broken lenses, aiming, rust, base and anchor-bolt condition, controls |

Finish with **General Comments** (access, traffic hours, trees, where controls are) and the
**Totals** block — the existing-source tally and the **number out when surveyed**. Older versions
of the form list fluorescent and metal halide lamp codes because they were built for relamp work;
today the tally sizes **LED retrofits or replacements**, and the outage count shows the customer
the condition they are starting from.

### What a senior tech checks on a crew's sheet
- Every row has quantity, source/watts, height, mounting and voltage (or "not verified").
- Pole sizes and mount types are complete — a missing tenon size is the most common reason a
  replacement head arrives and won't fit.
- Photo numbers match the photos.
- Hazards (leaning pole, open hand hole, exposed wire) were reported the same day, not just noted.

## Interior Surveys
The same worksheet carries pick lists of **interior fixture types** (1×4 and 2×4 troffers, strips,
case lights, vapor-tights, cans, track, floods) and **interior locations** (sales floor, backroom,
coolers, display cases, offices, restrooms, entries), so it works indoors as well. Until the
company's separate interior form is supplied, use this checklist:

> **Interior inspection checklist — to be replaced with the company interior survey form when
> provided.**
> - Area / location from the floor plan, fixture type, existing source and watts, quantity,
>   mounting height and ceiling type (grid, hard lid, open structure)
> - Lights out, flicker, mismatched color, yellowed or missing lenses, missing guards on lamps
> - Controls in the area: switches, sensors, time clock or panel relays, dimming
> - Emergency function: battery-backup fixtures, bug eyes and exit signs (charge indicators lit,
>   signs legible) — record on the emergency inspection spreadsheet
> - Access limits: racks, cases, food areas, hours the area can be worked
> - Voltage, photos and comments as on the exterior sheet

## From Survey to Recommendation
1. **Enter the groups into the asset inventory** (Lesson 3) so the survey becomes the baseline.
2. **Sort by condition** and apply the maintain / retrofit / replace worksheet (Lesson 4). Many
   outages or cycling on old HID heads usually point to replacement with LED, not relamping.
3. **Flag code and listing items**: retrofit kits must be listed for the fixture, and emergency
   lighting must stay functional through any change.
4. **Bundle the emergency inspection** results with the lighting proposal — failed units are
   usually the easiest repairs to approve.
5. **Repeat the same survey** at each planned visit so trends show up.

## Key Takeaways
- The company survey forms are checklists: same fields, every time, so the office can quote without a return trip.
- Map and number first; one row per group; record source/watts, height, mounting, pole size and voltage.
- Survey exteriors at dusk to catch outages and still see mounting details.
- Hazards found on a survey are reported the same day and made safe.
- The interior checklist here is temporary until the company interior form is provided.
- Surveys feed the inventory, the retrofit/replace decision and the service proposal.
