## Why Cleaning Still Matters
Lesson 02 showed that dirt is the largest recoverable loss in most spaces. Cleaning restores that
light, keeps controlled fixtures from driving harder (and hotter) to make up for it, and lets you
see damage early. Cleaning done wrong can also destroy a lens, break a wet-location seal or void a
warranty. The rule is simple: **identify the material, follow the manufacturer's instructions and
use only a compatible cleaner.**

## Step 1: Identify the Materials
| Part | Common materials | How to recognize it in the field |
|---|---|---|
| Lens or diffuser | Acrylic (PMMA), polycarbonate, glass, silicone optics | Labels or spec sheet; polycarbonate is common where impact resistance is needed (vapor-tights, parking); glass is heavier and colder to the touch |
| Reflector | Painted white steel, anodized or specular aluminum | Mirror-like or brushed metal vs. painted |
| Louvers and baffles | Painted or specular aluminum, plastic | Same as reflectors |
| Gaskets | Silicone, foam, rubber | Inside the lens frame of sealed fixtures |
| LED optics on the board | Small silicone or acrylic lenses over the LEDs | Visible after opening the fixture; do not touch |

When you are not sure, find the catalog number on the label and pull the manufacturer's
installation and maintenance instructions. Many manufacturers publish specific cleaning
instructions for each product family.

## Step 2: Match the Cleaner to the Material
Always follow the manufacturer's instructions first. Where they are silent, these general rules
apply:

| Material | Generally safe | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Acrylic | Mild, non-abrasive detergent in water; soft cloth; rinse | Ammonia and alcohol-based cleaners, solvents, abrasive pads (cause crazing and scratches) |
| Polycarbonate | Mild detergent in lukewarm water | Ammonia, strong alkaline cleaners, solvents such as acetone, abrasive pads (cause cracking and clouding) |
| Glass | Mild detergent or glass cleaner | Abrasives that scratch coatings; spraying liquid into the housing |
| Painted reflectors | Mild detergent, soft cloth | Abrasives, strong solvents |
| Anodized or specular aluminum | Mild neutral detergent, soft lint-free cloth, minimal pressure | Alkaline or acidic cleaners and abrasives (etch the finish); bare-hand fingerprints |
| Gaskets | Wipe with damp cloth only | Solvents and petroleum products (swell or harden the seal) |
| LED board and optics | Usually do not clean; only per manufacturer, often a gentle air blow-off | Touching, wiping or spraying anything onto the LEDs |

Read the **SDS** for every cleaner you bring on site. It lists the hazards, PPE and incompatible
materials, and it is part of the HazCom requirements covered in LT1-C01. Never mix cleaners.

## Step 3: Clean Safely
> **Safety:** Cleaning puts water and hands inside an electrical device at height. Lock out the
> circuit at its isolating device, verify absence of voltage live-dead-live with a tested meter,
> and use the correct ladder, scaffold or lift with fall protection as required. A wall switch or
> control system "off" is not lockout.

Procedure for a typical luminaire:

1. Confirm the scope, the fixture list and the cleaner selected for each material.
2. Notify occupants; barricade the work area below.
3. Apply LOTO and verify absence of voltage live-dead-live.
4. Set up access equipment and inspect it before use.
5. Wear the PPE the SDS and the task require (safety glasses, gloves; cut-resistant gloves for
   sheet-metal edges).
6. Remove the lens or door and set it on a padded surface or bring it down to a cleaning station.
   Do not drop dirt into an open housing.
7. Vacuum or wipe loose dust from the housing and reflector with a soft brush or HEPA vacuum.
8. Wash lenses with the selected cleaner, rinse with clean water and dry with a lint-free cloth.
   Clean both sides of the lens.
9. Wipe reflectors gently with the selected cleaner; dry.
10. Inspect for cracks, yellowing, damaged gaskets, loose wiring or heat damage. Log anything found.
11. Reinstall the lens with gaskets seated and every latch closed.
12. Remove LOTO per the procedure, restore power and confirm normal operation.
13. Record the cleaning in the inventory and log, with before/after readings where agreed
    (Lesson 06).

## Wet Locations and Sealed Fixtures
- Do not pressure-wash or hose a fixture unless the manufacturer's rating and instructions allow it.
  A damp-location or general fixture is not built for direct spray.
- Inspect gaskets every time a sealed fixture is opened. A pinched or hardened gasket lets
  moisture in; replace it with the manufacturer's part.
- Allow the fixture to dry before closing it; trapped moisture causes condensation and corrosion.

## Food Processing and Food Service Areas
- Coordinate with the facility's sanitation or food-safety lead before work. Many sites require
  approved cleaning chemicals only.
- Cover or remove exposed food, packaging and food-contact surfaces under the work area.
- Use no glass or brittle parts that could break into product unless the fixture design allows it;
  most food areas require shatter-resistant or enclosed fixtures.
- Account for every part and tool removed and returned; nothing may be left loose above a line.
- Follow the facility's hygiene rules (hair nets, smocks, hand washing).

## Occupied Spaces
Schedule cleaning for unoccupied hours when possible. When it must happen in occupied areas, work
in small zones, barricade below, use quiet methods (HEPA vacuums, hand washing at a cart) and keep
cleaners closed and labeled.

## Setting a Cleaning Interval
There is no single correct interval. Set it per area:

1. Classify the environment: clean (offices, classrooms), moderate (retail, light industry),
   dirty (manufacturing, kitchens, garages, dusty warehouses).
2. Measure light in a sample of fixtures at each visit.
3. Clean when the measured recoverable loss reaches an agreed threshold, for example a 10–15% drop
   that cleaning restores, or sooner where controls are compensating by raising output.
4. Adjust the interval after each cycle based on the data. Clean offices may go several years;
   very dirty areas may need cleaning every few months.

## Key Takeaways
- Identify the lens, reflector and gasket materials and follow the manufacturer's instructions.
- Mild detergent and water is the safe default; ammonia, alcohol, solvents and abrasives damage many
  plastics and finishes.
- Read the SDS for every cleaner; lock out and verify absence of voltage before cleaning.
- Keep wet-location seals intact and follow the facility's rules in food areas.
- Set cleaning intervals from the environment and measured, recoverable light loss.
