## Three Choices for Every Area
For each area in a customer's portfolio, a service program must eventually answer one question:
what do we do next?

| Option | What it means | Typical fit |
|---|---|---|
| **Maintain** | Keep the existing luminaires; clean, repair and replace components as they fail | Newer LED fixtures, low failure rate, light levels acceptable |
| **Retrofit** | Keep the housing; install a listed retrofit kit or TLEDs per instructions | Sound housings, good layout, legacy fluorescent or older LED in good fixtures |
| **Replace** | Remove the luminaire and install a new one | Damaged or obsolete housings, wrong distribution, poor layout, high failure rates, code triggers |

Group relamping is not one of the options; the company retrofits or replaces instead. LT5-C01/02
covers how to choose a retrofit type and write the scope. This lesson covers **when** and
**whether** to act, using service data.

## Step 1: Assess Condition
For the area, collect:

1. **Failure history** from the log (Lesson 03): failures per year, trend, causes.
2. **Light levels and uniformity** compared with the customer's target (LT4-C03/02).
3. **Physical condition:** housing corrosion, cracked or yellowed lenses, damaged
   gaskets, failed supports.
4. **Remaining legacy sources:** fluorescent or HID still in service and parts availability.
5. **Controls:** do they work, and do they meet what the customer needs?
6. **Warranty status:** in or out of warranty, and how long remains.
7. **Access cost:** ladder, lift, after-hours only, food or clean-room restrictions.

## Step 2: Check Code and Listing Implications
The choice is not only economic.

- **Retrofit kits** must be listed (or classified) for the purpose, installed exactly per the kit
  instructions and labeled as the instructions require. A retrofit that does not follow the
  instructions can leave the luminaire unlisted and is a code issue (NEC 110.3(B)).
- **TLEDs** must match the fixture: Type A works with a compatible ballast, Type B bypasses the
  ballast and may need non-shunted lampholders, Type C uses a remote driver. Label converted
  fixtures so the next tech does not install the wrong lamp.
- **Energy codes** adopted by many jurisdictions require controls (such as occupancy sensing or
  daylight responsive controls) when lighting is altered or luminaires are replaced beyond a
  certain extent. The thresholds vary by code and edition; confirm with the adopted energy code
  and the AHJ before you price.
- **Permits** may be required for retrofits and replacements; check local rules.
- **Wet, damp, hazardous or food locations** need luminaires rated for them. A retrofit cannot
  change a fixture's location rating.

> **Safety:** Condition assessment often happens above occupied spaces. Use the correct ladder or
> lift, and do not open fixtures or junction boxes unless the circuit is locked out and absence of
> voltage is verified live-dead-live. Suspect asbestos-containing ceilings or fireproofing stop
> the work until the customer clears them.

## Step 3: Compare Life-Cycle Cost
Simple payback (LT5-C01/04) compares energy savings to project cost. For service decisions you also
need **life-cycle cost (LCC)**: first cost + energy + maintenance labor, access and parts − incentives,
over the same period for every option.

**Example:** 40 first-generation LED high-bays, 9 years old, 30 ft mounting, 6,000 hours per year,
$0.12 per kWh. The failure log shows 7 driver failures last year and rising; readings are 62% of
initial. Ten-year comparison, not discounted:

| | Maintain | Retrofit kit | Replace |
|---|---|---|---|
| First cost (installed) | $0 | 40 × $460 = $18,400 | 40 × $650 = $26,000 |
| Incentive | $0 | $0 | −$1,600 |
| Watts per fixture | 220 W | 160 W | 150 W |
| Annual energy | 52,800 kWh = $6,336 | 38,400 kWh = $4,608 | 36,000 kWh = $4,320 |
| 10-year energy | $63,360 | $46,080 | $43,200 |
| Annual maintenance (parts, labor, lift) | 8 × $430 = $3,440 | $600 | $300 |
| 10-year maintenance | $34,400 | $6,000 | $3,000 |
| **10-year LCC** | **$97,760** | **$70,480** | **$70,600** |

Maintaining is clearly the most expensive choice, and light levels stay low. Retrofit and replace
come out almost even, so the decision falls to the other criteria: housing condition, whether the
new distribution improves aisle uniformity, warranty length and the listing of the kit. In a dusty
warehouse with corroded housings, replacement is the stronger recommendation; with clean, sound
housings, the retrofit is defensible. Always use the customer's real rates, hours and quotes.

## Step 4: Decide Using the Worksheet
Use the company decision worksheet. A shortened version:

| Question | Points toward |
|---|---|
| Housing damaged, corroded or wrong rating for the location? | Replace |
| Distribution or layout gives poor uniformity or glare? | Replace (redesign) |
| Housing sound, layout good, legacy source inside? | Retrofit |
| Failure rate rising, out of warranty, parts hard to get? | Retrofit or replace |
| LED, under warranty, low failure rate, levels on target after cleaning? | Maintain |
| Alteration triggers code-required controls? | Price controls into retrofit/replace |
| Access very costly (high bays, poles)? | Favor the longest-life, longest-warranty option |

**A new luminaire is the right answer** when the housing is failing, the distribution or layout is
wrong for the use, the location rating is wrong, a listed kit does not exist for the fixture, or the
retrofit and replacement LCCs are close and the new luminaire brings a longer warranty.

## Step 5: Phase the Portfolio
Few customers can do everything at once. Rank areas by safety first, then LCC savings, failure
rate, and access cost. Spread the work over budget years, and schedule retrofits or replacements for
areas just before their failure rate or warranty expiration makes maintaining them expensive.

## Key Takeaways
- Every area gets one of three decisions: maintain, retrofit or replace; group relamping is not an
  option.
- Assess failure history, light levels, condition, legacy sources, controls, warranty and access.
- Retrofits must follow listed kit or TLED instructions and labeling; alterations may trigger
  energy-code controls; confirm with the AHJ.
- Compare life-cycle cost including maintenance labor and access, not just energy payback.
- When costs are close, condition, distribution, listing and warranty decide; phase the portfolio
  by safety and value.
