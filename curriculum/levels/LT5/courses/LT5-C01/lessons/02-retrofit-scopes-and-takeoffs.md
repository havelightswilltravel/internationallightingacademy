## From Audit to Scope
A scope of work tells the customer, the estimator and the crew exactly what will be done and
what will not. A vague scope ("retrofit lighting to LED") invites disputes. A good scope
groups fixtures into **retrofit types** and states the solution for each.

## Choosing a Retrofit Approach
For each existing fixture type, pick one of four approaches.

| Approach | When it fits | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|
| Lamp replacement (TLED, LED HID replacement lamp) | Housing in good condition, budget-driven, quick install | UL Type A depends on compatible ballast; Type B requires rewiring and relabeling; limited controls options |
| Retrofit kit (UL 1598C listed) | Housing sound, want new optics/driver and dimming | Must be installed per kit instructions and labeled; installer modifies the fixture |
| New fixture | Housing damaged, layout change, high-performance optics or integrated sensors needed | Higher material cost; may require ceiling work |
| Remove / delamp | Over-lit space | Confirm uniformity and IES targets after removal |

Consider these factors in every selection:
- **Light level and quality:** match or improve on IES targets; confirm CCT and CRI with the
  customer (e.g., 3500 K or 4000 K, CRI 80+ for offices).
- **Controls:** will the new product dim on 0-10V? Does the energy code require occupancy,
  daylight or multi-level control for this scope? Integrated sensors may be cheaper than wall
  devices.
- **Rebate eligibility:** many utility programs require DesignLights Consortium (DLC) or
  ENERGY STAR qualified products. Check the program rules before selecting.
- **Voltage:** 120-277 V universal drivers simplify stocking; confirm whether any 347 V or
  480 V circuits exist.
- **Emergency lighting:** fixtures fed from emergency circuits or containing emergency
  ballasts need a matching emergency solution (emergency LED driver, UL 924 transfer device,
  or a new emergency unit). Never let a retrofit quietly remove egress lighting.

> **Safety:** Converting fixtures to Type B TLED or installing retrofit kits modifies the
> fixture. Follow the kit or lamp manufacturer's instructions exactly, apply the required
> labels, and do all work de-energized with LOTO and live-dead-live verification.

## Writing the Scope
Write scope lines that are specific and countable.

**Weak:** "Replace warehouse lights with LED."

**Strong:** "Remove (24) 400 W metal halide high-bays at 28 ft AFF in Warehouse A. Furnish
and install (24) LED high-bays, 24,000 lm nominal, 4000 K, 0-10V dimmable, with integral
occupancy sensor set to dim to 20% after 10 minutes vacancy. Reuse existing hook and cord
mounting and branch circuits. Recycle removed lamps as universal waste."

Every scope should also include:
- **Inclusions:** materials, labor, lift rental, disposal, commissioning, owner training.
- **Exclusions:** ceiling repair, asbestos abatement, painting, work above certain heights,
  replacement of damaged branch circuits, after-hours premium (unless included).
- **Assumptions:** existing circuits are in good working order, work hours, access, voltage.
- **Items requiring electrician / AHJ review:** new circuits, panel work, emergency system
  changes, permits and inspections where required.

## Building the Takeoff
A takeoff is the full list of materials and equipment needed to deliver the scope.

1. **Count retrofit types from the audit**, not from memory. Total by type and by area.
2. **Add accessories** per unit: mounting brackets, whips, sensors, wall controls, power packs,
   emergency drivers, labels.
3. **Add bulk materials:** connectors, wire, cable ties, low-voltage control cable, fasteners.
   A per-fixture allowance (e.g., 4 connectors per fixture) is acceptable if company standards
   define it.
4. **Add equipment:** lifts (with delivery and pickup), ladders, rolling scaffold.
5. **Add disposal:** lamp and ballast recycling containers, PCB ballast disposal, dumpster.
6. **Add attic stock / spares:** many customers want 2-5% spares; state it.
7. **Apply waste factor** to bulk items only (not to fixtures).

### Takeoff check
Have someone else total the counts from the audit independently. Every fixture count in the
takeoff must reconcile with the audit inventory. Any difference must be explained (e.g., "4
fixtures removed per delamping plan").

## Common Scoping Mistakes
- Forgetting fixtures in closets, stairwells, mechanical rooms and exterior areas.
- Missing that a fixture is on an emergency circuit or has an emergency battery ballast.
- Selecting a dimmable fixture but no compatible control.
- Assuming 120 V when the building lighting is 277 V.
- Ignoring mounting differences (T-grid vs hard-lid vs surface mount).
- Not pricing lift time for high ceilings or obstructed floors.
- Promising rebate amounts before confirming product eligibility and pre-approval rules.

## Key Takeaways
- Group fixtures into retrofit types and assign a specific solution to each.
- Choose among lamp replacement, retrofit kit, new fixture or delamp based on condition, quality, controls and rebates.
- Write countable scope lines with clear inclusions, exclusions and assumptions.
- Never let a retrofit remove or degrade emergency egress lighting.
- Reconcile every takeoff count against the audit inventory.
