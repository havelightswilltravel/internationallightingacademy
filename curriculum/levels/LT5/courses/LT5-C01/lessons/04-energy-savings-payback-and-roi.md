## Speaking the Customer's Language
Facility managers and owners approve projects based on money: how much it costs, how much it
saves, and how fast it pays back. an LT5 technician must calculate these numbers correctly and explain
them honestly, including the assumptions behind them.

## Step 1: Demand Reduction (kW)
For each retrofit line:

**kW reduction = (existing input watts - proposed input watts) x quantity / 1,000**

Example: 24 metal halide high-bays at 458 input watts (400 W lamp plus ballast losses)
replaced by LED high-bays at 150 W.
- (458 - 150) x 24 / 1,000 = **7.39 kW**

Use input watts from manufacturer data or an approved wattage table. Never use lamp wattage
alone for the existing system.

## Step 2: Energy Savings (kWh per year)
**kWh savings = kW reduction x annual operating hours**

Warehouse runs 16 h/day, 6 days/week, 52 weeks: 16 x 6 x 52 = 4,992 h/yr
- 7.39 kW x 4,992 h = **36,891 kWh/yr**

### Adding control savings
Occupancy or daylight controls reduce **hours** (or effective power), not fixture watts.
Apply control savings to the new system only, and use conservative factors from your
utility program or company standards. Example: if occupancy sensors are expected to reduce
runtime 25% in the warehouse:
- New system energy before controls: 0.150 kW x 24 x 4,992 = 17,971 kWh
- Control savings: 17,971 x 0.25 = 4,493 kWh
- **Total savings: 36,891 + 4,493 = 41,384 kWh/yr**

Do not apply the fixture savings and control savings both to the existing load; that
double-counts.

## Step 3: Cost Savings
**Annual cost savings = kWh savings x blended rate ($/kWh)**

The blended rate is total bill dollars divided by total kWh, taken from 12 months of bills.
It includes energy and demand charges averaged together. At $0.12/kWh:
- 41,384 x $0.12 = **$4,966/yr**

Optional additional savings (state them separately so the customer can judge):
- **Maintenance savings:** fewer lamp and ballast replacements and lift rentals.
- **Demand charge savings** if the utility bills demand separately and the lights run during
  the peak period.
- **HVAC interaction:** less lighting heat can reduce cooling load (and slightly raise heating
  load). Only include if your company has an approved method.

## Step 4: Net Project Cost
**Net cost = total installed cost - utility rebates/incentives**

Example: installed cost $14,800; DLC-qualified high-bays earn a $75 per fixture rebate:
24 x $75 = $1,800.
- Net cost = 14,800 - 1,800 = **$13,000**

> **Safety:** Customers sometimes ask to skip items to improve payback, such as leaving out
> emergency lighting replacements or code-required controls. Do not remove life-safety or
> code-required items to make the numbers look better. Present them as required scope.

## Step 5: Simple Payback
**Simple payback (years) = net cost / annual savings**
- 13,000 / 4,966 = **2.6 years**

Simple payback ignores the time value of money, energy price changes and equipment life, but
it is easy to understand and is the number most customers ask for first.

## Step 6: ROI
A simple annual return on investment:

**Simple ROI (%) = annual savings / net cost x 100**
- 4,966 / 13,000 x 100 = **38% per year**

Note that simple ROI is the inverse of simple payback (1 / 2.6 years is about 38%). Larger
customers may ask for **net present value (NPV)**, **internal rate of return (IRR)** or
**life-cycle cost**; these account for the time value of money and are usually prepared by an
estimator or energy engineer. Know what they mean so you can discuss them.

## Sensitivity: What If You Are Wrong?
Hours and rates are estimates. Show the customer a quick range:

| Operating hours | Annual savings | Simple payback |
|---|---|---|
| 3,500 h/yr | about $3,480 | 3.7 yr |
| 4,992 h/yr (assumed) | $4,966 | 2.6 yr |
| 6,000 h/yr | about $5,970 | 2.2 yr |

(Values approximate, scaling savings with hours.)

Being transparent builds trust. If the customer doubts the hours, offer light loggers to
measure them before finalizing.

## Presenting the Numbers
- One page: existing vs proposed watts, hours, kWh, $ savings, cost, rebate, payback.
- List assumptions: hours, rate, rebate status (pre-approved or estimated), maintenance
  savings method.
- Do not guarantee rebates until the utility confirms eligibility and any required
  pre-approval is received.
- Round sensibly. "2.6 years" is credible; "2.617 years" implies false precision.

## Key Takeaways
- kW reduction = (existing - proposed input watts) x quantity / 1,000.
- kWh savings = kW x hours; add control savings to the new load only.
- Cost savings = kWh x blended rate from actual bills.
- Simple payback = net cost / annual savings; simple ROI is its inverse.
- Never cut life-safety or code-required scope to improve payback.
- State assumptions and show a sensitivity range.
