## Strategy Before Products
Most customers ask "what is the cheapest LED?" A senior technician answers a better question:
**what result does this space need, and what is the lowest total cost to get it and keep it?**
Decide the strategy first, then choose products.

There are three strategy paths:

| Strategy | What it means | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Match the light** | Deliver about the same light levels with far less power | Levels already meet the target and occupants are happy |
| **Right-size down** | Deliver less light, because the space is over-lit today | Audit readings are well above the target (see LT4-C03) |
| **Deliver high, then trim** | Install enough lumens for end-of-life, then use high-end trim to set today's level | You want headroom for lumen loss and future layout changes |

Some readers will remember group relamping, where every lamp was replaced on a schedule. That
practice belongs to the fluorescent era. Today the choice is to retrofit or replace.

## TLED vs Retrofit Kit vs New Luminaire
| Factor | TLED (tube) | Retrofit kit | New luminaire |
|---|---|---|---|
| First cost | Lowest | Medium | Highest |
| Install labor | Lowest | Medium | Highest (removal, support, ceiling work) |
| Uses old housing, lens, sockets | Yes | Housing only | No |
| Controls and dimming | Limited | Usually 0-10 V; sensors optional | Widest choice, integral sensors, NLC-ready |
| Light quality and optics | Depends on old lens | New optic | New optic, best control of glare |
| Service life of the whole fixture | Old parts still age | Good | Best, single warranty |
| Typical warranty (verify) | 5 years on tube | 5-10 years | 5-10 years |

Notes from the field:
- **TLEDs** come in several types (plug-and-play on the ballast, ballast-bypass, external driver).
  Plug-and-play keeps the old ballast, which can still fail. Ballast-bypass tubes put line voltage
  on the sockets, so the fixture must get the manufacturer's modification label and the correct
  sockets. Never mix TLED types in one building without a clear labeling plan.
- **Retrofit kits** replace the guts of the fixture with a new driver and LED tray. They must be
  installed exactly per their instructions to keep their listing, and the modified fixture must be
  labeled.
- **New luminaires** make sense when housings are damaged, yellowed, rusted, or the layout is
  wrong, or when the customer wants networked lighting controls (NLC) with sensors in every fixture.

### Controls Add-Ons
Each option can include controls: a sensor per fixture, a wall sensor per room, daylight
dimming, or a networked system. Price controls as a separate line so the customer can see what
they add to cost and savings. Lesson 06 covers which controls fit which spaces.

## Don't Forget These Audit Line Items
- **Exit signs.** Incandescent and fluorescent exit signs run 24/7. Replace them with LED signs
  or listed LED retrofit kits. Hours are 8,760 per year, so savings are easy to prove.
- **Emergency units and battery ballasts.** Every fixture with an emergency ballast needs a
  matching LED emergency driver or a separate unit. Record battery age; old batteries are a
  service cost.
- **Disposal.** Count lamps, ballasts (and PCB suspects), batteries and housings. Disposal is a
  real cost; LT5-C03 lesson 06 covers the paperwork.

## Trial Installs and Mock-Ups
Before ordering 2,000 kits, install a **mock-up**: 4-10 fixtures of each option in a real space.
1. Pick a representative space (same ceiling, height and use as most of the building).
2. Install each option per instructions with LOTO and live-dead-live verification.
3. Measure light levels before and after at the same points.
4. Ask occupants and the facility manager for feedback (glare, color, flicker, noise).
5. Record actual install time per unit. Use it to correct your labor estimate.
6. Confirm controls work (sensor coverage, dimming range, no flicker at low end).

> **Safety:** A mock-up is real electrical work. Lock out the circuit, verify absence of voltage
> live-dead-live, and follow the product instructions. A quick "demo" done live is still
> energized work and is not allowed.

## Life-Cycle Cost (LCC)
Simple payback only looks at first cost and energy. **Life-cycle cost** adds everything the owner
will pay over a study period (often 10-15 years):

**LCC = installed cost + energy cost + service labor and parts + disposal - incentives**

> **Example figures only:** Every rate, price and cost in the examples below is made up for
> teaching. None of them are any company's actual labor rates, prices, costs or customer pricing.
> For real estimates and proposals, always use your company's current figures.

Example: 100 three-lamp T8 troffers at 88 input watts, 3,500 hours per year, $0.12 per kWh, 12-year
study. Today's energy cost is 100 x 88 W x 3,500 h / 1,000 x $0.12 = $3,696 per year, plus about
$900 per year in lamp and ballast service calls.

| Item (12 years) | Good: TLED | Better: retrofit kit | Best: new luminaire + sensor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Installed cost | $5,500 | $12,000 | $21,000 |
| Input watts per fixture | 36 W | 32 W | 30 W, 25% sensor savings |
| Energy | $18,144 | $16,128 | $11,340 |
| Service labor and parts | $5,400 | $1,200 | $600 |
| Disposal | $600 | $650 | $1,200 |
| Incentive (estimated) | -$1,500 | -$3,000 | -$5,500 |
| **12-year LCC** | **$28,144** | **$26,978** | **$28,640** |
| Simple payback | 1.7 years | 3.1 years | 4.6 years |

The cheapest first cost is not the cheapest to own. In this example, the retrofit kit wins on
LCC, the TLED wins on payback, and the new luminaire gives the best light and controls for
nearly the same lifetime cost. All three are honest choices. The owner decides based on budget,
how long they will own the building and how much they value quality.

Financial staff may discount future dollars (present value). If the customer asks for that,
involve your estimator or sales lead; keep the field version simple and transparent.

### Incentives
Utility programs often require DLC or ENERGY STAR listed products, pre-approval before purchase,
and sometimes controls. Show incentives as **estimated** until approved (see LT4-C02 lesson 04).

## Presenting Good / Better / Best
1. Restate the customer's goals in their words (budget, quality, maintenance, sustainability).
2. Show the existing condition: watts, hours, cost, light levels, problems found.
3. Present three options side by side with first cost, annual savings, payback and LCC.
4. Show what is the same in every option: life-safety items, disposal, code-required controls.
   These are never "optional" to make a number look better.
5. List assumptions (hours, rate, incentive status) and show a quick sensitivity check.
6. Recommend one option and say why, then let the customer choose.

## Key Takeaways
- Choose the strategy (match, right-size, or deliver-then-trim) before choosing products.
- TLEDs are cheapest up front, kits balance cost and life, new luminaires give the most control.
- Include exit signs, emergency units and disposal as audit line items.
- Prove the choice with a mock-up and real install times.
- LCC = installed cost + energy + service + disposal - incentives.
- Present good/better/best honestly; life-safety and code items are in every option.
