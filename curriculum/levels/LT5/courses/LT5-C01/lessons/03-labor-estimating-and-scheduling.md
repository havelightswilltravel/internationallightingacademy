## Why Labor Is the Hardest Number
Material prices come from a quote. Labor comes from judgment. Underestimate labor and the job
loses money or the crew gets rushed, which is when people skip safety steps. Overestimate and
the bid loses. an LT5 technician builds labor estimates from **units, conditions and history**, not
guesses.

## Labor Units
A labor unit is the time to install one item under normal conditions, usually in hours per
unit (or minutes). Typical sources:
- Your company's historical job data (best source).
- Time studies you perform on real installs.
- Published labor unit manuals (adjust to your crew).

**Example labor units (illustrative only; use your company's data):**

| Task | Normal conditions (labor hours per unit) |
|---|---|
| 2x4 troffer retrofit kit, T-grid, 9 ft ceiling | 0.50 |
| Type B TLED conversion, 2-lamp | 0.30 |
| New LED high-bay, reuse existing whip, 25 ft | 0.75 (plus lift time) |
| Wall occupancy sensor replacing switch | 0.40 |
| Emergency LED driver added to fixture | 0.50 |

These numbers include the installation itself, not travel, setup or cleanup. Those are added
separately so they can be adjusted by job.

## Condition Factors
Adjust labor units for conditions that slow work:

| Condition | Typical effect |
|---|---|
| Ceilings above ladder height | Add lift setup, moves and travel between fixtures |
| Occupied space (moving furniture, working around people) | Increase 10-30% |
| After-hours or weekend work | Premium wage rate; sometimes lower productivity |
| Hard-lid (drywall) ceilings | Access through fixture opening only; slower |
| Congested ceilings, high-rack aisles | Lift repositioning time |
| Secure or escorted areas | Waiting time |
| Long distance from parking/material staging | Material handling time |

Use percentages your company has validated. Write down every factor you apply so the estimate
can be reviewed.

## Building the Estimate
1. **Direct install labor** = quantity x labor unit, for each line item.
2. **Apply condition factors** to the affected lines.
3. **Add non-install labor:**
   - Mobilization and demobilization (loading, travel, unloading)
   - Daily setup and cleanup (often 30-60 minutes per crew per day)
   - LOTO and verification time per circuit or panel
   - Lift delivery coordination and daily inspections
   - Waste handling and recycling packaging
   - Commissioning, punch list and owner training
   - Supervision and documentation
4. **Add contingency** (commonly 5-10% depending on unknowns).
5. **Multiply by the burdened labor rate** (wage plus taxes, insurance and benefits, as
   provided by your company).

> **Safety:** Never shave LOTO, verification, lift inspection or JHA time out of an estimate
> to win a bid. If the time is not in the estimate, the crew will be pressured to skip it.
> Safety tasks are part of the labor, not overhead you can cut.

## Worked Example
Scope: 120 troffer retrofit kits in an occupied office, 9 ft T-grid ceilings, work during
business hours.

- Install: 120 x 0.50 h = 60.0 h
- Occupied space factor +15%: 60.0 x 1.15 = 69.0 h
- Setup/cleanup: 2 techs x 0.75 h x 5 days = 7.5 h
- Mobilization: 4.0 h
- LOTO/verification (6 circuits per day x 5 days x 0.1 h): 3.0 h
- Commissioning and punch: 6.0 h
- Subtotal: 89.5 h
- Contingency 10%: 9.0 h (rounded)
- **Total: 98.5 labor hours** (about 6 crew-days for a two-person crew at 8 h/day)

## Scheduling the Work
Turn hours into a schedule the customer can plan around.
- **Crew-days** = total hours / (crew size x productive hours per day).
- **Sequence by area** so each space is disrupted once, and so circuits can be locked out in
  logical groups.
- **Order long-lead items early.** Some fixtures, sensors and emergency drivers have lead times
  of several weeks.
- **Book lifts** to arrive when needed and leave when done; idle rentals cost money.
- **Plan inspections** and commissioning windows, and allow time for deficiencies.
- **Share a look-ahead** (two-week schedule) with the customer and crew.

## Tracking Against the Estimate
On the job, record actual hours by task. At closeout, compare actual to estimate. Over time
this builds the historical data that makes future estimates accurate. If a job is trending
over, tell your supervisor early; do not wait until closeout.

## Key Takeaways
- Build labor from quantities x labor units, then adjust for conditions.
- Add non-install labor separately: mobilization, setup, LOTO, lifts, commissioning, cleanup.
- Never remove safety tasks from an estimate to win work.
- Apply a documented contingency and the company's burdened rate.
- Convert hours to crew-days, sequence by area, and order long-lead items early.
- Track actual hours to improve future estimates.
