## Why Mentoring Is Part of Your Job
Every CALT was once an LT1 who learned from someone. The program depends on senior techs
passing on skills, habits and judgment. Mentoring also makes you better: explaining why we do
something forces you to understand it deeply. One of your LT5 skills requires mentoring an
LT1 to evaluator sign-off.

## The Demonstrate-Practice-Feedback Method
This method works for nearly every hands-on skill.

1. **Prepare.** Review the LT1 skill criteria sheet together so the mentee knows what
   "passing" looks like. Gather tools and set up a safe practice area.
2. **Explain.** Describe the task, the steps, and *why* each step matters, especially the
   safety steps.
3. **Demonstrate.** Perform the task at normal speed, then slowly, narrating each step.
4. **Guided practice.** The mentee performs the task while you watch closely and coach.
5. **Feedback.** Specific and immediate: what was done well, what needs to change, and how.
6. **Independent practice.** The mentee repeats the task with less coaching until it is
   consistent.
7. **Readiness check.** When the mentee performs the skill correctly multiple times without
   prompts, recommend them for evaluator sign-off.

> **Safety:** Correct unsafe actions immediately, before harm can occur. Stop the task, explain
> what was unsafe and why, demonstrate the correct method, and have the mentee repeat it. For
> critical safety skills such as LOTO and live-dead-live verification, there is no "close
> enough."

## Giving Useful Feedback
| Weak feedback | Useful feedback |
|---|---|
| "Good job." | "You tested your meter on a known live source before and after testing the fixture. That's exactly right." |
| "That's wrong." | "You tested the hot to ground but not hot to neutral and neutral to ground. Test all three combinations so you don't miss a voltage on a shared neutral." |
| "Be more careful." | "Your third point of contact was missing when you reached for the lens. Move the ladder closer instead of reaching." |

Feedback should be about the **behavior**, not the person. Balance it: point out what is done
right so it continues, and be clear about what must change.

## How Adults Learn on the Job
- **Relevance:** Connect the skill to real work they will do next week.
- **Practice:** People learn hands-on skills by doing, not by watching.
- **Mistakes in a safe setting:** Let mentees make non-hazardous mistakes in the lab and find
  them through testing. Never allow mistakes that create a hazard.
- **Pace:** People learn at different speeds. Keep the standard the same; adjust the time.
- **Questions:** Encourage them. A mentee who is afraid to ask will guess on the job.

## Keeping a Mentoring Log
Record date, skill practiced, time spent, what went well, what needs work, and next steps.
The log helps you plan, shows the evaluator the mentee's progress, and documents your LT5
mentoring skill.

## Resolving Conflict on the Crew
Conflict on a crew is normal: disagreements about methods, workload, schedules, or personality
clashes. Unresolved conflict hurts safety because people stop communicating.

### A simple approach
1. **Act early.** Small problems are easier to fix.
2. **Talk privately** with each person first. Listen more than you talk. Find out what the real
   issue is.
3. **Focus on the problem, not the person.** "The fixtures in 110 were installed without
   whips secured" rather than "You're sloppy."
4. **Find common ground.** Both people usually want the job done well and to go home on time.
5. **Agree on specific actions.** Who does what, by when.
6. **Follow up** to confirm the agreement is working.
7. **Escalate** to your supervisor if the conflict involves harassment, threats, safety
   violations that continue, or anything outside your authority. Follow company HR policy.

### Conflict with customers or other trades
- Stay calm and professional; do not argue on site in front of others.
- Listen and restate the concern so the person knows you understand.
- Do not make promises outside your authority (pricing, scope, schedule changes).
- Document the issue and inform your supervisor.
- Never compromise safety or code to resolve a dispute. If another trade or a customer pressures
  you to work energized or skip a step, the answer is no, politely and firmly.

## Key Takeaways
- Use demonstrate-practice-feedback, starting from the skill's written criteria.
- Correct unsafe actions immediately; critical safety skills have no "close enough."
- Give specific, behavior-based feedback that reinforces good work and corrects errors.
- Keep a mentoring log to plan training and document progress.
- Address crew conflict early, privately and on the problem, and follow up.
- Escalate harassment, threats or repeated safety violations; never trade safety for peace.
