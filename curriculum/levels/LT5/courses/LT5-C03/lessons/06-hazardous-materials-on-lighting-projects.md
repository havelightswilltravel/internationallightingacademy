## The Lead Owns the Waste Stream
A large retrofit removes thousands of lamps, hundreds of ballasts and a stack of old exit signs
and batteries. LT1-C06 lesson 05 taught how to pack lamps and clean up a broken one. At LT5, you
are responsible for the **whole waste stream**: staging it, shipping it, documenting it and
handing the customer proof that it was handled correctly. You also need to recognize building
hazards that are not yours to touch.

## What Comes Out of the Ceiling
| Item | Hazard | Typical handling (federal baseline; states may be stricter) |
|---|---|---|
| Fluorescent and HID lamps | Mercury | Universal waste lamps, recycled |
| Broken lamps | Mercury vapor and glass | Sealed container, labeled, recycled |
| Ballasts marked "No PCBs" | Generally low hazard; some contain DEHP | Recycled; ask the recycler |
| Pre-1979 ballasts not marked "No PCBs" | Possible PCBs | Treat as PCB; separate and document |
| Leaking ballasts | PCB or other oils | Stop; special cleanup and disposal |
| Exit and emergency batteries (NiCd, lead-acid, lithium) | Metals, fire risk for lithium | Universal waste batteries, terminals taped |
| Self-luminous (tritium) exit signs | Radioactive source, regulated | Never trash; return per the label's instructions |
| LED fixtures and drivers | Electronic waste | Recycle per state and company policy |

## Universal Waste Lamps
Under EPA's universal waste rules, most states let businesses manage spent mercury lamps with
simpler handling than full hazardous waste, as long as they are recycled. Some states add rules.
Our company recycles **all** lamps, including low-mercury lamps that might pass a landfill test.

Field rules:
1. Use containers that close and protect lamps from breaking (original boxes or recycler boxes).
2. Label each container (for example, "Universal Waste - Lamps") and write the **start date**.
3. Keep containers closed except when adding lamps.
4. Never tape lamps into bundles or crush them unless the company has an approved,
   state-allowed system.
5. Keep broken lamps in a separate sealed container and label it.
6. Ship before the storage time limit (often one year under federal rules; shorter by policy).

## PCB and DEHP Ballasts
- **PCBs** were used in ballast capacitors until 1979. Treat any ballast without a "No PCBs"
  label from a pre-1979 building as PCB-containing. Store them in a separate, labeled drum.
  PCB waste requires specific shipping paperwork and disposal under EPA rules; the recycler
  or a PCB disposal company handles that.
- **DEHP** is a different oily chemical used in the capacitors of many "No PCBs" ballasts made
  after PCBs were banned. Large volumes of DEHP ballasts can trigger federal reporting rules,
  so tell the recycler how many you have. Keep them separate from regular scrap metal.

> **Safety:** If a ballast is leaking, wet or tarry, stop. Don't touch it with bare hands. Keep
> people away, notify your supervisor and the customer, and follow the company spill procedure.
> Before removing any ballast, the circuit is locked out and verified live-dead-live.

## Broken HID Outer Bulbs and Mercury
A metal halide or mercury vapor lamp with a cracked or broken **outer bulb** can keep running
and give off harmful ultraviolet light. If you find one burning, de-energize the fixture and
replace or remove the lamp. A broken arc tube can also spill mercury and hot fragments. Treat
the area like a broken fluorescent lamp: ventilate, keep people out, clean up with the spill
kit, never vacuum. Report any customer fixtures still running HID lamps in open fixtures that
require protected lamps.

## Paperwork the Customer Keeps
The customer often needs proof for their environmental files, sustainability reports or
utility incentive. Before the job, confirm in writing **who is the waste generator** (the
building owner or our company), because that decides whose name is on the paperwork. Then:
1. Keep a daily log of containers filled: type, count, start date.
2. Ship with the recycler's shipping papers (bill of lading, or a manifest for PCB waste).
3. Get a **certificate of recycling** (and a certificate of disposal for PCB items) listing
   quantities or weights.
4. Reconcile the counts with the audit: 2,000 fixtures x 3 lamps should produce about 6,000
   lamps. A large gap needs an explanation.
5. Give the customer a closeout folder with copies of every document.

## Asbestos: Recognize, Stop, Report
Buildings from before about 1981 may contain asbestos in pipe and duct insulation, spray-on
fireproofing, ceiling tiles, plaster, floor tile and mastic, and even in some old high-heat
fixture insulation. OSHA's construction asbestos rule requires building owners to tell
contractors where asbestos-containing material (ACM) is located.

1. Before work, ask for the asbestos survey for your work areas.
2. If you find suspect material (damaged insulation, crumbling spray coating, debris on top of
   tiles), **stop work** in that area. Don't touch, sweep or vacuum it.
3. Keep people out, notify your supervisor and the facility contact.
4. Abatement is done only by **licensed asbestos contractors**. Never enter a containment area.
5. Resume only with written clearance that the area is safe.
6. Around occupants, use calm language ("we found some material that needs to be checked")
   and let the owner handle communication.

## Lead Paint
Paint in buildings from before 1978 may contain lead. Drilling, sanding or scraping painted
surfaces makes lead dust. In housing and child-occupied facilities (schools, daycares), EPA's
lead renovation rules require certified firms and lead-safe work practices when more than
small areas are disturbed. OSHA's lead rule protects workers in any building. If a job means
disturbing old paint, ask for testing and follow company procedure before work starts.

## SDS and HazCom
OSHA's Hazard Communication standard requires that chemicals on the job be labeled, that
Safety Data Sheets (SDS) are available, and that workers are trained. On lighting jobs that
includes cleaners, adhesives, sealants, spray foam, battery electrolyte and the spill kit.
Keep SDS copies in the job binder or app, check labels before use and review a new SDS at the
huddle when a new product arrives.

## Key Takeaways
- The lead owns the waste stream from staging through the customer's certificate.
- Label lamp containers with a start date, keep them closed and never crush them.
- Separate PCB suspects, DEHP ballasts, batteries and broken lamps. Stop for leaking ballasts.
- Confirm who the generator is and deliver shipping papers and recycling certificates.
- Asbestos: stop, keep out, report; only licensed contractors abate.
- Ask about lead paint in pre-1978 buildings, and keep SDS available for every chemical.
