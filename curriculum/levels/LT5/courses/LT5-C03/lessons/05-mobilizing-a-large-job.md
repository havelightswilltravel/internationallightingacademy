## Why Large Jobs Fail
On a job of 20 fixtures, a forgotten detail costs an hour. On a job of 2,000 fixtures, the same
detail repeats every day for six weeks. Large jobs rarely fail because of the installation
itself. They fail because material is in the wrong place, the lift can't reach the floor, the
crew is waiting for keys, or occupants were never told. The lead technician's job is to remove
those problems **before** the crew arrives.

## The Pre-Mobilization Call and Walk
Schedule a call and a site walk with the customer's facility contact one to two weeks before
the start. Use this checklist:

| Topic | Questions to settle |
|---|---|
| Access | Which entrance? Hours the building is open? Keys, key cards, alarm codes? |
| Badging | Background checks, safety orientation, badge lead time? |
| Deliveries | Dock location and hours? Dock height? Who receives freight? |
| Elevator | Freight elevator size, door height and weight limit? Must it be reserved? |
| Floors | Floor load limits for lifts, especially on raised floors or older slabs? |
| Staging | A lockable room for material, close to the work? Who else has a key? |
| Charging | Outlet and ventilated area to charge lift batteries? |
| Waste | Where can lamp and ballast containers and cardboard go? Dumpster? |
| Occupants | Occupied hours, sensitive areas (labs, data rooms, executive suites)? |
| Shutdowns | Who approves circuit outages, and how much notice? Panel access? |
| Life safety | Fire alarm or sprinkler concerns, hot-work rules, emergency contacts |
| Hazards | Known asbestos, lead paint or other hazards in work areas (ask for the survey) |

Write the answers in the job packet and share them with the crew.

## Staging and Material Flow
Plan material movement like a production line:
1. **Receive and count** deliveries against the takeoff. Note damage on the delivery receipt
   before signing.
2. **Stage by zone.** Break pallets down into carts labeled by floor and zone so each crew pulls
   only what it needs.
3. **Break down packaging at the staging area**, not in occupied spaces. Flatten cardboard daily.
4. **Bring old material back the same way.** Removed lamps go into closed, labeled containers;
   ballasts go into their own containers. Never leave old parts in a ceiling or a hallway.
5. **Keep a running count** of material used and returned so shortages show up days early.

## Lifts and Scaffolds
- Book lift deliveries to match the schedule; a rented lift sitting idle is wasted money.
- Confirm the lift fits through doors and into the elevator, and that the floor can carry it.
- Plan the route for each lift through the building and protect finished floors.
- Charge batteries overnight in a ventilated area away from exits and combustibles.
- Assign only trained, authorized operators. Inspect every lift and scaffold before each shift.

> **Safety:** Lead-acid batteries release hydrogen when charging. Charge in a ventilated area, no
> smoking or sparks nearby, and keep eyewash available where batteries are serviced. Never charge
> a lift in a corridor that serves as an exit route.

## After-Hours and Occupied-Space Work
Many large jobs run nights or weekends so occupants aren't disturbed.
- Confirm building security knows the crew, the hours and the contact person.
- Confirm HVAC, elevators and restrooms will be available after hours.
- Plan lighting for the work itself: when you de-energize a circuit at night, the area may go
  completely dark. Bring task lights and confirm egress lighting in the work area.
- Fatigue is a hazard. Rotate tasks, schedule breaks and don't stretch shifts to "finish a floor."

In occupied areas:
- Post notices a few days before work in each area.
- Cover desks and equipment with drop cloths before opening ceilings.
- Barricade under every lift and ladder. Keep aisles and exits clear.
- Leave every area clean, with ceiling tiles back in place, at the end of each shift.

## The Daily Plan
Each morning, run a 10-minute huddle at the staging area:
1. Review the JHA for the day's zones and any changes.
2. Assign zones, circuits to lock out, lifts and material carts.
3. Confirm shutdown approvals with the customer.
4. State the day's production target.

Each circuit is locked out by every worker on it, and verified live-dead-live before anyone
touches conductors. A new day means a new verification.

## Tracking Progress Against the Estimate
Compare daily production with the estimate so problems surface early.

Example: the estimate allows 0.6 hours per retrofit kit for 1,200 kits (720 hours). The crew of
four works 8-hour shifts (32 hours a day), so the plan is about 53 kits per day. After three
days the crew has installed 132 kits, which is 44 per day.

- Hours used per kit: 96 hours / 132 kits = 0.73 hours, about 21% over the estimate.
- At this rate, the job needs about 873 hours, about 153 hours more than planned.

Find the cause (material not staged, waiting for shutdowns, tougher ceilings than expected) and
fix it, or tell your supervisor so the schedule or a change order can be discussed. Don't hide
it by cutting safety steps.

## The Daily Customer Handoff
At the end of every shift:
1. Walk the finished areas with the facility contact, or send a short written summary.
2. Confirm every circuit worked today is re-energized and tested, and every fixture lights.
3. Report anything found: damaged ceilings, unlabeled circuits, hazards, out-of-scope requests.
4. Confirm tomorrow's areas and shutdowns.

## Punch Lists and Closeout
Keep a running **punch list** by zone: item, location, who owns it, date found, date fixed.
Close items as you go instead of saving them for the last week. Closeout includes the final
walk, controls checked and set, emergency fixtures tested, spare parts and warranty documents
turned over, waste records delivered (see lesson 06), and the staging room returned clean.

## Key Takeaways
- Large jobs fail on logistics, not installation. Settle access, elevator, staging, charging,
  waste and occupant issues before mobilizing.
- Stage material by zone and bring old material back out the same day.
- Plan lift routes, floor loads, charging and authorized operators in advance.
- Run a daily huddle and lock out and verify every circuit, every day.
- Track production against the estimate daily and report problems early.
- Hand off each day to the customer and close punch items as you go.
