## What Commissioning Is (and Is Not)
**Commissioning (Cx)** is a quality-focused process that verifies and documents that building
systems are designed, installed, tested and capable of being operated and maintained to meet
the **owner's requirements**. For lighting, it answers one question: *does the system actually
do what the owner and the code require, in every space, under real conditions?*

Commissioning is **not** just "turning the lights on." A system can light up perfectly and
still fail commissioning because sensors time out at the wrong delay, daylight zones never dim,
schedules are set to the wrong time zone, or emergency fixtures do not go to full output when
normal power is lost.

## Why It Matters for Lighting
- **Energy codes require it.** Current editions of ASHRAE 90.1 and the IECC require functional
  testing of lighting controls (occupancy sensors, time switches, daylight controls) before
  the owner accepts the building, with documentation provided to the owner. Confirm the exact
  requirements in the edition your jurisdiction has adopted.
- **Complaints and callbacks.** Most networked-control complaints ("lights keep shutting off
  on me") trace back to incomplete commissioning.
- **Savings depend on it.** Controls that are never set up deliver no savings.
- **Life safety.** Emergency lighting controls and transfer devices must be proven to work.

## Key Documents
| Document | What it is | Who usually writes it |
|---|---|---|
| Owner's Project Requirements (OPR) | What the owner needs the building/system to do | Owner with Cx provider |
| Basis of Design (BOD) | How the design meets the OPR | Designer/engineer |
| Sequence of Operations (SOO) | Exactly how controls should behave in each space | Designer/controls vendor |
| Cx Plan | Who does what, when, and how results are documented | Cx provider |
| Pre-functional checklists | Installation verification before testing | Cx provider; completed by installer |
| Functional Performance Tests (FPTs) | Step-by-step tests with expected results | Cx provider; executed with installer |
| Issues (deficiency) log | Every problem found and its resolution | Cx provider |
| Systems manual and training records | How to operate and maintain | Contractor/vendor, compiled by Cx provider |

ASHRAE Standard 202 describes the commissioning process in detail and is a good reference for
how these documents fit together.

## The Phases
1. **Pre-design and design:** Owner requirements are written; the design and sequences are
   reviewed for completeness. As a field tech you may be asked to review constructability:
   are sensors located where they can see occupants? Are daylight zones realistic?
2. **Construction:** Equipment is installed per drawings and manufacturer instructions.
   Submittals are checked against the design.
3. **Pre-functional (installation) verification:** Installer confirms every device is
   installed, wired, addressed and labeled correctly and the system is powered and online.
4. **Functional performance testing:** Each sequence is tested, step by step, against expected
   results. Failures are logged.
5. **Deficiency resolution and retest:** Issues are fixed and retested until they pass.
6. **Owner training and turnover:** Operators are trained; documentation is delivered.
7. **Post-occupancy (optional but valuable):** After several weeks or months of use, settings
   are fine-tuned based on real occupancy and complaints.

## Roles on the Team
- **Owner / owner's representative:** defines requirements, accepts the system.
- **Commissioning provider (CxA):** independent party who plans, witnesses and documents.
  On smaller projects, the electrical contractor may perform and document the testing.
- **Designer:** writes sequences and answers design questions.
- **Electrical contractor and lighting technicians:** install, complete pre-functional
  checklists, and execute FPTs.
- **Controls manufacturer / startup technician:** programs the system, especially networked
  controls.

As a CALT you will often be the person who actually runs the tests while the CxA witnesses.
Your job is to execute the tests honestly and record results exactly as observed.

> **Safety:** Commissioning involves ladder and lift work, opening devices, and sometimes
> interrupting normal power to test emergency lighting. Each of these needs a plan. Use LOTO
> for any wiring correction, verify absence of voltage before touching conductors, and
> coordinate any power interruption with the owner so occupied egress paths are never left
> dark.

## Integrity of Results
A recorded "pass" is a signature that says the test was done and the system met the expected
result. Never mark a test as passed that was not performed, and never "adjust" results to
match the expected value. A failed test that is honestly recorded and fixed is a success of
the process. A fake pass is a liability for you and your company, and can be a life-safety
problem when it involves emergency lighting.

## Key Takeaways
- Commissioning verifies the system meets the owner's requirements and code, not just that lights turn on.
- Energy codes require functional testing of lighting controls; confirm the adopted edition.
- Know the documents: OPR, BOD, sequence of operations, Cx plan, checklists, FPTs, issues log.
- Phases run from design review through installation checks, testing, retest, training and turnover.
- Plan safety for ladder, lift and power-interruption activities.
- Record results honestly; a fake pass is never acceptable.
