## Purpose of the Capstone
The capstone project proves that you can do what an LT5 technician does: take a lighting project from
existing conditions to a commissioned, documented, safe result, and explain your decisions.
It integrates everything from LT1 through LT5. The capstone is hands-on skill **LT5-S08** and
must be signed off by a qualified evaluator.

## Choosing a Project
Pick a real project when possible (a customer retrofit you are leading, or an approved
in-house facility such as the shop, office or a training lab). If no live project is available,
an evaluator may approve a **fully simulated project** using a real building for the audit and
the training lab for installation and commissioning.

**Minimum scope:**
- At least **10 spaces** or **40 luminaires**, including at least two different space types.
- At least one **lighting control strategy** that requires commissioning (occupancy/vacancy,
  daylight, scheduling, or networked controls).
- At least one **emergency lighting** element (existing system evaluation, emergency driver,
  UL 924 device, or exit sign/emergency unit replacement).

## Step 1: Proposal (Required Before Work Begins)
Submit a one- to two-page proposal to your evaluator that includes:
- Project location, spaces included and why the project qualifies
- Planned schedule and milestones
- Supervisor or customer approval to use the site
- Any known hazards or access constraints

Do not begin until the evaluator approves the proposal in writing.

## Step 2: The Six Deliverables

### Deliverable 1: Existing-conditions audit
- Space-by-space inventory: fixture type, lamp/ballast or driver, input watts, quantity,
  mounting height, controls, operating hours
- Illuminance survey compared to IES recommended targets
- Photos tied to space IDs
- Notes on condition, hazards (PCB-suspect ballasts, mercury lamps) and access

### Deliverable 2: Retrofit scope and product selection
- Retrofit approach for each fixture type (lamp, kit, new fixture or delamp) with justification
- Product selections with efficacy, CCT, CRI, DLC qualification status (if rebates apply),
  dimming and control compatibility
- Controls strategy and sequence of operations, checked against the adopted energy code
- Emergency lighting plan that maintains or improves egress lighting
- Inclusions, exclusions, assumptions and items requiring licensed electrician or AHJ review

### Deliverable 3: Takeoff, estimate and financial analysis
- Material takeoff reconciled to the audit
- Labor estimate with labor units, condition factors, non-install labor and contingency
- kW reduction, annual kWh and cost savings, rebates, net cost, simple payback and simple ROI
- Sensitivity check on operating hours

### Deliverable 4: Safety plan and installation plan
- Written JHA covering each major task with controls chosen by the hierarchy of controls
- LOTO plan: circuits and panels, group lockout method, verification method
- Access plan (ladders, MEWPs) with operator authorizations
- Crew plan and task assignments by level
- Waste handling plan (universal waste, PCB ballasts)
- A toolbox talk outline for the project

### Deliverable 5: Commissioning
- Pre-functional checklist for each space
- Functional performance tests for each control strategy and the emergency lighting element,
  completed with results
- Issues log showing each deficiency and its retest result

### Deliverable 6: Closeout package
- As-built drawings or marked-up plans showing fixtures, zones and circuits
- Final control settings
- Emergency lighting test records
- Owner training outline and attendance record (or a recorded training session for simulated
  projects)
- Warranty and product information
- Lessons learned: what went well, what you would change, actual vs estimated hours

> **Safety:** The capstone is real work. All installation, wiring corrections and commissioning
> steps follow LOTO, live-dead-live verification and the approved JHA. A safety deviation during
> the capstone is grounds for an automatic fail and a retake, regardless of the quality of the
> paperwork. Tasks requiring a licensed electrician or permit must be performed by, or under
> the supervision of, appropriately licensed personnel.

## Step 3: Presentation
Deliver a **15-20 minute presentation** to the evaluator (and, if possible, your supervisor and
the customer). Cover:
1. The problem: existing conditions and customer goals
2. Your solution and why you chose it
3. Financial results
4. How you kept the crew safe
5. Commissioning results and issues found
6. Lessons learned

Expect questions on code requirements, safety decisions, product choices, calculations and what
you would do differently. You must answer correctly or identify where you would find the
answer.

## How the Capstone Is Evaluated
| Area | What the evaluator looks for |
|---|---|
| Completeness | All six deliverables present and organized |
| Technical accuracy | Correct counts, calculations, product matching and code references |
| Safety | Complete JHA and LOTO plan; zero safety deviations in field work |
| Commissioning | Tests performed and documented honestly, deficiencies closed |
| Communication | Clear presentation, professional documents, correct answers to questions |
| Judgment | Correctly identifies items needing a licensed electrician or AHJ |

Each area is pass/fail. A fail in any area requires correction and resubmission of that area;
a safety fail requires a full retake of the field portion.

## Planning Tips
- Start early: the capstone typically takes several weeks alongside regular work.
- Use the templates and forms from LT5-C01 through LT5-C03.
- Keep everything in one organized folder (digital or binder) with a table of contents.
- Ask your mentor or supervisor to review drafts before submission.

## Key Takeaways
- The capstone (LT5-S08) integrates audit, scope, estimate, safety, installation, commissioning and closeout.
- Get written proposal approval before starting; minimum 10 spaces or 40 luminaires with controls and emergency lighting.
- Deliver all six deliverables and a 15-20 minute presentation.
- Any safety deviation fails the field portion.
- Identify all work requiring a licensed electrician or the AHJ.
