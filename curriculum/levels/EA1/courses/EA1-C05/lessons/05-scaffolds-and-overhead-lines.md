## Scaffolds in Electrical Work

Electricians use scaffolds for high-bay lighting, wall-mounted equipment, conduit runs on tall walls
and exterior work. Common types include **frame (fabricated) scaffolds**, **rolling/mobile tower
scaffolds**, **pump-jack** and **suspended** scaffolds. OSHA's construction scaffold standards are
**29 CFR 1926 Subpart L (1926.450–1926.454)**.

## Key Scaffold Requirements

| Topic | Requirement (paraphrased) | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Each scaffold and component must support its own weight and at least **4 times** the maximum intended load | 1926.451(a)(1) |
| Competent person | Scaffolds must be erected, moved, dismantled or altered only under a competent person's supervision, and inspected by a competent person **before each shift** and after any occurrence that could affect integrity | 1926.451(f)(3), (f)(7) |
| Footing | Base plates and mud sills on firm foundation; no unstable objects (bricks, blocks, buckets) used as supports | 1926.451(c)(2) |
| Platforms | Fully planked or decked between front uprights and guardrail supports as required; planks overlap or are secured to prevent movement | 1926.451(b) |
| Access | Safe access (ladder, stair tower, ramp) when platforms are more than **2 ft** above or below a point of access; cross braces must not be used as a means of access | 1926.451(e) |
| Fall protection | Required for workers on scaffolds more than **10 ft** above a lower level | 1926.451(g)(1) |
| Guardrail height | Top rail generally **38–45 in** for scaffolds manufactured or placed in service after January 1, 2000, with midrails about halfway | 1926.451(g)(4) |
| Training | Workers on scaffolds must be trained by a qualified person to recognize the hazards | 1926.454 |

Note the difference: general construction fall protection starts at **6 ft** (Subpart M), but on
scaffolds the trigger is **10 ft** (Subpart L).

### Mobile (Rolling) Scaffolds
- **Lock the casters** before anyone climbs on.
- Generally, do not ride a rolling scaffold while it is being moved. OSHA allows it only under
  specific conditions (such as height-to-base-width ratio of 2:1 or less, a level surface, and
  workers aware of the move) — most employers simply prohibit it.
- Keep the floor clear of holes, debris and cords along the path.

### Scaffold Tags
Many sites use a tag system: **green** = inspected and safe to use; **yellow** = usable with
special precautions (such as fall arrest); **red** = do not use. Tag colors are a site practice,
not an OSHA requirement, so learn your site's system — and never use a scaffold without a current
inspection.

## Overhead Power Lines

Contact with overhead power lines is a leading cause of electrocution in construction — from
ladders, scaffolds, MEWPs, cranes, long conduit sticks and even ladders carried upright.

### Clearance Basics

| Situation | Typical minimum clearance | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Unqualified employees and conductive objects near overhead lines **50 kV or less** | **10 ft** | 1926.600(a)(6) for equipment in construction; 1910.333(c)(3) for general industry |
| Scaffolds near **insulated** lines under 300 V | 3 ft | 1926.451(f)(6) |
| Scaffolds near **insulated** lines 300 V to 50 kV, and **uninsulated** lines under 50 kV | 10 ft | 1926.451(f)(6) |
| Cranes and derricks near lines up to 350 kV (when work zone may come closer) | **20 ft** unless specific procedures are followed | 1926.1408 Table A |

Clearances increase with voltage above 50 kV. The scaffold rule also allows closer work if the
utility de-energizes, relocates or installs protective coverings on the lines.

**Treat every overhead line as energized and uninsulated** until the utility or line owner confirms
otherwise. Weatherproof covering on overhead conductors is **not** insulation rated for contact.

> **Safety:** Before raising a ladder, scaffold, MEWP or long piece of conduit outdoors, look up
> and identify overhead lines. Maintain at least 10 ft from lines up to 50 kV (more for higher
> voltages), use a dedicated spotter when equipment could approach the minimum distance, and
> contact the utility to de-energize, relocate or cover lines when work must be closer. Only
> qualified line-clearance or utility workers may work within the minimum approach distances.
> On the premises side, building circuits you work on must be locked out and verified
> de-energized with a tested meter before work.

### Carrying Material Safely
- Carry ladders and conduit **horizontally**, especially near overhead lines and inside buildings
  with exposed bus or open-frame equipment.
- Use nonconductive (fiberglass) ladders for electrical work.

## Putting It All Together — Pre-Task Inspection

Before starting work at height on a construction site, an apprentice should be able to check:

1. Scaffold tag current and competent-person inspection done this shift.
2. Footing firm and level; casters locked; guardrails and midrails in place.
3. Access ladder or stair present; no climbing cross braces.
4. Fall protection plan for heights over 10 ft on scaffolds (6 ft elsewhere).
5. Overhead lines identified and clearances maintained.
6. Temporary power GFCI-tested; cords inspected.
7. Tools tethered where people work below; area below barricaded.

This checklist is the basis for skill **EA1-S09**.

## Key Takeaways
- Scaffolds (Subpart L) must support 4× intended load and be inspected by a competent person each shift.
- Fall protection on scaffolds is required above 10 ft; general construction trigger is 6 ft.
- Provide proper access for platforms more than 2 ft above or below; never climb cross braces.
- Lock casters; most employers prohibit riding rolling scaffolds.
- Keep at least 10 ft from overhead lines 50 kV or less; cranes generally 20 ft.
- Treat all overhead lines as energized and uninsulated.
