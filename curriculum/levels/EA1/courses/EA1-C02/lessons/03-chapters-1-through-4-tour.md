## Why a Tour?

You don't need to memorize the Code, but you do need a mental map of where things live. This
lesson walks through the articles of Chapters 1–4 you will use most in your first years, and
connects each to something you will see on the job.

### Working Space — 110.26

Equipment likely to require examination, adjustment, servicing or maintenance while energized
(panelboards, switchboards, many disconnects and controllers) must have clear working space. The
depth depends on the voltage to ground and what is behind you, per Table 110.26(A)(1):

| Nominal voltage to ground | Condition 1 | Condition 2 | Condition 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–150 V | 3 ft | 3 ft | 3 ft |
| 151 V and up (e.g., 277/480 V) | 3 ft | 3½ ft | 4 ft |

- **Condition 1:** exposed live parts on one side; no live or grounded parts on the other side
  (or they are guarded/insulated).
- **Condition 2:** exposed live parts on one side and grounded parts (such as a concrete or block
  wall) on the other.
- **Condition 3:** exposed live parts on both sides.

The working space must also be at least **30 in wide** (or the width of the equipment, if
greater) and generally **6½ ft high**, and the equipment door must be able to open at least 90°.
A 120/240 V residential panel (120 V to ground) needs 3 ft of depth no matter what is opposite.

> **Safety:** Working space is not storage space. A panel blocked by shelving or boxes delays
> emergency shutoff and forces workers into awkward positions near live parts. Keep it clear and
> report blocked equipment.

**240.4(D)** is a rule you'll use daily: unless specifically permitted elsewhere, overcurrent
protection for copper conductors may not exceed **15 A for 14 AWG, 20 A for 12 AWG and 30 A for
10 AWG** — even though Table 310.16 lists higher ampacities for those sizes.

## Key Takeaways
- Article 110 sets the ground rules: approval, listing instructions, workmanship, terminations, working space.
- Working space depth is 3 ft at 150 V or less to ground; 3, 3½ or 4 ft above that depending on condition.
- Article 210 holds most dwelling branch-circuit rules: GFCI, AFCI, required circuits, receptacle spacing.
- 240.4(D) limits 14/12/10 AWG copper to 15/20/30 A overcurrent protection.
- Wiring-method articles in Chapter 3 share a similar section pattern.
- Chapter 4 covers equipment: switches, receptacles, panelboards, luminaires, appliances, motors.
