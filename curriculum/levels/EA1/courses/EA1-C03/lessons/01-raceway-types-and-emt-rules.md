## Why Raceways?

A **raceway** is an enclosed channel designed to hold conductors. Raceways protect conductors
from physical damage, allow conductors to be added or replaced later, and — when metallic and
properly installed — can serve as an equipment grounding conductor. In commercial and industrial
work you will install far more raceway than cable, so speed and accuracy with raceway is a core
apprentice skill.

## Common Raceway Types

| Type | NEC article | Description | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EMT** — electrical metallic tubing | 358 | Thin-wall steel (or aluminum) tubing; **not threaded**; joined with set-screw or compression fittings | Exposed and concealed indoor commercial work — the workhorse of the trade |
| **IMC** — intermediate metal conduit | 342 | Threaded steel, wall thinner than RMC | Where physical protection is needed; services; outdoors |
| **RMC** — rigid metal conduit | 344 | Heaviest wall, threaded, galvanized steel (also aluminum, stainless) | Severe physical damage, services, hazardous locations, encased in concrete |
| **PVC** — rigid polyvinyl chloride conduit | 352 | Nonmetallic; solvent-welded joints; Schedule 40 and heavier Schedule 80 | Underground, corrosive and wet locations; Schedule 80 where exposed to physical damage |
| **ENT** — electrical nonmetallic tubing | 362 | Pliable corrugated nonmetallic tubing | Concealed in walls and ceilings, encased in concrete (where permitted) |
| **FMC** — flexible metal conduit | 348 | Spiral-wound interlocked metal ("Greenfield") | Short connections to luminaires and equipment, dry locations |
| **LFMC** — liquidtight flexible metal conduit | 350 | FMC with a liquidtight PVC jacket | Motors, HVAC equipment, wet locations, vibration |
| **LFNC** — liquidtight flexible nonmetallic conduit | 356 | Nonmetallic flexible liquidtight | Equipment connections, corrosive areas |

**Trade sizes** (1/2, 3/4, 1, 1-1/4, 1-1/2, 2, and so on) are names, not exact measurements. The
actual internal diameter of 1/2 trade size differs between EMT, RMC and PVC; that's why Chapter
9, Table 4 lists dimensions separately for each raceway type.

## EMT Fittings

- **Set-screw** fittings are fast and common in dry indoor locations. Tighten the screw until
  firm — overtightening can crack the fitting or dent the tubing.
- **Compression** fittings use a gland nut and ring. Fittings listed as raintight or for wet
  locations are typically compression type.
- Fittings encased in concrete or masonry must be the concrete-tight type, and those in wet
  locations must be suitable for that location (see 358.42). Read the fitting's packaging for its
  listing.
- **Insulated-throat connectors** or bushings protect conductors where they enter a box. Many
  specifications require them on all sizes; the NEC requires protection for 4 AWG and larger
  conductors entering enclosures (300.4(G)).

## Key EMT Installation Rules (2023 NEC, Article 358)

| Topic | Rule (paraphrased) | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Reaming | Cut ends must be reamed or finished to remove rough edges | 358.28(A) |
| Threading | EMT must not be threaded (except integral-coupling types made at the factory) | 358.28(B) |
| Bends | Bends must be made so the tubing isn't damaged and the internal diameter isn't effectively reduced; radius per Chapter 9, Table 2 | 358.24 |
| Number of bends | No more than the equivalent of four quarter bends (360° total) between pull points | 358.26 |
| Securing | Within 3 ft of each box, cabinet, conduit body or other termination | 358.30(A) |
| Supporting | At intervals not exceeding 10 ft | 358.30(A) |
| Couplings and connectors | Made up tight; concrete-tight where buried in concrete; suitable for wet locations where used there | 358.42 |

Some exceptions allow longer distances to the first support in specific situations — for
example, fishing EMT through finished walls — so read 358.30 fully when a situation doesn't fit
the general rule.

### Worked Example — Counting Supports
A straight run of EMT between two boxes is 34 ft long. How many straps are required at minimum?

- One strap within 3 ft of each box: 2 straps, leaving 34 − 3 − 3 = 28 ft between them.
- 28 ft ÷ 10 ft = 2.8 → the space must be divided into at least 3 spans, needing **2 more
  straps**.
- Minimum total: **4 straps**. Most journeymen will install more for a neater, straighter job.

## Cutting and Reaming

1. Measure and mark with a fine line; mark all the way around if using a hacksaw so the cut stays
   square.
2. Cut with a **band saw** (portable or stationary) or a **hacksaw** with a 24- or 32-TPI blade.
   Tubing cutters leave a large inside burr and are discouraged for EMT.
3. **Ream** the inside with a reamer or the reaming tool on your multi-bit screwdriver until the
   edge is smooth. A sharp edge will skin conductor insulation during the pull.
4. File any outside burr so the fitting seats fully.

> **Safety:** Fresh-cut EMT edges and burrs slice skin easily — wear cut-resistant gloves and
> safety glasses when cutting and reaming. Secure the tubing in a vise or pipe stand rather than
> holding it against your leg. Never cut, drill or modify an existing raceway that may contain
> energized conductors until the circuits are identified, locked out and verified de-energized.

## Physical Protection and Wet Locations

- Raceways installed underground are considered **wet locations**, and so is the interior of
  raceways in wet locations above grade. Conductors installed in them must be listed for wet
  locations (such as THWN-2 or XHHW-2) — see 300.5(B) and 300.9.
- Where raceway passes through a wall or floor that is a fire-rated assembly, openings must be
  firestopped per 300.21 using a listed system.
- Raceways must be continuous between boxes and fittings (300.10 for metal raceways,
  300.12 for mechanical continuity).

## Key Takeaways
- EMT (Article 358) is the most common commercial raceway: thin wall, not threaded.
- RMC and IMC are threaded and used where more protection is needed; PVC for underground/corrosive work.
- Ream every cut end; don't use a tubing cutter on EMT.
- Secure EMT within 3 ft of each termination and at least every 10 ft.
- Maximum 360° of bends between pull points.
- Underground raceways are wet locations — use wet-rated conductors.
