## Interview Structure

A 60–75 minute interview for a technician or electrician follows the same flow every time:

| Step | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Welcome | 3 min | Introductions, format, "I'll be taking notes" |
| 2. Background | 10–15 min | Selected position/background questions from the kit |
| 3. Technical (electricians) | 10–15 min | License, expertise, project and safety questions |
| 4. Behavior-based competencies | 25–30 min | The six to eight competencies chosen for this opening |
| 5. Realistic preview and ladder | 5 min | Job conditions, grade ladder, placement, pay range |
| 6. Candidate questions | 5–10 min | "What questions do you have for me or about our company?" |
| 7. Close | 2 min | Next steps and timeline |

Choose the questions **before** the first interview and use the same set for every candidate for
that opening. In the app, the digital scorecard lets you select the questions once per opening.

## Using the Background Questions Well

The kit's position/background section includes questions such as:

- "Walk me through a typical day at your last position."
- "Why did you/are you leaving your last position?"
- "How did you prepare for this interview?" and "What do you know about [our company]?"
- "What did you like most about your last job? What did you like least?"
- "What motivates you?" and "Where do you see yourself in five years?"
- "What questions do you have for me?"

These reveal motivation, fit with our ladder ("Motivated to make money and grow," "Long term"),
and preparation. They are not scored 1–9, so take notes and connect them to the profile.

Some background questions in the current kits need rewording or should move to a later stage. For
example, the questions about how much weight a candidate can lift, prior absences, being fired,
and current pay. Use the compliant versions taught in MGR-C04 and listed in your job profile.

## Technical Questions for Electricians

The electrician kit adds questions that check license and real-world competence:

| Kit question | What a strong answer includes | Follow-up probes |
|---|---|---|
| What is your electrical license number? Are you licensed in other states? | A number you can verify, class (journeyman/master), expiration | "When does it renew? Any CE hours due?" Verify later on the state lookup |
| What do you consider your area(s) of electrical expertise? | Specific systems: lighting controls, service upgrades, commercial tenant finish, motor controls | "Tell me about the last job where you used that." |
| Do you prefer technical work or fast-paced production? Troubleshooting vs. production? | An honest preference; either can fit different roles | "Give an example of a troubleshooting job you're proud of." |
| Tell me about the best electrical project you've been on. What made it memorable? | Their role, scope, what went well, teamwork | "What was your specific responsibility?" |
| Tell me about the worst electrical project. What made it memorable? | Honest account, no blaming everyone else, lessons learned | "What would you do differently?" |
| Tell me about a safe work experience. What made it safe? | Planning, job briefing, LOTO, verification of absence of voltage, PPE | "Who led the briefing? What did you check?" |
| Tell me about an unsafe work experience. What made it unsafe? | Recognized the hazard; ideally stopped work or raised it | "What did you do about it? What happened next?" |
| Have you worked out of booms/lifts? Any issues with heights? | Equipment types and training | Reword "issues with heights" to: "This job requires regular work from ladders, scissor lifts, and boom lifts. Can you perform that, with or without reasonable accommodation?" |
| What electrical tools do you have? | Reasonable kit for their level | Be careful not to make tool ownership a barrier if the company provides tools; see MGR-C04 |

> **Tip:** The unsafe-experience question is one of the most revealing in the kit. A candidate who
> describes working hot "because the customer couldn't lose power" without any discomfort is a
> serious concern. A candidate who stopped the job, even at a cost, is showing exactly the Caution
> competency our safety culture depends on.

## Panel Interviews

Two or three interviewers improve accuracy and reduce individual bias. To run a good panel:

1. **Huddle for five minutes before.** Agree who asks which questions and who leads.
2. **One question at a time.** Don't pile on.
3. **Everyone takes notes and scores every answer**, not just their own questions.
4. **Score independently.** No discussion until all scorecards are submitted in the app.
5. **Debrief later** in a calibration meeting (MGR-C05), not in the hallway.

Include a current senior technician on panels when possible; they spot technical bluffing and give
the candidate an honest view of the work.

## Closing Well

- Ask: "Is there anything else you'd like us to know that we didn't ask about?"
- Explain the next steps: reference checks, timeline for a decision, and who will call.
- Thank them. Every candidate is a potential customer or referral source.

> **Legal:** Don't make promises in the close ("You've got the job," "You'll start at LT3").
> Offers are made only after the selection process and approvals are complete.

## Key Takeaways
- Use the same interview structure and the same selected questions for every candidate.
- Background questions reveal motivation and preparation; note them against the profile.
- For electricians, verify the license and probe expertise, best/worst projects, and safe/unsafe experiences.
- Panels improve accuracy when interviewers plan roles, score independently, and debrief later.
- Close with next steps and no promises.
