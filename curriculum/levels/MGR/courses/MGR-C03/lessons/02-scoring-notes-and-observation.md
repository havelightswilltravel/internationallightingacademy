## The 1–9 Scale

Each behavior-based question on the kit is scored **1 to 9, with 9 the highest**, and marked
**Answered Y/N**. A nine-point scale is wide enough to separate candidates but only works if
everyone uses it the same way. Our company uses three bands with anchors:

| Score | Band | What the answer sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | **Weak** | No specific example, or a hypothetical; vague; blames others; the action described was poor, unsafe, or dishonest; no learning |
| 4–6 | **Acceptable** | A real example with some STAR parts; reasonable action; result is partly clear; limited reflection |
| 7–9 | **Strong** | Specific, recent, complete STAR; candidate's own actions are clear and effective; measurable result; honest about what went wrong and what they changed |

Use the middle of a band (2, 5, 8) as your starting point, then move up or down one point for
strengths or weaknesses within the answer.

### Anchored example: Caution

Question from the kit: *"Have you ever worked in a situation where the rules and guidelines were
not clear? Tell me about it."*

- **A "2" answer:** "Yeah, that happens all the time. I just figure it out and get the job done."
  *(No example. "Get it done" without checking suggests risk-taking.)*
- **A "5" answer:** "At the warehouse we got a new pallet wrapper and nobody explained the
  lockout. I asked my lead, and he showed me." *(Real example, right instinct to ask, but thin
  on details and result.)*
- **An "8" answer:** "Last spring on an HVAC job, the site had no clear rule for who could open the
  rooftop disconnects. I stopped, called my supervisor, and we got the building engineer to confirm
  the procedure. We lost about 40 minutes. Afterward I wrote up the site notes so the next crew
  had them, and my supervisor added it to our checklist." *(Specific, own actions, safe choice,
  result, and improvement.)*

### Anchored example: Personal Effectiveness

Question: *"Tell about a time when you took responsibility for an error and were held personally
accountable."*

- **"2":** "I don't really make mistakes; I'm pretty careful." *(Avoids the question.)*
- **"5":** "I ordered the wrong parts once and told my boss. We fixed it." *(Owns it, little detail.)*
- **"8":** "I misread a work order and installed 3000K lamps where the customer wanted 4000K across
  a whole floor. I told my lead that afternoon, came back on my own time to swap them, and started
  photographing the order before I pull parts. Haven't repeated it." *(Clear ownership, action,
  and a process change.)*

> **Tip:** Score each answer **right after** it is given, while it is fresh, and before you discuss
> the candidate with anyone. Do not go back and adjust scores to match your overall feeling at the
> end; that is how halo effect creeps in.

## Taking Notes That Hold Up

Notes are evidence. They support your scores in the calibration meeting and are part of the hiring
record. Good notes are:

- **Factual:** what the candidate said and did. Use short quotes. ("Called super, waited 40 min,
  wrote site notes.")
- **Job-related:** tied to the competency being assessed.
- **Free of protected or personal information:** never note age, accent, religion, pregnancy,
  family, disability, or anything about appearance unrelated to the dress code.
- **Free of labels:** write "gave no example; answered hypothetically" rather than "lazy" or "not
  a fit."

| Weak note | Strong note |
|---|---|
| "Great guy, good energy" | "Gave 3 specific examples; asked about grade ladder twice" |
| "Seems older, might not last" | (Never write this — age is protected) |
| "Bad attitude" | "Blamed former supervisor in 3 of 4 answers; no ownership" |
| "Smart" | "Explained ballast troubleshooting step by step; isolated fault logically" |

Tell candidates at the start that you'll be taking notes so they aren't thrown by it.

## Items to Watch for in Answers

The kit lists warning signs. Mark them when they appear:

1. **States thoughts or opinions but no specific example.** Redirect to a real time.
2. **Doesn't address the question asked.** Repeat the question once; if still off, note it.
3. **Focuses on their own needs** rather than what they can do or have done. ("I need a job close
   to home with weekends off.")
4. **Claims a key responsibility without evidence.** Ask *why* they believe it matters and for past
   examples showing it.
5. **Offers dependability and punctuality as a differentiator.** It's an expectation, not a
   standout.

## The Observe Checklist

Alongside the questions, the kit has an **Observe** checklist. Complete it right after the
interview:

- Dressed appropriately (for the interview; work clothes from a job site are fine)?
- On time?
- Knows anything about our company, or remembers after the first interview?
- Prepared (resume, references, notes, questions)?
- Confidence and enthusiasm?
- Listens?
- Responds to questions without dancing or rambling?
- Gives specific examples?
- Asks questions?
- Follows up with a thank-you letter or call?

These are useful signals, but they are **secondary** to the competency scores.

> **Legal:** Judge "dressed appropriately" against the job and the dress code, not personal taste.
> Religious attire, hairstyles associated with race, and similar characteristics are protected in
> many places. Do not mark someone down for them.

## Key Takeaways
- Score every behavior question 1–9 against shared anchors, starting at 2, 5, or 8 within the band.
- Mark Answered Y/N honestly and score immediately, before discussing the candidate.
- Write factual, job-related notes with short quotes; never note protected characteristics.
- Flag the "items to watch for" when they appear.
- Use the Observe checklist as a secondary signal, judged against the job, not personal taste.
