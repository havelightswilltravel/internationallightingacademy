## Start With the People Who Already Succeed

Before writing a "perfect candidate" list, look at the technicians who have done well here. Many of
them did not come from lighting. They were mechanics, warehouse workers, HVAC helpers, veterans,
or landscapers who were reliable, curious, and good with their hands. The company's interview kit
captures that pattern in its **Candidate Qualities / Requirements** checklist.

## The Company's Qualities List, Sorted

The checklist mixes three kinds of items. Sorting them tells you **how** to assess each one.

### Character and work-style qualities (assess by behavior-based interview and references)

| Quality on the kit | What it looks like on the job | Competencies in the kit that reveal it |
|---|---|---|
| Motivated to make money and grow | Asks how to move up a grade; finishes courses early | Ambition, Initiative |
| Self starter | Restocks the truck without being told; calls the customer back | Initiative |
| Positive attitude | Stays professional on a cold night call | Personal Effectiveness, Adaptability |
| Long term | Stable reasons for past moves; goals that fit our ladder | Background questions, Self Assessment |
| Goal oriented | Can describe a goal, the plan, and the result | Ambition |
| Good communicator | Explains a delay clearly to a facility manager | Communication, Listening, Customer Orientation |
| Professional in appearance and manner | Courteous, prepared, punctual to the interview | Observe checklist |
| Smart, but not necessarily book smart | Figures things out; practical judgment | Analytical Thinking, Evaluating Alternatives |
| Recognizes their strengths and weaknesses | Names a real weakness and what they are doing about it | Self Assessment |
| Mechanically inclined | Has fixed cars, built things, used tools | Background questions, work sample |
| Problem solver | Walks through a logical approach to a failure | Analytical Thinking, Problem Resolution |
| Safety conscious | Has stopped work when something was unsafe | Caution; electrician safe/unsafe questions |

### Job requirements (verify with documents or job-related questions)

- Valid driver's license and an acceptable driving record (for roles that drive).
- Reliable transportation to the shop or job site.
- Able to perform the physical work: lifting and carrying materials (the kit lists 50 lbs),
  climbing ladders, working outdoors up to 8 hours a day.
- Available for on call, odd hours, odd shifts, overtime, and travel of up to a few weeks,
  3–4 times a year.
- Able to acquire the required hand tools on the company's schedule.

### Preferences

- Knowledge of lighting and electrical is a plus but not necessary.

> **Legal:** Two items on the current list need careful handling. "Healthy" is not a job
> requirement and invites disability-related judgments; assess the specific physical functions
> instead. Physical, schedule, and travel requirements should always be framed as essential
> functions that the candidate can perform "with or without reasonable accommodation." MGR-C04
> covers the compliant wording. Have HR and counsel review the final checklist.

## Turning the Profile Into Interview Targets

Pick **six to eight competencies** that matter most for the role and use them for every candidate
for that opening. For an entry-level technician, a strong set is:

1. Caution (safety)
2. Initiative
3. Analytical Thinking
4. Customer Orientation
5. Personal Effectiveness (accountability)
6. Integrity
7. Teamwork
8. Adaptability

For an electrician, keep Caution and Analytical Thinking and add the technical questions in the
electrician kit (license, area of expertise, best and worst project, safe and unsafe experience).

## Must-Haves vs. Tie-Breakers

Write down, before you see a single resume, which items are **must-haves** (a candidate who lacks
one is not hired) and which are **tie-breakers**. Typical example for an entry-level technician:

| Must-have | Tie-breaker |
|---|---|
| Can perform essential physical functions, with or without accommodation | Prior lighting or electrical work |
| Valid license and acceptable driving record (driving roles) | Trade-school certificate |
| Available for on call and travel as described | Already owns some hand tools |
| Scores at or above 5 on safety-related competencies | Bilingual, if customers need it |

Deciding this in advance stops the common mistake of changing the standard to fit a candidate the
team already likes.

> **Tip:** "Dependable and on time" is an expectation, not a differentiator. The kit's own "Items to
> Watch for" list says so. A candidate who says "I'm always on time" has not yet told you anything
> that sets them apart.

## Key Takeaways
- Build the profile from the people who already succeed here, not an imagined ideal.
- Sort the qualities list into character qualities, verifiable requirements, and preferences, and assess each the right way.
- Choose six to eight competencies per opening and use them for every candidate.
- Set must-haves and tie-breakers before reviewing resumes.
- Replace "Healthy" with specific essential functions and use accommodation-aware wording.
