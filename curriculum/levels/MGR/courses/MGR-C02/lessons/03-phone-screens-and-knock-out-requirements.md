## Why Phone Screen

A 15–20 minute phone or video screen saves hours of in-person interviews. It confirms the
knock-out requirements, previews the job, and gives you a first look at communication. Use the
**same script for every candidate** for the opening.

## Knock-Out Requirements

A knock-out is a requirement that, if not met, ends consideration no matter how strong the rest of
the candidate looks. Knock-outs must be **true essential requirements, job-related, and applied to
everyone the same way**.

| Knock-out | When it applies | How to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Valid driver's license | Roles that drive a company vehicle or between sites | "This role drives a company vehicle. Do you have a valid driver's license?" |
| Acceptable driving record | Same roles; standard set by company policy and the insurer | "Our insurer requires a driving record check after an offer. Is there anything that would keep you from meeting a standard of [policy]?" |
| Reliable transportation | Roles that report to the shop or directly to job sites | "You'd report to [shop / job sites across the region]. Do you have a reliable way to get there on time, including early starts?" |
| Required license | Electrician roles | "This role requires a [state] journeyman license. What's your license number and class?" |
| Schedule and travel | All field roles with on call and travel | "The job includes an on-call rotation, nights some weeks, overtime, and travel a few times a year for up to a few weeks. Can you meet those requirements?" |
| Physical functions | All field roles | "The work includes lifting materials up to about 50 lbs, climbing ladders, and working outdoors up to 8 hours. Can you perform these functions, with or without reasonable accommodation?" |
| Work authorization | All roles | "Are you legally authorized to work in the United States?" (Ask everyone; don't ask about citizenship or national origin.) |

> **Legal:** Ask "Do you have reliable transportation?" in terms of getting to work on time — not
> "Do you own a car?" If driving is not part of the job, a car is not a requirement, and some
> jurisdictions treat car ownership requirements as potentially discriminatory. Driving record
> checks are consumer reports under the FCRA and are run after proper disclosure and authorization,
> normally after a conditional offer. See MGR-C04.

## A Phone Screen Script

1. **Open (1 min).** "Thanks for your time. This call is about 15 minutes. I'll tell you about the
   job, ask a few questions, and leave time for yours."
2. **Realistic preview (2 min).** Read the conditions from your job profile.
3. **Knock-outs (3 min).** Ask the questions above, in the same order, to everyone.
4. **Background (4 min).** "Walk me through your last two or three jobs and what you mainly did."
   "Why are you looking to leave / why did you leave?" (both from the kit).
5. **Two behavior questions (4 min).** For example, from the kit's Initiative and Caution sections:
   "How did you get work assignments at your most recent employer?" and "Tell me about a time the
   rules or guidelines weren't clear. What did you do?"
6. **Pay expectations (1 min).** "What pay range are you looking for?" Share the range for the role.
   Do not ask what they currently make (see MGR-C04).
7. **Their questions and next steps (2 min).**

Record answers right away on the screening form.

## What to Listen For

- Clear, direct answers versus rambling or dodging (the kit's Observe checklist: "Responds to
  questions? Does not dance or ramble").
- Did they research our company? The kit asks, "What do you know about [our company]?"
- Energy and interest in learning and growing.
- Consistency between the resume and what they say.

## Common Phone-Screen Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Small talk about family or hometown | Invites protected information | Keep small talk to the weather, traffic, or the job |
| Skipping knock-outs for a "great" candidate | Inconsistent treatment; wasted interviews | Ask everyone the same knock-outs |
| Selling the job without previewing the hard parts | Early turnover | Read the preview every time |
| Waiting days to call back strong candidates | Good techs get other offers fast | Schedule the interview on the call if they qualify |

> **Tip:** If a candidate volunteers protected information ("I'll need Fridays off for my
> church," "I have three kids"), don't follow up on it. Say, "Thanks. Let's focus on the job
> requirements," and continue. Accommodation requests are handled by HR through the proper process.

## Key Takeaways
- Use the same short phone-screen script for every candidate for an opening.
- Knock-outs must be essential, job-related, and applied identically.
- Ask about license, transportation, schedule, and physical functions in compliant, job-related terms.
- Ask desired pay, not current or past pay.
- Move fast on qualified candidates and document every screen.
