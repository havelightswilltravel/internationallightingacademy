## Build a Pipeline, Not a Panic

Most managers recruit only when someone quits, then hire the first warm body. The better approach is
a steady pipeline: relationships with a few reliable sources that send candidates all year. Track
where every hire came from and how long they stayed; after a year you will know which sources work.

## Sources That Produce Good Technicians

| Source | Why it works | How to tap it |
|---|---|---|
| **Employee referrals** | Current techs know the job and don't want to work with someone who will make them look bad | Offer a referral bonus paid after the new hire completes 90 days; remind techs at meetings |
| **Trade and technical schools** | Students have basic electrical theory and want a career | Speak at career days, offer job-shadow days, stay in touch with instructors |
| **Military transition programs** | Veterans bring discipline, safety culture, and often electrical or mechanical training | Contact base transition offices and veteran job boards; translate military experience into job terms |
| **Adjacent trades** | HVAC helpers, auto and diesel mechanics, sign installers, low-voltage and alarm techs, maintenance workers already use tools and troubleshoot | Target ads to these titles; value mechanical aptitude over lighting knowledge |
| **Supply houses and distributors** | Counter staff know products and contractors | Build relationships with local branch managers |
| **Former employees in good standing** | Known quantity, short ramp-up | Keep a list of "would rehire" leavers and check in |
| **Apprenticeship and workforce programs** | Pre-screened, motivated candidates | Contact your state workforce office and local pre-apprenticeship programs |

> **Tip:** Our qualities list says "Smart, but not necessarily book smart" and "Mechanically
> inclined." Someone who rebuilt a motorcycle engine may become a better troubleshooter than someone
> with a certificate and no hands-on history.

> **Legal:** Relying only on referrals can reproduce the makeup of your current crew and shut out
> qualified people. Use referrals alongside open, broadly advertised postings so the pool stays
> diverse and your process stays fair.

## Writing a Job Ad That Works

A good ad does three jobs: attracts the right people, discourages the wrong ones, and supports a
fair process. Structure:

1. **Title people search for.** "Lighting Service Technician — Paid Training, No Experience
   Required" beats "Field Associate II."
2. **Two-sentence hook.** What the job is and why it is a good career.
3. **What you'll do.** Five or six essential functions from your job profile.
4. **What you need.** Only the true must-haves: for example, a valid driver's license and an
   acceptable driving record (for driving roles), the ability to perform the physical work with or
   without reasonable accommodation, availability for on call and travel.
5. **Nice to have.** Lighting or electrical experience, trade-school training.
6. **Realistic preview.** On call, odd hours, travel a few times a year, outdoor work.
7. **What we offer.** Pay range (required by law in a growing number of states and cities),
   benefits, the training ladder, pay tied to grade, path toward the electrical track.
8. **Equal opportunity statement** as approved by HR.
9. **How to apply.**

### Words to avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Young, energetic team" | Signals age preference | "Fast-paced, active team" |
| "Recent graduates" | Can signal age preference | "Entry-level; no experience required" |
| "Native English speaker" | National origin | "Able to communicate with customers in English" (only if job-related) |
| "Must be in perfect health" | Disability | Specific physical functions with accommodation language |
| "Guys," "foreman" (as the only term) | Gender signaling | "Technicians," "lead," "crew lead" |
| "Clean-cut" | Can screen out religious or cultural grooming | "Professional appearance per company dress code" |

## Measuring Your Sources

Keep a simple log (the app's hiring records or a spreadsheet): source, applicants, phone screens,
interviews, offers, hires, and whether the hire was still here at 90 days and one year. Put money
and time into the sources that produce people who stay.

## Key Takeaways
- Recruit continuously from a few proven sources rather than in a panic.
- Referrals, trade schools, military transition programs, and adjacent trades produce strong technicians.
- Value mechanical aptitude and reliability over lighting knowledge for entry roles.
- Write ads with a searchable title, true must-haves, a realistic preview, the pay range where required, and the training ladder.
- Avoid wording that signals age, national origin, gender, religion, or disability preferences.
