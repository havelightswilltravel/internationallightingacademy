## Onboarding Is Retention

The first 90 days decide whether a new technician stays. People who feel unprepared, ignored, or
unsafe in their first weeks start job hunting almost immediately. A planned onboarding costs a few
hours of manager time and saves the far larger cost of rehiring.

## Before Day One

Use a pre-start checklist so the new hire's first impression is "these people were ready for me."

| Item | Owner | Done |
|---|---|---|
| Offer conditions cleared (background, driving record, drug test, as applicable) | HR | ☐ |
| Start date, time, location, parking, dress confirmed by phone | Manager | ☐ |
| Uniforms and PPE in the right sizes (hard hat, glasses, gloves, boots policy, hi-vis) | Manager | ☐ |
| Company tools issued or tool policy explained | Manager | ☐ |
| Phone, email, and app account created; enrolled at their starting level | Admin | ☐ |
| Vehicle assignment and insurance approval (driving roles) | Fleet / HR | ☐ |
| Mentor assigned and briefed | Manager | ☐ |
| First-week schedule written | Manager | ☐ |
| Crew told the new hire's name and start date | Manager | ☐ |

## Day One

1. **Greet them personally** at the agreed time. Don't hand them off to a stranger.
2. **HR paperwork**, including Form I-9 Section 1 (Section 2 within three business days).
3. **Safety first:** company safety orientation, PPE fitting and inspection, how to report hazards,
   injuries, and near misses, and the stop-work expectation. Make clear that new technicians never
   work on energized equipment and always follow lockout/tagout.
4. **Tour:** shop, stock, trucks, where things are.
5. **App walk-through:** their starting level, the first courses (the LT1 orientation and safety
   courses for entry hires), how hours and skill sign-offs work, and the grade ladder.
6. **Meet the mentor** and the crew.
7. **Review the 30/60/90-day plan** together (below).
8. **End-of-day check-in:** "What questions do you have? What surprised you?"

## The First Week

- Ride with the mentor on real calls; observe and assist with simple tasks under direct supervision.
- Complete the first safety courses before doing hands-on tasks they cover.
- Daily five-minute check-in with the manager or mentor.
- End-of-week conversation: what's going well, what's confusing, what they need.

> **Tip:** A new hire on day three will tell you things about your onboarding that a ten-year
> veteran no longer notices. Ask, write it down, and fix the gaps for the next hire.

## The 30/60/90-Day Plan

Write specific, measurable goals tied to the program. Example for an entry-level LT1 hire:

| Milestone | Goals | Check-in |
|---|---|---|
| **30 days** | Complete LT1 safety, PPE, ladder, and LOTO courses; demonstrate PPE inspection and ladder setup to the mentor; ride along on 15+ service calls; complete work orders correctly with mentor review | 30-day review with manager: progress, concerns, support needed |
| **60 days** | Complete most LT1 courses; perform relamps and supervised ballast replacements; log verified OJT hours; begin skill sign-offs with an evaluator | 60-day review: skills progress, customer feedback, fit with schedule and on call |
| **90 days** | All LT1 courses passed; on track for the LT1 final exam and remaining skills; handling routine calls with mentor nearby; joins on-call rotation if policy allows | 90-day review: confirm the path to LT2, set the next 90 days |

For placed experienced hires, build the plan around the gaps found in the placement assessment and
the skills needed for the next grade.

## Choosing and Supporting a Mentor

A good mentor is a strong, safe technician who **wants** to teach, not just the most senior person
available.

Mentor responsibilities:
- Model safe work every time — the new hire will copy what they see, not what they're told.
- Explain the "why" behind procedures.
- Let the new hire do the work, at the right level of supervision, as they're ready.
- Give honest, specific feedback daily.
- Tell the manager early about concerns.

Support the mentor: lighter workload during the first weeks, clear expectations, recognition, and
(where company policy provides) a mentor stipend. Check in with the mentor separately at 30, 60,
and 90 days.

> **Tip:** Pair the mentor and new hire on the same truck or crew whenever possible for the first
> month. Mentoring by phone from another job site doesn't work for a brand-new technician.

## Key Takeaways
- Onboarding is the first and most important retention tool.
- Have PPE, tools, accounts, vehicle, and mentor ready before day one.
- Day one covers safety, paperwork, the app and ladder, the mentor, and the 30/60/90 plan.
- Set specific 30/60/90-day goals linked to courses, skills, and OJT hours, and hold the check-ins.
- Choose mentors who are safe and want to teach, and support them.
