## When Someone Leaves

Some turnover is healthy: people move, retire, change careers, or weren't the right fit. But every
avoidable departure costs money, slows the crew, and often hurts customer service. Exit interviews
help you learn from departures; turnover cost calculations help you justify the time spent on
hiring and retention.

## Exit Interviews

### Who conducts it
Ideally someone **other than the direct supervisor** — HR or another manager — because the
supervisor relationship is often part of the reason. Some companies also use a short written or
online survey.

### When
In the last week of employment, or by phone or survey shortly after the person leaves (people are
often more candid once they've left).

### Questions to ask
1. "What prompted you to start looking?"
2. "What did you like most about working here?"
3. "What would you change about the job, the team, or the company?"
4. "How well did your training and the grade ladder prepare you and help you grow?"
5. "How would you describe the support you got from your supervisor?"
6. "Was the job what you expected when you were hired?" (Tests your realistic job preview.)
7. "Is there anything that would have kept you here?"
8. "Would you consider coming back in the future? Would you recommend us to a friend?"

### How to handle it
- Thank them and stay neutral. Don't argue or defend.
- Explain how the information will be used and who will see it.
- Take notes on themes, not gossip.
- If they raise harassment, discrimination, safety violations, or wage issues, pass these to HR
  immediately — they may require investigation even after the person leaves.
- Close on good terms. Former employees become customers, referral sources, and rehires.

> **Legal:** Exit interviews are not the place to negotiate separation terms or discuss references
> beyond company policy. Follow HR's guidance on what can be said about final pay, benefits
> continuation, and returning company property.

### Use the data
Every quarter, review exit themes across the team:
- Are people leaving in the first 90 days? Look at hiring, the job preview, and onboarding.
- Are they leaving at a certain grade? Look at the path and pay at that grade.
- Are they leaving one supervisor? Look at that supervisor's support and training.
- Are they leaving for the same competitor or the same reason? Address it directly.

## The Cost of Turnover

Turnover costs are mostly hidden, which is why they're easy to ignore. Estimates for skilled
hourly roles commonly run from a substantial fraction of annual pay up to the full annual pay or
more. Build your own estimate with a simple worksheet.

| Cost category | What to include | Example estimate |
|---|---|---|
| **Separation** | HR and manager time on the exit, final pay processing, unused PTO payout | $400 |
| **Vacancy** | Overtime paid to others to cover; lost or delayed jobs; customer impact | $4,000 (6 weeks of overtime and delayed work) |
| **Recruiting** | Job ads, referral bonus, manager and interviewer hours, background and drug tests | $2,500 |
| **Onboarding** | Paperwork, PPE, uniforms, tools, safety orientation | $1,500 |
| **Training** | Mentor time, evaluator time, reduced productivity while learning (often 3–6 months) | $9,000 |
| **Errors and risk** | Callbacks and rework, higher injury risk for new workers | $1,500 |
| **Estimated total** | | **≈ $18,900** |

*The figures above are illustrative only. Replace them with your own pay rates, hours, and costs.*

Example: a team of 20 technicians with 30% annual turnover loses 6 people a year. At about $19,000
each, that's roughly $114,000 a year — often more than the cost of a full-time technician. Cutting
turnover to 15% saves more than half of it.

> **Tip:** Track turnover two ways: **overall** and **first-year**. First-year turnover is the best
> measure of how well you're hiring and onboarding. Track it by source (MGR-C02) too.

## Closing the Loop

Retention links back to everything in this certification:
- **Define the role honestly** (MGR-C01) so people know what they're signing up for.
- **Source and screen** for people who want a long-term trade career (MGR-C02).
- **Interview for evidence**, not charm (MGR-C03), **legally and fairly** (MGR-C04).
- **Select carefully and place correctly** (MGR-C05).
- **Onboard, mentor, develop, recognize, and listen** (MGR-C06).

## Key Takeaways
- Exit interviews are best conducted by someone other than the direct supervisor, with neutral, open questions.
- Escalate any harassment, discrimination, safety, or wage concerns raised in an exit interview to HR immediately.
- Review exit themes quarterly by tenure, grade, supervisor, and reason.
- Turnover cost includes separation, vacancy, recruiting, onboarding, training, and errors; build your own estimate.
- Track overall and first-year turnover; first-year turnover measures hiring and onboarding quality.
