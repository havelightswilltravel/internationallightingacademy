## Why Calibrate

Even with good anchors, interviewers see different things and score differently. A **calibration
meeting** brings all the evidence together and turns individual scorecards into one
defensible decision. Done right, it is the single best defense against one loud opinion or one
interviewer's bias deciding the hire.

## Before the Meeting

1. **All scorecards submitted.** Every interviewer completes their own scores and notes (on the digital
   scorecard or a printed kit) before the meeting. No hallway lobbying.
2. **References and license verification done** for finalists (or scheduled, with the decision
   conditional on them).
3. **Prepare a summary sheet.** For each candidate: competency scores from each interviewer,
   average, the knock-outs, and must-haves.

## Reading the Scorecards

Look at the numbers, but don't let averages make the decision on their own.

| What you see | What it may mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| All interviewers within 1–2 points | Strong agreement | Confirm with notes, move on |
| A 3+ point gap on a competency | Interviewers heard different things or used the scale differently | Each reads their notes aloud; agree on what the evidence shows |
| One interviewer consistently higher or lower on every candidate | Leniency or strictness bias | Focus on that interviewer's notes rather than the numbers |
| Many N (not answered) marks | Candidate couldn't give examples, or questions weren't asked | Distinguish the two; don't penalize the candidate for an interviewer skipping a question |
| High scores everywhere except safety (Caution) | A must-have may not be met | Revisit the must-have list; safety is not traded for charm |

## Running the Meeting

1. **Restate the job profile and must-haves** before discussing anyone. Remind everyone the
   standard was set in advance.
2. **Go candidate by candidate, competency by competency.**
3. **Junior first.** The most junior interviewer shares scores and evidence first, then the next,
   with the hiring manager last. This limits anchoring on the boss's view.
4. **Evidence, not adjectives.** Ask "What did they say?" rather than "Did you like them?"
5. **Challenge bias politely.** If someone says "not a fit," "too old for this," "she won't like the
   lifts," or "he reminds me of me," ask for the job-related evidence. Comments about protected
   characteristics end there and are not recorded except as needed by HR.
6. **Bring in references and the Observe checklist** as supporting evidence.
7. **Decide.** Options: offer, hold as backup, or decline. A legitimate outcome is "no one met the
   standard — reopen the search."
8. **Record the decision and the reasons** for each finalist in the app, in job-related terms.

> **Tip:** Separate "great interviewee" from "great technician." The kit's Observe items
> (confidence, enthusiasm, follow-up thank-you) matter, but the competency evidence and references
> should carry the decision.

## Writing the Decision Record

Good:
- "Selected: highest scores on Caution (avg 8) and Analytical Thinking (avg 7); references
  confirmed safety habits and customer handling; meets all must-haves."
- "Not selected: Caution avg 3; described bypassing lockout to save time; one reference would not
  rehire."
- "Not selected: does not meet the driving-record standard for this driving role (per FCRA
  process handled by HR)."

Poor:
- "Just didn't click."
- "Overqualified."
- "Not a culture fit."

## Avoiding Common Calibration Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| The highest-ranking person speaks first and everyone agrees | Junior-first order |
| Lowering the bar because the position has been open a long time | Revisit the must-haves openly; if you change them, change them for everyone and reopen consideration |
| Comparing candidates to a former employee ("he's no Mike") | Compare to the profile and anchors |
| Ignoring a low safety score because everything else is high | Caution is a must-have in our trade |
| Deciding before references are complete | Make the decision conditional, or wait |

## Key Takeaways
- Calibrate only after every scorecard is submitted independently.
- Restate the job profile and must-haves, then go competency by competency with evidence.
- Use junior-first order and challenge non-job-related comments.
- Investigate large score gaps and consistent leniency or strictness through the notes.
- Record job-related reasons for every finalist's outcome.
