## Move Quickly

Good technicians usually have more than one option. Once the decision is made and approvals are in,
make the offer within a day or two. Delays signal disorganization and lose candidates.

## Setting the Starting Grade

Our program gives you a structured way to decide where a new hire starts, which also determines
pay where the company uses the pay-by-grade scale.

### Entry-level hires

Candidates without relevant lighting experience start at **Lighting Technician I (LT1)** and work
through the ladder. Prior mechanical or trade experience may help them advance faster, but
advancement still comes from passing courses, the final exam, verified on-the-job hours, and
observed hands-on skills.

### Experienced hires: placement

Experienced lighting technicians, or electricians joining the program, should not have to repeat
levels they've already mastered. Use the **placement process**:

1. **Placement assessment.** The candidate completes a documented assessment for the grade they
   claim: typically the knowledge exams for the levels below the target grade and evaluator-observed
   demonstrations of the key hands-on skills, especially the critical safety skills (PPE, lockout/
   tagout, live-dead-live verification, ladder and lift setup).
2. **Evaluator review.** A qualified evaluator reviews results and recommends a grade.
3. **Admin placement.** A company admin records the placement in the app at the appropriate
   LT grade (or, for qualifying candidates, an electrical track level), with a **written reason**
   referencing the assessment results. The app requires that reason.
4. **Starting pay** follows the pay scale for that grade.
5. **Gaps become the training plan.** Any skills not demonstrated during placement are assigned in
   the onboarding plan.

| Candidate | Typical starting point |
|---|---|
| No lighting or electrical experience | LT1 |
| Some lighting maintenance experience (relamping, ballast swaps) | Assessment for LT2 |
| Experienced fixture and controls troubleshooter | Assessment for LT3 or LT4 |
| Licensed electrician joining the lighting team | Assessment for the appropriate grade; licensing verified (MGR-C05 Lesson 1) |

> **Tip:** When in doubt, place one grade lower and set clear 30/60/90-day goals to earn the next
> grade quickly. Placing too high puts a new hire on tasks they can't yet do safely and hurts
> confidence; placing a bit low with a fast, clear path is motivating.

> **Legal:** Use the same placement standard for everyone. Set pay from the grade scale, not from
> the candidate's pay history (banned in some places), and apply the same rules when candidates
> negotiate, so similarly placed people are paid alike.

## Preparing the Offer

Confirm with HR before the call:

- Job title, starting grade, and pay rate from the grade scale.
- Status (full-time, non-exempt for overtime purposes in most technician roles), schedule, on-call
  and travel expectations.
- Benefits summary and when they begin.
- Tool, phone, vehicle, and uniform policies.
- Start date, reporting location, and first-day details.
- **Conditions** of the offer, as applicable under company policy and law: background check,
  driving-record check, drug test, post-offer medical exam (if required for all in the job
  category), license verification, and Form I-9 completion.

## Making the Offer

1. **Call first.** A verbal offer is personal and lets you answer questions. Say: "We'd like to
   offer you the Lighting Technician position at Grade LT2, at $__ per hour."
2. **Explain the ladder.** Show the next grade and its pay rate, and what it will take to get there.
3. **State the conditions clearly.** "The offer is conditional on a background check, driving-record
   check, and drug test, which HR will send you."
4. **Give a decision deadline** that is reasonable (often two to three business days).
5. **Follow with a written offer letter** from HR. Avoid language that promises guaranteed or
   permanent employment unless HR intends it.
6. **Notify other finalists** only after the offer is accepted and conditions are cleared, so you
   keep backups available. Decline the rest courteously and promptly.

## Handling Negotiation

Technicians may ask for more. Options that keep pay fair and consistent:

- Explain that pay follows the grade scale, and that the fastest raise is earning the next grade.
- Offer an earlier placement re-assessment (for example, at 60 days) if they believe they qualify
  for a higher grade.
- Discuss non-pay items within policy: start date, tool allowance timing, schedule.
- If an exception is needed, get HR approval and document the job-related reason.

## Key Takeaways
- Make the offer quickly after approvals.
- Entry-level hires start at LT1; experienced hires complete a documented placement assessment and are placed at the right grade by an admin with a written reason.
- Pay follows the grade scale, not pay history; apply the same rules to everyone.
- Call with a verbal offer, explain the ladder and conditions, then send HR's written offer.
- Handle negotiation through the grade ladder, re-assessment, and documented exceptions.
