## The Company Procedure

The Master Troubleshooting Guide gives this procedure for breakers:

1. Check for breaker amperage tolerance levels. **Do not open the dead front of a panel unless you
   have specific electrical training.**
2. Write it up for an electrician to replace the breaker if it trips intermittently **within the
   tolerance range**.
3. Check for loose connection or overheating of wire, breaker or panel.
4. Wiggle the switch arm (breaker handle). This can sometimes be an indicator of a weak breaker.

Components: panel, bus bar, breaker, wire.

## The Dead-Front Rule

The **dead front** is the inner cover that surrounds the breaker handles and covers the bus bars,
breaker terminals and conductors. With it in place, only the handles are exposed. With it removed,
energized bus and lugs are exposed — a shock and **arc flash** hazard.

| You are... | You may |
|---|---|
| A lighting technician (not qualified for panel work) | Open the panel **door**, read the directory, operate breaker handles (unless signs of damage/heat), look, listen, feel the dead-front surface, scan with a thermal camera from outside, use a circuit tracer receiver on the handles |
| A qualified person under the company's NFPA 70E program, with training, PPE and authorization for the task | Remove the dead front, take energized measurements, perform thermography of terminations, and — if also authorized for the repair — replace breakers |

Breaker replacement is **write it up for an electrician** unless the company has specifically
qualified you. Even a qualified person replaces a breaker with the panel de-energized whenever
feasible, following lockout/tagout.

## Expanded Field Procedure

### Stage 1 — Gather information

1. Interview the customer: which lights go off, how often, how long after turning on, what changed,
   weather, recent work. Write it down.
2. Find the panel and breaker from the directory; confirm by the method in Lesson 3 if needed.
3. Note the breaker **type and markings visible on the handle**: rating (15/20 A), poles,
   GFCI/AFCI test button and trip indicator, SWD/HID if visible.

### Stage 2 — Look, smell, feel, listen (dead front stays on)

4. Look for discoloration, soot, melting or damage on the dead front, door and breaker handle.
5. Smell for overheated insulation. Listen for buzzing or crackling.
6. With the back of your hand, feel the breaker handle and the dead front next to it, compared with
   similar breakers. If you have a thermal camera, scan and save an image.
7. **Stop here and write it up immediately** if you find burning smell, melting, soot, arcing
   sounds or a handle too hot to touch. Do not operate that breaker.

### Stage 3 — Wiggle the breaker handle

8. With the breaker **on** and its loads operating, gently move the handle a small amount within
   its ON position — do **not** push it toward OFF or force it.
9. Watch the circuit's lights (have a helper or use a visible fixture).
   - Lights flicker or drop out = loose connection at the breaker or a weak breaker/bus connection.
   - Handle feels loose, sloppy or does not stay firmly in ON = worn mechanism.
10. Record what you observed. Either finding goes on the write-up — the repair is behind the dead
    front.

### Stage 4 — Check the load against the rating (the "tolerance" check)

11. **Without opening the dead front**, determine the load:
    - Total fixture input watts/VA from nameplates (Lesson 3), **or**
    - Measure current with a clamp meter **at an accessible point outside the panel** where a single
      circuit conductor can be isolated within the meter's jaws **without exposing energized parts**
      (clamping hot and neutral together reads near zero) — in practice this is rare, so the usual
      answer is the nameplate total, or a qualified person measures at the breaker.
12. Compare with the breaker rating and the 80% continuous limit:

| Result | Conclusion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Load over rating or over 80% continuous | Overload — breaker doing its job | Recommend reducing/splitting load or a new circuit (electrician) |
| Load within rating; trips intermittently | Likely weak breaker or heating connection | **Write it up for an electrician to replace the breaker** and check terminations |
| Trips instantly on reset | Short or ground fault on the load side | Leave off and tagged; investigate de-energized on the load side or write up |
| GFCI/AFCI trips | Leakage, arcing or possible nuisance | Inspect the load side de-energized (wet fixtures, damaged cable, loose splices); write up if not found |

### Stage 5 — Load-side checks within your scope

13. If a short or ground fault is suspected, **lock out** the circuit (breaker off, lock and tag),
    **verify absence of voltage** live-dead-live at the fixtures or boxes you will open, and inspect
    for pinched whips, damaged cords, water in fixtures, burned splices and failed ballasts or drivers.
    Disconnecting fixtures one section at a time (half-splitting, LT2-C06) helps isolate the fault.
14. Repair within your scope (replace a shorted fixture whip, failed driver, wet fixture
    components), restore and observe.

> **Safety:** Lock out the breaker before working on any part of the circuit; a tripped breaker is
> **not** a lockout. Verify dead with a tested meter before touching conductors. Never hold a
> breaker handle closed, tape it on, or replace it with a larger size.

## Writing a Proper Electrician Work-Up

A good write-up lets an electrician arrive with the right breaker and fix it on the first visit.

| Section | Include |
|---|---|
| Location | Building, room, panel name and location |
| Circuit | Breaker number, rating, poles, type (standard/GFCI/AFCI), visible markings |
| Complaint | Customer's words, frequency, timing (instant/delayed), when it started |
| Load | Fixtures on the circuit, total input watts, calculated amps, % of rating; or measured current and where measured |
| Observations | Wiggle-test result, warmth, discoloration, smell, thermal image |
| Work done | Load-side checks performed, parts replaced, results |
| Recommendation | "Replace 20 A 1-pole breaker, circuit 17, panel LP-2 — trips intermittently at 4.7 A measured load (24% of rating). Inspect breaker terminal and bus connection for heating." |
| Priority | Routine / soon / urgent (any heat damage = urgent) |
| Attachments | Photos of directory, breaker label, thermal image |

Also note any **code or safety concerns** you saw: missing directory, breakers used as switches
without SWD/HID marking, missing knockouts or filler plates, blocked working space in front of the
panel.

## Key Takeaways
- Never remove the dead front unless you are trained, qualified and authorized for that task.
- Wiggle the breaker handle gently within the ON position; flicker or a loose handle points to a weak breaker or loose connection.
- Compare actual load to the rating; intermittent trips well within tolerance mean write it up for an electrician to replace the breaker.
- Stop and escalate immediately on burning smell, melting, soot or arcing.
- A complete work-up has location, circuit, complaint, load, observations, work done and a specific recommendation.
