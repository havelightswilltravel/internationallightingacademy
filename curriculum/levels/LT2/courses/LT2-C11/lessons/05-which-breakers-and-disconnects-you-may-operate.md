## Why This Lesson Exists

Our safety meetings keep coming back to one question: **"What breakers and disconnects can a
technician reset, and which panels should we stay away from?"** Lessons 1–4 taught why breakers
trip and how to assess a trip without opening the dead front. This lesson draws the line on what
you may put your hand on at all.

## What the Standards Say

Operating a breaker is an "interaction with equipment" under NFPA 70E. Its arc-flash risk table
(NFPA 70E Table 130.5(C)) treats the **normal operation** of a breaker or switch as unlikely to
cause an arc flash **only when all of these are true**:

- The equipment is **properly installed** and **properly maintained**.
- **All doors are closed and secured, and all covers (including the dead front) are in place.**
- There is **no evidence of impending failure**: no arcing, overheating, burning smell, loose or
  bound parts, visible damage or deterioration.

If any one of those is false, operating the device is an arc-flash exposure, and it belongs to a
qualified person in the right PPE (LT4-C07). OSHA also forbids reclosing a circuit after a protective
device opens until it is determined that it is **safe to do so** (29 CFR 1910.334(b)(2)). That is the
basis for the one-reset rule.

> **Scope varies by state and city.** In some areas a lighting technician may operate or reset
> breakers and disconnects under the company's license; in others it must be done by, or under the
> supervision of, a licensed electrician, and some jobs need a permit and inspection. Before you do
> this on a job, confirm with your supervisor that it is allowed in that location and that you are
> authorized for it. When in doubt, stop and ask.

## Our Company's List (to be confirmed)

**Per company policy (to be confirmed by the safety program owner)**, a lighting technician:

**May operate**, after checking the three conditions above:
- A **lighting branch-circuit breaker** in a panelboard with the **door closed and dead front
  intact**, to lock out or to restore power (LT1-C06).
- A **lighting disconnect switch or fixture disconnect** you have identified, in good condition.
- **One reset attempt** on a tripped lighting branch breaker, standing to the side of the panel,
  face turned away, after the Lesson 4 checks and only if nothing looks, smells or sounds wrong.
  If it trips again, **stop and write it up** for an electrician.

**May not operate** (write it up and call your supervisor):
- **Switchgear, switchboards, motor control centers and main breakers** of any size.
- Anything labeled **medium voltage** (over 1,000 V). Our company does not have the PPE or the
  qualification for medium-voltage equipment.
- Any device whose arc-flash label shows a hazard above what your issued PPE covers, or that has
  **no label** on equipment large enough to need one.
- Any panel with a **missing dead front, missing breaker blanks, open slots, damage, heat or
  burning smell**.
- **Fused disconnects** that need fuses pulled or replaced; fuse work is for qualified persons.
- Breakers that serve **anything other than lighting** (HVAC, refrigeration, computers, life-safety
  systems) unless the site contact has approved it in writing.

> **Safety:** Never reset a breaker more than once, never hold a breaker handle closed, and never
> use the breaker as a test switch. A breaker that trips again is telling you a fault is still there.

## When You Can't

If the breaker or disconnect you need is on the "may not" list, the answer is the same as for
energized work: **stop, photograph the equipment and its label, and call your supervisor.** A
qualified electrician (LT4-C07) or the customer's facility staff can operate it, or the job is
rescheduled.

## Key Takeaways
- Operating a breaker is low risk only with the door closed, covers on, good maintenance and no
  signs of failure (NFPA 70E Table 130.5(C)).
- Per company policy (to be confirmed): lighting branch breakers and identified lighting
  disconnects in good condition only, with one reset attempt at most.
- Stay away from switchgear, switchboards, MCCs, main breakers and anything medium voltage.
- Damaged, hot, unlabeled or open panels are a stop: photograph, write up, call.
