## What Track Lighting Is and Where You Find It

Track lighting is a run of surface-mounted or pendant-hung **track** — an extruded aluminum
channel with copper conductors inside — that powers movable **track heads** (also called track
fixtures). Heads clip in anywhere along the track and can be aimed. You will find track in retail
stores, galleries, museums, restaurants, showrooms, hotel lobbies, offices and homes.

Because heads can be moved, added and swapped, track runs are frequently changed by store staff.
That makes **compatibility** and **overloaded tracks** common service problems.

## Track Types: H, J and L

Track systems from different makers are **not** universally interchangeable. In North America
there are three common configurations, known in the trade by letters that trace back to the
original manufacturers:

| Type | Also called | Distinguishing features (commonly described — confirm with the maker's literature) |
|---|---|---|
| **H** | Halo style | Usually **three** contact points on the adapter |
| **J** | Juno style | **Two** contacts, set closer together than L |
| **L** | Lightolier style | **Two** contacts, set farther apart than J |

A head made for one type **may physically fit** another type's track but make poor or no contact,
or even reverse polarity. Some makers sell heads with interchangeable adapters, and some
"universal" adapters exist — check the label.

> **Safety:** Never force a head into a track it is not made for, and never modify an adapter.
> A poorly seated adapter arcs, overheats and can make the metal head energized. Use only heads
> and accessories listed for that track system.

### How to identify the track type

1. Look for a label inside the track channel, on the live end or on the canopy.
2. Read the head adapter: most are stamped with the system letter or brand.
3. Compare the contact spacing and polarity tab with the manufacturer's literature.
4. Photograph the end of the track and the adapter for the work order.

## Single-Circuit vs Multi-Circuit Track

| Track | Conductors | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Single-circuit | One hot, one neutral, ground through the housing or a separate conductor | One switch or dimmer controls all heads |
| 2-circuit | Two hots sharing a neutral (or separate neutrals), plus ground | Two groups of heads switched or dimmed separately |
| 3-circuit | Three hots, neutral(s), ground | Common in large retail and galleries |

On multi-circuit track, each head has a **circuit selector** (a lever, rotary switch or a choice of
adapter position). A head set to an unpowered or switched-off circuit looks exactly like a dead
head. Checking the selector is one of the first things to do on an outage call.

## Line-Voltage vs Low-Voltage Track

| Track | Typical voltage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Line-voltage track | 120 V (some 277 V commercial systems) | Most common; heads may contain their own transformer or driver |
| Low-voltage track/rail | 12 V or 24 V | Fed by a remote transformer; carries high current, so it is limited in length and wattage |
| Monorail | Usually 12 V or 24 V; some 120 V | A single bendable rail, often hand-shaped, suspended on standoffs |
| Cable (wire) systems | 12 V or 24 V | Two tensioned bare or insulated cables carry current; heads clip across both |

Low-voltage rail and cable systems are covered in more depth in LT2-C10.

## Track Components

| Component | What it does | Typical failures |
|---|---|---|
| **Live-end feed** (end feed) | Where the branch-circuit conductors connect to the track; mounts to a junction box | Loose terminal screws, overheated connections, cracked housing |
| **Floating canopy** | Covers the junction box and connects to the track at any point along it (not only at the end) | Same as live end; loose mounting |
| **Center or mid-run feed** | Feeds power into the middle of a run | Same |
| **Track section** | Carries conductors; 2, 4, 6, 8, 12 ft typical | Bent or crushed channel, debris in slot, field cut with burrs |
| **Straight connector** | Joins two sections end to end | Loose or misaligned contacts — downstream sections dead |
| **L, T and X connectors** | Turns and branches | Same; polarity must be maintained |
| **Flexible connector** | Adjustable angle joint | Wear from repeated bending |
| **Dead end** (end cap) | Closes the unpowered end; covers exposed conductor ends | Missing cap leaves conductors exposed |
| **Pendant/stem kits** | Hang track below a high ceiling | Loose set screws, supports not rated |
| **Track heads** | The fixtures (see Lesson 2) | Lamps, sockets, transformers, ballasts, drivers |
| **Adapters/accessories** | Pendant adapters, outlet adapters (where listed) | Overloading |

## Track Load

The track itself has a current rating, commonly **20 A** for line-voltage commercial track (check
the label). The branch circuit feeding it may be 15 A or 20 A. The total wattage of the heads on
each circuit of the track must stay within **both** the track rating and the branch circuit's
capacity, applying the 80% continuous-load concept (LT2-C11). Lesson 3 shows how to check this.

## Key Takeaways
- Track lighting = powered channel + movable heads; it is common in retail, galleries and restaurants.
- H, J and L track types are not interchangeable; identify the system before ordering heads or parts.
- Multi-circuit track heads have a circuit selector; a wrong setting looks like a dead head.
- Live-end feeds and floating canopies connect the branch circuit to the track; connectors carry power between sections, and dead ends cover the far end.
- Never force or modify an adapter; use only listed parts for that track system.
