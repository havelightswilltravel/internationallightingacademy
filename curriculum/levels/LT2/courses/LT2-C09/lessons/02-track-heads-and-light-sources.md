## Anatomy of a Track Head

Every track head has the same basic parts:

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Adapter** | Locks into the track, picks up power from the track conductors, and on multi-circuit track selects the circuit |
| **Stem/yoke and swivel** | Supports the head and allows tilt and rotation; contains the internal wiring |
| **Power component** (if any) | Transformer, ballast or LED driver — inside the adapter box, the stem or the head |
| **Lamp holder or LED module** | Socket for a lamp, or a fixed LED array |
| **Head body/heat sink** | Holds the source, dissipates heat |
| **Optics and accessories** | Reflector, lens, louver, snoot, color filter, glare shield |
| **Switch** (some heads) | A local on/off switch on the head or adapter |

## Head Types by Light Source

### Incandescent and halogen heads (line voltage)

- Use lamps such as PAR20, PAR30, PAR38, BR30 or line-voltage halogen.
- No power component: line voltage goes straight from the adapter to the socket.
- Fail by: burned-out lamp, worn or burned socket, broken wiring at the swivel, poor adapter
  contact.
- **Continuity testing is meaningful**: with the head removed from the track, you can check from
  the adapter contacts to the socket contacts (company procedure).

### Ceramic metal halide (CMH) ballasted heads

- Use lamps such as PAR, T4, T6 or G12 base CMH lamps, with an **electronic ballast** in the
  adapter box or head.
- Fail by: lamp end-of-life (cycling, color shift), ballast failure, ignitor failure (often built
  into electronic ballasts), socket damage.
- The ballast must match the lamp **wattage** and type (for example, a 39 W ballast for a 39 W CMH
  lamp). Wrong lamps cause short life or no start.
- CMH lamps need a few minutes to warm up and a cool-down before restrike after a power blip.
  Do not condemn a head that is simply restriking.

### LED heads

- Most are **integrated LED**: the LED module and driver are built in; some take LED lamps.
- Fail by: driver failure, LED module failure, thermal problems (dirty heat sink, enclosed
  location), dimming incompatibility (flicker, drop-out).
- Continuity from adapter to module is not a valid test — there are electronics in the path.
  Replace the head, or the driver/module if the maker sells them as parts.

### Low-voltage heads on line-voltage track

- Use 12 V lamps such as MR16 or MR11 with a **transformer** (magnetic or electronic) built into
  the adapter or head.
- Fail by: lamp, socket (bi-pin sockets wear and burn), transformer.
- Low-voltage lamps on a line-voltage socket would fail instantly — never bypass the transformer.

## Comparing Head Types

| Head | Power component | Typical lamp | Continuity check adapter→socket? | Usual repair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Incandescent/halogen | None | PAR/BR | Yes | Lamp, socket or head |
| CMH | Electronic ballast | PAR/T4/T6 CMH | No (ballast in path) | Lamp, ballast or head |
| Integrated LED | Driver | Built-in | No | Head (or driver if available) |
| Low-voltage | Transformer | MR16/MR11 | Only up to transformer primary | Lamp, transformer or head |

## Lamps and Load

The lamp wattage marked on the head's label is a **maximum**. Over-lamping causes overheating,
socket and wiring damage, and fire risk.

> **Safety:** Lamps and heads get extremely hot. Halogen and CMH lamps can cause serious burns
> within seconds of contact. Turn off the head, let it cool, and use gloves. Some CMH and halogen
> lamps must only be used in heads with a lens because they can rupture — follow the label
> (often marked for enclosed or open fixtures).

## Aiming Track Heads

Good aiming is part of the job, especially in retail and galleries. A common rule of thumb is to
aim at about a **30° angle from vertical** at the object: steep enough to avoid glare in people's
eyes, shallow enough to avoid deep shadows and reflections on glass.

1. Turn off the head (or work with it on only if aiming must be judged with light — keep hands
   off hot surfaces and use the aiming locks, not the lamp).
2. Loosen the tilt and rotation locks.
3. Aim at the center of the object, adjust, and lock both axes.
4. Check from normal viewing positions for glare.
5. Use accessories (snoots, louvers, spread lenses) as the design calls for.
6. Note the aiming in the work order if the customer has a lighting plan.

## Parts Identification on Heads

Record from the head label:

- Manufacturer, catalog number and **track type** (H/J/L or brand)
- Voltage, lamp type and **maximum wattage**
- For ballasted heads: ballast model, lamp wattage and type
- For LED heads: wattage, CCT (e.g., 3000 K), CRI, beam angle, dimming type
- For low-voltage heads: transformer type (magnetic or electronic) and VA rating

## Key Takeaways
- Track heads differ by source: incandescent/halogen, CMH ballasted, LED and low-voltage with a transformer.
- Continuity from adapter to socket is a valid test only on incandescent/halogen heads.
- Ballasted heads require a ballast matched to the lamp wattage and type; CMH needs warm-up and cool-down time.
- Never exceed the head's maximum lamp wattage; let heads cool before handling.
- Aim at roughly 30° from vertical and lock both axes; record the full catalog data when ordering.
