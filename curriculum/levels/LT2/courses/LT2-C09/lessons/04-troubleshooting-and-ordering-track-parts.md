## The Company Procedure

The Master Troubleshooting Guide gives this order for track lighting:

1. Verify there is at least one good lamp in the track.
2. If outages are sporadic, check for power at the sockets before replacing lamps or ballasts.
3. If there is no power at the socket but other fixtures on the track work, check for a switch on
   the track fixture, check for a good connection to the track, or move the fixture to a slightly
   different spot on the track.
4. If moving the fixture does not correct the problem, test continuity (incandescent or halogen
   only) between the track connection points on the fixture and the socket.
5. If the fixture is ballasted, replace the ballast if other fixtures on the track are operating.
6. If the entire track is out, check power and connections at the entrance fitting (live-end feed
   or floating canopy).

The procedure below follows that order and adds the safety steps and checks that make it work in
the field.

## Step-by-Step Procedure

### Stage 1 — Size up the problem

1. **Talk to the customer.** How many heads are out? Since when? Were heads moved or added? Were
   lamps changed? Is it constant or intermittent?
2. **Look at the whole run.** Which heads work and which do not? Draw a quick sketch numbering
   the heads, feed location, connectors and circuits.
3. **Check the switch or dimmer** controlling the track and, for multi-circuit track, every
   circuit's control.
4. **Verify at least one good lamp in the track** (company step 1). If a head works with a known
   good lamp, the track is powered at that point and you can use that lamp to test other heads
   **of the same type**.

### Stage 2 — Head-level checks

5. **Check for a local switch** on the head or adapter and confirm it is on.
6. **Check the circuit selector** on multi-circuit heads — set to the correct, powered circuit.
7. **Check the track connection.** Turn the head off at its switch (or turn off the track),
   remove it, inspect the adapter contacts for bending, burning or dirt, and reinstall it fully
   locked. **Move the head a few inches** to a different spot on the track; a bad spot in the
   track conductor or debris in the slot can prevent contact.
8. **Check for power at the socket** — especially when outages are sporadic (company step 2).
   - De-energized method (preferred for techs): with the head removed from the track, check
     continuity from adapter contacts to socket contacts (incandescent/halogen only — see step 9).
   - Energized method: measuring voltage at an exposed socket is energized diagnostic work. Only
     qualified persons do it, with the PPE and procedures required by the company's NFPA 70E
     program. A non-contact tester is a screening tool only; it does not prove absence of voltage.
9. **Continuity test (incandescent or halogen heads only)** — company step 4. With the head
   removed from the track and the lamp out:
   - Set the meter to continuity/ohms.
   - Probe from the adapter's hot contact to the socket's center contact; then from the neutral
     contact to the socket shell (or the second pin). Wiggle the swivel and stem while testing —
     internal conductors often break at the pivot.
   - Open circuit = broken wire or bad socket: replace the socket or the head.
10. **Ballasted heads** (CMH): if other heads on the same track work and the head has a known good
    lamp of the correct type and wattage, **replace the ballast** (company step 5) — or replace the
    head if the ballast is not sold separately or the head is near end of life.
11. **LED heads:** no continuity test through the driver. If a known good head works in the same
    spot and this one does not, replace the head (or driver/module where available).
12. **Low-voltage heads:** check the lamp, the bi-pin socket for burned or loose pins, then the
    integral transformer (see LT2-C10).

### Stage 3 — Track-level checks

13. **Several heads dead beyond one point:** suspect the **connector** (straight, L, T, flex)
    between working and non-working sections.
14. **Entire track out** (company step 6): confirm the breaker is not tripped and the control is
    on, then inspect the **entrance fitting** (live-end feed or floating canopy).
    - Lock out the circuit(s) and verify dead (live-dead-live) at the feed.
    - Remove the cover and check for loose, burned or backed-out conductors and a feed not fully
      seated in the track.
    - Re-terminate or replace the feed per Lesson 3.
15. **Restore power and verify**: every head lights, intermittent heads stay on when gently
    moved, and there is no buzzing or heat at the feed or connectors.

> **Safety:** Lock out the circuit before opening a feed, canopy or connector. Remove heads from
> the track with the head switched off or the track de-energized, and let lamps cool. If the
> breaker trips when power is restored, do not reset it repeatedly — find the cause or write it up.

## Symptom Guide

| Symptom | Check first |
|---|---|
| One head out, others fine | Lamp, head switch, circuit selector, adapter contact, move the head |
| Head flickers or drops out | Adapter seating, worn adapter contacts, broken wire at swivel (wiggle test), dimmer compatibility (LED) |
| All heads past a joint dead | Connector |
| Entire track dead | Breaker, switch/dimmer, live-end feed/canopy connections |
| CMH head cycles on and off | Lamp end of life, overheating, wrong lamp/ballast wattage |
| Breaker trips when heads added | Overload — calculate load (Lesson 3) |
| Scorch marks on track or adapter | Poor contact arcing — replace damaged parts; write up track if damaged |

## When to Write It Up

- Breaker trips on restoring power or repeatedly under normal load.
- The feed's junction box, the branch-circuit wiring or the track channel is heat-damaged.
- The track needs a new circuit, a new box location, or the track is installed in a location it
  is not listed for.
- The load exceeds the circuit and the fix requires circuit changes.

## Ordering Track Parts

Compatibility is everything. Before ordering, record:

| Part | Must match |
|---|---|
| Track section | Brand, **system type (H/J/L)**, single/2/3-circuit, color/finish, length |
| Connectors, feeds, dead ends | Same brand and system type; number of circuits; finish |
| Heads | Track type, voltage, source (LED/CMH/halogen/LV), wattage, CCT, CRI, beam angle, dimming type, finish |
| Lamps | Base, shape, wattage, beam angle, voltage (120 V vs 12 V) |
| Ballasts (CMH) | Lamp wattage and type, input voltage, the head maker's part number |
| Transformers (LV heads) | Magnetic vs electronic, VA, the head maker's part number |

Photograph the head label, the adapter and the track end. When a customer asks to "match" an
existing run, get catalog numbers rather than relying on appearance — heads from different
systems can look identical.

## Key Takeaways
- Follow the company order: good lamp → power at sockets → head switch, track connection, move the head → continuity (incan/halogen only) → ballast on ballasted heads → entrance fitting when the whole track is out.
- Check circuit selectors on multi-circuit track before condemning a head.
- Continuity tests are done with the head removed from the track; energized socket testing is for qualified persons only.
- Heads dead beyond a joint point to the connector; a whole dead track points to the feed.
- Order by system type and catalog number — never by appearance.
