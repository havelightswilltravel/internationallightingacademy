## What a Technician Does With Track

Lighting technicians commonly relamp and re-aim heads, replace failed heads, add or move heads,
replace connectors and dead ends, and re-terminate a live-end feed or floating canopy **on an
existing, identified circuit** after lockout. New branch circuits, new junction boxes and changes
to the circuit are electrician work unless your company has qualified you.

## Feeding a Track

| Feed method | Where it connects | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Live-end feed | End of a run, over a junction box | Most common |
| Floating canopy | Anywhere along the track, over a box | Allows the box to be away from the track's end |
| Mid-run or "T" feed | Middle of a run or at a junction | Splits long runs |
| Cord-and-plug kit (where listed, residential) | Receptacle | Not for commercial permanent installs unless the system is listed for it |

### Re-terminating a live-end feed (on an existing circuit)

1. Identify the circuit feeding the track and every circuit (on multi-circuit track, all of them).
2. **Lock out and tag out** each circuit.
3. At the feed, **verify absence of voltage** on every conductor with a tested meter
   (live-dead-live), including between each hot and the neutral, each hot and ground, and
   neutral and ground.
4. Remove the feed cover and inspect: discolored insulation, loose screws, burned terminals,
   cracked housings. A heat-damaged feed is replaced, not repaired.
5. Land conductors per the feed's markings — **hot to the hot terminal, neutral to the neutral
   (polarity matters: the neutral is the conductor that goes to the screw shell of a lamp socket),
   ground to the ground screw**. Multi-circuit feeds have one terminal per circuit.
6. Torque terminals to the maker's values where given.
7. Make sure the feed is fully seated into the track and the track is secured to the structure
   per the instructions (spacing of mounting clips/screws, toggles in drywall, or attachment to
   framing).
8. Reinstall the cover, remove locks per procedure, restore power and test every head.

> **Safety:** Treat every track as energized until you have locked it out and verified dead.
> Exposed track conductors are **live along the whole length** of the slot, including the far end
> if the dead end cap is missing. Do not insert tools or fingers into the slot.

## Connectors and Dead Ends

- Every joint is a potential failure point. If heads beyond a connector are dead while heads
  before it work, suspect the connector.
- On multi-circuit track, connectors must keep each circuit's conductor (and polarity) lined up.
  A connector installed backward can swap circuits or polarity.
- Every unfed end needs a **dead end cap**. Do not leave a track end open.
- Field-cutting track: cut square, deburr, and install the dead end as the instructions require.
  Some systems require the conductors to be set back from the cut end.

## Load Limits

There are **three limits** to check, and the lowest one controls:

| Limit | Where to find it | Typical value |
|---|---|---|
| Track rating | Track label / catalog | 20 A per circuit (commercial line-voltage); confirm |
| Branch circuit | Breaker and conductor size | 15 A or 20 A |
| Dimmer or control rating | Device label | e.g., 600 W, 1000 W; LED derating |

Lighting that stays on for three hours or more is a **continuous load**. A practical rule used in
lighting work: keep continuous load at or below **80%** of the breaker rating (see LT2-C11).

### Example

A 120 V, 20 A branch circuit feeds a single-circuit track:

- 80% of 20 A = **16 A**; 16 A × 120 V = **1,920 W** maximum continuous load.
- The store wants 12 heads at 75 W halogen PAR = 900 W — OK.
- Later they add 10 more 150 W heads = 1,500 W, bringing the total to 2,400 W — **over** the limit.
- Converting to 15 W LED heads: 22 × 15 W = 330 W — well within the limit.

For heads with transformers, ballasts or drivers, use the **input** watts or VA on the head label,
not the lamp watts — losses are real.

> **Safety:** An overloaded track or circuit is a fire risk and will cause nuisance tripping. If a
> customer keeps adding heads, document the load calculation and recommend a fix (LED conversion,
> splitting heads between circuits, or a new circuit by an electrician).

## Supporting the Track

- Follow the manufacturer's spacing for supports; the track must not sag.
- Pendant and stem kits must be rated for the weight and attached to structure.
- Suspended ceiling installs need support independent of the ceiling grid unless the system is
  listed for grid mounting with the proper clips.

## Damp, Wet and Special Locations

Most track is listed for **dry locations only**. Do not install standard track in showers,
outdoors, over pools, or within reach of sinks unless the product is specifically listed for that
use. The NEC also restricts track from certain locations (for example, where it is likely to be
physically damaged, where concealed or run through walls, and within a defined zone around
bathtubs and showers); check the
AHJ-adopted edition and the product listing, and write it up if an existing install looks wrong.

## Key Takeaways
- Re-terminating a feed on an existing circuit is tech work after lockout and live-dead-live verification; new circuits and boxes are electrician work.
- Track slots are live along their entire length; every unfed end needs a dead end cap.
- Connectors are common failure points; heads dead beyond a connector point to it.
- Check track rating, branch circuit (80% for continuous load) and dimmer rating — the lowest limit controls.
- Use input watts/VA of heads with power components when totaling load.
