## What the NEC Is — and Isn't

The **National Electrical Code (NEC)** is NFPA 70, published by the National Fire Protection
Association and updated every three years (2017, 2020, 2023, 2026...). It is a **minimum
safety standard** for electrical installations. It is not a design manual or an
instruction book, and it does not become law until a state, county or city **adopts** it.

- The **Authority Having Jurisdiction (AHJ)** — usually the electrical inspector or building
  department — decides which edition applies, interprets it, and approves installations.
- Many jurisdictions adopt the NEC with local amendments, and many are one or two editions
  behind. **The edition adopted by your AHJ governs**, not the newest book on the shelf.
- This program references the **2023 NEC** unless noted.

Lighting technicians are not expected to design to the code. You are expected to recognize
when work you are doing touches the code, to avoid creating violations, and to flag
conditions that look wrong. EA1 covers the NEC in depth; this lesson gives you the map.

## Structure of the Code

| Level | Example | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter | Chapter 2 — Wiring and Protection | Chapters 1–4 apply generally; 5–7 modify them for special conditions; 8 is communications; 9 is tables |
| Article | Article 210 — Branch Circuits | Each article covers one subject |
| Part | Part II of an article | Groups related sections |
| Section | 210.19 | Article number, period, section number |
| Subdivision | 210.19(A)(1) | Letters and numbers in parentheses |

Three kinds of text matter:
- **Mandatory rules** use "shall" or "shall not."
- **Exceptions** follow a rule and describe when it does not apply or applies differently.
- **Informational notes** explain or recommend; they are **not enforceable** on their own.
  The 3% voltage-drop recommendation you met in LT2-C01 is an informational note.

## Article 110 Highlights for Technicians

Several Article 110 rules affect almost every service call:

1. **Listed equipment shall be installed and used according to its listing and labeling
   instructions** (110.3(B)). This is why you must follow the retrofit kit's instructions
   exactly and why "it works" is not the same as "it's legal."
2. **Neat and workmanlike manner** (110.12). Inspectors can and do reject sloppy work —
   loose cables, unsupported whips, open knockouts.
3. **Electrical connections** (110.14). Terminals must be suitable for the conductor material
   (copper vs aluminum) and must be tightened properly. Temperature ratings of terminals
   limit which ampacity column you can use (next lessons).
4. **Working space** (110.26). Panelboards need clear working space. For systems up to 150 V
   to ground (such as 208Y/120 V), the minimum depth is generally 3 ft; for 151–600 V to ground
   (such as 480Y/277 V) it is 3 ft to 4 ft depending on what is across from the equipment. The
   space must be at least 30 in wide (or the width of the equipment) and 6½ ft high.
   **Do not store ladders, carts or materials in front of panels.**
5. **Arc-flash hazard warning** (110.16). Panelboards in other than dwelling units that are
   likely to be worked on while energized must be field-marked to warn of arc-flash hazard.

> **Safety:** If you arrive at a panel blocked by storage, clear it (or have the customer
> clear it) before working. Blocked working space is both a code violation and an escape
> hazard if something goes wrong.

## Finding an Answer: A Simple Method

1. **Use the index** at the back of the book with a keyword ("luminaire, tap conductors").
2. **Go to the article** and read the scope (first section) to confirm you are in the right
   place.
3. **Read the whole section**, including exceptions and informational notes.
4. **Check for modifications** in Chapters 5–7 if the location is special (wet, hazardous,
   healthcare, emergency systems).
5. **Ask your lead or the AHJ** when in doubt. Write down the section you looked at.

## Key Takeaways
- The NEC (NFPA 70) is a minimum safety standard; it becomes law only when adopted, and the AHJ's adopted edition governs.
- Structure: chapter → article → part → section → subdivision. "Shall" is mandatory; informational notes are not.
- Articles 110, 210, 240, 300 and 310 cover most of what an LT2 touches; 410 covers luminaires.
- Install listed equipment per its instructions (110.3(B)) and keep work neat and workmanlike.
- Keep panelboard working space clear — 3 ft minimum depth, 30 in wide, 6½ ft high in typical cases.
