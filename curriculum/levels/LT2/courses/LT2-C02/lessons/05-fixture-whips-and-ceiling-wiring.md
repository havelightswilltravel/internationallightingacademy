## What a Fixture Whip Is

A **fixture whip** is a short length of flexible wiring method — usually **flexible metal
conduit (FMC)** with conductors inside, or **MC cable** — that runs from a junction box to a
luminaire. Whips let fixtures in suspended ceilings be relocated and absorb vibration.

Manufactured whips come in standard lengths (commonly 6 ft) with connectors installed and
conductor tails stripped. Field-made whips are built from FMC or MC, listed fittings and
building wire.

### Key NEC limits for luminaire whips

| Item | Rule of thumb (verify in your adopted code) |
|---|---|
| Tap length | Luminaire tap conductors in raceway or MC/AC cable run from an outlet box to the luminaire; the NEC (410.117) sets this length at a minimum of 18 in and a maximum of 6 ft |
| 3/8-in FMC | Trade size 3/8 FMC is permitted for luminaire taps within that 6-ft limit |
| FMC as ground | FMC may serve as the EGC only when the total length in the ground return path is 6 ft or less, the circuit is 20 A or less, and listed fittings are used. **Company standard: always include a green EGC in every whip** |
| Conductor temperature | Use conductors rated for the temperature marked on the luminaire (often 90°C); recessed fixtures commonly require 90°C supply wire |
| Support | In an accessible ceiling, a short unsupported length (up to 6 ft) from the last point of support to the luminaire is permitted for FMC and MC — the rest of the run must be secured per the wiring method's article |

## Installing a Whip: Step by Step

1. **Plan.** Confirm the circuit, voltage and fixture type from the work order or lighting
   plan. Confirm the whip conductors suit the circuit (size, type, temperature rating).
2. **De-energize and verify.** Lock out the circuit. Open the junction box and verify absence
   of voltage on **every** conductor, including neutrals and any other circuits in the box
   (live-dead-live).
3. **Mount the box end.** Remove a knockout, install the whip connector, and tighten the
   locknut so it bites into the box. MC connectors must be listed for MC; insert the
   anti-short bushing ("red head") on AC cable and where required on MC per the manufacturer.
4. **Route the whip.** Keep it off the ceiling tiles' backs where practical, clear of hot
   surfaces, sharp edges and moving equipment. Do not tie or support it from the ceiling grid
   support wires or from other trades' pipe, duct or conduit.
5. **Connect at the fixture.** Remove the fixture's wiring-compartment cover, open a
   knockout, install the connector, and bring conductors into the compartment.
6. **Splice.** Hot to fixture black (or the driver input marked for the circuit voltage),
   neutral to white, EGC to the fixture's green screw or lead. On dual-voltage drivers
   ("120–277 V"), there is a single line lead; on older multi-tap ballasts, use the correct
   tap and cap unused taps individually.
7. **Close up.** Fold conductors neatly, replace the compartment cover, and replace the box
   cover.
8. **Support the fixture.** In a suspended ceiling, luminaires must be securely fastened to
   the ceiling framing members (grid) with listed clips or other approved means. Many
   specifications and seismic requirements also call for independent support wires from the
   fixture to the structure.
9. **Restore and test.** Remove your lock, energize, and confirm the fixture operates from its
   normal switch or control and that nothing else on the circuit changed.

> **Safety:** A junction box often contains several circuits and sometimes a shared neutral.
> Turning off the breaker for "your" fixture does not make the box safe. Test every
> conductor before touching, and treat any conductor you cannot verify as live.

## Ceiling Wiring Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Secure cable and raceway to structure or to independent support wires identified for that purpose | Lay cables on ceiling tiles or drape them over the grid |
| Keep wiring clear of the ceiling grid's own support wires unless independent support wires are installed | Support wiring from ducts, sprinkler pipe or other trades' supports |
| Maintain access to junction boxes | Bury boxes above hard ceilings without access |
| Close unused openings in boxes and fixtures | Leave knockouts open ("rodent highway" and fire path) |
| Keep the fixture's thermal clearance — respect "Type IC" vs "Non-IC" markings on recessed fixtures | Pack insulation against a non-IC recessed fixture |

The NEC requires that raceways, cable assemblies and boxes be securely fastened in place and
generally not supported by ceiling grid support wires unless those wires are independent and
identified for the purpose (Article 300).

## Common Problems Found During Service

- **Whip disconnected from the box** (connector pulled out) — ground path lost; reattach with
  a proper connector.
- **Missing fixture compartment cover** — conductors exposed to the ballast or driver's heat
  and to damage.
- **Connectors loose at the fixture knockout** — re-tighten; FMC grounding relies on tight
  fittings.
- **Unlisted splices or tape-only splices** — replace with listed connectors.
- **Whip longer than 6 ft** or daisy-chained fixture to fixture beyond what the fixture is
  listed for — note and report. Only fixtures marked as suitable for through-wiring may be
  used as a raceway for circuit conductors.

## Key Takeaways
- Fixture whips (FMC or MC) connect a junction box to a luminaire; the NEC limits luminaire taps to 18 in–6 ft.
- Always pull a green EGC in the whip; FMC as an EGC has strict limits.
- Use conductors rated for the luminaire's marked temperature.
- Lock out and verify every conductor in a junction box — other circuits and shared neutrals are common.
- Secure fixtures to the ceiling grid with listed clips and don't support wiring from the grid's support wires or other trades' work.
- Only fixtures marked for through-wiring may carry other circuit conductors.
