## Run the Movie Before You Roll the Camera

Good technicians do the job twice: once in their head, and once with their hands. Before you start,
you should be able to **envision the task from start to finish**, like playing a movie of the whole
job: arriving, setting up, every step of the work, testing, cleanup, paperwork and leaving.

When you can see the whole job:

- You spot hazards **before** you are standing in them.
- You know what tools, equipment and material you need, so nobody makes trips back to the truck.
- You know the order of steps, so you do not have to undo work.
- You know what "done" looks like, so the job is right the first time.
- You know what you do **not** know, so you can ask before it matters.

When you cannot see the whole job, you are reacting instead of working. That is when people take
shortcuts, forget a step, or get caught off guard.

## How to Build the Movie

### 1. Read Everything You Were Given

Work order, project specifics sheet, survey, fixture schedule, manufacturer instructions, site notes,
photos from the last visit. Read them **at the shop**, not in the parking lot. Write down every
question you have.

### 2. Walk the Job

When you arrive, before you unload anything, walk the work area. Look for:

- **Where the work is:** which fixtures, how high, what is under them.
- **Access:** doors, parking, loading dock, elevator, stairs, attic hatch, roof access.
- **Hazards:** the public, traffic, other trades, power lines, wet floors, racking, heat, ceilings
  with insulation or debris.
- **Power:** where the panel is, what the panel schedule says, controls such as time clocks,
  photocells, sensors and contactors.
- **Space:** where to stage material, where the ladder or lift will go, where removed material and
  recycle boxes will go.
- **People:** who the site contact is, who else is working nearby, when the area is busiest.

### 3. Say the Sequence Out Loud

Break the job into steps from start to finish. For each step, attach three things:

- **The hazards and controls** (Lesson 3).
- **What you need** for that step: tools, equipment, material, people.
- **What has to be true before the next step** can start.

### 4. Find the Gaps

Go back through your sequence and look for the places where you said "and then somehow..." or "I think
we..." Those are your gaps. Each gap is a question to ask (Lesson 7).

## If You Can't See the Whole Job, Ask for the Full Scope

If you cannot see the whole job, or you do not know what the next step is, **ask your trainer for the
full scope of work** before you start. That is not admitting you are lost. It is how professionals
work.

Good ways to ask:

- "Before we start, can you walk me through the whole job, start to finish?"
- "What does done look like on this one?"
- "I've got it up to installing the fixture. What happens after that: do we test here, or wait until
  the whole row is in?"
- "Is there anything on this job that isn't on the work order?"
- "Where are we going after this?"

A trainer would much rather spend two minutes explaining the full job than spend twenty minutes
fixing a step you did out of order.

> **Safety:** If you do not know what the next step is, you do not know what the next hazard is either.
> Do not start a task you cannot see to the end. Ask first.

## Worked Example: Exterior Pole Fixture Replacement

Here is the movie a trainer would expect you to be able to tell for replacing a failed parking lot
pole head with a new LED fixture, working as a two-person crew with a lift operated by an authorized
crew member.

| # | Step | Hazards and controls | What you need | Before the next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check in with the site contact | Unexpected lot traffic: ask about deliveries and busy times | Work order, site contact name | Approval for the shutdown and the work zone |
| 2 | Walk the lot and the pole | Overhead lines, trees, curbs, slopes, soft ground | Flashlight, notebook | Lift path and setup spot chosen |
| 3 | Set up the work zone | Traffic and pedestrians: cones, vehicle placement, beacons, work lights, hi-vis | Cones, caution tape, work lights | Zone closed, ground person in place |
| 4 | Find and lock out the circuit | Shock, time clock or photocell: lock out, check controls | Panel schedule, lock, tag, meter, gloves | Circuit locked out |
| 5 | Inspect and position the lift | Tip-over, overhead lines: pre-use inspection, path walk, spotter, 10 ft from lines | Lift, outrigger pads if needed | Lift set and level |
| 6 | Verify dead at the fixture | Shock, HID capacitor: live-dead-live at the fixture | Meter, gloves, glasses | Verified dead |
| 7 | Remove the old head | Dropped parts, weight: tool lanyards, nobody under the platform, lower in the platform | Hand tools, line or space on the platform | Old head on the ground |
| 8 | Install the new head | Pinch points, wiring errors: follow the instructions, correct connectors, seal against water | New fixture, connectors, instructions | Mounted, wired, secure |
| 9 | Restore and test | Re-energizing while someone is exposed: all clear, remove lock, test (cover the photocell if needed) | Photocell cover or the control override as directed | Light works |
| 10 | Tear down and clean up | Traffic again: keep the zone closed until everything is packed | Recycle space for the old head | Lot open |
| 11 | Close out | Missed paperwork: photos, counts, notes, tell the site contact | Work order, phone | Signed off |

If you could tell this movie before you parked, you would have the cones, the work lights, the
photocell cover and the right connectors on the first trip. You would know the time clock might turn
the lights back on. You would know your job as ground person is traffic and staging, and you would
already be thinking about the old head going in the truck.

## Common Gaps New Techs Miss

- **The end of the job.** Testing, cleanup, recycling, photos, paperwork and telling the site contact
  are part of the job, not extras.
- **Controls.** Time clocks, photocells, sensors and contactors that can turn lights back on or make a
  good fixture look bad.
- **Where things go.** Where old fixtures, packaging and recycle go, and how they get back to the
  shop.
- **Access.** Keys, roof hatches, lift entrance width, freight elevator size, parking for the lift
  trailer.
- **The people.** Who needs to know before the lights go off, and who signs off after.
- **What changes during the job.** Opening time, shift change, deliveries, weather, darkness.

> **Safety check:** Pick a task on tomorrow's work order. Write the steps from parking to driving away.
> Next to each step, write the main hazard and the highest control you can use. If any step has a
> hazard with only PPE next to it, ask yourself whether a barricade, a different piece of equipment or
> a change in timing could do better.

> **Think ahead:** For the same task, add a column: "What I need staged before this step." Then load
> the truck from that column. Most trips back to the shop happen because nobody ran the movie all the
> way to the end.

## Key Takeaways
- Envision every task from start to finish before you start: arrival, setup, each step, testing, cleanup, paperwork and leaving.
- Read everything at the shop, walk the job before unloading, and say the sequence out loud.
- For every step, know the hazards and controls, what you need and what must be true before the next step.
- If you cannot see the whole job or do not know the next step, ask your trainer for the full scope of work before you start.
- If you do not know the next step, you do not know the next hazard.
