## Everything at Once

You have learned the pieces one at a time:

- Your evolution is in your hands (Lesson 1).
- Safety is the foundation (Lesson 2).
- Hazards and the hierarchy of controls for every task (Lesson 3).
- Stop, call and improve the training when you are not trained (Lesson 4).
- See the whole job before you start (Lesson 5).
- Think seven steps ahead, with no idle hands (Lesson 6).
- Questions are your second foundation (Lesson 7).
- Step back, check course and keep the pace (Lesson 8).
- Never be caught off guard (Lesson 9).

On a real day, you do all of them at once, over and over. This lesson walks through one day for an LT2
technician working with a trainer, so you can see how the pieces fit. The jobs and places are made up
for teaching, but every situation is one our crews have faced.

## The Night Before

Your supervisor sends tomorrow's work orders: a relamp and ballast check in an open retail store in the
morning, a parking lot pole light at dusk, and a recessed fixture replacement reached from an attic at
a small office in between.

You read all three work orders and run the movie for each one (Lesson 5). Your question list:

- Store: Can any of the work be done before opening? Is there a lift on site, or ladders only?
- Office: How do we get into the attic? How hot is it expected to be?
- Parking lot: Is the lift on the trailer, or is the bucket truck coming? Who is operating it?
- All three: Is there anything on these I haven't been trained to do?

You text the questions to your trainer, set your alarm, and pack water and food. You check yourself:
rested, nothing that would slow you down. Fit for duty.

## At the Shop

You arrive early, and you are working at start time. Your trainer answers your questions: the store
will let you start one hour before opening in the front aisles; the attic is accessed from a scuttle
hole in a closet; the lift is a towable boom lift that your trainer will operate, and you are the ground
person.

You do the vehicle walk-around, then load from your "what I need" list: ladders, lamps, ballasts, the
lockout kit and meter, cones and caution tape, work lights, recycle boxes, a walk board for the attic,
the fan, the photocell cover. You check the trailer hitch, chains and lights. You secure the load.

> **Think ahead:** The pole job is last, after dark. That means the work lights and photocell cover must
> be on the truck now, and the lift battery or fuel must be checked now, not at 6 pm in a parking lot.

## Job 1: Relamp and Ballast Check in an Open Store

**Pre-task brief (you lead it, your trainer listens):**

- **Task:** relamp 12 troffers in aisles 1 to 3 and check two fixtures for failed ballasts.
- **Sequence:** check in with the manager, walk the aisles, barricade aisle 1, lock out, verify,
  relamp, check ballasts, test, move to aisle 2.
- **Hazards and controls:** public under the ladder: **elimination** by doing aisles 1 and 2 before
  opening; **engineering** barricades for aisle 3 after opening. Shock: **elimination** by lockout at the
  breaker, live-dead-live with gloves. Fall: platform ladder (**substitution**), three points of
  contact. Broken lamps: box them right away, broken lamp procedure if needed. Ballasts without a "No
  PCBs" label: treat as PCB, separate and count.
- **Training and authorization:** both of us trained on relamping and ballast replacement; no lift.
- **Material:** 24 lamps plus spares, two ballasts, recycle boxes.
- **Next steps:** after aisle 3, recycle boxed and loaded, then the office.
- **When we stop:** if a ballast is leaking, if the manager wants work over customers, or if we find
  something we aren't trained for.

**During the work:**

- While your trainer relamps the first fixture, you have the next ladder set at fixture 2 and the
  lamps unboxed on the cart (**no idle hands**).
- After the first fixture, you both step back and look from the floor: one lamp is not seated right.
  Fixed now, before it repeats (**step back**).
- You ask: "This ballast has no label at all. Treat it as PCB?" Answer: yes. You knew it, but you
  asked to verify (**questions**).
- Your trainer moves quickly between fixtures. You keep up, carrying the next box of lamps as you go,
  but you set up each ladder at a normal careful pace (**pace, after the gate**).
- At 9:55 you notice employees unlocking the front doors (**awareness**). You tell your trainer, check
  the barricades on aisle 3, and you both agree to finish the fixture in hand and move the remaining
  aisle 2 work to after the barricade is up.

## Job 2: The Attic

**Pre-task brief:**

- **Task:** replace one failed recessed housing above the conference room, reached from the attic.
- **Hazards and controls:** heat: **elimination** by checking whether the housing can be replaced from
  below first; it can't, so go now while it is still morning; **engineering** fan and work light;
  **administrative** water, short work periods, your trainer at the scuttle checking on you. Falling
  through the ceiling: **engineering** walk board across the joists. Shock: lockout and live-dead-live at
  the fixture. Nails, trusses, insulation: look before you move, hard hat, long sleeves, gloves, glasses,
  dust mask available.
- **When we stop:** suspected asbestos, droppings or nests, or anything we are not trained for.

**The change:** Halfway to the fixture with the flashlight, you see that the housing is not in the
open. It sits under loose, gray-brown, pebble-like insulation that looks like vermiculite, which can
contain asbestos. You have not been trained to work around it.

**What you do (Lesson 4):**

1. **Stop.** You do not touch or move the insulation.
2. **Make it safe.** You back out carefully on the walk board and close the scuttle. Power stays
   locked out until your supervisor says otherwise.
3. **Call.** "We're at the office for the conference room housing. The housing is buried in what looks
   like vermiculite insulation. I'm not trained to work around possible asbestos, and I haven't
   disturbed it. We've closed the attic. Do you want us to leave it locked out or restore it, and what
   do we tell the site contact?"
4. **Inform the site contact** with your supervisor's direction: the work is on hold until it can be
   assessed.
5. **Suggest training.** From the truck, you use the feedback/suggestion button in the training app:
   "Task: recessed housing from an attic. Ran into: suspected vermiculite. Suggest: a short lesson with
   photos of what vermiculite looks like and the exact steps to take when we find it."

The job is called with your supervisor's buy-off and documented on the work order. Nobody is upset.
That is the job done right.

## Job 3: Parking Lot Pole Light After Dark

**Pre-task brief:** You lead it again. The sequence is the one from Lesson 5. Your trainer adds one
thing you missed: the lot lights are on a contactor with a photocell **and** a time clock, so both must
be considered when you lock out and when you test.

**Your job as ground person:**

- Keep traffic and pedestrians out of the zone: cones, vehicle placed to shield the work, beacons,
  hi-vis vest.
- Watch the lift, the overhead lines and the platform. Nobody under it.
- Have the new fixture unboxed, the connectors ready and the lifting plan set before the old head comes
  down (**think ahead**).
- Build the space on the trailer for the old head before it reaches the ground.

**The change:** Twenty minutes in, the wind picks up and a light rain starts. You notice the trees
moving and call it out. Your trainer lowers the platform to check the wind against the lift's limit.
It is under the limit, but the rain makes the lot slick. You both step back: the fixture is mounted and
wired, only the final connections and test are left. You agree to finish carefully, with the platform
lowered between steps, and to stop if it gets worse (**step back, adjust, never caught off guard**).

After the test, the light works. You keep the zone closed until everything is packed, then open the
lot, take photos, and tell the site contact.

## End of the Day

- Recycle boxes labeled and loaded, PCB ballasts counted and kept separate.
- Truck cleaned out and restocked for tomorrow; one damaged cord tagged out and reported.
- Work orders completed with counts, photos, the attic hold and the reason.
- A quick talk with your trainer: "What would you have done differently?" Your trainer says you
  forgot the time clock at the lot. You write it in your notebook.
- At home: thirty minutes of study on emergency ballasts and time clocks, and three questions written
  down for tomorrow (**your evolution is in your hands**).

> **Safety check:** Look back at the day. In each job, find one hazard that was controlled by
> elimination, one by engineering, one by administrative controls and one by PPE. Then find the moment
> in each job where conditions changed and the plan had to change with them. That is what every day
> looks like.

## What Comes Next

The rest of LT2 teaches you new tasks: power systems, panels and meters, ballasts and drivers, LED
retrofits, troubleshooting and projects. In every one of those lessons, you will be asked to name the
hazards and controls, see the whole task, and think through the next steps before you start. That is
not extra. That is the job.

You will also be evaluated on two skills from this course:

- **LT2-S24, Lead a pre-task safety and planning brief for an assigned task.** You lead the brief, like
  the ones in this lesson, while an evaluator listens.
- **LT2-S25, Forecast and keep the crew productive on a live job.** An evaluator watches you on a real
  job: thinking ahead, staging, removing bottlenecks, asking questions, stepping back, keeping pace
  safely and reacting to a change.

## Key Takeaways
- A professional's day uses every habit at once: prepare the night before, brief every task, forecast, ask, step back, keep pace and stay aware.
- Lead the pre-task brief: task, sequence, hazards, controls in hierarchy order, training, material, next steps and when you would stop.
- When conditions change, step back and adjust; when you are not trained, stop, make it safe, call and suggest training.
- End every day clean, documented and with a lesson learned, then invest your own study time.
- Every task in the rest of LT2 builds on this foundation.
