## Why This Lesson Exists

Lessons 1 to 4 covered the retrofit products and how they are listed and wired. This lesson is
about **workmanship** - the install standards our company has added after seeing the same
problems in photos at our monthly meetings: fixtures screwed into bare drywall, fixtures off
center, exposed splices, a new fixture hanging by its wire nuts, ruined fixtures from wrong
voltage, and exterior upgrades nobody checked after dark.

> **Safety:** Every step in this lesson is done with the circuit locked out and verified dead
> (LT1-C06), except the voltage and function checks, which are taken by a qualified person in
> the PPE the company requires.

## Step 1: Measure Before You Mount

Before installing any replacement fixture or retrofit kit - and always on exterior jobs -
**measure the supply voltage** (L-N, L-L, L-G) and compare it with the new product's label
(LT2-C03). Our company has destroyed many new fixtures by assuming. If the reading does not match
the label, stop.

## Mounting in Drywall: The Company Procedure

Drywall (gypsum board) has almost no holding power for screws.

- **Never use self-tapping metal screws or wood screws in drywall alone.** They pull out.
- **Use toggle bolts in drywall, and get them snug - not tight.** If you use a drill or impact
  driver, use it only to bring the toggle close. **Do the final tightening by hand** until the
  fixture is flush with the ceiling, **then stop.** Tighten further only if the fixture is still
  loose. Overtightening crushes the gypsum and the toggle loses its grip.
- **Old or water-damaged drywall that crumbles will not hold even a toggle.** Fasten to the
  framing (joists or blocking) instead. Carry a stud finder; without one, you are guessing.
- **Support the fixture from the box or the structure** as the manufacturer and the NEC
  require (NEC Article 410, luminaire support; Article 314, boxes). **Conductors and wire nuts
  are never supports.** On one of our jobs, a fixture that had pulled loose was held up only
  because the wire nuts happened to be tight.

## Workmanship Rules

- **Wiring must be concealed** - every splice inside the box, the fixture wiring compartment or a
  raceway. Exposed splices and loose cable under a fixture are a safety issue, not a cosmetic
  one.
- **Center the fixture** on the box, the room feature or the old footprint. If it cannot be
  centered, **get approval before** you install it off center.
- **Straight rows.** Sales may write "one for one." If the existing fixtures are crooked and the
  new ones could easily go in a straight line, ask before you install.
- **Grid ceilings:** lift tiles with clean hands, do the dirty work, wipe the new fixtures, wash
  up, then replace tiles (LT1-C07).

## HID Conversions: Company Notes

Lesson 4 covered LED HID-replacement lamps. Our company's field notes add:

- **Metal halide ballasts are being phased out.** We are using up our HID ballast stock and will
  move to LED replacement lamps or new fixtures.
- **Fit matters.** Replacement lamps come in different body sizes (for example, sized for a
  BT-shape or a smaller ED-shape envelope). Choose the one the fixture can actually hold without
  blocking the optic.
- **Color-selectable lamps:** set and record the color temperature.
- **Customer standards rule.** Some customers do not yet allow ballast-bypass lamps in their
  exterior fixtures. Follow the customer's standard; if unsure, write it up.
- **Label every converted fixture** as required by the lamp's listing (Lesson 1).

When the answer is a **new exterior fixture**, write up: measured primary voltage; where the
fixture sits (property line or mid-lot, which decides the light distribution); pole shape (round
or square); whether a photocell is needed; and photos.

## Shunted vs Non-Shunted Sockets

Our meetings use the same picture every time: in a **shunted** socket the two contacts are
connected inside the socket, so current has one path; in a **non-shunted** socket each contact
has its own terminal. Single-ended ballast-bypass tubes need **non-shunted** sockets at the
powered end (Lesson 3). If you are not sure, test it - never guess.

## Exterior Upgrades: Controls and Function

- **Factory photocells on new fixtures:** many exterior LED fixtures ship with a photocell
  already installed. If the site is already controlled (time clock, contactor, building
  photocell), that **redundant control makes troubleshooting much harder** later. Company rule:
  **disconnect or remove the factory photocell** unless you are specifically told to keep it -
  and note it on the work order.
- **Test function after every exterior upgrade.** We always check interior upgrades; exterior
  ones get missed because the controls are hard to find. Operate the lights through their
  control (or a cover test on the photocell). **If you could not test function, say so on the
  work order.**
- **Watch for emergency units.** Exterior wall packs with battery backup must be replaced with
  emergency-rated fixtures - count them before the job is quoted.

## Key Takeaways

- Measure the supply voltage and compare it with the label before mounting anything.
- No self-tappers or wood screws in drywall; toggles snug, final turn by hand, framing if the drywall is weak.
- Support fixtures from boxes or structure - never from conductors or wire nuts.
- Conceal all wiring, center fixtures (or get approval), and question crooked "one-for-one" layouts.
- Choose HID replacement lamps that fit, set the color, follow customer standards and label conversions.
- Disconnect redundant factory photocells, test exterior function, and note it if you could not.
