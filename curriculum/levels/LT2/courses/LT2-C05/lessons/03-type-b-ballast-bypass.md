## What Changes in a Type B Conversion

In a Type B conversion you **remove (or disconnect) the ballast** and connect line voltage
— 120 V or 277 V — directly to the lampholders. The driver is inside the tube. This
eliminates ballast losses and ballast failures, but it means:

- **Line voltage is present at the sockets** whenever the circuit is on.
- The fixture **can no longer accept fluorescent lamps**.
- Socket type and wiring must match the specific tube exactly.

Type B is the conversion you will do most often at LT2, and it is skill LT2-S03.

## Single-Ended vs Double-Ended

| | Single-ended | Double-ended |
|---|---|---|
| Power connection | Line **and** neutral both connected at **one end** | Line at **one end**, neutral at **the other end** |
| Other end | Non-powered — mechanical support only (some designs) | Powered (neutral) |
| Powered-end socket | **Must be non-shunted** — the two contacts carry line and neutral separately | Often shunted or non-shunted per instructions, because both pins at each end carry the same conductor |
| Marking on tube | Powered end marked (e.g., "AC input this end") | Often marked L and N ends or "either end" per design |

Never mix single-ended and double-ended tubes in a fixture, and never guess — **the tube's
wiring diagram rules**. Some double-ended products require connection to only one pin per
end; others to both. Read the label on the tube and the instructions in the box.

> **Safety:** If a single-ended tube's powered end is installed in a **shunted** socket,
> line and neutral are shorted together through the socket. The result can be a tripped
> breaker, a burned socket, or an arc at the lampholder. Testing sockets before you wire is
> mandatory.

## Testing Sockets: Shunted or Non-Shunted?

1. Lock out and verify absence of voltage (you are already doing this — see the procedure).
2. Disconnect the socket's wires (or test a socket out of the fixture).
3. Set the meter to continuity.
4. Touch one probe to each of the socket's two contacts.
   - **Beep (continuity)** → **shunted**.
   - **No beep (OL)** → **non-shunted**.
5. Mark tested sockets or replace as needed.

Shunted and non-shunted sockets often look nearly identical. Clues (such as one vs two wire
holes per side, or part numbers) help but are not proof. **Test.**

## Type B Conversion Procedure (Single-Ended Example)

1. **Prepare.** Confirm the tube model, its wiring diagram (single- or double-ended), voltage
   rating (most are 120–277 V), and the labels supplied. Have replacement non-shunted
   sockets of the right style on hand.
2. **Lock out and verify.** Lock out the circuit. At the fixture, verify absence of voltage on
   all conductors with a tested meter (live-dead-live). Watch for emergency or unswitched
   feeds and for other circuits passing through the fixture.
3. **Remove lamps and the wireway cover.** Dispose of fluorescent lamps as universal waste.
4. **Identify the supply conductors** (hot, neutral, ground) where they enter the fixture and
   the ballast leads going to the sockets.
5. **Remove the ballast.** Cut ballast leads close to the ballast or disconnect at
   connectors, leaving enough length on socket wires if you will reuse them. Check older
   ballasts for "No PCBs" labeling (LT1-C06). Removing the ballast entirely is preferred over
   leaving a disconnected ballast in the fixture; if the instructions allow leaving it, cap
   and secure all leads.
6. **Test and replace sockets as required.** For single-ended tubes, install **non-shunted**
   sockets at the powered end. Make sure the socket style fits the fixture's mounting.
7. **Wire per the diagram.**
   - Powered end: hot to one contact, neutral to the other contact of each powered socket,
     using the conductors and connectors specified.
   - Non-powered end: leave unwired unless the instructions say otherwise.
   - Use listed connectors; no exposed copper; pull-test every splice.
   - Keep conductors away from sharp edges and the wireway cover's edges.
8. **Ground.** Verify the fixture housing is connected to the EGC.
9. **Apply labels** exactly as instructed — typically a "modified luminaire / do not use
   fluorescent lamps" label and a "line voltage at lampholders / risk of shock" warning near
   the sockets, visible during relamping.
10. **Install tubes** with the powered end in the powered sockets. Close the wireway.
11. **Restore power and verify.** All tubes light; no flicker; fixture responds to its normal
    switch or control.
12. **Document.** Record the fixture location, tube model, and that a bypass conversion was
    performed.

Double-ended conversions follow the same steps, except line is wired to the socket(s) at one
end and neutral to the socket(s) at the other end, per the diagram.

## Relamping a Converted Fixture

Inserting or removing tubes in a Type B fixture with power on exposes you to line voltage —
for example, if one end is in an energized socket while you hold the other end. Many newer
tubes include pin-shock protection, but **company procedure is to lock out before relamping
any Type B fixture**.

## Common Mistakes Found on Inspection

| Mistake | Hazard |
|---|---|
| No labels applied | Next tech handles energized sockets; fluorescent lamps reinstalled |
| Shunted sockets on single-ended powered end | Short circuit / arcing at socket |
| Mixed wiring in one fixture (some positions bypassed, some on ballast) | Confusion, damaged lamps, shock hazard |
| Ballast left in place with exposed lead ends | Contact with energized leads, heat |
| Wrong tube type installed later (Type A in a bypassed fixture) | Lamp damage, possible failure |
| Emergency battery ballast left connected to bypassed sockets | Emergency function lost or damaged; possible hazard — see LT3-C04 |

## Key Takeaways
- Type B puts line voltage on the sockets; the fixture can never again take fluorescent lamps.
- Single-ended: line and neutral at one end — powered-end sockets must be non-shunted. Double-ended: line one end, neutral the other.
- Test sockets for continuity before wiring; looks are not proof.
- Lock out and verify, remove the ballast, wire exactly per the tube diagram, ground the fixture, apply every label.
- Lock out before relamping a Type B fixture, and don't leave fixtures partly converted.
