## What a Low-Voltage Lighting System Is

A **low-voltage lighting system** steps line voltage (120 V or 277 V) down to **12 V or 24 V**
with a transformer and runs the lamps at that lower voltage. You will find low-voltage lighting
in recessed downlights and display cases, under-cabinet and cove lighting, monorail and cable
systems, retail and hospitality accent lighting, and landscape lighting.

Why use it? Small lamps with tight, precise beams (the classic MR16), slim fixtures, and — for
landscape and cable systems — the safety and installation advantages of Class 2 circuits.

The trade-off: at low voltage the **current is high** for the same wattage, so connections, cable
size and voltage drop matter much more (Lesson 2).

| Power | At 120 V | At 12 V |
|---|---|---|
| 50 W | 0.42 A | 4.2 A |
| 300 W | 2.5 A | 25 A |

## Common Low-Voltage Lamps and Bases

| Lamp | Description | Base | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MR16** | 2-inch multifaceted reflector, halogen or LED | GU5.3 (bi-pin, 5.3 mm) or GX5.3 | Recessed, track, display, landscape |
| **MR11** | 1⅜-inch reflector | GU4 or GZ4 (4 mm bi-pin) | Small accent fixtures, cases |
| **PAR36** | 4½-inch sealed beam, screw terminals | Screw terminals or spade lugs | Landscape, older display and track |
| **G4** | Tiny bi-pin capsule | G4 (4 mm pins) | Under-cabinet, puck lights, cases |
| **GY6.35** | Larger bi-pin capsule | GY6.35 (6.35 mm, thicker pins) | Task lights, pendants, cable systems |
| **AR111** | 111 mm reflector | G53 | High-end display/retail |

Notes for the field:

- Base names describe pin spacing in millimeters (GU5.3 = 5.3 mm). A G4 lamp will not fit a
  GY6.35 socket and vice versa.
- MR16 and similar lamps come in **narrow spot to wide flood** beams (for example 10°, 24°, 36°,
  60°). Match the beam angle when relamping; the wrong beam ruins a display.
- Halogen capsules (G4, GY6.35) must not be touched with bare fingers — skin oil shortens life.
  Handle with a clean cloth or gloves.
- Check whether the fixture is rated for **open** lamps or requires a lens.

## Transformers

### Magnetic transformers

- Iron core and copper windings; heavy; may hum.
- Output is a true sine wave at 12 V (or 24 V).
- Very tolerant of light loads and of long secondary runs; often adjustable by multiple taps on
  landscape units.
- Dimmed with a **magnetic low-voltage (MLV)** dimmer.
- Fail by: open winding (no output), thermal protector tripping from overload, buzzing.

### Electronic transformers

- Small, light, quiet; really a high-frequency power supply.
- Often have a **minimum load** (e.g., 20 W) as well as a maximum; below minimum they may not
  start or may flicker.
- Limited **secondary lead length** (often a few feet to the lamp) because of high-frequency
  output — check the label.
- Dimmed with an **electronic low-voltage (ELV)** dimmer (reverse-phase/trailing-edge) unless the
  label says otherwise.
- Fail by: internal electronic failure (no output), shutdown from overload or overheating,
  flicker with incompatible lamps.

### LED drivers are not transformers

A **constant-voltage LED driver** (12 or 24 V DC) used for tape light looks similar but outputs
**DC**. Halogen lamps can run on it, but many electronic transformers will not reliably run LED
lamps. Never substitute without checking the label.

## Remote vs Integral Transformers

| Arrangement | Description | Service notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Integral** | Transformer built into the fixture housing, track adapter or head | Replace the fixture's transformer with the maker's part, or the fixture |
| **Remote** | Transformer mounted away from the lamps — above a ceiling, in a cabinet, outdoors on a post or wall | Must stay accessible; one transformer may feed several fixtures; voltage drop over secondary runs |

> **Safety:** The **primary** side of every transformer is line voltage (120 V or 277 V). Lock out
> and verify absence of voltage (live-dead-live) before touching primary connections. A remote
> transformer above a ceiling may be on a different circuit than you expect — trace it.

## Reading a Transformer Label

| Label item | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Input | 120 V AC, 60 Hz, 1.0 A | Primary voltage and current |
| Output | 12 V AC, 300 VA | Secondary voltage and capacity |
| Min/Max load | 20–60 W | Electronic units: stay inside this range |
| Type | Magnetic / Electronic | Determines dimmer type |
| Dimming | MLV / ELV / non-dim | Dimmer to use |
| Class 2 | "Class 2 transformer" mark | Output limited for Class 2 circuits |
| Thermal protection | "Thermally protected" | Shuts off when overheated |

## Key Takeaways
- Low-voltage systems run lamps at 12 or 24 V from a transformer; current is about 10× higher than at 120 V for the same watts.
- Know the common lamps and bases: MR16 (GU5.3), MR11 (GU4), PAR36, G4, GY6.35.
- Magnetic transformers are heavy, tolerant and use MLV dimmers; electronic transformers are compact, have minimum loads and lead-length limits, and usually use ELV dimmers.
- Transformers may be integral to the fixture or remote; the primary is always line voltage.
- Read the label for input, output VA, min/max load, type and dimming before replacing anything.
