## Why This Matters

For years our company's supervisors wrote a **load list** for every project: what goes on the vehicle
for that job. Those lists were written for real jobs, so no one list fits the next job. What they show
is the **general needs** of a project: ladders, connectors, rags, recycle containers, tape, a cart and
trash cans, plus extra items for each type of work. The library guide *Project Load Lists: What to
Bring* collects those general needs.

The point of this lesson is that you should be able to plan and load for a project **without being
told**. A forgotten box of disconnects, a missing ladder or a vehicle that is too small costs a trip
back to the shop, or a crew standing around on the first night.

## Plan From Size, Job Type and Specifics

Three things tell you what to bring:

1. **Project size.** Small (a few days), medium (up to a few weeks) or large (takes months). The
   longer the job, the more supplies, material and recycle you handle.
2. **Job type.** A full fixture change-out, an LED kit retrofit, exterior and pole work, an LED canopy,
   cooler and case lighting, high ceilings and lifts, or an out-of-town run. Each adds its own items;
   a job with more than one type of work gets the items for each.
3. **Project specifics.** The **project specifics sheet** (Lesson 2) and the survey give the scope,
   ceiling heights, lifts, how material gets to the site, who supplies what, seismic or wiring
   requirements, and how recycling is handled. Fixtures, kits, drivers and lamps come from the
   supervisor's instruction and the service order.

Build the load in layers: the **core kit** for every project, then what the **size** needs, then the
items for each **job type**. The core kit is 6 ft and 8 ft ladders, wire nuts and lever-nut
connectors / in-line disconnects, rags, drop cloths, a vacuum (or the site's, if allowed), a cart,
trash cans with wheels, garbage bags, tape gun and tape, recycle containers, a marker, shrink wrap,
cones and caution tape, and a tablet with the recaps and timecards loaded. Cleaner and spray bottles
come only when cleaning is in scope. Plus your own drill, bits, meter and lockout kit, **for every
person**.

Each job playbook in the library ends with a short load list for that job type.

### Change-out or retrofit?

Most of today's projects are **full fixture change-outs**. Some still require **retrofitting**
(LED kits, or ballasts and sockets/lampholders). Bring retrofit parts when the project specifics call
for them; leave them off when they do not, and ask if you are unsure.

## The Vehicle and Trailer

Know early whether the job needs a **project-style vehicle or a trailer**, and arrange it with your
supervisor before load day. Ask: is there room for the ladders (and are they strapped)? Is there a lift
to tow? How much material goes out, and how much recycle comes back? Old fixtures and lamps often take
more room than the new material.

## Scaling Quantities

Work quantities out from the survey instead of copying the last job's numbers.

| Item | How to work it out |
|---|---|
| Recycle containers | Check how many lamps each container holds, then lamps / capacity. Round **up**. |
| In-line disconnects | One per fixture where the sheet calls for them, plus a few spares |
| Wire nuts / lever-nut connectors | Splices per fixture (from the instructions) x fixtures, plus spares |
| Rags, garbage bags | More for a larger job; more again for lenses, reflectors and canopies |
| Ladders and lifts | By ceiling height in each area on the survey |
| Spares (connectors, drivers, sockets) | More on an out-of-town run, where you cannot run back to the shop |

Recycle containers come in many sizes. One common size holds about 50 four-foot lamps, but a smaller
box, 8 ft lamps or LED tubes change the number, so check the container you are taking.

> **Tip:** Write your working on the sheet ("720 lamps / 50 per container = 14.4, load 15"). If the
> supervisor checks the load, they can see how you got the number.

## Job Specifics to Watch For

- **New wiring runs.** When new fixtures and power are run, support the new MC cable and wiring so it
  does not lie on the ceiling tile. Bring clips and supports suited to the job and install them per
  code.
- **Seismic support.** Lay-in (T-grid) fixtures require earthquake (seismic) ties: a company rule. Lag
  bolts go into wood beams and the tie wires connect to them; a beam clamp can replace a lag in many
  situations; in some cases you may tie the wire directly to the beam. Requirements depend on the local
  building code and seismic area, so follow the project specifics and your supervisor. Bring seismic
  wire, and lags or beam clamps, as the job requires.
- **Lighting vans.** Van standard stock differs by location but typically includes a mix of
  fluorescent, CFL, LED and HID lamps, drivers, ballasts and sockets, a few LED fixtures, and local
  parts such as photocells, time clocks, electrical tape and screws. The shop does not pull those items
  again for van jobs; check that your van stock covers the work.

## Checking Off, Shortages and Substitutions

- **Check items off as they go on the vehicle,** not from memory afterwards.
- **Report shortages before you leave the shop.** If the shelf is short, call your supervisor while
  it can still be ordered or sent.
- **Ask before substituting.** A different connector, ladder or fastener may not be approved for the
  job.
- **Expensive material** (canopy fixtures, LED kits): count it when loading and again on site, handle
  it with care, and report damaged cartons with photos before leaving the shop.
- **Customer-supplied or staged material:** check the recap summary for what the customer supplies,
  and count delivered material on arrival.

## Safety Equipment Is on the List Too

Cones and caution tape, lift equipment (a spare plug for scissor-lift repairs), load binders and
chains, and ratchet straps for long ladders are part of the load. Secure every load, check the trailer
hitch, ball lock and chains, and on a rental make sure the spare key is mounted and its location
recorded.

## Recycling, Pallets and Photos

- **Reuse new-lamp boxes for old lamps.** Tape **both ends** closed, with **no lamps showing**, and label
  every box **OLD/USED LAMPS FOR RECYCLE**.
- **Keep all recycle together,** never mixed with good material. At the shop it goes to the area of the
  warehouse that management designates.
- **Ask before starting a new pallet.** Locations are sometimes combined on one pallet.
- **Shrink-wrap the pallet** and **note it on the ticket**.
- **Always photograph the recycle pallet.** Our company has been blamed for poor palletizing; your
  photo is the proof.

So the load must include the tape, the marker and the shrink wrap, even when the lamps are already on
site.

## Worked Example

*Example figures only (hypothetical).* A one-store LED kit retrofit, a small project (a few days):

- Sales floor: 200 troffers, 3 lamps each, 10 ft ceiling. Back room: 40 two-lamp strips, 16 ft
  ceiling, scissor lift on the sheet. In-line disconnects required on every fixture.
- Kits and drivers delivered to the site; you load everything else. The recycle containers on the
  shelf hold about 50 four-foot lamps each.

| Layer | What goes on | Working |
|---|---|---|
| Vehicle | Trailer for the scissor lift, arranged the week before | Lift needed in the back room at 16 ft |
| Core | 2 x 6 ft, 2 x 8 ft ladders; rags; drop cloths; cart; 2 trash cans; garbage bags; tape gun and tape; marker; shrink wrap; cones and caution tape; tablet | Core kit |
| Recycle containers | 14 | Lamps: 200 x 3 + 40 x 2 = 680. 680 / 50 = 13.6, round up to 14 |
| Disconnects | about 250 | 240 fixtures, one each, plus spares |
| Connectors | per the kit instructions, x 240, plus spares | Splices per kit x fixtures |
| Lift work | spare scissor-lift plug, binders and chains for the trailer | Back room at 16 ft |
| Not loaded | Fluorescent sockets and ballasts | Not in the project specifics |

At the shop the disconnect shelf has only 180. You call your supervisor **before leaving**, and
another 70 are sent with the kit delivery. On the last night, the old tubes go into the new-kit
boxes, taped and labeled, onto one shrink-wrapped pallet, photographed, and noted on the ticket.

> **Safety:** A full vehicle is heavy and loads shift. Ladders and long cartons must be strapped, and
> lifts chained and bound on the trailer. Never leave a lift or ladder unsecured to save time.

## Key Takeaways
- Plan the load from the project size, the job type and the project specifics; load without being told.
- Know early whether the job needs a project-style vehicle or a trailer, and arrange it.
- Build the load in layers: core kit, size, job type. Bring retrofit parts only when the job calls for them.
- Scale quantities from the survey: check each recycle container's capacity and calculate; one
  disconnect per fixture plus spares.
- Support new wiring off the ceiling tile, and follow the specifics for seismic ties on lay-in fixtures.
- Check items off as you load, ask before substituting, and report shortages before leaving the shop.
- Recycle lamps in taped, labeled boxes, kept together and apart from good material; ask before starting
  a new pallet; note it on the ticket and always photograph the pallet.
