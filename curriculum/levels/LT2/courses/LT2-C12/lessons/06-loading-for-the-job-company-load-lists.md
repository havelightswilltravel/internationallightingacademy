## Why Load Lists Exist

For years our company's supervisors wrote a **load list** for every project: what goes on the truck
for that job. Many of those lists are now in the library guide *Company Load Lists*, generalized and
with the customer details removed. They are from relamp work, which our company no longer does, but
most of what a crew needs has not changed: ladders, connectors, cleaning supplies, recycle boxes,
tape, carts and trash cans. The old fluorescent sockets and ballasts on those lists now come along
**only if the project specifics call for it**.

The point of this lesson is that you should be able to load for a project **without being told**. A
forgotten box of disconnects or a missing ladder costs a trip back to the shop, or a crew standing
around on the first night.

## The Loading Plan: Specifics Sheet Plus Load List

The **project specifics sheet** (Lesson 2) and the **load list** together are the job's loading plan.

- The **specifics sheet** tells you the scope, the site, ceiling heights, lifts, how material gets to
  the site, who supplies what, and how recycling is handled.
- The **load list** tells you what goes on the truck. Kits, fixtures, drivers and lamps come from the
  supervisor's instruction and the service order; the load list covers everything else.

Build the load in three layers:

1. **Core kit** for every project: 6 ft and 8 ft ladders, wire nuts and lever connectors, spray
   bottles, cleaner concentrate and the 5-gallon container, clean rags, drop cloths, vacuum (or the
   site's, if allowed), the cart, trash cans with wheels, garbage bags, tape gun and tape, recycle
   boxes, marker, shrink wrap, cones and caution tape, and a tablet with the recaps and timecards
   loaded. Plus your own drill, bits, meter and lockout kit, **for every person**.
2. **Size add-on**: small, standard or large project. The guide shows example quantities from past
   lists. They are examples, not standards; the survey sets the real numbers.
3. **Job-type add-ons** for each kind of work on the job: LED kit retrofit, fixture replacement with
   disconnects, exterior and pole work, LED canopy, cooler and case lighting, high ceilings and lifts,
   out-of-town travel, and material staged at the site or supplied by the customer.

Each job playbook in the library ends with a short load list for that job type that follows the same
layers.

## Scaling Quantities

The company's lists say it plainly: quantities are "per location, quantity per ballast". Work them out
from the survey instead of copying last job's numbers.

| Item | How to work it out |
|---|---|
| Recycle boxes | Project lamps / 50 = boxes needed. Round **up**. |
| In-line disconnects | One per fixture where the sheet calls for them, plus a few spares |
| Wire nuts / lever connectors | Splices per fixture (from the kit instructions) x fixtures, plus spares |
| Rags, garbage bags, spray bottles | More for a larger job; more again for lenses, reflectors and canopies |
| Ladders and lifts | By ceiling height in each area on the survey |
| Spares (connectors, drivers, sockets) | More on an out-of-town run, where you cannot run back to the shop |

> **Tip:** Write your working on the load list ("720 lamps / 50 = 14.4, load 15 boxes"). If the
> supervisor checks the truck, they can see how you got the number.

## Checking Off, Shortages and Substitutions

- **Check items off as they go on the truck,** not from memory afterwards.
- **Report shortages before you leave the shop.** If the shelf is short, call your supervisor while
  it can still be ordered or sent.
- **Ask before substituting.** A different connector, ladder or fastener may not be approved for the
  job.
- **Lighting vans:** standard van stock (wire nuts, standard sockets, ladders) is not pulled again by
  the shop when techs go out in vans. Check that your van stock covers the job.
- **Expensive material** (canopy fixtures, LED kits): count it when loading and again on site, handle
  it with care, and report damaged cartons with photos before leaving the shop.
- **Customer-supplied or staged material:** check the recap summary for what the customer supplies,
  and count delivered material on arrival.

## Safety Equipment Is on the List Too

Cones and caution tape, lift equipment (rope locks, a spare plug for scissor-lift repairs), load
binders and chains, and ratchet straps for long ladders are part of the load. Secure every load,
check the trailer hitch, ball lock and chains, and make sure the rental truck's spare key is mounted
and its location recorded.

## Recycling, Pallets and Photos

These rules come straight from the company's lists:

- **Reuse new-lamp boxes for old lamps.** Tape **both ends** closed, with **no lamps showing**, and label
  every box **OLD/USED LAMPS FOR RECYCLE**.
- **Separate pallet.** Lamps and batteries for recycling go back on a **separate pallet**, never mixed
  with good material.
- **Ask before starting a new pallet.** Locations are sometimes combined on one pallet.
- **Shrink-wrap the pallet, call the office from the site** to arrange pickup, and **note it on the
  ticket**.
- **Always photograph the recycle pallet.** Our company has been blamed for poor palletizing; your
  photo is the proof.

So the load must include the tape, the marker and the shrink wrap, even when the lamps are already on
site.

## Worked Example

*Example figures only (hypothetical).* A one-store LED kit retrofit:

- Sales floor: 200 troffers, 3 lamps each, 10 ft ceiling. Back room: 40 two-lamp strips, 16 ft
  ceiling, scissor lift on the sheet. In-line disconnects required on every fixture.
- Kits and drivers delivered to the site; you load everything else.

| Layer | What goes on the truck | Working |
|---|---|---|
| Core | 2 x 6 ft, 2 x 8 ft ladders; spray bottles, concentrate, 5-gallon container; rags; drop cloths; cart; 2 trash cans; garbage bags; tape gun and tape; marker; shrink wrap; cones and caution tape; tablet | Standard core kit |
| Recycle boxes | 14 boxes | Lamps: 200 x 3 + 40 x 2 = 680. 680 / 50 = 13.6, round up to 14 |
| Disconnects | about 250 | 240 fixtures, one each, plus spares |
| Connectors | per the kit instructions, x 240, plus spares | Splices per kit x fixtures |
| Lift work | rope lock, spare scissor-lift plug, binders and chains if the lift is towed | Back room at 16 ft |
| Not loaded | Fluorescent sockets and ballasts | Not in the project specifics |

At the shop the disconnect shelf has only 180. You call your supervisor **before leaving**, and
another 70 are sent with the kit delivery. On the last night, the old tubes go into the new-kit
boxes, taped and labeled, onto one shrink-wrapped pallet, photographed, with the pickup call noted
on the ticket.

> **Safety:** A full truck is heavy and loads shift. Ladders and long cartons must be strapped, and
> lifts chained and bound on the trailer. Never leave a lift or ladder unsecured to save time.

## Key Takeaways
- The project specifics sheet plus the load list is the job's loading plan; load without being told.
- Build the load in layers: core kit, size add-on, job-type add-ons.
- Scale quantities from the survey: recycle boxes = lamps / 50, rounded up; one disconnect per fixture
  plus spares.
- Check items off as you load, ask before substituting, and report shortages before leaving the shop.
- Recycle lamps in taped, labeled boxes on a separate, shrink-wrapped pallet; ask before starting a new
  pallet; call the office, note it on the ticket and always photograph the pallet.
