## What the Sheet Is

The **project specifics sheet** is the one- or two-page set of instructions that goes with every
project service order. It tells the crew what is special about *this* job: the site, the customer's
expectations, the logistics and the reporting. It travels with the **survey** (the room-by-room
fixture count and scope) and the **service order** (the material and labor record you will update
and get signed).

The same sections show up on our company's sheets year after year, because the same things go
wrong when they are missed. Below is each one and why it matters. A blank fill-in version is the
library guide **Project Specifics Sheet Template**.

## 1. Job Header and Crew Leader
**What it says:** job name, service order, dates, and a blank: *"Crew leader ______ to complete the
QC checklist per supervisor's instruction, or no less than once per day."*
**Why it matters:** one person owns communication, paperwork and quality. If nobody's name is on
that line, everybody assumes someone else is calling the office.

## 2. Scope and Survey
**What it says:** "Follow the survey and do only the areas listed unless otherwise noted." "Make
changes to the survey if quantities or fixture types differ, or if special equipment is needed."
"Make changes to the totals on the service order if the amount you install differs."
**Why it matters:** the survey is what was sold. Work outside it is unpaid; work missing from it
causes shortages. Correcting it as you go warns the office before the last night.

## 3. Site Contact and Game Plan
**What it says:** "Speak to the manager before starting to come up with a game plan: where to start,
where the high-traffic areas are, where to leave removed material."
**Why it matters:** the customer knows things the survey does not: a room that is busy until noon,
a vault that can only be opened with a manager present, a sales floor that has to look normal by
opening time.

## 4. Hours, Access and Security
**What it says:** start and stop times, days, how you get in and how you leave. Past sheets covered
night-only work so the crew would not block fuel pumps or parked cars, locking up and setting the
alarm after hours, and giving security days of notice to block off parking stalls.
**Why it matters:** access problems waste whole shifts. Leaving a building unlocked or setting off
an alarm can lose a customer.

## 5. Protecting the Site
**What it says:** cover desks, computers, machinery, appliances and open food with plastic; clean up
spills right away; keep areas neat; use the site's vacuum where allowed; clean thoroughly at the end
of every shift.
**Why it matters:** you are working over someone's business. Dust on a keyboard or debris in a food
case is what the customer will remember.

## 6. Cleaning and Finish Rules
**What it says:** clean reflective surfaces and prismatic lenses with cleaner; **dry wipe only** on
parabolic louvers and downlight cans; never leave fingerprints; wear clean gloves when handling new
parts. On exterior LED jobs, scrape old gasket and rust from the housing before installing.
**Why it matters:** the wrong cleaner leaves streaks on specular surfaces, and fingerprints on a new
reflector look like sloppy work from the floor.

## 7. Tools and Equipment
**What it says:** special items in capital letters, such as *"You will need drills for each person
on the crew."* Lift types, whether a lift can use the elevator, where it is stored on site, and shop
equipment (carts, trash cans, ladders) that must be checked out.
**Why it matters:** one missing drill can idle a tech for a whole shift.

## 8. Material Delivery, Staging and Counts
**What it says:** whether material ships to the site or goes on the truck, where it can be stored,
and that you must count it against the order and photograph anything missing or damaged right away.
**Why it matters:** a shortage found on day one can be reordered; one found on the last night means
a return trip.

## 9. Special Fixture Instructions
**What it says:** how to handle emergency fixtures (label and leave alone, rewire, or replace),
switching patterns such as checkerboard in two-switch gyms, guards or lenses over open food and in
coolers, a required manufacturer, how to secure kits that do not match the housings.
**Why it matters:** these change from job to job, so they are easy to get wrong. Never start cutting
out or adding emergency equipment until you have specific direction.

## 10. Wiring Details
**What it says:** install a disconnect on every new ballast or driver where required and record the
count; replace sockets as needed, and get approval if you need more than you were sent with.
**Why it matters:** disconnects and extra parts are billable and are counted at closeout.

## 11. Recycling and Disposal
**What it says:** where removed lamps, ballasts and old fixtures go, how boxes are closed and
labeled, how PCB ballasts are separated and counted, whether the customer or our company recycles,
and what goes in the site dumpster.
**Why it matters:** recycling is regulated, and it is where crews most often get the company in
trouble. Company policy: all lamps are recycled, every removed magnetic ballast goes to the
recycler and is counted, and suspect PCB ballasts are kept separate (see LT1-C06 Lesson 5).

## 12. Production Expectation
**What it says:** how the job was bid, for example a number of fixtures or kits per tech per hour,
and what to do if you are not meeting it: step back, ask why, change your strategy or write down
the reasons.
**Why it matters:** the bid already assumed a pace. Falling behind quietly is how jobs lose money.

## 13. Reporting
**What it says:** who gets the daily recap, how and when; morning call-ins; time cards; travel
paperwork; photos. **Why it matters:** the office cannot fix what it does not hear about.

## 14. Be the Professional
**What it says:** *"Our company is the lighting professional on the job."* Write up anything that
would make the job look unprofessional when done, even if it is outside the scope: yellowed or
broken lenses, missing guards, damaged fixtures, objects blocking the light, fingerprints.
**Why it matters:** the customer judges the whole room, not just the fixtures you touched.

## 15. Vehicle and Shop Return
**What it says:** keep the project vehicle clean as you go; at the end, clean out the vehicle and
trailer and return all equipment.

> **Safety:** Anything on the sheet about hours, travel limits and driver logs is a safety rule, not
> paperwork. Long shifts plus long drives cause crashes. Follow the work-hour and driving limits on
> your sheet and in company policy.

## Key Takeaways
- The sheet, the survey and the service order travel together. Read all three before you leave.
- The same sections repeat because the same things go wrong when they are missed.
- Stay within the survey, correct it when reality differs, and keep the service order totals current.
- Special fixture instructions (emergency, switching, guards, manufacturer) change from job to job.
- Recycling, counts and reporting are part of the job, not extras.
- Write up anything that would make the finished room look unprofessional.
