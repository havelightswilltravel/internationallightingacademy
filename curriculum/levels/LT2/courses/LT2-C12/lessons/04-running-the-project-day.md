## Kickoff Day

The first shift sets the tone for the whole job. A strong first shift on our company's projects
has looked like this:

1. **Check in with the site manager.** Introduce the crew, confirm start and stop times, and go over
   the logistics: where material and equipment are stored, where removed material goes, and any
   areas that are off limits. Write down the manager's name.
2. **Walk the job with the manager and take before photos.** Photograph each area as it is, including
   anything already damaged: broken lenses, stained tiles, dead fixtures, scuffed floors. Before photos
   protect the customer and our company if a question comes up later.
3. **Agree on the game plan.** Where to start, where the high-traffic areas are, what must look normal
   by opening time.
4. **Count the material.** Compare what is on site with the order. Photograph anything missing or
   damaged and send it to your supervisor **immediately**, not at the end of the shift.
5. **Stage the work.** Set up the staging area, carts, recycle boxes and trash cans before the first
   fixture comes down. If the vehicle was only needed to haul material and equipment, return it as
   directed and report directly to the site for the rest of the job.

## Organize the Crew for Flow

Projects are won or lost on flow: keeping everybody productive and nobody waiting. Ideas that worked
on past company projects:

- **Split dirty work from clean work.** On kit retrofits, have one or two people gut the old fixtures
  and others install the new kits. Gutting is dirty, and dirty hands leave fingerprints on new
  reflectors.
- **Feed the lifts.** On high ceilings, one person on the ground can keep two lifts working by keeping
  a stock of material on carts near each lift and taking away old material. Keep the lifts close
  enough that the ground person can serve both, but never working directly over each other.
- **Reassess when someone is waiting.** If the ground person cannot keep up, or the lifts keep waiting
  for material, stop and change the setup: reposition, add a cart, or drop to one lift.
- **Keep old material moving.** Set up an empty recycle box at the start and replace it before it
  overflows.

> **Safety:** Barricade under every lift and ladder. Lock out and verify absence of voltage
> live-dead-live on every circuit before working on conductors, and lock out again after every
> shutdown change. Never let a site manager, a schedule or a production target talk you into working
> hot. If the site requires a spotter or another rule stricter than ours, follow it; if you cannot,
> stop and call your supervisor. Nobody rides or moves a rolling scaffold with anyone on it.

## Track Production

Every project was bid with a production expectation, such as a number of fixtures or kits per
technician per hour. That number is meant to cover all your time on the clock, including setup,
travel, cleanup and paperwork, so the hands-on pace has to be higher than the bid number.

> **Example figures only:** The numbers below are made up for teaching. They are not our company's
> labor units, bid rates or piece rates. Use the figures on your own project specifics sheet.

*Example:* the sheet says the job was bid at 4 kits per tech per hour. A crew of three works an
8-hour shift, so the bid assumes about 3 x 8 x 4 = 96 kits per shift. Tonight the crew installed 70.
That is about 27% behind. If that repeats for four shifts, the crew falls more than a full shift behind.

When you are behind, our company's instruction has always been the same: **take a step back and ask
why.** Then either change the strategy or write down the reasons (obstructions, material not staged,
waiting on shutdowns, fixtures over partitions that need extra time) on a separate note and give it
to your supervisor after the shift. Some projects are bid or paid by the piece; the same habit applies.
Never make up the gap by skipping lockout, quality checks or cleanup.

## Quality Checks as You Go

- **The crew leader checks everyone's work** at least once per shift, using the QC checklist, and gets
  problems fixed right away, before more fixtures are done the same wrong way.
- **Step back and look from the floor.** After a few fixtures, look down the row: alignment, lens and
  door fit, color match between new and old, no gaps between lamps and sockets, no wires hanging out,
  no fingerprints.
- **Mount it right every time.** Every kit or fixture is secured per the manufacturer's instructions.
  Do not leave a fixture until you are sure it is secure.
- **Wire it neatly.** Splices belong inside the fixture wiring compartment or a box, with excess slack
  trimmed and dressed, never exposed (NEC 300.15).

## Document as You Go

Keep notes during the shift, not from memory afterward:
- Counts: fixtures completed by area, kits, drivers, disconnects and sockets used, extra screws or
  securing hardware and where it was used
- Survey changes: different quantities or types, areas needing special equipment
- Fixtures skipped and why, with a marked location (for example, fixtures over partitions that could not
  be reached safely, or emergency fixtures left for direction)
- Damage found or caused, with photos and the name of the manager you told
- Anything that would look unprofessional when the job is done

If you break something on site, tell a manager right away, get their name, photograph it and report it
to your supervisor.

## Customer Communication

Talk to the site contact at the start and end of each shift. Tell them what was done, what is next,
and anything you need from them. If they ask for extra work outside the survey, do not just do it:
write it down and pass it to your supervisor. If they ask you to do something you think is unsafe or
outside our agreement, stay polite, write down their name and request, and get your supervisor involved.

## End of Every Shift

- Box, close and tape removed lamps on both ends so no lamps show; remove guards from old lamps first.
- Keep PCB ballasts separate, count them, and record the count.
- Stack recycle material in the agreed area, or bring it back to the shop if the sheet says so.
- Clean up thoroughly: covers off, debris vacuumed, equipment stored out of the way.
- Confirm every circuit you worked is back on and the lights work.
- Send the daily recap by the method and time on your sheet: what was done, what remains, counts,
  problems, and how the shift went.

## Key Takeaways
- Kickoff day: check in, walk with before photos, agree on a game plan, count material, stage.
- Organize for flow: split dirty and clean work, feed the lifts, reassess when someone waits.
- Production targets cover all paid time; if you are behind, step back, ask why and report.
- The crew leader checks quality every shift; step back and look from the floor.
- Document counts, skips, damage and survey changes during the shift.
- End every shift clean, with recycling sorted, circuits restored and the recap sent.
