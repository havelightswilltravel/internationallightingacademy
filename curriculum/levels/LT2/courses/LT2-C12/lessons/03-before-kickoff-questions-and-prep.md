## The Goal: Nobody Has to Remind You

Our company's managers used to spend a lot of time reminding crews of the same things before every
project. The point of this lesson is to make those reminders unnecessary. If you can work through
the questions below on your own and bring your supervisor only the ones you cannot answer, you are
thinking like a project tech.

Use the library guide **Project Kickoff Checklist** on every job.

## Step 1: Read the Packet Like an Owner

Before you leave the shop, read the project specifics sheet, the survey and the service order
together. As you read, ask:

- **Does the survey match the material list?** Add up the fixtures on the survey and compare them
  with the kits, fixtures, drivers and connectors on the order. A mismatch now is a phone call; a
  mismatch on the last night is a return trip.
- **Does anything look odd?** A room listed as two-lamp fixtures in a building where you know
  four-lamp fixtures are common; an area that needs a lift but no lift is listed; emergency fixtures
  with no instructions.
- **What does "done" look like?** Is there a sample install, a photo, or a manufacturer's
  instruction that shows the finished product?

## Step 2: The Pre-Kickoff Questions

Group your questions so they are easy to work through. Answer as many as you can from the
packet; take the rest to your supervisor.

| Category | Questions to answer before day one |
|---|---|
| People | Who is the crew leader? Who is the site contact, and how do I reach them after hours? Who is my supervisor contact during the job, and who is the backup after hours? |
| Schedule | What are the start and stop times and days? Is weekend work an option if we fall behind? Are there blackout times (store open, peak hours, events)? |
| Access and security | Which door? Keys, badges, alarm codes, escorts? How do we lock up and leave? Does security need notice to block off parking or areas? |
| Site conditions | Ceiling heights and types, obstructions over partitions or racking, occupied areas, food areas, coolers, sensitive equipment, known hazards (ask for the asbestos survey if ceilings will be disturbed) |
| Lifts and ladders | What lift or ladder does each area need? Will it fit through doors and in the elevator? Where is it stored and charged? Does the site require a spotter or other lift rules? |
| Material | Shipped to site or on our truck? Where is it stored? Who receives deliveries? Is it customer stock or our stock? |
| Tools | Drills and bits for every person, chargers, spare batteries, cleaning supplies, plastic sheeting, carts, trash cans, tape |
| Special instructions | Emergency fixtures, switching patterns, guards over food, required manufacturer, sample install required? |
| Shutdowns | Who approves circuit outages? Are panels accessible and labeled? Which circuits feed life-safety or emergency lighting? |
| Recycling and trash | Who recycles: us or the customer? Which boxes and labels? Where do spent materials and pallets go? Is there a forklift, or do we hand-carry? What goes in the site dumpster? |
| Reporting | Who gets the daily recap, by what method, by what time? Is there a morning call-in? What photos are required? |
| Travel | Hotel, per diem, driving limits and driver logs if the job is out of town |

## Step 3: Load Out

Load the vehicle the day before if you can (Lesson 6 and the library guide *Project Load Lists: What to Bring* cover
the company's load lists in detail). Typical project load-out from past company jobs:

- Drills with the right driver bits **for every person on the crew**, plus chargers
- Hand tools, meters and lockout kits for every person
- Spare sockets, connectors, disconnects, wire, screws and approved securing hardware
- Plastic sheeting, drop cloths, cleaner, clean rags, clean gloves
- Empty recycle boxes and tape, carts, trash cans, extension cords and task lights for night work
- Labels, a marker, a clipboard with the survey, recap sheets and graph paper for site diagrams

**Check new material before it leaves the shop.** Fixtures and LED kits are expensive and easy to
damage. If a carton is crushed or torn, open it at the shop, photograph any damage and report it to
your supervisor right away. A damaged fixture discovered at the top of a lift is a wasted lift trip
and an empty socket.

> **Safety:** Secure loads in the vehicle and trailer. Long fixtures and LED kits can shift during a
> stop. Check the trailer hitch, chains and lights, and the tie-downs on any lift you tow, before you
> leave the yard.

## Step 4: The Sample Install

On many projects the crew leader does a **sample install** before kickoff, often with the customer
watching. This one fixture becomes the standard every other fixture must match: mounting, wire
dressing, labeling, cleanliness, lens and trim fit.

Use the sample install to answer real questions:
- How long does one fixture really take? Compare it with the production expectation on the sheet.
- Do the kit's mounting brackets fit these housings, or will some need another approved method?
- Where will the wires go, and will they be fully inside the fixture wiring compartment?
- Does the customer like the result? Get their approval before the crew starts.

## Step 5: Raise Problems Before They Start

Before the first shift, tell your supervisor about anything that does not add up: material counts
that do not match the survey, fixtures that do not match the kit, equipment that will not fit, or
instructions you do not understand. Our company's sheets have always ended the same way: *"Contact
your supervisor if any part of these procedures does not make sense."* That is not a formality.

## Key Takeaways
- Read the specifics sheet, survey and service order together and compare counts before leaving.
- Work through the pre-kickoff questions yourself; take only the unanswered ones to your supervisor.
- Every person needs their own drill, bits, meter and lockout kit.
- Inspect new fixtures and kits at the shop and report damage with photos immediately.
- A sample install sets the standard, tests the timing and gets the customer's approval.
