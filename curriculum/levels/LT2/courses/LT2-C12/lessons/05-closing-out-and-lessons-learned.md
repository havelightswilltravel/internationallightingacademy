## Closing Out

A project is not finished when the last fixture lights. It is finished when the customer has signed
off, the counts are right, the material is where it belongs and the office has everything it needs to
bill. Past company closeout checklists covered these steps.

### Counts and Paperwork
- **Final counts on the service order:** fixtures or kits installed, drivers or ballasts, disconnects,
  sockets, wire, screws and other hardware, and anything taken from our truck stock rather than
  customer stock.
- **Survey corrected** wherever quantities, types or areas changed.
- **Skipped fixtures listed** with location and reason, so the office can schedule them or explain them.
- **Recycling recorded:** number of recycle pallets or boxes, where they are, and the PCB ballast count.

### Material
- **Leftover good material:** count it, pack it, label it clearly and keep it apart from recycle material.
  Show the manager where it is so nobody throws it away or ships it as waste. Whether it stays as customer
  stock or comes back to the shop depends on the job instructions.
- **Recycle pallets:** stack neatly, shrink-wrap well, label each one (for example "1 of 3") as the work
  order or recycler requires, and **photograph each pallet**. Photos protect our company if a shipper
  later claims a pallet was packed wrong.
- **Pickups:** make the call to schedule recycle and return pickups, or tell your supervisor how many
  pallets are waiting. If the site has no forklift, plan to hand-carry box by box.
- **Rental equipment:** call off rental lifts as directed and record the call-off number.

### Punch List and Walk-Through
- Walk the finished job with the manager. Fix punch items on the spot when you can.
- Take after photos of each area, matching the before photos.
- Get the service order and any customer completion form signed (and stamped, if the customer uses a
  stamp). Write down who walked the job with you.
- If you photograph paperwork to send to the office, photograph the page alone, flat, in focus and
  readable, so it prints like a document and not a picture of a desk.

### Leaving the Site
- Clean the work areas and staging area; remove every trace of our company's materials unless told otherwise.
- Clean out the project vehicle and trailer, and return all equipment to the shop.
- Hand deliver the completed paperwork and checklist to your supervisor.

### Feedback
Tell your supervisor what worked and what did not: material that was short, steps that took longer than
bid, a better crew setup, questions you wish had been on the sheet. That feedback is how the next
project specifics sheet gets better.

## Lessons From Past Company Projects

These short case studies come from our company's own project files. Names and places are removed.

**1. The survey said two-lamp fixtures.** An office retrofit was bid for two-lamp fixtures, but many
fixtures actually had four lamps. The crew was told to match what was in each fixture, note how many
four-lamp fixtures they found and where, and report the shortfall **as soon as possible** so material
could be ordered. *Lesson:* compare the real site to the survey on day one and report differences
immediately, not when you run out.

**2. Fixtures over partitions.** Some fixtures sat over cubicle walls with pipes above them or were
screwed to the grid. The crew wanted to mark them and skip them. The bid had already allowed extra time
for them, so they still had to be done. Fixtures that truly could not be reached without damage were
marked, left alone and listed on the recap with their location. *Lesson:* hard fixtures are usually in
the bid; ask before assuming you can skip them, and never force a fixture you might damage.

**3. LED cooler strips hanging by their wires.** On an early LED cooler retrofit, a few light bars were
not seated properly in their mounts and ended up hanging by the wires. A tech had to go back. The
customer also pointed out loose wire slack, exposed connectors and a leftover bracket. *Lesson:* seat
and secure every part, trim and dress wiring so splices are inside an enclosure, remove leftover
hardware, and step back to look before you leave. A callback costs more than doing it right.

**4. Canopy kits that did not match the housings.** On a gas station canopy LED retrofit, some existing
housings did not match the sizes in the kit's mounting instructions, and some old housings were loose or
rusted. The crew was told to use the closest bracket fit and secure it, add an approved secondary
restraint where the size did not match, **not install a kit they were unsure would stay secure**, and
call the supervisor. They also drew a neat graph-paper diagram of each canopy showing north, the
building and every problem fixture, and kept time spent securing housings separate on the service order.
*Lesson:* follow the manufacturer's instructions, stop when a fixture cannot be secured, and document
conditions so extra work can be billed.

**5. Emergency fixtures nobody planned for.** On several retrofits, emergency ballasts were not in the bid.
Depending on the customer, the right answer was to label and leave them, rewire existing units, or
replace them from a stock supply. *Lesson:* never start cutting out or adding emergency equipment
without specific direction, and report how many and what type you left.

**6. The manager who wanted a spotter.** A site manager asked the crew to use a spotter for lifts. *Lesson:*
when a site asks for a safety measure, do not argue. Do other work that does not need the lift, get
the manager's name and request to your supervisor right away, and work the lift only once it is settled.

**7. Night work in a parking structure.** A fixture replacement in a parking structure could only happen at
night, in areas security had coned off. The crew had to give security the schedule ahead of time and
tell them about any changes. *Lesson:* when someone else controls your access, your schedule is their
schedule, so keep them informed.

> **Safety:** A loose fixture over a fuel island, a parking stall or a store aisle can fall on someone
> long after you leave. If you are not certain a fixture or housing is secure, do not leave it. Stop,
> secure it with an approved method or de-energize and make it safe, and call your supervisor.

## Key Takeaways
- Close out with correct counts, a corrected survey, skipped fixtures listed and recycling recorded.
- Keep leftover good material separate and labeled; photograph every recycle pallet.
- Walk the job with the manager, fix punch items, take after photos and get the sign-off.
- Clean the site and vehicle, return equipment and hand in the paperwork.
- Past jobs teach the same lessons: compare site to survey early, secure everything, ask before skipping,
  never improvise on emergency equipment, and give feedback so the next sheet is better.

## Job playbooks

Each job type our company runs now has a **job playbook** in the Library (category **Job Playbooks**):
service calls, interior LED kit retrofits, interior fixture replacements, exterior wall pack and area
light replacements, LED canopies, cooler and case lighting, pole light removal, emergency lighting
inspections, site surveys and sign service. Each one sets out what "done right" looks like, what to
confirm before you leave the shop, the problems we see most, and the close-out paperwork, so your
supervisor does not have to repeat it on every job. Each playbook ends with a load list for that job
type, drawn from the library guide *Company Load Lists* (see Lesson 6). The case studies in this lesson, and other near misses and
lessons learned from our safety meetings, are also written up as short discussion cards in the Library
(category **Case Studies**) for use at safety and operations meetings.
