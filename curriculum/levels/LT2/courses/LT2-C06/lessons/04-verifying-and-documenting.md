## "It Works" Isn't the Finish Line

A repair isn't complete until you've confirmed it works **the way the customer uses it** and
you've recorded what you did. Skipping either one leads to callbacks, finger-pointing, and
unsafe conditions for the next tech.

## Verifying the Fix

### 1. Operate it normally
Use the **normal** control — the wall switch, occupancy sensor, dimmer, time clock, or
building automation schedule. A fixture that lights when you jumper it at the junction box
but not from the sensor isn't fixed.

### 2. Check the whole circuit and neighbors
Confirm other fixtures on the same circuit and control still work. Opening splices in shared
boxes can disturb other circuits. Check the breaker you locked out is back on and the panel
door is closed.

### 3. Run it long enough
Some faults appear only when components warm up. For a cycling or thermal problem, run the
fixture long enough to pass the point where it used to fail (often 15–30 minutes, or longer
for HID warm-up). For intermittent problems, discuss a follow-up check with your lead.

### 4. Measure
Take and record readings that prove the system is healthy:

| Reading | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Supply voltage (H–N, H–G, N–G) | Fixture input | Confirms correct, stable supply |
| Load current | Circuit, with a clamp meter | Confirms expected load; baseline for future |
| Breaker holds | Panel | Fault cleared |
| Light output (visual or meter) | Space | Customer expectation met (LT4-C03 covers surveys) |

### 5. Check for root cause
Ask: **Did I fix why it failed?** If a driver failed from heat, did you correct the heat
problem or report it? If a ballast failed early because the wrong lamps were installed,
did you correct and label?

### 6. Leave it clean and safe
All covers, lenses and wireway covers installed. Labels applied (retrofits). No debris above
the ceiling or on the floor. Ceiling tiles back in place and undamaged. Waste lamps and
ballasts packed for proper disposal.

> **Safety:** Before re-energizing, follow your company's LOTO release procedure (LT1-C06):
> tools and materials removed, all guards and covers replaced, all workers clear, everyone
> who may be affected notified, then remove **your own** lock. Never remove someone else's
> lock.

## Documenting the Work

Documentation is part of the repair. Good records:
- Let the next tech start where you left off instead of repeating your tests
- Show the customer the value of the work
- Reveal patterns (the same fixture type failing across a building)
- Protect you and the company if questions come up later

### What to record

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Location | Bldg 2, 3rd floor, Room 314, fixture row 2, #3 from window |
| Complaint | Customer reported fixture flickering for a week, then dark |
| Verified symptom | Fixture dark; others on circuit and switch OK |
| Tests and readings | 277 V H–N at driver input; 0 V DC driver output |
| Cause | Failed LED driver; housing very hot — insulation laid on top of fixture |
| Repair | Replaced driver (make/model) like-for-like; removed insulation from fixture top |
| Verification | Operated from wall switch and sensor; ran 20 min; all OK |
| Follow-up | Recommend checking other fixtures in Room 314 for insulation contact |
| Circuit | Panel LP-3, circuit 17 (directory corrected from "spare") |
| Parts | (1) driver, (2) lever connectors |
| Photos | Before/after, labels, nameplates |

### Writing tips
- **Be specific.** "Fixed light" tells no one anything. "Replaced failed 2-lamp T8 IS ballast;
  sockets OK" tells the next tech everything.
- **Record readings with units.** "Voltage good" is opinion. "276 V H–N" is data.
- **Note what you did NOT fix**, and why: "Breaker for circuit 12 runs warm to touch —
  reported to lead for evaluation."
- **Photos** of nameplates, labels and before/after conditions are fast and valuable.
- **Use the customer's language** for location (room numbers, landmarks), not just
  drawing references.

## Closing With the Customer

Before leaving:
1. Tell the customer what you found and what you did, in plain language.
2. Show them it works.
3. Mention any related problems or recommendations (without overselling — note them on the
   work order for your lead).
4. Explain anything they might notice (HID restrike delay, new LED color, sensor timeout).
5. Get a signature if the work order requires it.

## Key Takeaways
- Verify using the normal control, check neighbors and the whole circuit, and run it long enough to catch thermal problems.
- Record measured readings with units, not opinions.
- Fix or report the root cause.
- Follow the LOTO release procedure; remove only your own lock.
- Document location, complaint, verified symptom, tests, cause, repair, verification, follow-up and circuit — be specific and add photos.
- Close with the customer in plain language.
