## Why Testing Is Required
Batteries age, lamps fail, chargers die, and people accidentally wire units to switched legs. The
only way to know emergency lighting will work is to test it. NFPA 101 requires periodic testing
of emergency lighting (Section 7.9.3), NEC Article 700 requires testing and written records, and
the AHJ and insurance inspectors look for the records.

## The Required Tests (NFPA 101 battery-powered emergency lighting)
| Test | Interval | Duration | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Functional test** | Every 30 days (monthly) | **At least 30 seconds** | Unit transfers to battery and lights |
| **Annual test** | Every 12 months | **Full 1.5 hours (90 minutes)** | Battery can carry the load for the full required time |
| **Written records** | Every test | — | Kept for inspection by the AHJ |

Equipment must be fully operational for the full duration of each test. Units that fail must be
repaired or replaced, and the repair documented.

**Self-testing/self-diagnostic units:** NFPA 101 allows battery units that automatically perform
the 30-second test at least every 30 days and a diagnostic routine, and indicate failures with a
status light. These still need a periodic **visual check of status indicators** and records,
and the annual 90-minute test requirements still apply (self-testing units may perform it
automatically). Computer-based self-test/self-diagnostic systems that log results are also
permitted. Read the unit's instructions to learn its indicator codes (e.g., charging, battery
fault, lamp fault, charger fault).

**Generator and central systems:** Generator-fed emergency lighting is tested under NFPA 110
(generator) requirements and the facility's maintenance program; central inverters per the
manufacturer and code. At LT3 you may assist with these tests, but they are usually coordinated
by facilities staff or a specialty vendor.

## Planning the Test
1. **Get the customer's permission and agree on timing.** An annual test leaves units with
   drained batteries for many hours afterward — ideally schedule it so the building isn't
   occupied, or test portions of the building at a time so not all units are discharged at once.
2. **Get the unit list** — the emergency lighting log or a floor plan with unit IDs.
3. **Plan access:** ladders, a remote test wand or magnet for high units, keys for test switches,
   a flashlight (some areas will be very dark during a breaker-off test).
4. **Know your method:**
   - **Test switch / button** — tests the unit's own transfer, battery and lamps.
   - **Remote test** (infrared wand or magnet) — for units with that feature.
   - **Breaker-off test** — turning off the normal lighting circuit(s) tests the whole system as
     an outage would, including whether units are fed from the correct circuit. This is the most
     realistic test but requires coordination; the breaker must be restored afterward.
5. **Warn occupants** and post signs if you'll be switching off normal lighting.

> **Safety:** Testing by turning off branch-circuit breakers darkens the space — make sure no one
> is working on ladders, machinery or stairs in the area. Use a flashlight. Opening a panelboard
> cover to operate breakers exposes you to energized parts; follow your employer's NFPA 70E
> procedures for operating breakers and wearing PPE. Never leave a breaker off at the end of the
> test without the customer's knowledge.

## Monthly Test Procedure (Each Unit)
1. Confirm the charge indicator is lit (normal charging) before testing. Note any unit with no
   indicator.
2. Press and hold the test switch (or use the remote/breaker method) for **at least 30 seconds**.
3. Watch that the lamps come on promptly and stay on with steady output for the full time.
4. Release and confirm the unit returns to normal/charging.
5. For exit signs: check the word EXIT is fully lit and legible, chevrons are correct, faces aren't
   damaged, and the sign isn't blocked.
6. Check heads are aimed along the egress path.
7. Record the result for that unit.

## Annual 90-Minute Test Procedure
1. Ensure units have had their full charge time (e.g., new units need their initial charge — check
   the manufacturer's instructions, often 24 hours).
2. Start the test for the group of units (breaker off, or units with a timed test function).
3. Record the start time. **Check every unit at the end of 90 minutes** — and preferably partway
   through to catch early failures.
4. A unit that goes dark, dims badly, or shuts off before 90 minutes **fails**.
5. Restore normal power; confirm every unit returns to charging.
6. Record each unit's result, the duration, and the restoration time.
7. Remind the customer that the units need many hours (often up to 24) to recharge — the building
   has reduced emergency capacity until then.

## Records
A complete record for each test includes:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date and time | 2026-10-08, 6:30 p.m. |
| Unit ID and location | EM-2-14, 2nd floor east corridor |
| Test type | Monthly 30-second / Annual 90-minute |
| Result | Pass / Fail (with description) |
| Corrective action | Replaced battery, retest passed 2026-10-09 |
| Tester's name/initials | J. Smith |

Many customers use software or the unit's self-diagnostic logs. Whatever the format, records must
be retained and available to the AHJ.

## Common Failures and Fixes
| Finding | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Charge light off | No unswitched power, failed charger, tripped/missing feed | Verify supply; correct wiring or replace unit |
| Lights during test but fails before 90 min | Battery at end of life or undersized | Replace battery (correct type), retest |
| Doesn't light on test | Failed lamp/LED head, battery disconnected, failed transfer | Inspect connections, replace components |
| Goes to battery whenever lights are switched off | Wired to switched leg | Correct to unswitched hot of same circuit |
| Exit sign dim | Failed LEDs, low battery, tritium near end of life | Repair/replace; handle tritium per procedure |

## Key Takeaways
- Monthly: at least 30 seconds. Annually: the full 90 minutes. Record every test.
- Self-testing units still require visual checks of indicators and records.
- Plan annual tests so the building is not left without charged units while occupied.
- Check every unit at the end of the 90 minutes; anything that fails early has failed.
- Document results and corrective actions for the AHJ.
