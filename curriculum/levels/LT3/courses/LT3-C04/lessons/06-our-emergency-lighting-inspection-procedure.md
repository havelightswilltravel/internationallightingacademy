## Why We Have Our Own Procedure
Lesson 4 covered what NFPA 101 requires: a **30-second functional test every 30 days** and a
**full 1.5-hour (90-minute) test every year**, with written records (NFPA 101, 7.9.3). This lesson
teaches **how our company performs and documents the annual 90-minute inspection** using the
**company 90-minute inspection spreadsheet**. The customer buys this record to show the fire
marshal or insurance inspector, so the paperwork is part of the job.

The library guide **Emergency Lighting Inspection Checklist** (category: Inspection Checklists)
mirrors the spreadsheet field by field. Keep it open on your phone while you work.

## The Company Procedure, Step by Step

### Step 1: Check in and get a layout
Check in with the site manager and **ask for a floor plan or layout**. Use the map to mark each
unit and draw in problem fixtures. The map and the spreadsheet work together: the map shows
*where* a unit is, the spreadsheet says *what* it is and what failed. Agree on timing: batteries
need up to 24 hours to recharge afterward.

### Step 2: Walk every unit and check the indicator
Before turning anything off, walk to every exit sign, bug eye, combo unit and battery-backup
fixture and **look for the charge (LED) indicator**.
- Indicator lit: the unit has power and the charger is working.
- Indicator dark: **verify the unit is receiving power** (a qualified tech checks the supply). If
  power is good and the indicator is still dark, **the unit is bad**. If there is no power, it is a
  circuit problem to write up, not a unit failure.

### Step 3: Shut off the emergency breakers and start the timer
With the manager's permission, **turn off all the breakers that feed the emergency units** and
**start a 90-minute timer** right away. Write the start time on the form.

> **Safety:** Operating breakers means working at an open panel with energized parts behind the
> dead front. Follow the company's NFPA 70E procedures and wear the required PPE; only operate
> breakers you are qualified and authorized to operate. Warn occupants, keep people off ladders
> and stairs in darkened areas, and carry a flashlight.

### Step 4: Quick check that every unit transferred
Walk the route quickly. **Every charge indicator should be off.** Any unit that does **not light
immediately** is written up for replacement (note what failed — see Step 5).
- **If some indicators stay on, you have not found all the emergency breakers.**
- **Company rule: if the breakers are not labeled for emergency lighting, do not continue the
  inspection.** Restore anything you turned off and write it up for an electrician to identify and
  label the breakers. Guessing can drop a cooler or a register lane, and NEC 408.4 requires
  circuits to be identified in the panel directory.

### Step 5: Fill out the form, tag failures, do approved PM
While the timer runs, **fill out the inspection spreadsheet** for every unit (fields below) and
**tag each defective fixture with the problem** — battery, lamp/head, or charging board. If the
customer approved it, do other preventive maintenance (cleaning faces, re-aiming heads, replacing
missing covers). Stay inside your work scope; don't open anything energized.

### Step 6: Read the results at 90 minutes
At the end of 90 minutes walk the route again. **Units still lit pass. Units that went dark or
dimmed badly before 90 minutes fail.** Our rule of thumb: **a unit that lit at the start but did
not last is a battery.** Confirm that before ordering a battery alone — the indicator should have
been lit in Step 2 (a dead charger never charged the battery), and a unit more than about ten
years old usually gets a new unit, not a battery.

Then **restore every breaker**, confirm each indicator comes back on, and tell the manager the
units are recharging.

### Step 7: Get replacement information
For every failure, record the **model and manufacturer** for a replacement unit, and the
**battery information** (voltage, capacity, part number) if a battery is all that is needed.

### Step 8: Take pictures
**Photograph every failed component and the fixture.** Get a **full elevation picture** (the unit
in its location, so someone can find it) and a **close-up of the model and date code label.** Put
the picture number on the spreadsheet row.

## The Spreadsheet, Column by Column

**Header:** site name and site number, **S/O #** (the service order / work order number),
address, city, state, zip, site contact and phone.

| Column | What to record | Pass / fail or notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fixture Location | Where the unit is, matching the number you wrote on the map | Specific enough for the next tech to find it |
| Fixture Description | Exit, bug eye, fluorescent (battery-backup fixture), combo, etc. | Use the form's terms |
| Fixture Height | Mounting height | Tells the office what ladder or lift the repair needs |
| Model/MFG* | Manufacturer and model from the label | Required for any failed unit |
| Housing Color* | White, black, etc. | Replacement must match |
| Exit Sign Letter Color* | Red or green | Must match the local requirement and the other signs |
| LED? | Is the unit or sign LED | Older incandescent signs are retrofit or replacement candidates |
| 90min Pass/Fail | Result at the end of the timer | Pass only if lit for the full 90 minutes |
| Failed Component* | Battery, lamp/head, charging unit, etc. | Your diagnosis from Steps 2, 4 and 6 |
| Battery or Ballast Code* | Battery part/voltage, or the emergency ballast or LED emergency driver model | Needed to quote the part |
| Picture #* | Photo number for the failure | Every failure gets a photo |
| Notes | Damage, aiming, blocked signs, wrong circuit, etc. | Anything the customer must act on |

Fields with an asterisk are required **for failed units**; fill what you can for every unit.

**Footer:** circle the **panel voltage (120 or 277)**; enter the **totals and voltage for EXIT
signs, EMERGENCY units, combo units and bug-eye units**; then **Surveyed By, Title and Date**.

## Turning the Form into an NFPA 101 Record
NFPA 101 wants each test's date, result and corrective action kept for the AHJ. Make sure the
finished record also shows the **start and end times** and, once repairs are done, the
**corrective action and retest date**. Send the form, map and photos to the office the same day.

## Self-Diagnosing Units
Some product lines are **self-diagnosing** and our procedure notes they do not need our manual
90-minute walk. That is only true when the unit **automatically runs both the monthly 30-second
and the annual 90-minute tests** and reports faults on its indicator. Someone still has to
**look at the indicators every 30 days and keep written records**, and the AHJ has the final say.
Record self-diagnosing units on the form with their indicator status.

## Key Takeaways
- Check in, get a layout, and mark units on the map as you go.
- Check every charge indicator before the test; dark indicator with good power = bad unit.
- Turn off only labeled emergency breakers; if they are not labeled, stop and write it up.
- Units that don't light at the start fail now; units that don't last 90 minutes are usually the battery.
- Every failure gets model/MFG, battery or ballast code, a full elevation photo and a label close-up.
- Restore all breakers and confirm charging before you leave; turn in a complete record.
