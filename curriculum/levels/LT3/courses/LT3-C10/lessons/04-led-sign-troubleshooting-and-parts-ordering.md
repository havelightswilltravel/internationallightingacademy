## The Company Procedure, in Step Order
The company guide's LED sign procedure is sound. Below it is expanded into plain steps with the
safety steps added.

### A. Before you test anything
1. Ask what is out: whole sign, one letter, part of a letter, or random modules? Is it
   intermittent (rain, cold, time of day)?
2. Check the **time clock, photocell and switches**. A sign that is "out" in daylight may simply
   be off.
3. Check the **sign disconnect** and breaker position. A tripped breaker is written up for an
   electrician if it trips again or shows signs of damage — do not keep resetting it.

### B. Whole sign out
4. **Check line voltage** at the power supply input (qualified person, energized test under the
   employer's NFPA 70E program). No line voltage → the problem is upstream (breaker, clock,
   photocell, wiring); write branch-circuit work up for an electrician.
5. If line voltage is good, **test secondary voltage** off the power supply — with the modules
   connected (Lesson 2).
6. If secondary voltage is good, **test the low-voltage connections** from the supply to the
   letters and along each string.
7. If secondary voltage is **not** good: lock out, **verify and remake the secondary connections**,
   then re-test. Remember that some supplies will not read output with no load. Only after the
   connections are confirmed good and a known-good load still gets no output is the supply
   replaced.

### C. Sporadic outages (some letters or modules out)
8. Lock out the sign disconnect and verify absence of voltage (live-dead-live) before opening
   letters or the supply compartment.
9. **Test and remake connections** at the first dark module and the connection just before it.
   A break in a parallel string darkens everything beyond that point.
10. **Swap modules that are out with known-good modules** to identify the bad ones. If a
    known-good module lights in that position, the original module is bad.
11. Always **test and retest connections** — the company guide notes these are "more often than
    not the issue."
12. Look for the cause: water in the can, blocked drain holes, modules fallen off, chafed wire,
    overloaded supply.

### D. Repair and close up
13. Replace failed parts with matching parts (below). Use connectors suited to the location.
14. Secure modules mechanically where adhesive has failed; keep drain holes clear.
15. Remove your lock, restore power and verify the whole sign lights evenly. Measure voltage at
    the farthest module if the end looks dim.
16. Record what you found and replaced on the work order.

> **Safety:** Signs are often on building faces, poles or canopies. Use the MEWP or ladder
> practices from your training, barricade the area below, and lock out the sign disconnect —
> not just the time clock — before opening letters or power supply compartments.

| Symptom | Likely cause | First check |
|---|---|---|
| Whole sign dark | Clock/photocell, breaker, disconnect, line voltage, supply | Controls and line voltage |
| One letter dark | Supply feeding that letter, feed wire, first connection | Secondary at supply, connection at letter entry |
| Part of a letter dark | Connector or wire break at the first dark module | Connections at that point |
| Random single modules dark | Failed modules, water damage | Swap with known-good |
| Far end dim or color-shifted | Voltage drop, overloaded supply | Voltage at far module, supply load |
| Sign flickers or cycles | Supply overloaded, overheated or partly shorted | Load vs rating, water, pinched wire |

## Parts Ordering

### Modules — matching matters
Customers notice a letter that is a different color or brightness. Record and match:

- Manufacturer, series and part number (from the module or the sign shop's records).
- Voltage (12 V or 24 V).
- Color, and for white the **color temperature** (e.g. 6500 K vs 7100 K) and binning if listed.
- **Brightness** (lumens per module) and beam angle — a brighter module next to older ones creates
  hot spots.
- Number of modules needed plus spares. If you cannot match, consider re-moduling the whole letter
  (or the whole sign) so it looks even.

### Power supplies
- Same output voltage as the modules.
- Wattage sized so the connected load is **about 80 %** of the rating or less.
- Class 2, number of outputs, input voltage range.
- Location rating (wet/damp, IP rating) and mounting size so it fits the raceway or enclosure.
- Dimming or control compatibility if the sign is dimmed.

### Connectors and materials
- Connector type and size for wire gauge and location (IDC, gel-filled, solder and heat shrink).
- Feed wire gauge and length, mounting tape/straps or screws, sealant, flex and fittings.

## When to Escalate or Write It Up
- No line voltage, repeated breaker trips, damaged branch-circuit wiring or a missing/inoperable
  sign disconnect.
- Water inside the line-voltage compartment, scorch marks or melted parts.
- Structural damage to the sign or its mounting.
- Signs needing re-design, re-moduling of a whole sign face or a permit-level change — refer to
  the sign specialist.

## Key Takeaways
- Whole sign out: controls → line voltage → secondary voltage → low-voltage connections.
- Sporadic outages: lock out, test connections, swap with known-good modules.
- Connections are the most common failure — recheck secondary connections before condemning a
  supply.
- Match modules on voltage, color temperature, brightness and series; size supplies to about
  80 % load.
- Escalate line-voltage, branch-circuit and structural problems.
