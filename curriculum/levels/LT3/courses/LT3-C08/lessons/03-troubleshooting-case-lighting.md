## Think Before You Open the Case
A dark case can be a defrost cycle, a switch, one lamp, a ballast or driver, a harness, or the circuit
itself. The company procedure moves from the **easiest and most likely** to the **hardest and most
invasive**. Follow it in order and do not skip the early steps.

## The Company Procedure in Plain Step Order
### Step 1 — Check the switches
Most cases have a **light switch** on the case (often at the end panel, in the canopy, or under the
kick plate), and some stores also switch case lights from a panel or controls system. Confirm the
switch is on and that a lighting schedule is not turning the case off.

### Step 2 — Rule out defrost
If the case could be in defrost, **mark the location and come back in about half an hour**. Check the
controller display or ask the manager. If the lights return, the case was in defrost.

### Step 3 — Check the lamps
Look for obvious failures: dark ends, broken tubes, an LED strip out. Swap in a known-good lamp of the
same type.

> **Safety:** Before removing lamps, tube guards or covers, turn the case lights off and, before touching
> any socket contact or wiring, lock out the case lighting circuit and verify absence of voltage using
> live-dead-live. Remember that case switches and defrost controls are not lockouts. Many case lighting
> circuits are 120 V or 277 V.

### Step 4 — If not the lamps, check the sockets
With the circuit locked out, inspect sockets for **breaks and burns**: cracked bodies, melted plastic,
blackened or corroded contacts, loose lamp fit. Moisture and arcing cause most socket failures. Replace
damaged sockets with matching moisture-rated sockets.

### Step 5 — If the sockets look good, locate the ballast and test
Open the case only as far as needed to **locate the ballast** — canopy, raceway, header, hood or door.
Then test for **proper line voltage at the ballast input**.

- Voltage testing at the ballast with the circuit energized is **energized diagnostic work**. Do it
  only if you are qualified under the employer's NFPA 70E program and wearing the required PPE.
- If you are not qualified, do the de-energized checks (visual inspection, continuity of ballast leads
  and harness under LOTO) and have a qualified person take the voltage reading.

### Step 6 — If line voltage is correct, deal with the ballast
- **Verify the ballast is remote-rated if it is not located in the hood or the door.**
- **Replace the ballast with the correct remote ballast for the lamp application** (lamp type and
  count, remote lead length, starting temperature, voltage). Do this under LOTO with absence of voltage
  verified.
- Restore power and test.

### Step 7 — If the ballast does not solve it, check the wiring harness
Under LOTO, check **continuity of the wiring harness** from the ballast to each socket. Look for open
conductors, crushed wires at hinges or mullions, and corroded plug connectors.

- **If the harness can be repaired without breaking the seals, make the repair** using moisture-rated
  connectors and methods acceptable to the case manufacturer.
- **If the seals must be broken to replace the harness, stop.** Turn the service over to the manager to
  call a case specialist or refrigeration company.

### Step 8 — If there is not proper line voltage
- Verify the case is **not in defrost mode**.
- **Check for tripped breakers.** Reset a breaker once only if your company allows it and nothing looks
  damaged; if it trips again, there is a fault.
- If neither explains it, **write it up for an electrician**.

## Why the Seals Matter
Refrigerated cases are built with **foam insulation and sealed panels**. The harness may run through
insulated walls, door frames or mullions. Breaking these seals can let in moisture that becomes ice,
damage insulation, cause condensation and sweating, and can affect the manufacturer's warranty.
Refrigerant lines may also run nearby. Work that requires breaking seals belongs to a **case specialist
or refrigeration contractor**.

## Quick Fault Table
| Symptom | Likely cause | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Whole lineup dark, comes back in 30 min | Defrost cycle | Note and close; tell manager |
| Whole lineup dark, no defrost | Switch, breaker, schedule, distribution block | Steps 1, 8 |
| One door or one lamp dark | Lamp, socket, connector, strip | Steps 3, 4 |
| Several lamps on one ballast dark | Ballast or driver | Steps 5, 6 |
| Lamps flicker or slow to start in freezer | Ballast not cold-rated or failing; cold lamp | Check ratings |
| Intermittent outage on one door | Harness break at hinge, wet connector | Step 7 |
| Burned smell, melted socket | Moisture arcing, loose contact | LOTO, replace, write up wiring needs |

## Key Takeaways
- Follow the order: switches, defrost, lamps, sockets, ballast, harness, line voltage.
- Lock out and verify before touching sockets, ballasts or wiring; case controls are not lockouts.
- Energized ballast voltage checks are for qualified persons only.
- Replace with the correct remote-rated ballast for the lamp application.
- Repair harness only if no refrigeration seals must be broken; otherwise turn it over to the manager
  for a case specialist.
- No line voltage: check defrost, check breakers, then write it up for an electrician.
