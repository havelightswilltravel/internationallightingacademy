## Checking In
Grocery and convenience stores are busy, public, and regulated for food safety. Every case lighting
call starts at the manager's office or service desk:

1. Introduce yourself, your company and the work order.
2. Ask which cases are affected and whether there is any known defrost schedule, recent refrigeration
   work, or ongoing issue.
3. Ask whether product needs to be moved and who will do it. **Store staff usually handle product.**
4. Ask about the store's food-safety and glass policies, and any areas you must avoid (pharmacy,
   cash office).
5. Find out where the electrical panels serving the cases are and who can unlock them.

## Food Safety
You are working above and around food that customers will eat. Treat it that way.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Keep tube guards and lens covers on every lamp over food | A broken lamp can scatter glass and phosphor onto product |
| Cover exposed food before opening a canopy or removing lamps | Dust, debris and screws fall |
| If a lamp breaks over food, stop and tell the manager | The manager decides what product must be discarded under store policy |
| Do not put tools, parts or ladders on product or shelves with food | Contamination and damage |
| Keep doors closed as much as possible | Warm air raises product temperature and causes frost |
| Do not leave a case open or uncovered for long | Product temperature rules may require the store to discard it |
| Clean up all debris, packaging and old lamps | Nothing left in the case or aisle |

Coordinate long jobs with the manager. A case that is opened up for an hour may need product moved to
a walk-in first.

> **Safety:** Broken fluorescent lamps contain a small amount of mercury. Follow your company's
> broken-lamp cleanup procedure, keep customers away, and dispose of lamps and ballasts through the
> company's recycling program. Some older ballasts may contain PCBs; handle and dispose of them per
> company policy.

## Customer Etiquette
- **Block off your work area** with cones or a cart and keep the aisle passable.
- Ladders in aisles must be attended at all times; never leave one standing alone in a store.
- Keep tools in a bucket or bag, not on the floor.
- Be polite to shoppers. If someone asks for an item behind your work, help them or find a staff member.
- Do not discuss the store's problems, prices, or other customers with shoppers.
- Avoid peak hours for big jobs if the manager prefers — early morning is often best.
- Keep your language, phone calls and music professional.

## Working Safely in a Cold, Wet Area
- Floors in front of cases can be wet from condensation or mopping. Watch your footing.
- Gloves help with cold metal but must not interfere with safe tool handling.
- Watch for sharp sheet-metal edges inside canopies and raceways.
- Do not lean on glass doors or step on case parts; use a proper ladder.

> **Safety:** Lockout in a store still means a lock and tag on the breaker. Tell the manager which
> circuit you are locking out and what else it may feed (other cases, signs, registers). Verify absence
> of voltage at the work point using live-dead-live before touching wiring.

## When to Escalate
Turn the work over or write it up when:

| Situation | Who handles it |
|---|---|
| Harness replacement would require breaking refrigeration seals | Manager calls a **case specialist or refrigeration company** |
| Case not cooling, refrigerant smell, ice buildup, controller alarms | Manager calls the **refrigeration contractor** |
| No line voltage that is not explained by defrost or a tripped breaker | **Electrician** |
| Breaker trips again after one reset | **Electrician** |
| Burned conductors at the distribution block or in the raceway | **Electrician** |
| Warranty case still under manufacturer service | Manager contacts the case manufacturer or installer |

Never adjust refrigeration controls, defrost settings or thermostats. That is the refrigeration
contractor's work, and changes can spoil product.

## Writing the Service Order
Include:

1. Store, aisle, case lineup and door or section number
2. Case manufacturer, model and serial number
3. Complaint and what you checked in order (switches, defrost, lamps, sockets, ballast, harness, voltage)
4. Parts replaced with full part numbers (for example, remote ballast model, lamp type)
5. Parts needed and why
6. Anything turned over to the manager for a case specialist, with the manager's name
7. Any product or glass incident and what the manager decided
8. Photos of the data plate, the failed parts and the finished work

## Key Takeaways
- Check in with the manager; staff move product, you do not.
- Keep tube guards and lenses on, cover food, and report any broken lamp over product.
- Block your area, attend ladders, and be courteous to shoppers.
- Never adjust refrigeration or defrost settings.
- Escalate seal-breaking harness work to a case specialist and unexplained voltage problems to an electrician.
- Document the case data plate, steps taken, parts and photos.
