## The Company Component List
The company procedure lists these components for case lighting: **case, lamps, sockets, ballasts, wire
harness, reflectors, tube guards, lenses and distribution block.** Learn what each one does and how it
fails.

| Component | What it does | How it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Case** | The refrigerated cabinet, with its canopy, raceway, mullions and seals | Damage, water leaks, broken seals, condensation |
| **Lamps** | Fluorescent tubes (often T8, sometimes T5 or older T12) or LED tubes/strips | Burned out, dim from cold, broken |
| **Sockets (lamp holders)** | Hold the lamp and connect it to the ballast | Cracked, burned or corroded contacts from moisture and heat |
| **Ballasts / drivers** | Start and run fluorescent lamps, or power LEDs | Fail open, fail to start lamps in the cold, overheat |
| **Wire harness** | Pre-made wiring that runs through the case frame and mullions to each socket | Broken conductors, pinched wires, corrosion at connectors |
| **Reflectors** | Direct light onto the product | Bent, dirty, missing — reduces light, not usually an outage |
| **Tube guards** | Clear plastic sleeves over lamps; hold glass if a lamp breaks and help keep cold off the tube | Cracked, missing, cloudy; missing guard is a food-safety problem |
| **Lenses** | Covers over canopy or shelf lights | Cracked, yellowed, fogged with water |
| **Distribution block** | Terminal block where the incoming circuit splits to lights in each section | Loose or corroded terminals, overheating |

> **Safety:** Glass lamps over food are a food-safety hazard. Tube guards (shatter-resistant sleeves)
> and lens covers must be in place on every lamp over exposed food. Never leave a case with a missing
> guard.

## Remote Ballasts and Drivers
Many cases keep the ballast or driver **away from the lamp**, because heat and cold both shorten
ballast life and the cold section has little space. This is called a **remote-mounted ballast**.

Where to look:
- **Canopy** at the top front of an open case
- **Raceway** — the wiring channel along the top, bottom or back of the case
- **Door frame header** on reach-in cases
- Inside the **hood** or the **door** on some designs
- On top of the case or in an electrical box at the end of a lineup

The company procedure says: if the ballast is **not located in the hood or the door, verify it is a
remote ballast**. A remote ballast has longer allowed lead lengths between the ballast and the lamps.
A standard ballast installed remotely may not start the lamps, especially in the cold.

### Replacing a remote ballast
**Replace the ballast with the correct remote ballast for the lamp application.** Match:

1. Lamp type, wattage and number of lamps
2. Remote-mounting rating and maximum lead length to the farthest lamp
3. Minimum starting temperature for the case
4. Input voltage (120 V or 277 V, or universal)
5. Starting method (instant start, programmed start) as specified
6. Physical size and mounting so it fits the canopy or raceway

Ballast labels show a wiring diagram and often a maximum remote distance. Read it before ordering.

## LED Case Retrofits
Many stores now use **LED case lighting**. The most common reach-in retrofit is the **door-mullion LED
strip**: a long sealed strip that mounts on each mullion and lights the shelves on both sides.

| Item | What to know |
|---|---|
| Strip length | Matched to the door height; measured from the case or door frame |
| Single-sided or double-sided | End mullions light one side; center mullions light both sides |
| Temperature rating | Must be rated for the case (freezer strips are labeled for low temperature) |
| Driver | Usually a remote driver in the header; one driver may feed several strips |
| Connectors | Sealed plug connectors between strips and harness; must be fully seated and dry |
| Color temperature | Stores often specify different CCTs for frozen, dairy, meat or produce |

LED advantages in cases include less heat in the case, better cold performance and longer life.
Common LED failures are a failed driver, a wet or loose connector, or a damaged strip.

## Parts Identification and Ordering
When you find a failed part, record:

- **Case manufacturer, model and serial number** from the case data plate (often inside the case or
  on the end panel). Case manufacturers supply exact replacement lamps, harnesses and LED kits.
- **Case temperature class** (medium or low temperature)
- **Lamp:** type, length, wattage, CCT, base
- **Ballast or driver:** full model number, input voltage, number of lamps/outputs, remote rating,
  minimum starting temperature, output current or voltage for LED drivers
- **Socket:** style and whether it is sealed
- **LED strip:** brand, length, single or double sided, connector type
- **Tube guard and lens:** length and diameter
- Door number and location in the lineup

## Key Takeaways
- Know the company component list: case, lamps, sockets, ballasts, wire harness, reflectors, tube
  guards, lenses and distribution block.
- Ballasts are often remote — look in the canopy, raceway or header.
- Replace with the correct remote ballast for the lamp application and case temperature.
- LED door-mullion strips are the common retrofit; check length, sides, rating and connectors.
- Record the case data plate and full part numbers before ordering.
