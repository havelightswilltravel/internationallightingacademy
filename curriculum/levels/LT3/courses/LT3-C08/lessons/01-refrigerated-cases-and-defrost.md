## Where Techs Find Refrigerated Case Lighting
Grocery stores, convenience stores, pharmacies, dollar stores and big-box retailers all sell cold and
frozen products from **refrigerated display cases**. A typical supermarket may have hundreds of feet
of cases, and the lighting inside them is what sells the product. Customers judge freshness by what
they see, so a dark case is a priority call for a store manager.

Case lighting is different from ceiling lighting because it lives in a **cold, wet, tightly sealed
environment** and its power is often tied to the refrigeration system.

## Types of Display Cases
| Case type | Description | Where the lights usually are |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach-in (glass door)** | Frozen or dairy cases with glass doors in a frame; each door opens separately | Vertical lamps or LED strips on the **door mullions** (the posts between doors) and sometimes a top canopy light |
| **Open multideck** | Tall open-front case with several shelves and an air curtain; dairy, produce, meat, beverages | **Canopy** light at the top front, plus optional lights under each shelf |
| **Island / coffin** | Low open-top or sliding-lid case, often frozen; shoppers look down into it | Sometimes a canopy above, often no internal light; lights may be in a raised center section |
| **Deli / service case** | Curved or flat glass front, staff serve from the back; meat, deli, bakery, seafood | Under the top glass or in a top rail, aimed down onto product |
| **Walk-in cooler with display doors** | Beverage cooler doors stocked from a walk-in behind | Door-frame mullion lights; ballasts or drivers often in the top frame |

Cases are usually installed in **lineups** — several cases bolted end to end and wired together.
One electrical circuit may feed lights for a whole lineup.

## How Refrigeration Affects the Lights
### Defrost cycles
The cooling coil (evaporator) in a case collects frost. Several times a day the case controller runs a
**defrost cycle** to melt it, using electric heaters, hot gas, or simply shutting off cooling. During
defrost, depending on how the case is wired and set up:

- Lights may be turned **off** on purpose to reduce heat load
- Fans stop and the case warms slightly
- Door anti-sweat heaters may switch differently

Defrost typically lasts from a few minutes to around 30–45 minutes, depending on the case and the
refrigeration contractor's settings.

### The company rule: mark it and come back
The company procedure says: **if there is potential for defrosting, mark the location and come back in
about half an hour.** In practice:

1. Ask the manager or check the case controller display — many show "DEF" or a defrost icon.
2. Look for clues: no fan noise, frost melting, temperature display rising.
3. Write down the case number or location (aisle, case lineup, door number).
4. Do other work and return in about 30 minutes.
5. If the lights come back on, the "outage" was a defrost cycle. Tell the manager and close the call.

> **Safety:** A case in defrost is **not de-energized**. Lights, heaters and fans can restart at any
> moment on the controller's schedule. Never treat a defrost cycle, an OFF light switch, or a case
> controller as a lockout. Before touching wiring, open and lock out the correct breakers and verify
> absence of voltage using live-dead-live.

### Cold-temperature ratings
Cold is hard on lighting. Fluorescent lamps are dimmer when cold and need ballasts rated to start them
at low temperature. Every lamp, ballast, driver, socket and LED strip used in a case must be rated for
the case's temperature:

| Component | What to check |
|---|---|
| Fluorescent ballast | Minimum starting temperature (for example 0 °F or −20 °F); listed for the lamp type and length |
| LED driver | Operating temperature range covers the case; listed for damp or wet locations where required |
| LED strip | Rated for refrigerated/freezer use; correct length for the door or canopy |
| Lamps | Cold-temperature or jacketed lamps where used |
| Sockets | Moisture-resistant, often with gaskets or sealed lamp holders |

Medium-temperature cases (dairy, produce, meat) run roughly 28–40 °F. Low-temperature (frozen)
cases run around −20 °F to 0 °F. Always check the actual rating on the component label against the
case.

## Moisture and Condensation
Every time a door opens, warm humid store air meets cold surfaces and condensation forms. Water and
ice get into sockets, connectors and wiring. This is why case lighting uses gaskets, sealed sockets,
tube guards and lenses, and why so many case failures show corrosion, green copper, or burned socket
contacts. Anti-sweat heaters in door frames help keep the glass clear, but they also mean there is
extra wiring in the frame.

## Key Takeaways
- Know the four main case types and where each keeps its lights.
- Defrost cycles can turn case lights off; mark the location and come back in about 30 minutes.
- Defrost is never a lockout — lock out and verify before touching wiring.
- Every lamp, ballast, driver and strip must be rated for the case temperature and moisture.
- Moisture causes much of the corrosion and socket damage you will see.
