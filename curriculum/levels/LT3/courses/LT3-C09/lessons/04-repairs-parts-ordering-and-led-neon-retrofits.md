## Typical Repairs

| Repair | Who usually does it | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Reset/cycle an SGFP transformer after fixing the cause | Authorized technician | Never reset repeatedly without finding the fault |
| Replace a boot or PK housing | Authorized technician or sign specialist | Locked out, discharged; match size to the glass/electrode |
| Replace GTO | Authorized technician or sign specialist | Same or higher voltage rating, routed off metal |
| Replace a transformer | Qualified person / sign specialist | Must match secondary voltage, current, SGFP and location rating |
| Replace a glass unit | Neon glass bender fabricates; installer mounts | Glass is custom-made to the pattern |
| Retrofit to LED | Sign specialist with a listed retrofit kit | Changes the sign's listing — see below |

> **Safety:** Every repair on the secondary side is done with the sign disconnect locked out,
> absence of voltage verified on the primary (live-dead-live), and stored charge discharged per
> the employer's procedure. Handle broken glass with cut-resistant gloves and eye protection;
> phosphor-coated argon tubes contain a small amount of mercury, so bag broken glass and follow
> the company's disposal rules.

## Replacing a Transformer — Matching Checklist
A replacement must match the original (or the sign's design), not just "fit":

1. **Primary voltage** (120 V, 277 V, etc.).
2. **Secondary open-circuit voltage** (e.g. 9 kV, 12 kV, 15 kV) — too low and long runs will not
   strike; too high overdrives the glass and stresses insulation.
3. **Secondary current** (e.g. 30 mA vs 60 mA) — affects brightness and electrode life.
4. **SGFP** — replacement units must provide the protection required by code.
5. **Midpoint-grounded vs other configuration** and lead arrangement.
6. **Indoor or outdoor/wet rating** and mounting style (enclosure, raceway mount).
7. **Load (length of tubing and diameter)** — the sign specialist sizes the transformer to the
   footage of glass; many manufacturers publish tubing-length charts.

## Parts Ordering — What to Record
Photograph and write down before you leave the job:

- Full transformer label (all ratings and model number).
- Glass: color/gas (red neon, white phosphor, etc.), tube diameter (in millimeters), approximate
  length, and a pattern or photo for any glass that must be bent.
- GTO rating and length needed; number and size of boots and PK housings.
- Number of tube supports and type; tie wire.
- Enclosure, flex and connector sizes; disconnect condition.
- Sign location (which elevation, which letter counted from the left), and lift needs.

## LED Neon Alternatives
**LED neon** (often called "neon flex") is a flexible LED strip in a diffused silicone or PVC
jacket that looks like a continuous glowing tube. Compared with glass neon it:

- Runs on **Class 2 low voltage** (commonly 12 V or 24 V DC) instead of thousands of volts.
- Uses much less energy and does not contain gas or mercury.
- Is far less fragile, and is often used for new signs and decorative work.
- Has its own failure points: water at cut ends and connectors, overheated or mis-sized power
  supplies, and voltage drop on long runs (covered in LT3-C10).

Channel letters are also commonly retrofitted with **LED modules** in place of glass neon.

## Retrofitting a Neon Sign to LED
A retrofit is not just swapping parts. Key points:

1. Use a **listed retrofit kit** for signs (evaluated to UL 879A) or components specified by the
   sign manufacturer, installed per the kit instructions.
2. **Remove** the neon transformer, GTO, boots and PK housings that are no longer used, or
   follow the kit instructions for any that remain — abandoned high-voltage parts confuse the
   next technician.
3. Install the **Class 2 LED power supply** in a suitable location and enclosure, with primary
   wiring in proper raceway and the sign disconnect still in place.
4. The NEC requires a retrofitted sign to be **marked** to show it has been modified; apply the
   label supplied with the kit where the next person will see it.
5. Keep drain holes open and seal penetrations so the new LED system does not suffer water
   intrusion.

Retrofits are usually performed by sign specialists or under their direction, because they
change how the sign is listed and may require a permit from the AHJ.

## When to Write It Up
- No disconnect, or a disconnect that cannot be locked.
- Primary wiring, branch circuit or grounding problems.
- Signs with heat damage, scorching, melted plastic or evidence of arcing to the cabinet.
- Glass fabrication or transformer replacement beyond your authorization.
- Recommend LED retrofit when the sign has repeated SGFP trips, multiple failed units, or the
  customer wants to reduce maintenance.

## Key Takeaways
- Do all secondary repairs locked out, verified and discharged.
- Match a replacement transformer on primary voltage, secondary voltage and current, SGFP,
  configuration and location rating.
- Record full label data, glass color/diameter/length and hardware counts for every parts order.
- LED neon and LED modules run on Class 2 low voltage; retrofits require listed kits and a
  retrofit marking.
- Escalate missing disconnects, primary wiring problems and anything beyond your authorization.
