## Your Role at LT3
At the LT3 level you are the person who **finds the problem and describes it accurately**. In most
companies, replacing a contactor, a coil or a relay card, working behind a panel dead front, and
changing branch-circuit wiring belong to an electrician — unless your employer has trained and
qualified you for that specific task. A good write-up lets the electrician arrive with the right
part and finish in one trip.

## Identifying and Ordering Parts
Record everything from the nameplate (see Lesson 1) and add the field information:

| Item to record | Example | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer and full catalog number | Brand, frame, series and suffix | Kits and coils fit only the exact frame |
| Holding type | Electrically or mechanically held | Wrong type changes how the controls work |
| Poles and NO/NC arrangement | 8-pole, all NO | Must switch every circuit as before |
| Contact rating and load type | 30 A, rated for electronic/LED | LED inrush welds contacts on general-purpose units |
| Contact voltage rating | 600 V max | Must be at least the circuit voltage |
| Coil voltage, AC or DC | 120 V AC | Wrong coil burns up or never pulls in |
| Auxiliary contacts | 1 NO + 1 NC | Needed for status to the BMS or pilot lights |
| Enclosure | NEMA 3R, outdoor | Must suit the location |
| Control devices | HOA switch, pilot light, control fuse size | Replace in kind |
| Relay panel info | Panel name, relay number, card model, firmware if shown | Monitoring service and electrician need it |

**Common replacement parts:**
- Complete contactor (same holding type, poles, ratings)
- Replacement coil for the exact frame
- Contact kit (fixed and moving contacts, springs) for the exact frame
- Arc hood or arc chute set
- Auxiliary contact block
- HOA selector switch and pilot light (check the mounting hole size)
- Control fuse and fuse holder
- Relay or relay card for a lighting control panel (usually ordered by or through the controls vendor)

Never substitute a general-purpose motor contactor for a lighting-rated one without the electrician's
approval. Lighting contactors are rated for the high inrush of many ballasts or drivers switching
at once.

## When to Escalate or Write It Up
Write it up for an electrician when you find:

- No load voltage on a closed pole (failed contact) — contactor replacement or rebuild
- A coil that has correct voltage but will not pull in
- Burned, melted or overheated conductors or terminals
- A breaker that trips again after one reset
- Voltage on load terminals with the contactor open (welded contacts or back-feed)
- Missing arc chutes, missing covers, or water inside the enclosure
- Any relay-panel hardware fault the monitoring service cannot clear
- Anything requiring removal of a dead front or work on branch circuits

Stop work and notify your supervisor and the customer immediately if you find signs of active
arcing, smoke, or an enclosure that is energized where it should not be.

## Writing the Service Order
A good write-up answers **what, where, what you measured, and what is needed.**

1. **Location:** building, room, panel name, enclosure label.
2. **Equipment:** manufacturer, catalog number, coil voltage, poles, holding type.
3. **Complaint:** in the customer's words.
4. **What you checked and found:** for example, "Line voltage present on L1–L6. Coil 120 V present
   with HOA in HAND, contactor pulls in. No voltage on T4. Pole 4 contact burned."
5. **Wiring needs:** every burned, overheated or loose conductor you saw, with its terminal.
6. **Recommendation:** replace contactor or rebuild (only after confirming with an electrician),
   parts list with catalog numbers.
7. **Temporary condition left:** HOA position, any bypass active, any circuit left off, and who at
   the site approved it.
8. **Photos:** nameplate, overall enclosure, the specific damage.

## Talking With the Customer
- Explain in plain language: "The switch inside this box that turns your parking lot lights on has a
  burned contact. An electrician needs to replace it."
- Tell them about any temporary condition, such as "I've left section 3 in HAND so it stays on until
  the repair."
- If a monitoring service is involved, tell the customer what the service saw and what they changed.

> **Safety:** Never leave an enclosure open, a cover off, or a bypass active without telling the
> person in charge of the site and writing it on the service order. Close and latch every enclosure
> before you leave.

## Key Takeaways
- At LT3 you diagnose and document; hardware replacement is usually electrician work.
- Record the full catalog number, holding type, poles, coil voltage and load rating every time.
- Use lighting-rated contactors for lighting, especially LED loads.
- Write up failed contacts, non-pulling coils, burned wiring and relay-panel faults.
- Every write-up states location, equipment, measurements, wiring needs, recommendation and any
  temporary condition left in place.
