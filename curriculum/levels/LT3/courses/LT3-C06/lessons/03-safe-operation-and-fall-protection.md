## Why MEWP Incidents Happen
The leading causes of serious MEWP incidents are **electrocution** (contact with power lines),
**tip-overs**, **falls from the platform**, **ejection** from boom platforms (catapulting), and
**crush/entrapment** against overhead objects. Nearly every one is preventable with good
selection, inspection, positioning and discipline.

## Fall Protection — What the Rules Say
| MEWP | Primary fall protection | Personal fall protection |
|---|---|---|
| **Boom lifts (Group B)** | Guardrails | **Required.** OSHA requires a body harness and lanyard attached to the boom or platform for aerial lifts. Use a **full-body harness** with a lanyard attached to the **manufacturer's designated anchorage** on the platform. |
| **Scissor lifts (Group A)** | Guardrails (OSHA treats scissors as mobile scaffolds) | Generally not required by OSHA when guardrails are complete, but **follow the manufacturer's instructions, ANSI A92.22 risk assessment and your employer's policy** — many companies require harness and restraint lanyard on all MEWPs |

**Restraint vs arrest:**
- A **fall restraint** system (short lanyard) keeps you from reaching the edge where you could fall.
  This is the preferred approach in a boom platform because a fall-arrest lanyard may be too long
  to stop you before you strike the ground or structure at low heights.
- A **fall arrest** system stops a fall in progress. If arrest is used, the lanyard and anchorage
  must be rated for it and the clearance below must be adequate.
- Use the lanyard length and type the MEWP manufacturer specifies.

**Anchor only to the platform's designated anchorage points.** Never tie off to the luminaire, a
pole, a beam, a sprinkler pipe or an adjacent structure — if the platform moves, you could be
pulled out of the basket.

**Harness inspection before each use:** webbing (cuts, burns, fraying, chemical damage),
stitching, D-ring and buckles (cracks, distortion), labels legible, lanyard and snap hooks (gates
close and lock, no deformation), and impact indicators not deployed. Remove any harness that has
arrested a fall.

## Rules on the Platform
1. **Keep both feet on the platform floor.** Never stand or sit on guardrails or midrails.
2. **Never use ladders, buckets, boxes or planks** on the platform to gain height.
3. **Keep the gate or chain closed** while elevated.
4. **Don't exceed rated capacity** — people + tools + materials (fixtures are heavy: an LED
   high-bay retrofit pallet can quickly overload a small platform). Respect separate indoor/outdoor
   ratings and extension-deck ratings.
5. **Don't carry loads that extend beyond the platform** or that catch wind (large signs, panels)
   unless the manufacturer allows it.
6. **Don't climb out of an elevated platform** onto structures unless the work plan specifically
   provides a safe method and fall protection (generally, don't).
7. **Don't use the MEWP as a crane** or tie it to structures.
8. **Secure tools** with tool lanyards or bags; keep the area below barricaded.

## Driving and Positioning
- **Drive only within the manufacturer's limits** for slope and surface, with the platform lowered
  when required. Avoid driving elevated near drop-offs or on uneven ground.
- **Boom lifts:** Hitting a pothole or curb while elevated can whip the platform and **catapult**
  the occupants. That's a key reason harnesses are required in booms. Drive slowly, watch the
  path, and travel with the boom lowered and retracted when possible.
- **Look in the direction of travel.** Use a spotter in congested areas, near people, or where
  visibility is limited.
- **Position the platform** so the work is within easy reach — close enough that you don't lean
  out. Re-position rather than over-reach.
- **Watch overhead** while elevating; stop before you contact anything.
- **Outdoors:** Monitor wind. Stop work and lower the platform if wind approaches the machine's
  rating, during lightning, or if conditions change.

## Electrical Work from a MEWP
Servicing a luminaire from a MEWP combines two hazards. Plan both:
- Apply **LOTO** to the luminaire circuit at the panel before you go up whenever the work requires
  de-energized conditions, and **verify absence of voltage at the fixture** from the platform with
  a tested meter (live-dead-live) — carry your proving unit.
- A MEWP platform is **not insulated** unless it is a specifically rated insulated aerial device
  that is tested to its rating. Standard scissors and booms provide **no** protection from
  electrical contact.
- A shock at height can cause a fall; that's one more reason to be tied off in a boom.

## Ending the Job
1. Lower and retract the platform fully; drive to a level, approved parking area.
2. Shut down per the manufacturer's procedure; remove the key (or secure controls) to prevent
   unauthorized use.
3. Charge batteries in a ventilated area away from ignition sources (charging lead-acid batteries
   releases hydrogen). Refuel only with the engine off, outdoors, per procedure.
4. Report any problems found during use.

> **Safety:** Never disable or bypass a MEWP safety device — tilt alarms, load-sensing systems,
> limit switches, enable switches or interlocks. If a safety device prevents a function, that's
> the device doing its job: stop and find out why.

## Key Takeaways
- Booms: full-body harness and lanyard attached to the designated platform anchorage — always.
- Scissors: guardrails are primary; follow manufacturer and employer rules for personal fall
  protection.
- Both feet on the floor, gate closed, no ladders or rail-climbing, never over capacity.
- Drive carefully, watch overhead, re-position instead of over-reaching, and monitor wind.
- Standard MEWPs are not insulated; LOTO and verify at the fixture before work.
