## Why We Have Our Own Rules
The earlier lessons covered what OSHA and ANSI A92 require. This lesson is **our company's policy**
for boom and scissor lifts, taken from the safety handbook every technician signs. Where our rule is
stricter than the standard, **our rule applies**. Breaking these policies leads to disciplinary
action, but the real reason they exist is that every one of them traces back to a way people get
hurt on lifts: falls, electrocution, tip-overs and being crushed.

## Who May Operate
- **Only qualified people operate our lifts.** Qualified means you were trained on that kind of lift
  and the company has **authorized** you to run it. You are qualified separately for each type: being
  signed off on a scissor does not qualify you on an articulating boom.
- Read the operator's manual for the machine in front of you and follow the manufacturer's setup and
  elevating instructions. The manual must stay with the machine.
- You will be **retrained** if the job site brings a hazard you haven't been trained on, if you move
  to a different type of lift or fall protection, or if your work shows you've lost the skill
  (OSHA 29 CFR 1926.454(c) for scissors as mobile scaffolds; ANSI A92.24 for all MEWPs).
- When picking a lift, think about the **load it must carry** (you, tools, fixtures), the **work site
  dimensions** (doorways, aisles, reach) and the **power source** (electric indoors, rough-terrain
  outdoors).

## The Company Minimum Inspection
You visually inspect the lift **before you get on it, before each shift, and after anything that
could have damaged it** (a bump, a curb strike, a near tip). Our minimum list:

| Area | Check |
|---|---|
| Power | Battery and connection points |
| Hydraulics | Visible leaks, hoses, cylinders, valves and connections |
| Electrical | Wiring and connectors |
| Controls | Ground controls, platform controls and emergency controls, each function on each panel |
| Structure | Basket and boom stability; outriggers or stabilizers if equipped |
| Platform | Doors, latches, top rails, midrails, toe boards, chains or cables; ladder steps and rails |
| Labels | Safety and warning decals in place and readable |
| Paperwork | **Annual inspection tag in place and current** |

Add the manufacturer's items (tires, pothole guards, tilt alarm, emergency lowering) from Lesson 2.
ANSI A92.22 also requires a frequent inspection and an annual inspection by a qualified mechanic; the
annual must be no more than 13 months old. No current tag, no lift.

> **Safety:** A lift that fails inspection gets a **red "Out of Service" tag** and is reported. Never
> use a red-tagged lift, and never remove someone else's tag. A rental lift that shows up with a
> problem goes back to the rental company.

## On the Platform (Booms and Scissors)
- Don't raise the platform until **all guardrails are in place** and the gate or chain is closed.
- **No ladders, buckets or boxes** on the platform. Don't stand on guardrails for extra reach, and
  never lean out over the rails to do the work. Move the lift instead.
- Don't exceed the manufacturer's **height or load rating**. Count yourself, a helper, tools and the
  new fixtures.
- **Flat, level ground only.** Set the brakes, and chock the wheels if you must stop on an incline.
- **Outriggers and stabilizers:** if the lift has them and the manual calls for them, set them on
  solid ground or pads before you raise.
- **Never override a safety device** and never modify a lift without the manufacturer's written
  approval.

## Tie-Off: Booms vs Scissors
**Booms (anything that can reach out past its wheel base, like an articulating or telescopic boom):**
company policy requires a **full-body harness with the lanyard clipped to the anchor point inside the
lift during all operation of the lift.** That means from the moment you step in, including when the
basket is lowered and you are driving to the next pole. Use the lanyard length the manufacturer
specifies. A pothole or curb can whip the boom and throw you out (OSHA 29 CFR 1926.453(b)(2)(v)).

**Never tie off to anything outside the platform**: not the pole, the fixture, a beam or a sprinkler
pipe. If the lift moves, you get pulled out. If a boom has no designated anchor point, don't use it
and call your supervisor.

**Scissors:** OSHA treats a scissor as a mobile scaffold, and **the guardrail system is your fall
protection.** Our rules: reattach the entry chain or close the gate **the moment you step on**, keep
both feet on the deck and stay inside the rails. If the scissor's manual or the site requires a
harness, wear one and clip only to the manufacturer's designated anchor.

Our fall protection program: the company provides harnesses, lanyards and **trauma (relief) straps**
and trains you on them. You inspect your harness before each use and report defects at once; a
competent person inspects harnesses periodically. Tie-off points must be rated for **5,000 lb** per
person, and the lanyard must be set so you can't free fall more than **6 ft**
(OSHA 29 CFR 1926.502(d)).

## Key Takeaways
- Only trained and authorized operators, qualified separately for each lift type.
- Company minimum inspection before mounting, every shift and after any incident; red-tag failures;
  current annual tag or no lift.
- Guardrails in place before raising; no ladders, no standing on rails, no leaning out.
- Booms: harness and lanyard clipped inside the basket during all operation, even lowered.
- Never tie off outside the platform.
- Scissors: guardrails are the fall protection; chain or gate closed the moment you step on.
