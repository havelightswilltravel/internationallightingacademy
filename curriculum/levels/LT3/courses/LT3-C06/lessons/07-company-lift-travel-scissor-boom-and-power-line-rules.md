## Picking Up From Part I
Part I covered who may run our lifts, the company inspection and tie-off. This part covers how we
move lifts, the extra rules for scissors and booms, and our rules for signs and poles near power
lines. These are **company policy**, signed by every technician; where they are stricter than OSHA
or ANSI, our rule applies.

## Traveling and Moving
- Drive slowly, face the direction of travel, and know which way the platform is pointed before you
  touch the joystick.
- Lowering the platform, not raising it, is what makes a lift stable for travel.
- **Company rule: no traveling across the job site elevated.** Keep elevated driving to small moves
  in the immediate work area, only if the manufacturer allows it. Tell everyone before you move.
- Watch overhead: sprinkler pipes, doorways, joists. Never put yourself between the rail and a beam.
- On a scissor, stay off edges guarded only by building handrails; those rails won't stop a lift.
- Never raise a scissor while it is on a truck or trailer.

## Scissor Rules
- **Don't jump on or off a raised scissor**, and **never stand or reach under a raised scissor**.
- Don't overload it, and remember the rating is often **lower with the extension deck out**.
- A scissor is not a material hoist or a personnel elevator.
- **A spotter is required on most interior service work, especially during the customer's business
  hours.** The spotter keeps shoppers and carts out and watches your blind side.
- No oxygen or fuel cylinders stored on the deck.

## Boom Rules
- Never walk under the boom to get to the platform.
- Don't rest the boom or basket on a steel structure.
- Keep your hands inside the basket while raising or lowering.
- Hot work (grinding, cutting): fire extinguisher in the basket and a fire watch below.
- **Ground controls are not used on an occupied lift except in an emergency.** The person in the
  basket controls the lift. The ground person must still know how to lower it for rescue.

## Power Lines at Signs and Poles
- Stay **at least 10 ft from lines of 50 kV or less**, counting you, your tools and the lift.
- **Company rule: for lines over 50 kV, the power company must shield them before you work on anything
  under them.** That is stricter than OSHA, which lets an unqualified worker keep 10 ft **plus 0.4 in
  for every kV over 50 kV** (for example, about 10 ft 8 in from a 69 kV line) (29 CFR 1910.333(c)(3)).
  Our rule takes the guesswork out: over 50 kV, the utility shields it first.
- Never work within 10 ft of a line the power company has not covered ("booted"). Even when lines are
  covered, **treat them as bare** and stay clear.
- If you can't keep that margin, stop and call your supervisor.

> **Safety:** A standard boom or scissor is **not insulated**. If any part of the lift, your tools or
> a fixture you are holding gets inside 10 ft of a line, the whole machine can become energized and
> anyone touching it from the ground can be killed. Measure before you set up, not after you raise.

- De-energize the sign or pole whenever possible: lock the circuit off and tag it (LOTO).
- Cone off the area below you; dropped tools and parts hurt people.

## Winter Lift Work
- Clear ice, snow and slush from the platform floor, steps and controls before you climb on.
- Check the wind against the lift's rating; winter fronts bring gusts.
- Refit your harness over winter layers; a harness adjusted for a T-shirt is too tight or won't
  close properly over a coat, and one adjusted over a coat is loose in spring.
- Frozen ground can feel solid and then **thaw and soften under an outrigger or wheel** during the
  day. Use pads and recheck the setup after a few hours.

## Housekeeping
Keep material stacked so it can't fall, clear trash often, and never block exits, stairways, fire
equipment or the space in front of electrical panels with your lift or material.

## Key Takeaways
- Lower the platform for travel; no traveling across the job site elevated.
- Never jump on or off, stand under or reach under a raised scissor; watch the extension-deck rating.
- Spotter for interior work, especially during the customer's business hours; follow stricter site rules.
- Booms: never walk under the boom, keep hands inside, and the person in the basket runs the lift.
- 10 ft from lines 50 kV and under; higher voltage must be shielded by the power company first;
  treat covered lines as bare and call your supervisor if you lack the margin.
- De-energize and lock out sign and pole circuits whenever possible, and cone off below.
