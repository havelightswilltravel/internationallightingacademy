## Why This Lesson Exists

Lessons 6 and 7 gave you our company's lift policies. This lesson adds the **lessons learned**
from our monthly safety meetings: damage, near misses and breakdowns on our own trucks and
lifts. None of them were caused by a broken machine. All of them were caused by a small choice.

## Lift Trucks: Watch the Front End

Our lift trucks carry the **bucket and a step platform hanging off the front**. They swing wide
and grab signs, canopies, poles and parked cars **when you turn the wheel and when you back up**.
One of our trucks was **totaled** when its bucket caught a structure during a turn. If you do not
drive these trucks often, be extra careful; use an observer to back whenever you can.

## Control the Area Under the Bucket

- **Harness and lanyard on in the bucket at all times**, and inspect each lift before use (Lesson 6).
- **Cone off the whole area around the truck**, not just behind it. A safety-meeting photo showed
  a boom working over a store's **entrance** with cones only at the rear of the truck, and
  customers walking under the bucket.
- **Talk to the site manager about the plan** first: close or redirect the entrance, use another
  door, or do the work after hours. If people keep walking into your zone, stop work.
- **Spotter** on interior lift work, especially during business hours (Lesson 7).

## No Modifications, Ever

**Nothing is added to, drilled into or removed from a lift without the manufacturer's written
approval** (ANSI A92.22; 29 CFR 1910.67(b)(2)). Real examples from our fleet:

- **Drain holes drilled in a bucket** to let rain out. They weaken the structure and can fail the
  inspection.
- **A bracket screwed to a basket** to hang a drill. Even small screw holes can fail the annual
  inspection and take the truck out of service.
- Homemade spare-tire or trailer mounts. Repairs follow the manufacturer's design only.

Hang tools on the attachment points the maker provides, and dump water by tipping the bucket as
the manual allows.

## The Jib

Some of our lift trucks have a material-handling **jib and winch**:

- **Not trained on the jib? Don't use it.**
- **Inspect the winch line before each use**; any fraying, kinks or crushing means it is replaced.
- **Coil the line under tension**, even just hand pressure. A tight coil followed by a loose coil
  can let the load **drop suddenly** when the loose wrap slips.
- Never exceed the jib's rated load at the extension you are using.
- **Stow the jib before you drive.** Left up, it is the highest point on the truck.

Rigging the load itself is covered in LT3-C12.

## When the Truck Won't Cooperate

- **PTO won't engage?** On some of our trucks a **12 V circuit breaker** on the passenger side
  protects the PTO circuit. The **red button is test only**; reset it with the **lever**. Check it
  before you call for help.
- **Boom works but won't go up or down?** On trucks with a **24 V boom battery bank**, low bank
  voltage is a likely cause. Call the equipment service line and check fuses and relays first.
  Then measure the bank (24 V is the minimum) and each battery. Charge or replace batteries only as
  the manufacturer and your supervisor allow; wear eye protection around batteries.
- **Diesel lift trucks:** never let one run low on fuel while the boom is up, and keep at least a
  **half tank in winter** so the fuel does not gel.
- **Idling:** shut it off unless you are running the PTO or cooling the cab.

## Scissor Lifts and the Customer's Building

- **Weight on tile.** Our standard 19-ft slab scissor weighs about **3,300 lb**; our man lift weighs
  about **1,100 lb**. Tile cracks under concentrated loads. **Write up the man lift, or send
  plywood** to spread the scissor's load. Some customers require floor protection on every visit.
- **Single doors:** before bringing a lift through, **inspect the door and frame, photograph any
  existing damage and tell the manager**. Otherwise we get blamed the day after we leave. Go slow
  and use a spotter every time you enter or exit with a lift.
- **Man-lift straddle:** follow the instructions on the label exactly. Best practice is to set it up
  once in the shop, with someone experienced, before you load it.
- **Chargers:** unplug by the **plug, not the cord**. No charge light? Check and reset the GFCI.
- Keep scissors **covered or inside through the winter**, and report any fluid leak.

## Scaffolding

Use scaffolding **only if you have been trained by a competent person** on that type (Lesson 5).

> **Safety:** Every story in this lesson ended without an injury. That was luck. Inspect, cone,
> spotter, no modifications, and when the machine does something unexpected, stop and call.

## Key Takeaways
- The front-mounted bucket and step platform grab things when turning and backing.
- Cone the whole area under the bucket and talk to the site manager about entrances.
- No drain holes, brackets or other modifications without the manufacturer's written approval.
- Jib: trained only, inspect the line, coil under tension, stow before driving.
- PTO breaker: red button tests, lever resets. Boom won't move: check the 24 V bank.
- Scissors on tile: man lift or plywood; photograph door damage first; spotter at doors.
