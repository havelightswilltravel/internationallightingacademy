## Important: What This Course Is — and Isn't
This course covers the **knowledge** you need to work safely from mobile elevating work
platforms (MEWPs). It does **not** authorize you to operate one. Under ANSI A92.22 and A92.24
(and OSHA's general training requirements), you may operate a MEWP only after:

1. **Operator training** — classroom/knowledge **and** a practical (hands-on) evaluation by a
   qualified person, for the MEWP Group and Type you will use; and
2. **Familiarization** on the specific make and model — controls, safety devices, decals,
   manual, and any special features — before first use.

Your employer documents both. LT3 skills LT3-S08 and LT3-S09 are signed off only after that
authorized training.

## What Is a MEWP?
"MEWP" is the ANSI term for machines that lift people to a working position on a platform:
scissor lifts, vertical mast lifts, and boom lifts (telescopic and articulating). Vehicle-mounted
aerial devices (bucket trucks) are covered by a separate standard (ANSI A92.2) and OSHA rules for
vehicle-mounted elevating and rotating work platforms; many principles here still apply.

Lighting technicians use MEWPs constantly — high-bays in warehouses, gymnasium fixtures, parking
lot poles, building-mounted floods, atriums.

## Groups and Types
The ANSI A92 standards classify MEWPs by **Group** (where the platform can go) and **Type** (how
it travels).

| Classification | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Group A** | Platform stays **within** the machine's tipping lines (it goes straight up) | Scissor lift, vertical mast lift |
| **Group B** | Platform can extend **beyond** the tipping lines | Telescopic and articulating boom lifts |
| **Type 1** | Travel only when stowed (lowered) | Push-around vertical mast lift |
| **Type 2** | Travel while elevated, controlled from the **chassis** | Less common |
| **Type 3** | Travel while elevated, controlled from the **platform** | Most self-propelled scissors (3A) and booms (3B) |

A self-propelled scissor lift is typically a **3A**; a self-propelled boom lift is a **3B**.
Training is specific to the categories you're trained on.

## The ANSI A92 Family (U.S.)
| Standard | Covers | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **ANSI A92.20** | Design, calculations, safety requirements and test methods | Manufacturers |
| **ANSI A92.22** | **Safe use** — responsibilities, risk assessment, safe use plan, inspections, rescue | Owners, users (employers), operators, occupants, supervisors |
| **ANSI A92.24** | **Training** requirements for operators, occupants and supervisors | Trainers, employers |

In Canada, the parallel CSA B354 series applies. OSHA regulations for aerial lifts (for example,
29 CFR 1926.453 in construction and 1910.67 for vehicle-mounted platforms in general industry) and
OSHA's scaffold rules (which OSHA applies to scissor lifts) are legal requirements; the ANSI
standards are consensus standards that OSHA and courts often look to for good practice.

## Key A92.22 Concepts
- **Safe use plan:** Before MEWP work, the employer ("user") assesses the job: selecting the right
  MEWP, hazards (energized lines, traffic, ground conditions, weather, overhead obstructions),
  fall protection, and **rescue** procedures.
- **Roles:** The **operator** controls the MEWP. **Occupants** are other people on the platform —
  they also need training on safety, fall protection and how to use the emergency controls in case
  the operator is incapacitated.
- **Supervisors** of MEWP operators must be trained on the standard's requirements too.
- **Inspections:** pre-use inspection by the operator before each shift (Lesson 2); **frequent
  inspections** and **annual inspections** by a qualified mechanic per the manufacturer and A92.22.
  Check the machine's inspection decal or records.

## Modern Safety Features (A92.20 machines)
Machines built to the current A92.20 standard include features older machines may lack:
- **Load-sensing system** — warns and stops platform movement if the platform is overloaded.
- **Tilt alarm and interlocks** — stop elevation or driving when the chassis is out of level beyond
  limits.
- **Wind speed ratings** — outdoor-rated machines have a maximum wind speed rating on the decal
  (commonly 28 mph / 12.5 m/s); **indoor-only** machines are rated for zero wind and must not be
  used outdoors.
- **Platform capacity decals** showing maximum persons and weight, which may differ for indoor and
  outdoor use or for extended decks.

## Choosing the Right MEWP for Lighting Work
| Job | Suitable MEWP | Considerations |
|---|---|---|
| Warehouse high-bays over open floor | Slab scissor (3A) | Floor load, rack aisles, overhead sprinklers |
| Gym or atrium with obstructions below | Articulating boom (3B) or narrow mast lift | Floor protection, weight on finished floors |
| Parking lot poles | Boom lift or bucket truck | Ground conditions, traffic, wind, overhead lines |
| Building-mounted wall packs over landscaping | Articulating boom | Soft ground, slopes, curbs |
| Low ceilings in tight rooms | Push-around vertical mast (1A) | Door widths, floor load |

> **Safety:** Using the wrong machine — an indoor-only scissor outdoors, a slab scissor on gravel,
> a boom with insufficient reach that tempts you to lean out — is one of the root causes of MEWP
> incidents. Choose the machine for the job, not the job for the machine you have.

## Key Takeaways
- Only trained, authorized and familiarized operators may use a MEWP.
- Group A stays within tipping lines (scissors, mast lifts); Group B extends beyond (booms).
- Type 1 travels stowed; Type 3 travels elevated from platform controls.
- A92.20 = design, A92.22 = safe use, A92.24 = training; OSHA rules are the legal baseline.
- Respect machine ratings: capacity, wind, slope, and indoor/outdoor use.
