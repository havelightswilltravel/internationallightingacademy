## The Exterior Lighting Family
| Fixture | Typical mounting | Typical voltage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wall pack** | Building wall, 10–20 ft | 120–277V | Often controlled by a button photocell or a contactor; full-cutoff styles reduce glare |
| **Area/site light ("shoebox")** | Pole top, 15–40 ft | 208/240/277/480V | Parking lots; arm or slip-fitter mount; often a twist-lock photocontrol receptacle |
| **Floodlight** | Pole, wall, roof | 120–480V | Adjustable aim — re-aim after service |
| **Canopy light** | Under canopies (gas stations, entries) | 120–277V | Damp or wet location ratings matter |
| **Bollard** | Ground-mounted, 3–4 ft | 120–277V | Frequently hit by vehicles and mowers; base-mounted wiring |
| **Post-top / decorative** | Short poles, 10–16 ft | 120–277V | Walkways, campuses |
| **Roadway (cobra head)** | Utility or owner poles | 120–480V | Often utility-owned — know who owns the fixture before touching it |

## Exterior Electrical Characteristics
- **Higher voltages:** Site lighting is often on **277V** (from a 277/480V system) or **480V**
  (line-to-line) to reduce voltage drop over long runs. Treat every exterior luminaire as
  potentially 480V until you measure. Your meter and PPE must be rated for it.
- **Long runs:** Hundreds of feet of underground conductors mean significant voltage drop and
  capacitance. Low voltage at the last pole can cause drivers to shut down or flicker.
- **Multiple poles per circuit:** One branch circuit daisy-chains through many poles, often with
  **in-line fuses** in each pole's hand hole (Lesson 3).
- **Surge exposure:** Long runs and outdoor locations collect lightning-induced surges. Most LED
  area lights include a surge protective device (SPD) module — often rated 10 kV/10 kA or
  higher (product spec sheets list the rating). Failed SPDs are a common service item.
- **Wet and damp locations:** Luminaires must be **listed and marked suitable for the location**
  — "suitable for wet locations" where exposed to rain and sprinklers, "damp" under canopies and
  covered areas. Check the label before replacing.

## Optics, Cutoff and Aiming
Exterior LED fixtures use precise optics. When replacing or re-aiming:
- Keep the same **distribution type** (IES Type II, III, IV, V) — a Type IV forward-throw fixture
  on a property line pole should not be replaced with a Type V symmetric one, or you'll light the
  neighbor's windows and leave dark areas.
- Many jurisdictions have **light trespass / dark-sky ordinances** and require full-cutoff or
  shielded fixtures. Don't tilt a full-cutoff fixture up to "throw it further" — that creates
  glare and may violate the ordinance.
- Restore **house-side shields** and backlight controls if the original had them.
- Use the **BUG rating** (backlight, uplight, glare) on spec sheets to compare.
- Floodlights: mark the aiming angle before removal, re-aim to the same position.

## Typical Service Calls
| Complaint | First suspects |
|---|---|
| Whole lot dark | Contactor/control (LT3-C03), breaker, photocontrol, time clock |
| One pole dark | Pole fuse, luminaire driver/SPD, photocontrol on that fixture, broken conductor between poles |
| Several poles at the end of the run dark | Open conductor or fault between poles; tripped breaker if all dark |
| Lights on during the day | Failed-on photocontrol, contactor HOA in HAND, welded contacts, shorting cap installed where a photocontrol should be |
| Cycling at dusk | Photocontrol seeing its own light or another fixture's light |
| Breaker trips when wet | Ground fault in underground conductors, splices or a flooded fixture/hand hole (Lesson 4) |
| Water inside lens | Failed gasket, wrong mounting orientation, cracked lens |

## Working on Exterior Fixtures Safely
High fixtures require a MEWP (LT3-C06) or bucket truck with trained operators. Additional
exterior hazards:

- **Traffic:** Parking lots and driveways — set up cones and barricades, wear high-visibility
  apparel, and use a spotter when needed.
- **Weather:** No pole or aerial work in lightning, high wind or icy conditions.
- **Wildlife and insects:** Wasp nests in fixtures and hand holes are common — look before
  reaching.
- **Utility-owned equipment:** Roadway lights on utility poles may be utility-owned and near
  utility conductors; only qualified utility workers may work there.

> **Safety:** Exterior lighting circuits are frequently 277V or 480V, with long runs and multiple
> fixtures fed from one breaker. Before opening a luminaire or hand hole, identify the circuit
> from the panel directory, apply LOTO, and verify absence of voltage at the work location with
> a meter rated for the voltage (live-dead-live). Never rely on the photocontrol or contactor
> being "off" — daytime does not mean de-energized, and a contactor can close at any time.

## Key Takeaways
- Know the fixture family and its typical voltage; assume 480V until measured.
- Replacement fixtures must match location rating (wet/damp), distribution type and cutoff.
- Exterior circuits are long, surge-prone and often fused at each pole.
- Use the symptom table to split control problems from pole/fixture problems.
- Traffic, weather and height hazards add to electrical hazards — LOTO at the panel, not the
  photocontrol.
