## Plan Before You Pick
Every lift gets a plan, even a small one. Before anyone touches the rigging, the crew should be able to
answer these out loud:

1. **What does it weigh?** (Lesson 2.) Round up.
2. **What is lifting it,** and is that machine rated for this load at this reach?
3. **What rigging,** and is its WLL in the hitch we're using more than the load?
4. **Where does it go?** A clear laydown spot with blocking (dunnage) so the sling can be pulled out.
5. **What's overhead?** Power lines, trees, canopies, building edges.
6. **Who does what?** Operator, signal person, tag-line handler, and who keeps the public out.
7. **What could go wrong,** and what do we do if it does?

## The Right Machine for Lifting
A pole is a **suspended load**. It must hang from equipment **designed and rated by its manufacturer
to lift loads**, used within its load chart. Depending on the job, our supervisor assigns a boom or
bucket truck with a rated material-handling jib and winch, a boom truck or crane service, or a rented
machine with a rated lifting attachment.

> **Safety:** Company policy, from our lift training: **a lift is never used as a crane unless the
> manufacturer designed it to lift loads that way.** Never hang a pole from a scissor or boom
> platform, its guardrails or its basket, and never tie a sling to a MEWP to "help" another machine.
> Overloading a platform or side-loading a boom is how lifts tip over.

If the lifting machine is a crane or boom truck covered by **OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart CC**, its
operator must be qualified or certified for it, the rigger must be qualified, and the power-line rules
are stricter than for a lift: unless the line voltage is confirmed and a larger table distance
applied, **no part of the crane, load or rigging may come within 20 ft of a line up to 350 kV**
(1926.1408). With the voltage confirmed by the utility, the minimum distances in **Table A of
1926.1408** apply (for example, 10 ft up to 50 kV and 15 ft over 50 to 200 kV). Use the rule that gives
the most room. Our own 10 ft rule for lifts is the minimum, not the target.

**Long objects near lines.** A pole is long, and it swings and tips. Plan the whole path, not just the
starting point: picture the pole at full height, during lay-down and on the truck, and keep **every
part of it** (and the boom, load line and sling) outside the clearance distance the whole time. A
**tag-line holder** is touching the load: if the pole or boom contacts a line, the person on the tag
line is in the path to ground. Use a dry, non-conductive tag line, keep it as long as practical, and
stop the lift if the load drifts toward a line. Our full overhead and underground line rules, and
what to do after a contact, are in LT3-C05 Lesson 5 (Overhead & Underground Power Lines on Site Work).

A MEWP still has a job here: technicians use one to reach the heads and the top of the pole. In the
boom basket you are **tied off to the platform anchor at all times**, never to the pole you are
rigging (LT3-C06).

## One Signal Person
- Name **one signal person** before the lift. The operator takes movement signals from that person
  only.
- **Anyone can give a stop signal, and the operator must stop.**
- Agree on the signals at the pre-lift talk. Use the **standard hand signals** (ASME B30 / OSHA
  1926 Subpart CC, Appendix A). Our old rigging handout said any signals the operator and rigger agree
  on are fine. Today's rule is tighter: standard signals are the default, and a non-standard signal is
  used only when the standard one doesn't fit the job **and** everyone agreed to it beforehand.
- If the operator can't see the signal person, use radios on a dedicated channel and talk
  continuously.
- If a signal is unclear, the operator **stops** and asks.

**The five signals you must know:**

| Signal | How it's given |
|---|---|
| **Hoist** (raise the load) | Forearm upright, index finger pointing up, small circles |
| **Lower** | Arm down, index finger pointing down, small circles |
| **Stop** | Arm straight out, palm down, swing the arm back and forth level |
| **Emergency stop** | Both arms straight out, palms down, swing both back and forth |
| **Move slowly** | Give the motion signal with one hand and hold the other hand still just in front of it |

## The Load Zone
Most rigging injuries are not caused by broken slings. They happen to **people standing in the wrong
place.**
- **Never stand, walk or reach under a suspended load.** Not for a second, not to steady it.
- **Barricade a fall zone** around the pole with cones or tape. A pole that tips falls its full
  length, and the heads can bounce farther, so make the zone **larger than the pole is tall**. Our
  company policy is to cone off any area where something could fall on someone. Keep cars out of it.
  In an open lot, move the customer's vehicles or ask them to.
- **Use a tag line** (a hand line tied to the base of the pole) to control swing and rotation from a
  distance. Near power lines, use a dry, non-conductive line. Never wrap a tag line around your hand.
- **Watch pinch points.** Keep fingers out from between the base plate and anchor bolts, and between
  the pole and the truck or trailer. Guide with the tag line or with flat palms on the side of the
  load, never with fingers under it.
- **Stand clear of the butt.** When a pole is laid down, the bottom end can kick out sideways.
- **Watch the weather.** Wind catches pole heads like a sail. Stop if gusts make control hard.

## Lift Smoothly
1. **Take a strain:** lift just enough to put light tension on the rigging. Stop. Check that the choke
   is above the center of gravity, the hook latch is closed and the shackle pin is seated.
2. **Test lift:** a few inches only, and only once the pole is free (Lesson 4). Watch the balance; if
   it tilts badly, set it back down and re-rig.
3. **Move slowly.** No jerks, no sudden stops, no swinging over people or vehicles.
4. **Set it down on blocking,** then let the slack out before anyone unhooks.

## Key Takeaways
- Plan the weight, the machine, the rigging, the laydown spot, the overhead hazards and the roles.
- Only equipment rated for lifting carries the pole; a MEWP is never used as a crane.
- Cranes and boom trucks under Subpart CC: 20 ft from lines up to 350 kV unless voltage is confirmed.
- One signal person; standard signals; anyone can call stop.
- Never under the load; fall zone larger than the pole is tall; tag line, not hands.
- Take a strain, test lift, move slowly, set down on blocking.
