## When We Do This
We remove pole lights that have been **hit by a vehicle**, are leaning or cracked, or have
**snapped off at the base plate**. The goals: **nobody gets hurt, the rest of the lot keeps working,
and we leave with everything needed to order the replacement.** This is our company's procedure.

## Tools You Will Need
From our company's pole-removal list:
- **A very large adjustable (crescent) wrench**, the kind sold by the big home centers or an
  industrial supplier.
- **A breaker bar.** Our tip: a piece of **2 in conduit** from the home center slips over the wrench
  handle for leverage.
- **A 6, 8 or 10 ft web sling and a 3/4 in "clevis"** (a rated, marked screw-pin anchor shackle;
  Lesson 1).

Also bring: lock, tag and LOTO kit; a tested voltage meter and proving unit; insulated hand tools;
wet-location wire connectors; **lots of electrical tape and a plastic cup** (see step 9); penetrating
oil and a wire brush; a tape measure; a camera or phone; a notepad or the job form; bags for hardware;
cones and caution tape; a tag line; blocking (dunnage); and your PPE (hard hat, safety glasses,
gloves, high-visibility vest, safety-toe boots).

> **Safety:** A breaker bar multiplies your force, and it can also break a wrench or let you fall when
> the nut lets go. Seat the wrench jaws fully on the nut, set the jaws tight, pull toward you rather
> than pushing, keep your footing, and never stand where a slip puts you into the pole or the base.
> If the nut won't move, use penetrating oil and wait; don't add more pipe.

> **Scope varies by state and city.** In some areas a lighting technician may disconnect and remove
> light poles under the company's license; in others it must be done by, or under the supervision
> of, a licensed electrician, and some jobs need a permit and inspection. Lifts with cranes or boom
> trucks need a certified operator, and local rules may add requirements. Before you do this on a
> job, confirm with your supervisor that it is allowed in that location and that you are authorized
> for it. When in doubt, stop and ask.

## The Procedure

**1. Survey and plan.** Photograph the pole, heads, base and damage before you touch anything. **Look
up:** our 10 ft rule from lines 50 kV and under applies to the lift, the pole and the rigging, and
higher voltage must be shielded by the power company first. A crane or boom truck needs more room
(Lesson 3). Estimate the weight, pick the lifting machine and choose the laydown spot. **Treat a
damaged pole as unstable;** a cracked weld or a pole held up only by its wiring can go at any time.

**2. Barricade.** Cone off a fall zone larger than the pole is tall. Move vehicles out of it.

**3. De-energize and lock out.** Find the circuit (panel, contactor or time clock), switch it off,
**lock and tag it** per company LOTO, and **verify absence of voltage at the hand hole** with a tested
meter (live-dead-live). A knocked-down pole may have pulled its wires loose: **treat every exposed
conductor as live until you've tested it.** Other poles on the same circuit will go dark; tell the
customer.

**4. Record the fixtures.** Before anything comes apart, get the **fixture make, model and
manufacturer** from the label, and note whether **one or both fixtures** need replacing. If the heads
are good and the plan calls for it, remove them from a MEWP (tied off to the platform anchor) to
lighten the pole and protect them.

**5. Disconnect the pole wiring.** At the hand hole, disconnect the pole leads from the branch
circuit splices and fuse holders. Free the supply conductors so the lift won't drag them.

**6. Rig.** Inspect the sling and shackle. Choke the sling **high on the shaft, above the center of
gravity**, just under the arms or tenon so it can't slide up and off. Protect the sling from the pole's
corners. Pin through the sling eye, bow on the running part, eye on the hook with the latch closed.
**Take a light strain**, just enough that the pole can't fall when the last nut comes off.

**7. Remove the anchor nuts and washers.** Work from the side away from any lean. Clean and oil the
threads first, then break each nut loose with the wrench and breaker bar. Note whether there are
**leveling nuts** under the base plate. **Bag every nut and washer and take them with you;** they tend
to disappear if we leave them on the pole.

**8. Lift and lay down.** On the signal person's command, lift the pole **straight up** until the base
plate clears the bolts; dragging it sideways bends bolts and ruins threads. Control swing with the tag
line, set the base end on blocking, and lower the top. Stay clear of the butt. On a snapped-off pole,
remove the shaft and the broken base plate the same way.

**9. Make the base safe.** Insulate the base wiring so nobody gets shocked and water stays out.
Our company rule is **lots of electrical tape, preferably a plastic cup over the ends, and lots more
tape.** Do it in this order:
- If the circuit **feeds other poles**, those conductors will be **live** when power is restored.
  The splices that carry power on to the next pole must be **remade with wet-location-rated
  connectors** so the rest of the lot still works.
- **Cap each dead-end conductor individually** with a connector or insulating cap.
- Then tape, cover the ends with the cup, and tape the cup in place.
- Protect the anchor bolt threads (thread caps or tape) for the new pole, and leave a cone on the
  base; exposed bolts are a trip and bumper hazard.

**10. Restore and check the lot.** Remove your locks per company LOTO and re-energize. Then **check
all the other outdoor lighting** (cover the photocell or use the clock override). The stress the pole
put on the wiring when it was hit or pulled can break splices or conductors at other poles. Report
anything dark.

**11. Collect and haul.** Take the good fixture(s) **with all mounting hardware**, the bagged nuts and
washers, and your measurements (Lesson 5). Secure the pole on the truck or trailer, flag any overhang,
and haul it away per the job plan. Tell the customer what's still energized and what is on order.

> **Safety:** If at any step the plan stops matching what you see, such as a heavier pole, a line
> closer than you thought, or a nut that won't come off, **stop, set the load down and call your
> supervisor.** Nobody gets in trouble for stopping a lift.

## Key Takeaways
- Tools: big adjustable wrench, 2 in conduit breaker bar, 6/8/10 ft web sling, 3/4 in rated shackle.
- Survey and look up, barricade, then lock out and verify at the hand hole; treat a damaged pole and
  its wiring as unstable and live.
- Record the fixtures, disconnect the pole leads, choke above the CG and take a strain before the
  last nut comes off.
- Bag and keep every nut and washer.
- Make the base safe: remake feed-through splices, cap dead ends, then tape, cup, tape.
- Re-energize and check every other light in the lot.
