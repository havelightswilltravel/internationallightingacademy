## Why the Details Matter
A replacement pole has to bolt onto the **anchor bolts that are already in the concrete**, and the new
heads have to bolt onto the new pole. If one number is wrong, the replacement doesn't fit, and the
customer's lot stays dark while we reorder. So, per our company procedure, before we leave the site we
capture:
- **Fixture make, model and manufacturer**, and whether one or both fixtures need replacing
- The **bolt pattern where the fixtures mount** to the pole
- The pole's **overall height and width**, and its **color**
- The **concrete base bolt pattern**

Our company's advice: **it's best to draw it out with the measurements on it.** The drawing does not
need to be to scale. Then take photos with the tape measure in the frame.

## The Base Bolt Pattern
Most parking-lot poles sit on a **square base plate** held by **four anchor bolts** arranged on a
circle, the **bolt circle**, around the opening where the wires come up.

![Top view of a square pole base plate with four anchor bolts on a dashed bolt circle around the pole opening, showing the adjacent center-to-center, diagonal center-to-center and bolt diameter measurements](/media/rigging/bolt-pattern-measurement.svg)

Measure three things:

**1. Adjacent center-to-center:** the distance between the centers of two **neighboring** bolts
(along one side of the square).

**2. Diagonal center-to-center:** the distance between the centers of two **opposite** bolts, across
the opening. On a four-bolt square pattern this is the same as the **bolt circle diameter**, which is
the number pole suppliers usually ask for.

**3. Bolt diameter:** the size of the bolt's threaded rod, not the nut. On the poles we usually see
it is **1/2 in or 3/4 in**, but larger poles use 1 in or more, so measure every time. Measure across
the threads with a caliper or a bolt gauge; matching the nut to a known wrench size is a good
cross-check.

**How to measure center-to-center without finding the center:** hook the tape on the **left edge of
one bolt** and read at the **left edge of the next bolt**, the same edge on both. That distance equals
center-to-center, because you skipped half a bolt at each end.

**Check your numbers.** On a square pattern, the **diagonal is about 1.41 times the adjacent
measurement.**
- Example: adjacent 11 in × 1.41 = about 15-1/2 in diagonal. If you measured 15-1/2 in, you're
  right. If you measured 14 in, measure again.
- If the bolts are not square (a rectangle), record **both** side lengths and the diagonal.

**Also record:**
- **Number of bolts** (four is common, but don't assume).
- **Bolt projection:** how far the bolts stick up above the concrete, and whether there are leveling
  nuts. The new base plate, nuts and washers have to fit on that length.
- **Condition:** bent, rusted or damaged threads, cracked concrete. Bent bolts may have to be repaired
  before a new pole can go up; tell your supervisor.
- **Base plate size** of the old pole, if you have it (square size and thickness), and the bolt slot
  or hole size.

## The Fixture-Mount Bolt Pattern
The heads mount to the pole in one of a few ways. Draw what you see and measure it:
- **Drilled arm mount:** the pole has a hole pattern for the arm, often two or more bolt holes plus a
  wire hole. Measure **hole-to-hole spacing** (same-edge method), **hole diameter**, and the distance
  from the **top of the pole** to the pattern.
- **Tenon top:** a short round post on the top of the pole. Measure the **outside diameter and
  height** of the tenon.
- **Number and position of heads:** single, two at 180°, two at 90°, three or four, and the arm
  length.
- Bring the **good fixture(s) and all of the mounting hardware** back with you. They help confirm
  the pattern and may be reused.

## The Pole
With the pole on the ground:
- **Overall height:** measure from the bottom of the base plate to the top of the pole (not counting
  heads or the tenon unless you note it separately).
- **Width:** shaft size at the base and at the top. Note **square or round**, and **straight or
  tapered**. If you can see the wall at a cut or the hand hole, note its thickness.
- **Material:** steel (a magnet sticks) or aluminum (it doesn't).
- **Color and finish:** dark bronze, black, white, gray, galvanized or other. A photo in daylight helps
  the customer match it.
- **Hand hole:** location and size.

## The Fixtures
From the label inside the door or on the housing:
- **Manufacturer, model or catalog number**
- Wattage or lumen package, voltage, light source (LED or HID lamp type)
- Distribution type, if marked, and any photocell or control
- **Condition:** does one fixture need replacing, or both?

If a label is gone, photograph the fixture from several angles and record its dimensions.

## A Simple Record Sheet

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Base bolts | Number ___ · Adjacent c-c ___ · Diagonal c-c ___ · Bolt dia. ___ · Projection ___ |
| Base condition | Bolts / threads / concrete ___ |
| Pole | Height ___ · Width at base ___ top ___ · Square/round · Straight/tapered · Material ___ · Color ___ |
| Fixture mount | Tenon OD/height ___ or hole pattern ___ · Heads: number and orientation ___ |
| Fixtures | Make ___ · Model/catalog ___ · Watts/V ___ · Replace one / both |
| Photos | Base with tape · pole · heads · labels · damage |

> **Safety:** Measure the base **only after** the conductors are capped and taped and the circuit is
> locked out, or after you've confirmed which conductors are energized. Don't kneel over open wiring
> to read a tape. Keep the cone on the base when you're done.

## Key Takeaways
- Capture fixture make/model/manufacturer, the fixture-mount pattern, pole height, width and color,
  and the base bolt pattern before you leave.
- Base pattern: adjacent c-c, diagonal c-c (the bolt circle) and bolt diameter, plus number of bolts
  and projection.
- Measure same edge to same edge; check that the diagonal is about 1.41 × the adjacent.
- Draw it with the numbers on it (not to scale) and photograph it with the tape in view.
- Take good fixtures and all mounting hardware with you.
