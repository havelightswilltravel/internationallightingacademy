## The Open-Book Trap
"Open book" sounds easy, but with roughly 2 to 3 minutes per question, a candidate who has to
hunt for every answer will run out of time. The goal is to know the code well enough to answer
many questions from memory and to **verify** in the book quickly, while finding the unfamiliar
ones within about two minutes.

## How the NEC Is Organized (2023 NEC)
| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Article 90 | Introduction: purpose, scope, enforcement, arrangement |
| Chapter 1 | General: definitions (Art. 100), general requirements (Art. 110) |
| Chapter 2 | Wiring and protection: branch circuits, feeders, load calculations, services, OCPD, grounding and bonding (Articles 200–250) |
| Chapter 3 | Wiring methods and materials (Articles 300–399) |
| Chapter 4 | Equipment for general use: cords, switches, receptacles, panelboards, luminaires, appliances, heating, motors, A/C, generators, transformers, batteries (Articles 400–490) |
| Chapter 5 | Special occupancies: hazardous locations, garages, health care, assembly, carnivals, agricultural, manufactured buildings, RVs, marinas (Articles 500–590) |
| Chapter 6 | Special equipment: signs, cranes, elevators, welders, pools, PV, fire pumps, EVSE (Articles 600–695) |
| Chapter 7 | Special conditions: emergency, standby, interconnected sources, ESS, power-limited circuits, fire alarm, optical fiber (Articles 700–770) |
| Chapter 8 | Communications systems (Articles 800–840) |
| Chapter 9 | Tables: conduit and conductor dimensions, conductor properties, power-source limits |
| Annexes | Informative: conduit fill tables (Annex C), examples (Annex D), and more |

**Chapters 1–4 apply generally; Chapters 5–7 modify or supplement them; Chapter 8 stands alone
unless it references the others** (90.3). This rule answers many "which article governs"
questions.

## Article Structure and Numbering
- Articles are divided into **Parts** (Roman numerals), then sections (e.g., 250.66), then
  subdivisions: (A), (1), (a).
- Within most articles, the first sections follow a pattern: **.1 Scope**, **.2 Definitions**
  (many definitions were consolidated into Article 100 in the 2023 edition), **.6 Listing**.
- In wiring-method articles (Chapter 3), the same section numbers usually cover the same topic:
  **.10 Uses permitted**, **.12 Uses not permitted**, **.20 Size**, **.22 Number of conductors**,
  **.24 Bends — how made**, **.26 Bends — number in one run**, **.30 Securing and supporting**,
  **.100 Construction**, **.120 Marking**. Knowing this lets you jump to 358.30 (EMT support)
  without the index.

## The Index Method
1. Identify the **key noun** of the question (e.g., "receptacle," "bathroom," "pool").
2. Go to the **index** at the back. Look up the noun, then the sub-entry ("Bathrooms →
   receptacles").
3. Turn to the section and read the whole subdivision, including exceptions.
4. Check that the article's scope fits the question (dwelling vs. other, under vs. over
   1000 V).

If the index fails, use the **table of contents** at the front (or at the start of each article)
to identify the article and part. Always confirm your answer against the text rather than a tab
summary.

## Tabbing Your Book
Commercially printed tab sets are permitted by many states, but **check the candidate bulletin
first**. Common rules:
- Permanently affixed tabs are usually allowed; tabs may need to be bound to the page.
- **Highlighting and underlining** are usually allowed.
- **Handwritten notes, sticky notes with writing, loose pages, and inserted cheat sheets are
  usually prohibited.** A book with prohibited material can be confiscated, or you may be denied
  entry.

### Must-Have Tabs (2023 NEC numbering)
| Tab | Why |
|---|---|
| Art. 100 Definitions | Many questions turn on a definition |
| 110.26 Working space | Table 110.26(A)(1) depths |
| 210.8 GFCI / 210.12 AFCI / 210.52 dwelling receptacles | Heavily tested |
| Art. 220 Load calculations (Art. 120 in 2026) | Calculation questions |
| 230 Services; 240.6(A) standard OCPD sizes | Every OCPD question |
| 250.66, 250.102(C)(1), 250.122 | GEC, bonding jumper, and EGC sizing |
| 300.5 Burial depths | Table 300.5 |
| 310.15 and Table 310.16 | Ampacity, correction, adjustment |
| 314.16 Box fill / 314.28 Pull boxes | Calculations |
| 430 Motors (Tables 430.248, 430.250, 430.52) | Motor circuit sizing |
| 450.3 Transformer protection | Calculations |
| 680, 517, 500–501 | Special occupancy questions |
| 700/701/702 | Emergency vs. standby |
| Chapter 9 Tables 1, 4, 5, 8 and Annex C | Conduit fill and voltage drop |

Highlight key numbers in tables and the words **"not less than," "not more than," "shall,"**
and **"shall not"** in heavily tested sections.

## Practice Drill: Three Lookups
Try each with a timer (answers use 2023 NEC numbering):
1. *Maximum distance between EMT supports?* Chapter 3 pattern → 358.30 → securely fastened
   within 3 ft of each box and at intervals not exceeding **10 ft**.
2. *Minimum cover for direct-buried UF cable under a residential lawn?* Index "Burial depths" →
   Table 300.5 → **24 in** (column 1), unless another column (e.g., GFCI 120-V residential
   branch circuit) applies.
3. *Copper EGC for a 60-A circuit?* 250.122 → Table 250.122 → **10 AWG** copper.

> **Safety:** Fast lookups are a job skill too. In the field, the same habits — read the whole
> section, check exceptions, confirm scope — keep you from installing something that fails
> inspection or, worse, creates a shock or fire hazard.

## Key Takeaways
- Know the chapter structure and the 90.3 rule (Chapters 5–7 modify 1–4; Chapter 8 stands
  alone).
- Use repeating section numbers in Chapter 3 wiring-method articles to jump straight to answers.
- Index → section → read exceptions → confirm scope.
- Tab and highlight only as your bulletin allows; never write notes in the book where prohibited.
- Drill until most lookups take under two minutes.
