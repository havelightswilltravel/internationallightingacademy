## Do the Math on Your Time
Suppose your bulletin says **90 questions in 270 minutes** (4.5 hours). That is 3 minutes per
question on average. But questions are not equal:

| Question type | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Known from memory, quick verify | 30–60 seconds |
| Straight lookup | 1–2 minutes |
| Multi-step calculation | 3–6 minutes |
| Hard lookup or unfamiliar topic | Can take 8+ minutes if you let it |

The danger is the last row. Five questions at 10 minutes each consume almost an hour.

### Budget Example
| Phase | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Pass 1: answer everything you can in ≤ 2 minutes; flag the rest | ~150 |
| Pass 2: flagged calculations and harder lookups | ~90 |
| Pass 3: remaining flagged items, then review | ~30 |
| **Total** | **270** |

Set checkpoints: for a 90-question, 270-minute exam, aim to be through question 30 by about
the 75-minute mark on pass 1, and question 60 by about 150 minutes.

## The Two-Pass (Flag-and-Return) Method
1. **Read the question and all choices.**
2. If you know it or can find it within your time cap (≈2 minutes), answer it.
3. If not, **choose your best guess, flag it, and move on.** Most computer-based exams allow
   flagging and returning — confirm in your bulletin. Never leave a blank: an unanswered
   question is always wrong, and most exams have no guessing penalty.
4. On later passes, work only flagged items.
5. Change an answer only when you find code text that proves the first answer wrong.

## Reading the Question
Exam writers test reading as much as knowledge.

- **Underline qualifiers** (mentally or on scratch paper): *minimum, maximum, not less than,
  shall not, dwelling, other than dwelling, copper, aluminum, continuous, 75°C, wet location.*
- **Identify the occupancy and voltage.** A receptacle requirement for a dwelling unit kitchen
  differs from a commercial kitchen.
- **Identify what is being asked for.** "What size conductor" vs. "what size overcurrent
  device" vs. "what ampacity."
- **Watch units:** feet vs. inches; VA vs. kVA; mm vs. in; one-way vs. round-trip length.

### Example
*"What is the maximum overcurrent protection for 12 AWG copper THHN conductors supplying a
general-purpose branch circuit?"*

Table 310.16 lists 12 AWG THHN at 30 A in the 90°C column — a tempting choice. But 240.4(D)
limits overcurrent protection for 12 AWG copper to **20 A** unless specifically permitted
elsewhere (e.g., motor circuits). The answer is 20 A.

## Calculation Strategy
- Write the formula first, then plug numbers in.
- Keep values unrounded until the final step.
- Check that the answer is reasonable (a 5-hp motor will not need 2/0 conductors).
- If your answer is not among the choices, check: one-way vs. round-trip length, single- vs.
  three-phase formula, 125% applied or not, the right temperature column, and next-size-up rules.
- When choices are close, the difference is usually one of those common errors.

## Eliminating Wrong Answers
Even when you cannot find the section, you can often eliminate choices:
- Values that are not standard (e.g., a 37-A breaker — 240.6(A) has no such size).
- Answers in the wrong units.
- Absolutes that contradict principles you know ("never permitted" when you remember an
  exception).

## Managing Stress and Fatigue
- Take the scheduled break if offered; eat and hydrate beforehand.
- If you hit a run of hard questions, flag and move — the next ten may be easy.
- Visit the testing center (or its website photos) beforehand so the location is not a surprise.
- Arrive early with the exact ID the bulletin requires. Late arrival commonly forfeits the fee.

> **Safety:** Testing day often follows a full work week. Do not schedule overtime or hazardous
> energized work the night before. Rest is part of exam preparation and is a safety practice on
> the job every day.

## Key Takeaways
- Calculate your average time per question from your bulletin and set checkpoints.
- Use two or three passes: answer quick questions first, flag the rest, never leave blanks.
- Underline qualifiers — minimum/maximum, occupancy, material, temperature, units.
- Check 240.4(D), next-size-up rules, and one-way vs. round-trip lengths before choosing a
  calculation answer.
- Eliminate impossible choices, and change answers only with code proof.
