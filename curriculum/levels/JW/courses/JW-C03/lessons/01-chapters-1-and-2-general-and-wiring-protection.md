## How to Use This Review
This is a **high-yield summary**, not a substitute for reading the code. Section numbers follow
the **2023 NEC**; confirm the edition named in your state's candidate bulletin (the 2026 NEC
renumbered content, including moving load calculations to Article 120). Read each section in
your own book as you go and highlight what is allowed by your bulletin.

## Chapter 1 — General
### Article 100 Definitions to Know
Accessible (as applied to equipment vs. wiring methods), readily accessible, approved, listed,
labeled, identified, qualified person, continuous load (maximum current expected for **3 hours
or more**), dwelling unit, bonding jumper, grounded conductor vs. grounding conductor, branch
circuit (multiwire, individual, general-purpose, appliance), feeder, service point, in sight
from (visible and not more than **50 ft**), and location (damp, dry, wet).

### Article 110 Highlights
| Topic | Key rule |
|---|---|
| 110.3(B) | Install listed equipment per its listing and labeling instructions |
| 110.14(C) | Terminations: circuits ≤ 100 A or 14–1 AWG use 60°C ampacity unless equipment is marked 75°C; > 100 A or larger than 1 AWG use 75°C |
| 110.16 | Arc-flash hazard warning labels on equipment likely to be examined or serviced while energized (other than dwellings) |
| 110.26(A) | Working space depth by voltage to ground and condition (Table 110.26(A)(1)); width 30 in or equipment width; height 6½ ft |
| 110.26(C)(2) | Large equipment (1,200 A or more and over 6 ft wide): an entrance at each end of the working space (with exceptions) |
| 110.26(E) | Dedicated electrical space above panelboards and switchboards to 6 ft above the equipment or the structural ceiling |

**Table 110.26(A)(1) — minimum depth (selected)**
| Voltage to ground | Condition 1 | Condition 2 | Condition 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–150 V | 3 ft | 3 ft | 3 ft |
| 151–600 V (151–1000 V in 2023) | 3 ft | 3½ ft | 4 ft |

Condition 1: exposed live parts on one side, no live or grounded parts on the other (or
insulated). Condition 2: live parts one side, grounded parts (such as concrete or block walls)
on the other. Condition 3: exposed live parts on both sides.

### Article 250 Grounding and Bonding
| Topic | Key rule |
|---|---|
| 250.24 | Main bonding jumper at the service; grounded conductor brought to each service disconnect |
| 250.50, 250.52 | All electrodes present must be bonded into the grounding electrode system: metal water pipe (10 ft in earth), building steel, concrete-encased (20 ft of ½-in rebar or 4 AWG copper), ground ring (2 AWG, 20 ft, 30 in deep), rods, plates |
| 250.53 | Rod: 8 ft in contact with soil; single rod must be 25 Ω or less or be supplemented; two rods at least 6 ft apart; water pipe must be supplemented |
| 250.66 | GEC size from service conductor size; rod-only GEC need not exceed 6 AWG Cu; concrete-encased-only need not exceed 4 AWG Cu |
| 250.102(C) | Supply-side bonding jumper sized from Table 250.102(C)(1) (12.5% rule above 1100 kcmil Cu) |
| 250.122 | EGC from OCPD rating: 15 A → 14, 20 A → 12, 30–60 A → 10, 100 A → 8, 200 A → 6, 300 A → 4, 400 A → 3 (copper); increase proportionally when ungrounded conductors are upsized for voltage drop |

> **Safety:** Grounding and bonding questions are not academic. A missing main bonding jumper
> or a neutral bonded downstream of the service can energize metal enclosures and water pipes.
> When inspecting grounding in existing equipment, treat the GEC and bonding conductors as
> possibly carrying current — never disconnect a GEC or bonding jumper on an energized system.

## Key Takeaways
- Memorize 110.14(C) terminal temperature rules and the 110.26 working space table.
- Know the 210.8 GFCI and 210.12 AFCI lists in your exam edition, and the 210.52 receptacle
  spacing rules (6 ft/12 ft, 24 in countertop).
- Service clearances 10/12/15/18/24½ ft; one-family dwelling service at least 100 A.
- 240.4(D) small-conductor limits and the 240.6(A) standard sizes appear on nearly every exam.
- Size GECs from Table 250.66 and EGCs from Table 250.122.
