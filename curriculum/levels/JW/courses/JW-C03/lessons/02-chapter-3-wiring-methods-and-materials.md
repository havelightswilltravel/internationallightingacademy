## The Pattern Is Your Friend
Chapter 3 articles (2023 NEC; confirm your exam edition) share section numbers: **.10** uses
permitted, **.12** uses not permitted, **.20** size, **.22** number of conductors, **.24** bends
(how made), **.26** bends (number in one run — **360° total** between pull points), **.28**
reaming and threading, **.30** securing and supporting, **.42** couplings and connectors,
**.60** grounding, **.100** construction, **.120** marking.

## Article 300 — General Requirements for Wiring Methods
| Section | Key rule |
|---|---|
| 300.3(B) | All conductors of a circuit (including grounded and EGC) in the same raceway, cable, or trench |
| 300.4(A) | Cables through wood framing: holes at least **1¼ in** from the nearest edge, or protect with a steel plate at least **1/16 in** thick |
| 300.4(D) | Cables parallel to framing: at least 1¼ in from the nearest edge |
| 300.4(E) | Cables and raceways under metal-corrugated roof decking: at least 1½ in from the lowest surface of the decking |
| 300.5 | Underground installations (Table 300.5 minimum cover) |
| 300.11 | Secure wiring independently; ceiling support wires may be used only under specific conditions |
| 300.14 | At least **6 in** of free conductor at each box, and at least 3 in outside the box opening for openings less than 8 in |
| 300.21 | Firestop penetrations of fire-resistance-rated walls, floors, and ceilings |
| 300.22 | Wiring in ducts, plenums, and other air-handling spaces |

### Table 300.5 — Minimum Cover (selected, 0–1000 V)
| Wiring method (general locations) | Cover |
|---|---|
| Direct burial cables or conductors | 24 in |
| RMC or IMC | 6 in |
| Nonmetallic raceways listed for direct burial without concrete encasement | 18 in |
| Residential branch circuits, 120 V or less, GFCI-protected, ≤ 20 A | 12 in |
| Under streets, highways, roads, alleys, driveways, and parking lots (all methods) | 24 in |

Read the column headings and footnotes for the exact location (under a building, in a trench
under 2-in concrete, etc.).

## Article 310 — Conductors for General Wiring
- Table 310.16 — the most-used ampacity table (not more than three current-carrying conductors,
  30°C ambient).
- 310.10(G) / parallel conductors: generally **1/0 AWG and larger**, same length, material,
  size, insulation, and termination.
- 310.12 — single-phase dwelling services and feeders: 83% rule.
- 310.15 — ambient correction, adjustment, and rooftop rules.

## Article 312 and 314 — Cabinets and Boxes
| Section | Key rule |
|---|---|
| 314.16 | Box fill |
| 314.20 | Boxes in noncombustible walls may be set back up to ¼ in; in combustible walls, flush or projecting |
| 314.23 | Box support methods |
| 314.27(A) | Ceiling outlet boxes for luminaires: designed and marked to support at least **50 lb** (unless marked for more) |
| 314.27(C) | Ceiling fan boxes: listed and marked for fan support; fans over 35 lb up to 70 lb need the box marked with the maximum weight |
| 314.28 | Pull boxes (conductors 4 AWG and larger): straight pull — **8 ×** trade size of the largest raceway; angle or U pull — **6 ×** the largest raceway plus the sum of other raceways on the same wall and row |

### Pull Box Example
A box has a 3-in raceway and a 2-in raceway on the left wall making angle pulls to the top wall.
Minimum distance from the left wall to the opposite wall: (6 × 3) + 2 = **20 in**.
For a straight pull with a largest raceway of 3 in: 8 × 3 = **24 in**.

## Uses Not Permitted — Common Exam Items
| Wiring method | Not permitted (examples) |
|---|---|
| NM (334.12) | As service-entrance cable; in commercial garages with hazardous locations; embedded in concrete; in wet or damp locations; exposed in buildings exceeding the construction-type limits |
| EMT (358.12) | Where subject to severe physical damage; for support of luminaires except conduit bodies no larger than the tubing |
| PVC (352.12) | In hazardous (classified) locations except as permitted; for support of luminaires; where subject to physical damage unless identified (Schedule 80); in ambient temperatures over 50°C unless listed otherwise |
| FMC (348.12) | In wet locations; hoistways; underground; embedded in concrete |
| ENT (362.12) | Exposed in buildings exceeding three floors above grade (except as permitted); direct burial; where subject to physical damage |

## PVC Expansion
PVC expands about **4 in per 100 ft per 100°F** of temperature change (Table 352.44 gives
exact values; approximately 4.06 in). An expansion fitting is required where the expected
change in length is ¼ in or more in a straight run between securely mounted items.

> **Safety:** Wiring method selection is a safety decision. NM cable installed in a wet location
> can track and fail; EMT used where severe physical damage is expected can be crushed into its
> conductors. When modifying existing wiring, lock out and verify absence of voltage before
> cutting into any raceway or cable — conductors from multiple circuits may share a raceway.

## Key Takeaways
- Use the Chapter 3 section-number pattern (.10, .12, .22, .26, .30) to jump straight to answers.
- 300.4: 1¼ in from framing edges or 1/16-in steel plates; 300.14: 6 in free conductor.
- Table 300.5 cover: 24 in direct burial, 6 in RMC/IMC, 18 in PVC, 12 in residential GFCI circuits.
- Pull boxes: 8 × straight, 6 × angle plus others in the row.
- Learn support intervals: NM 4½ ft, MC 6 ft, EMT/RMC 10 ft, PVC by size.
