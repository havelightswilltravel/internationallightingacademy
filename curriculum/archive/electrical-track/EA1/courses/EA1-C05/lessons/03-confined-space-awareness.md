## Why Electricians Need This

Electrical work often happens in spaces never designed for people: **underground vaults, manholes,
handholes, utility tunnels, crawl spaces, attics, tanks and large equipment enclosures.** Hazardous
atmospheres in these spaces can kill in minutes — and a large share of confined-space fatalities
are would-be rescuers who rushed in to help.

This lesson is **awareness level only.** It does not qualify you to enter a permit-required
confined space. Entry requires site-specific training under your employer's program.

## Definitions (29 CFR 1926 Subpart AA)

OSHA's construction confined-space standard is **1926 Subpart AA (1926.1200–1926.1213)**; general
industry uses 1910.146.

A **confined space** is a space that:
1. Is large enough for a worker to bodily enter and perform work;
2. Has **limited or restricted means of entry or exit**; and
3. Is **not designed for continuous employee occupancy**.

A **permit-required confined space (PRCS)** is a confined space that also has one or more of:
- A **hazardous atmosphere** or the potential for one;
- Material that could **engulf** an entrant (sand, grain, water);
- An internal configuration that could **trap or asphyxiate** (inwardly converging walls, floors
  sloping to a small cross-section);
- Any other **recognized serious safety or health hazard** — including exposed energized
  electrical equipment.

## Atmospheric Hazards

| Hazard | Acceptable range / concern |
|---|---|
| **Oxygen** | Normal air is about 20.9%. OSHA treats below **19.5%** as oxygen-deficient and above **23.5%** as oxygen-enriched (fire hazard) |
| **Flammable gases/vapors** | Hazardous at **10% or more of the lower flammable limit (LFL)** — e.g., methane in sewers and vaults |
| **Toxic gases** | Hydrogen sulfide (sewers, decaying matter), carbon monoxide (engine exhaust, generators near openings) — must be below permissible exposure limits |

**Testing order:** oxygen first, then flammables, then toxics. Oxygen is tested first because
many combustible-gas sensors need adequate oxygen to read correctly. Test at several levels —
some gases are heavier than air (hydrogen sulfide) and some lighter (methane).

### Worked Example — Interpreting a Reading
A four-gas meter at a vault opening reads: O₂ 19.0%, LEL 4%, CO 3 ppm, H₂S 0 ppm.

- O₂ 19.0% is **below 19.5%** → oxygen-deficient → **do not enter.**
- Something is displacing or consuming oxygen. Ventilate, re-test, and have the entry supervisor
  evaluate before anyone enters.

## Roles in Permit Entry

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Authorized entrant** | Knows the hazards, uses equipment properly, communicates with the attendant, exits when told or when a hazard is recognized |
| **Attendant** | Stays **outside** the space for the whole entry, monitors entrants and conditions, summons rescue, and **never enters** to rescue |
| **Entry supervisor** | Verifies conditions, signs the permit authorizing entry, and terminates entry when needed |
| **Rescue service** | Trained and equipped team available in time to respond; non-entry rescue (retrieval line and tripod) is preferred |

## Electrical Hazards in Confined Spaces

Energized equipment inside a vault or manhole is itself a hazard that can make a space
permit-required. In addition:
- Cramped spaces force close approach to live parts and make escape difficult.
- Water and moisture increase shock risk.
- Arcing equipment can ignite flammable atmospheres.
- Electric tools and lights brought into a space must be appropriate for the conditions (for
  example, GFCI-protected or low-voltage, and suitable if flammable atmospheres may be present).

> **Safety:** Before any work in a vault or manhole, energy sources inside must be identified,
> isolated and locked out, and absence of voltage verified with a tested meter
> (live-dead-live), unless qualified workers are performing justified energized work under an
> energized work procedure. Atmospheric testing comes first — never put your head into the
> opening to "take a look" before the air has been tested.

## Rescue — The Most Important Rule

If an entrant collapses, **do not go in**. Call for the rescue team, use the retrieval system if
one is rigged, and keep others out. The same atmosphere that dropped the entrant will drop you.

## What You Should Do as an Apprentice

1. Recognize spaces that may be confined spaces and ask before entering.
2. Look for **posted signs** ("DANGER — PERMIT-REQUIRED CONFINED SPACE, DO NOT ENTER").
3. Never enter without a permit, training and the required equipment.
4. Never remove a manhole cover or open a vault without authorization and barricades.
5. Report any changes in conditions, odors or symptoms (dizziness, headache) immediately.

## Key Takeaways
- Confined space: enterable, limited entry/exit, not designed for continuous occupancy.
- Permit-required spaces add atmospheric, engulfment, entrapment or other serious hazards.
- Acceptable oxygen: 19.5%–23.5%; flammables below 10% LFL; toxics below PELs.
- Test in order: oxygen, flammables, toxics — at multiple levels.
- Attendants never enter; would-be rescuers are frequent victims.
- This lesson is awareness only — entry requires specific training and a permit.
