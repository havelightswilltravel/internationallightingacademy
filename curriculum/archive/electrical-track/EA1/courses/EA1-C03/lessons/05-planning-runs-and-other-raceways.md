## Plan Before You Bend

Good conduit work starts with a plan, not with the bender. Before you cut anything:

1. **Walk the route.** Identify obstructions, structural members, other trades' work and
   available support points.
2. **Locate pull points.** Boxes and conduit bodies break the run into pull sections.
3. **Count degrees** in each pull section — no more than **360°** (358.26 for EMT; similar
   limits apply to other raceway types).
4. **Choose bend angles** that fit the space and keep the pull easy.
5. **Check fill and size** (EA2 covers conduit fill in detail using Chapter 9).
6. **Mark supports**: within 3 ft of each termination and no more than 10 ft apart for EMT.

### Worked Example — Counting Degrees
A pull section from a panel to a junction box contains:

| Bend | Degrees |
|---|---|
| 90° out of the panel top | 90 |
| 30° offset to get onto the beam (two 30° bends) | 60 |
| 45°/22½° three-point saddle over a pipe | 90 |
| 90° turning down the wall | 90 |
| **Total** | **330°** |

330° is within the 360° limit — but just barely. Adding a box offset (another ~20°) still fits;
adding another 30° offset would not. In that case, install a pull box or conduit body to start a
new pull section. Experienced electricians often keep sections well under 360° for long or heavily
filled runs, because pulling tension rises sharply with each bend.

## Working Conduit in Place

- **Measure in place** where possible. Tape measurements from finished surfaces are more reliable
  than measurements from plans.
- Run conduit **parallel and perpendicular** to building lines. Diagonal "point-to-point" runs
  look unprofessional and may violate 110.12 (neat and workmanlike) in exposed work.
- **Group** parallel conduits and keep bends concentric or consistent where runs turn together.
- Leave **pull strings** in empty raceways, and cap ends to keep out debris and water during
  construction.

## Bending Other Raceway Types

### Rigid Metal Conduit (RMC) and IMC
- Hand benders for RMC/IMC are larger and heavier; mechanical, hydraulic and electric benders
  are common for 1 in and larger.
- Conduit must be **cut square, reamed and threaded**. Standard conduit threads are tapered;
  use cutting oil, and clean off chips.
- Threaded joints must be **made up wrench-tight** to maintain electrical continuity for the
  equipment grounding path.
- Bend radius must meet Chapter 9, Table 2 for the conduit size and bender type.

> **Safety:** Power threaders and electric benders can grab clothing, gloves and hair. Use the
> foot switch properly, keep loose items away from rotating parts, and follow the
> manufacturer's guidance on gloves near rotating equipment. Support long sticks with pipe
> stands so they can't whip.

### Rigid PVC Conduit
- PVC is bent by **heating** it evenly with a listed heater box or heating blanket until it is
  pliable, then forming it and holding it until it cools. Use plugs or a bending spring for larger
  sizes to prevent flattening.
- **Factory elbows** are commonly used underground.
- PVC **expands and contracts** much more than steel with temperature. 352.44 requires
  **expansion fittings** where the expected length change between securely mounted points is
  **1/4 in or greater**. This matters for exposed outdoor runs and rooftop runs.
- Solvent-cement joints need clean, primed (if required by the cement manufacturer) surfaces and
  a quarter-turn twist to spread the cement.
- PVC is **not** an equipment grounding path — a wire-type EGC must be installed.

### Flexible Raceways (FMC, LFMC, LFNC)
- Use flex for **short connections** to vibrating or adjustable equipment: motors, transformers,
  luminaires in lay-in ceilings.
- Whether flex can serve as the equipment grounding conductor depends on its type, length, size
  and fittings (see 250.118 and the individual articles). When in doubt, install a wire-type EGC.

## Fixing Bending Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Stub slightly over 90° | Pull back with the bender reversed or by hand on short stubs |
| Offset slightly short | Add a few degrees equally to both bends |
| Dog-leg | Small dog-legs can sometimes be corrected by twisting with two benders; large ones — recut |
| Kink, crush or flat spot | **Cut it out.** Never install damaged raceway (358.24) |
| Wrong length | Recut and re-ream; add a coupling only where it doesn't compromise appearance or specifications |

Scrap is cheaper than a failed inspection or a damaged conductor during the pull.

## Tolerances in This Program

For skill sign-off (EA1-S01 and EA1-S02), bends must measure **within 1/8 in** of the target
dimension, angles must be true to a level, and finished pieces must lie flat without dog-legs.
Many contractors expect the same on exposed work.

## Key Takeaways
- Plan the route, pull points, degree count and support locations before bending.
- Keep each pull section at or below 360° of bends — add a pull point if needed.
- Run exposed conduit parallel to building lines in a neat and workmanlike manner.
- RMC/IMC must be cut, reamed, threaded and made up tight; PVC is heat-bent and needs expansion fittings where length change is 1/4 in or more.
- PVC requires a wire-type EGC.
- Cut out and replace kinked or flattened raceway.
