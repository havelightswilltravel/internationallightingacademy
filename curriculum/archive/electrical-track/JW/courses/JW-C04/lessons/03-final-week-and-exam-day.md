## The Final Week
The last week is for **consolidation**, not new material.

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| 7 days out | Last full-length timed practice exam; review the error log |
| 6–4 days out | Short timed sets on weakest topics; re-run calculation drills; spot-check tabs |
| 3 days out | Review your one-page fact sheet; confirm testing center location, route, parking |
| 2 days out | Light review only; prepare and pack materials |
| 1 day out | Rest. Brief fact-sheet review. Normal dinner, early bedtime |
| Exam day | Light breakfast, arrive early, trust your preparation |

## Materials Checklist
Check against your **candidate bulletin** — the list below is typical only.
- [ ] Admission letter or confirmation number
- [ ] Government-issued photo ID exactly matching your registration name (some states require
      two forms)
- [ ] Approved code book edition, tabbed and highlighted only as allowed
- [ ] Any other allowed references
- [ ] Approved calculator (and spare batteries if permitted)
- [ ] Glasses, layered clothing (testing rooms vary in temperature)
- [ ] Nothing prohibited: phones, smartwatches, notes, papers — leave them in the car or locker

## At the Testing Center
1. Arrive **at least 30 minutes early** (or as the bulletin states). Late arrivals may forfeit
   the appointment and fee.
2. Check-in typically includes ID verification, photograph, signature or palm scan, and
   inspection of your reference books for prohibited notes.
3. Use the tutorial time to learn the testing software's flag and review features.
4. Note the clock and your checkpoints. Start pass 1.

## During the Exam
- Read every word of the stem; underline qualifiers on your scratch paper.
- Two minutes maximum on pass 1; best guess, flag, move on.
- Use your breaks to eat, hydrate, and reset.
- Never leave a question blank.
- In the final 10 minutes, make sure every question has an answer.

## After the Exam
- Many computer-based exams give a pass/fail score report at the testing center; others mail
  results.
- **If you pass:** follow your state's licensing steps — application, fees, proof of experience,
  insurance or bonding if required — and record your license number and renewal and continuing
  education requirements. You may not work as a journeyman until the license is issued.
- **If you don't pass:** the score report usually lists performance by topic area. Check the
  retake waiting period and fee, update your error log from the topic breakdown, and schedule
  targeted study. Many successful electricians passed on a second attempt.

> **Safety:** Your license is a public trust that you will install work that is safe. Continue
> to follow NFPA 70E, lockout/tagout, and verification of absence of voltage on every job —
> licensure means you are now expected to model these practices for apprentices.

## Key Takeaways
- Final week: consolidate, rest, no new topics in the last 48 hours.
- Build your own fact sheet for study — never bring notes into the exam.
- Bring exactly what the bulletin allows; arrive early with the correct ID.
- Two-pass strategy, no blanks, use the breaks.
- After passing, complete the state's licensing steps; after a miss, use the topic report to
  target study.
