## Why Practice Exams Work
Reading the code builds knowledge. **Practice exams build speed, stamina, and judgment** — the
skills that actually decide whether you pass. Candidates who take several full-length timed
exams under realistic conditions consistently report the real exam felt familiar. This course's
question bank is designed in the style of a journeyman exam: mixed topics, open-book lookups,
and calculations, based on the **2023 NEC**. If your state tests a different edition (some are
adopting the **2026 NEC**, which moved load calculations to Article 120 and renumbered other
content), verify each answer's section number in your own edition.

## Set Up Realistic Conditions
Match your state's candidate bulletin as closely as you can:

| Condition | Match the real exam by... |
|---|---|
| Number of questions | Same count (e.g., 80, 90, or 100) |
| Time limit | Same total time, one sitting, scheduled breaks only |
| References | Only the books your bulletin allows, tabbed and highlighted as allowed |
| Calculator | The same approved non-programmable model you will bring |
| Environment | Quiet, no phone, no internet, no help |
| Format | Computer-based if possible (the program's final exam is computer-based and timed) |

The program's JW final exam (80 questions, 240 minutes, 75% to pass) is designed to mirror a
typical journeyman exam. Treat every practice attempt as if it counts.

## A Practice Schedule for the Final Months
| Week | Activity |
|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnostic: one full-length timed exam with no prior review. Score it. Build your error log. |
| 2–3 | Targeted study of your weakest two topic areas; 25-question timed sets daily |
| 4 | Second full-length exam |
| 5–6 | Targeted study; calculation drills; code-lookup drills under 2 minutes each |
| 7 | Third full-length exam; review |
| 8 | Light review, fact sheet, rest; no new topics in the final 48 hours |

Aim to score **at least 10 percentage points above** your state's passing score on practice
exams before your test date — real-exam nerves usually cost a few points.

## The Error Log
After each exam, record every missed question **and every guessed question you got right**
(a lucky guess is a weakness too).

| # | Topic | Category | Correct source | Why I missed it | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 17 | Box fill | Calculation | 314.16(B) | Forgot device counts as 2 | Re-read 314.16(B)(4); 10 drills |
| 33 | GFCI | Lookup | 210.8(A) | Didn't know list for my edition | Re-highlight 210.8(A) |
| 51 | Motors | Misread | 430.32 | Used table FLC for overloads | Underline "nameplate" |
| 64 | Pools | Time | 680.22 | Ran out of time on pass 1 | Flag faster |

### Error Categories
| Category | Typical fix |
|---|---|
| **Didn't know / couldn't find** | Re-read the article; add or improve a tab; index practice |
| **Calculation error** | Re-work with the formula written out; drill similar problems |
| **Misread the question** | Underline qualifiers; slow down on the stem, not the lookup |
| **Changed a right answer** | Change only with code proof |
| **Time** | Enforce the 2-minute cap and the flag-and-return method |

Patterns matter more than single misses. If 40% of your misses are "misread," more studying of
the code will not help — reading discipline will.

## Scoring and Readiness
| Practice score vs. passing score | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Below passing | Focus on fundamentals and your weakest topics; postpone if allowed and practical |
| 0–5 points above | Borderline — keep practicing; work on speed and reading |
| 5–10 points above | Likely ready; continue timed practice |
| 10+ points above, consistently | Ready — schedule or keep your date |

## Reviewing Questions You Got Right
For each correct answer, ask: *Could I find the source again in under two minutes? Was I sure?*
If not, the question belongs in your log too.

> **Safety:** Practice exams test code knowledge, but the code is a minimum safety standard.
> When a question reminds you of a field practice that doesn't match the code, raise it with
> your supervisor — the exam is a chance to correct unsafe habits before they become accidents.

## Key Takeaways
- Take full-length, timed practice exams under the same conditions as your state exam.
- Keep an error log that includes lucky guesses, and categorize each miss.
- Fix the category, not just the question — reading errors need reading discipline.
- Aim for practice scores about 10 points above your state's passing score.
- Verify section numbers in the exact NEC edition your state tests.
