## Scope
Chapter 4 (2023 NEC; confirm the edition your state tests) covers equipment used in almost every
installation: cords, switches, receptacles, panelboards, luminaires, appliances, heating
equipment, motors, air-conditioning, generators, transformers, and batteries. Motors and
transformers were reviewed as calculations in JW-C02; this lesson covers the other rules.

## Article 400 — Flexible Cords and Cables
- 400.10 uses permitted (pendants, wiring of luminaires, portable appliances, connection of
  appliances to facilitate frequent interchange or for maintenance and repair, etc.).
- 400.12 uses **not** permitted: as a substitute for fixed wiring; run through holes in walls,
  ceilings, or floors; through doorways or windows; attached to building surfaces; concealed
  behind walls, ceilings, or floors; installed in raceways except as permitted.
- Ampacity from Table 400.5(A)(1) and (A)(2).

## Article 404 — Switches
| Section | Key rule |
|---|---|
| 404.2(C) | A grounded conductor is required at most switch locations that control lighting loads (with exceptions) |
| 404.8(A) | Switches and breakers used as switches: center of the operating handle not more than **6 ft 7 in** above the floor or working platform |
| 404.14 | General-use snap switches: loads not exceeding the switch rating; motors ≤ 80% of ampere rating |

## Article 406 — Receptacles
| Section | Key rule |
|---|---|
| 406.4(D) | Replacements: GFCI required where now required by code; AFCI or tamper-resistant where required; non-grounding replacements options (GFCI-protected and marked "No Equipment Ground") |
| 406.9 | Wet locations: 15- and 20-A receptacles outdoors in wet locations need an "extra-duty" in-use (bubble) cover and must be weather-resistant |
| 406.12 | Tamper-resistant receptacles required in dwelling units and many other occupancies (child care, schools, hotels, health care waiting areas, etc.) |

## Article 408 — Switchboards and Panelboards
- 408.4: every circuit and modification legibly identified on a circuit directory; no
  identification that depends on transient conditions (e.g., "Bob's office").
- 408.36: each panelboard protected by an overcurrent device not exceeding the panelboard rating.
- 408.41: grounded conductor terminations — each grounded conductor terminates in its own
  individual terminal.

## Article 410 — Luminaires
| Topic | Key rule |
|---|---|
| Bathtub/shower zone (410.10(D)) | No cord-connected, chain-, cable-, or cord-suspended luminaires, track, or ceiling fans within 3 ft horizontally and 8 ft vertically from the top of the bathtub rim or shower threshold |
| Clothes closets (410.16) | Surface-mounted incandescent or LED with completely enclosed light source: **12 in** from storage space; surface fluorescent: **6 in**; recessed incandescent or LED with completely enclosed source: **6 in**; recessed fluorescent: **6 in**. Open or partially enclosed lamps not permitted |
| Recessed luminaires (410.116) | Non-Type IC: ½ in from combustibles; 3 in from thermal insulation |
| Retrofit kits (410.6) | Listed retrofit kits installed per instructions |

## Article 422 — Appliances
- 422.10: branch-circuit rating not less than the marked rating; water heaters (storage, 120
  gallons or less) are considered **continuous loads** — 125% (422.13).
- 422.16: cord-and-plug connection of specific appliances (disposals, dishwashers, trash
  compactors, range hoods) with cord length limits.
- 422.31: disconnecting means for permanently connected appliances — for appliances over 300 VA
  or ⅛ hp, a disconnect within sight or capable of being locked in the open position.

## Article 424 — Fixed Electric Space-Heating Equipment
- Fixed electric space heating is a **continuous load**: branch-circuit conductors and OCPD at
  **125%** of the total load (424.4(B)).
- Baseboard heaters are not installed below receptacle outlets unless the instructions permit.

## Article 440 — Air-Conditioning and Refrigerating Equipment
- Use the nameplate **minimum circuit ampacity (MCA)** for conductors and **maximum overcurrent
  protection (MOCP)** for the breaker or fuse rating.
- 440.14: disconnecting means within sight from and readily accessible from the equipment.
- 210.63: a 125-V receptacle within 25 ft of HVAC equipment for servicing.

### Example
A condenser nameplate reads MCA 23.6 A, MOCP 40 A. Use 10 AWG copper (30 A at 60°C) and a
breaker not larger than 40 A (a 30-A or 35-A breaker is acceptable if the unit starts reliably,
but never larger than the MOCP). Note that 240.4(D) does not limit OCPD for A/C equipment
conductors sized under Article 440 — the MOCP governs.

## Article 445 — Generators
- 445.13: conductors from the generator terminals to the first OCPD have ampacity of at least
  **115%** of the generator nameplate current.
- 445.18: generators have a disconnecting means; one- and two-family dwellings need an
  emergency shutdown outside (2020 and later).

## Article 450 — Transformers (non-calculation rules)
- 450.9: ventilation; clearances marked on the transformer.
- 450.13: accessibility — transformers readily accessible to qualified persons, with exceptions
  for dry-type units 1000 V or less in hollow spaces or open locations.

## Article 480 — Storage Batteries
- Ventilation to prevent hydrogen accumulation for vented lead-acid batteries.
- Working space and disconnecting means requirements; battery systems over 60 V DC need a
  disconnect (480.7).

> **Safety:** Equipment-level rules such as disconnect location (422.31, 440.14) exist so that a
> worker can lock out the equipment where they are working. Always use the disconnect within
> sight, lock and tag it, and verify absence of voltage at the equipment terminals before
> servicing — never rely on a thermostat or control switch.

## Key Takeaways
- Cords are not a substitute for fixed wiring and may not run through walls or be concealed.
- Switch and breaker handles: maximum 6 ft 7 in.
- Closet luminaires: 12 in surface incandescent/enclosed LED; 6 in for fluorescent and recessed.
- Water heaters (≤ 120 gal) and fixed space heating are continuous loads — size at 125%.
- A/C uses nameplate MCA and MOCP; generator conductors at 115% of nameplate current.
