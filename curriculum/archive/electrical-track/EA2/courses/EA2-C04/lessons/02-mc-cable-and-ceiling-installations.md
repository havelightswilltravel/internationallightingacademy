## Type MC Cable (Article 330)

**Metal-clad (MC) cable** is a factory assembly of insulated conductors in a metal sheath. It is the
most common branch-circuit wiring method above commercial ceilings and in metal-stud walls because
it installs quickly and needs no pulling.

| MC variety | Description | Grounding path |
|---|---|---|
| Standard interlocked MC | Interlocking aluminum or steel armor with a green insulated EGC inside | The **insulated EGC** is the grounding conductor; the armor alone is not an EGC |
| MC with armor listed as an EGC (often sold as "MCAP" or similar) | Aluminum interlocked armor with a bare aluminum bonding/grounding conductor in contact with the armor; the combination is listed as an EGC | Armor plus bonding conductor, when terminated per the listing |
| Smooth or corrugated continuous sheath | Welded/continuous sheath | Sheath may be listed as EGC |
| Jacketed MC | MC with PVC jacket for wet, direct burial or corrosive locations where listed | Per listing |

> **Field tip:** Read the cable's printed marking and the manufacturer's literature. If you are
> not sure the armor is listed as an EGC, use and terminate the green insulated EGC.

## Uses (330.10 and 330.12)

MC is permitted for services, feeders and branch circuits, exposed or concealed, in most dry and
(when listed) wet locations. It is **not** permitted where subject to physical damage, and certain
corrosive conditions require protection or jacketed cable. Check the specification — some
projects limit MC to concealed branch circuits and short luminaire or equipment whips.

## Installing MC

1. **Cut properly.** Use a rotary armor cutter, not a hacksaw that can nick conductors. Cut across
   one convolution; twist and slide off the armor.
2. **Anti-short bushings.** Type AC cable requires an insulating anti-short bushing at each
   termination (320.40). Many MC fittings and manufacturers' instructions call for them as well;
   install them whenever required by the fitting or the specification.
3. **Fittings.** Use connectors **listed for the specific type of MC** — interlocked-armor fittings
   are not the same as fittings for smooth-sheath or jacketed cable.
4. **Bending radius.** For interlocked or corrugated armor, the minimum bending radius is **7 times**
   the cable's external diameter (330.24). Tight kinks damage conductors.
5. **Securing and supporting (330.30).** In general, MC cable must be:
   - secured within **12 in.** of every box, cabinet, fitting or other termination (for cables with
     four or fewer conductors, 10 AWG and smaller), and
   - secured at intervals not exceeding **6 ft**.
   Horizontal runs through framing members spaced 6 ft or less are considered supported and secured.
6. **Unsupported lengths.** MC may be unsupported where it is fished in finished walls, and in
   accessible ceilings it may be unsupported for limited lengths (up to **6 ft** from the last
   support) to make connections to luminaires or equipment.

## Support Above Suspended Ceilings (300.11)

Suspended-ceiling grid wires are designed to carry the ceiling — not electrical wiring. Section
300.11 requires raceways and cables above a ceiling to be supported by the building structure or
by **independent support wires** secured at both ends. Key points:
- Do not lay cables on ceiling tiles or tie them to the grid or grid wires (except where the code
  specifically permits branch-circuit wiring for equipment attached to the grid, installed per the
  ceiling manufacturer's instructions).
- Within **fire-rated** floor-ceiling or roof-ceiling assemblies, independent support wires must be
  **distinguishable** from the ceiling's support wires — by color, tagging or other effective means.
- Cable ties and support hardware used above ceilings must be suitable for the space.

## Spaces Used for Environmental Air (300.22)

Above-ceiling spaces used as return-air plenums ("other spaces used for environmental air") limit
the wiring methods that may be used. MC cable **without** a nonmetallic outer jacket, EMT, RMC and
IMC are generally permitted; nonmetallic raceways and nonmetallic-jacketed cables are restricted
unless specifically listed for the use. Ask the foreman whether the ceiling is a plenum before
choosing materials.

## Workmanship (110.12)

The NEC requires installations to be done in a "neat and workmanlike manner." On a commercial
ceiling that means:
- Cables run parallel or perpendicular to structure, bundled reasonably and labeled per specification
- No excess loops coiled above the ceiling
- Boxes accessible, with covers installed
- Cables not draped across ducts, sprinkler pipes or lights

### Worked Example — Support Count
An MC home run travels 40 ft from a junction box to the first luminaire box, then a 5 ft whip
drops to a troffer.
- Secure within 12 in. of each box at both ends of the 40 ft run.
- Interior supports at no more than 6 ft: 40 ft ÷ 6 ft = 6.67 → **at least 7 spaces, so 6 interior
  supports** plus the two end securements (spacing them evenly at about 5 ft 9 in. or less).
- The 5 ft whip is within the 6 ft unsupported allowance for luminaire connections in an
  accessible ceiling.

> **Safety:** Ceiling work places you on ladders or lifts near energized lighting circuits.
> Before connecting to an existing luminaire or junction box, identify the circuit, lock out the
> breaker, and verify absence of voltage at the box live-dead-live. Watch for shared neutrals —
> multiwire branch circuits are common in lighting. Wear eye protection when reaching above
> ceiling tiles (dust, debris, fiberglass).

## Key Takeaways
- Standard interlocked MC needs its insulated EGC; only MC listed with an EGC armor/bonding
  conductor combination can use the armor.
- Use fittings listed for the MC type; respect the 7× bending radius.
- Secure within 12 in. of boxes and at intervals not exceeding 6 ft; luminaire whips may be up to
  6 ft unsupported in accessible ceilings.
- Support from structure or independent, identifiable support wires — never from the ceiling grid.
- Check for plenum (environmental air) spaces before choosing materials.
