## Choosing a Raceway

The engineer usually specifies the raceway type by location in Division 26 of the specifications
(for example, "EMT in dry interior locations above 8 ft; RMC where exposed to damage; PVC
underground"). Your job is to install it correctly and to recognize when field conditions call
for a question. Each raceway has its own NEC article with "uses permitted," "uses not permitted,"
"securing and supporting," and "bends" sections.

| Raceway | NEC Article | Strengths | Limits (summary) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EMT** (electrical metallic tubing) | 358 | Light, fast to bend, serves as an EGC, most common commercial raceway | Not threaded; not where subject to severe physical damage; corrosion protection required in some environments |
| **IMC** (intermediate metal conduit) | 342 | Threaded, strong, lighter than RMC | Heavier labor than EMT |
| **RMC** (rigid metal conduit) | 344 | Maximum physical protection, threaded, permitted in nearly all locations | Heavy; threading equipment needed |
| **PVC** (rigid polyvinyl chloride conduit) | 352 | Corrosion-proof, ideal underground and in wet/corrosive areas | Not an EGC (pull a wire EGC); expands with temperature; Schedule 80 required where subject to physical damage; temperature limits |
| **FMC / LFMC** (flexible metal / liquidtight) | 348 / 350 | Final connection to motors, transformers, equipment subject to vibration | Limited as EGC; support rules differ |

## Support and Securing (paraphrased; verify in the code book)

| Raceway | Secure within … of each box/termination | Support at intervals not exceeding |
|---|---|---|
| EMT | 3 ft | 10 ft |
| RMC / IMC | 3 ft | 10 ft (longer spacing permitted for straight runs with threaded couplings, per the article's table) |
| PVC | 3 ft | By trade size per Table 352.30 — e.g., 3 ft for ½–1 in.; 5 ft for 1¼–2 in. |
| FMC / LFMC | 12 in. | 4½ ft (with exceptions for short lengths at terminals and luminaire whips) |

The EMT and RMC/IMC articles allow the 3 ft securing distance to be increased to 5 ft where
structural members do not readily permit fastening within 3 ft. Straps must be listed or approved
for the purpose, and raceways must be supported from the **building structure** — not from other
trades' ductwork or pipes, and not from ceiling grid support wires except as specifically
permitted (300.11).

## Bends Between Pull Points

For EMT, IMC, RMC, PVC, FMC and LFMC, there may be **no more than 360° of total bends** between
pull points (boxes, conduit bodies). Two 90s plus two 45° offsets (each offset has two bends) is:
90 + 90 + (2 × 45) + (2 × 45) = **360°** — the limit. A kick or saddle adds more. Count every
bend before you start, and plan a pull point if needed.

## Fittings and Terminations

- **Dry locations:** EMT setscrew or compression fittings.
- **Wet locations:** fittings must be **raintight** (typically compression); in concrete,
  **concrete-tight**.
- **Ream every cut end.** Burrs strip insulation during the pull (the raceway articles require
  ends to be reamed or otherwise finished to remove rough edges).
- **Insulating bushings:** where raceways contain **4 AWG or larger** ungrounded conductors, the
  conductors must be protected by a fitting with a smooth, rounded insulating surface (300.4(G)).
- **Dissimilar metals:** avoid aluminum fittings on steel conduit in wet locations, and use
  corrosion-protection compounds where specified.

## PVC Expansion

PVC expands far more than steel: about **4 in. per 100 ft for a 100°F temperature change**.
(Coefficient approximately 3.38 × 10⁻⁵ in./in./°F.) Section 352.44 requires expansion fittings
where the expected length change is **¼ in. or more** between securely mounted items such as boxes
or cabinets.

**Worked example:** An exposed rooftop-to-wall PVC run is 80 ft long between boxes. Expected
temperature range is 10°F to 100°F (90°F change).
ΔL = 3.38 × 10⁻⁵ × (80 × 12 in.) × 90 = 0.0000338 × 960 × 90 ≈ **2.9 in.**
Far more than ¼ in. → install an expansion fitting (sized for the movement and set according to
the installation temperature, per the manufacturer's chart), with straps that allow the conduit
to slide.

## Protection From Damage (300.4) and Burial (300.5)

- Holes through wood framing must be at least 1¼ in. from the nearest edge, or protected by a steel
  plate, for cables and some raceways; EMT, RMC, IMC and PVC are generally exempt from the plate
  requirement.
- Minimum cover depths for underground installations are in **Table 300.5**. In general locations
  (not under buildings, roads or driveways), for 0–1000 V circuits: RMC and IMC 6 in.; PVC and
  other approved raceways 18 in.; direct-burial cables 24 in. Lower depths or greater depths apply
  in specific locations — always check the table and the specifications.
- Raceways entering a building from underground or passing between areas of different
  temperature must be **sealed** to prevent condensation from entering equipment (300.7(A) and 300.5(G)).

## Penetrations

When a raceway passes through a fire-rated wall, floor or ceiling, the penetration must be
firestopped using a listed system so the fire rating is maintained (300.21). Keep the firestop
system's listing documents for the inspector.

> **Safety:** Cutting, threading and bending create sharp edges, flying chips and pinch points.
> Wear eye protection and cut-resistant gloves, support long conduit on stands when cutting, and
> keep hands clear of threader dies. When working overhead on ladders or lifts, follow the fall
> protection rules from LT1/LT3. Before tying into existing energized boxes or panels, the circuit
> must be locked out and verified de-energized.

## Key Takeaways
- Each raceway has an NEC article covering uses, supports and bends; the specification chooses the type.
- EMT and RMC: secure within 3 ft of terminations, support every 10 ft; PVC support spacing depends on size.
- Never exceed 360° of bends between pull points.
- Ream every cut, use raintight fittings in wet locations, and insulating bushings for 4 AWG and larger.
- PVC grows about 4 in. per 100 ft per 100°F — install expansion fittings where movement is ¼ in. or more.
