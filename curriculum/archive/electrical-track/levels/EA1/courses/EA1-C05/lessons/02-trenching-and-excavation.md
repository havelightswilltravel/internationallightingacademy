## Why Trenching Kills

Electricians dig trenches for underground conduit, site lighting, services and feeders. A cubic
yard of soil can weigh around **3,000 lb** — about as much as a small car. Trench collapses happen
without warning and give no time to escape; workers who are partly buried can be crushed or
suffocated. OSHA's excavation standards are **29 CFR 1926 Subpart P (1926.650–1926.652)**.

## Definitions

- **Excavation:** any man-made cut, cavity, trench or depression in the earth's surface formed by
  earth removal.
- **Trench:** a narrow excavation (in relation to its length) made below the surface; in general,
  the depth is greater than the width, and the width at the bottom is not greater than 15 ft.
- **Protective system:** a method of protecting workers from cave-ins — sloping, benching, shoring
  or shielding (trench boxes).
- **Competent person:** for excavations, someone trained in soil analysis, protective systems and
  the requirements of Subpart P, with authority to take corrective action.

## Before You Dig — Utility Locates

- Call **811** (or use the online ticket system) before digging, as required by state law. Wait the
  required time for utilities to mark their lines.
- Learn the **APWA color code**: red = electric; yellow = gas/oil/steam; orange =
  communications; blue = potable water; green = sewer/drain; purple = reclaimed water; pink =
  temporary survey marks; white = proposed excavation.
- Private lines (site lighting, feeders to outbuildings) are usually **not** marked by 811 — use a
  private locator or the owner's drawings.
- Hand-dig or vacuum-excavate within the tolerance zone around marked lines (state law defines the
  distance, commonly 18–24 in on each side).

## Key OSHA Requirements

| Requirement | Rule | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Protective system | Required for excavations **5 ft or deeper**, unless made entirely in stable rock. Under 5 ft, required if a competent person finds cave-in potential | 1926.652(a)(1) |
| Engineered design | Protective systems for excavations **deeper than 20 ft** must be designed by a registered professional engineer | 1926.652(b), (c) |
| Means of egress | Stairway, ladder, ramp or other safe exit in trenches **4 ft or deeper**, within **25 ft** of lateral travel | 1926.651(c)(2) |
| Spoil piles | Keep excavated material and equipment at least **2 ft** from the edge (or use retaining devices) | 1926.651(j)(2) |
| Inspections | Competent person inspects daily before work, as needed during the shift, and after rain or other hazard-increasing events | 1926.651(k) |
| Hazardous atmospheres | Test where oxygen deficiency or hazardous atmospheres could reasonably exist in excavations deeper than **4 ft** | 1926.651(g) |
| Water | No work in excavations with accumulated water unless adequate precautions are taken | 1926.651(h) |

## Soil Classification and Sloping

Appendix A to Subpart P classifies soil; Appendix B gives maximum allowable slopes for excavations
less than 20 ft deep.

| Soil type | Description (simplified) | Max slope (H:V) | Approx. angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stable rock** | Natural solid mineral matter | Vertical | 90° |
| **Type A** | Cohesive soils like clay with high unconfined compressive strength; no fissures or vibration | ¾ : 1 | 53° |
| **Type B** | Cohesive soils of medium strength, angular gravel, silt; previously disturbed soils that aren't Type C | 1 : 1 | 45° |
| **Type C** | Granular soils (sand, gravel), submerged soil, soil with seeping water | 1½ : 1 | 34° |

Soil that has been **previously disturbed** (such as backfill around existing utilities) or is
subject to **vibration** from traffic or equipment can't be classified as Type A. When in doubt,
the competent person must treat soil as Type C.

### Worked Example — Sloped Trench Top Width
You need a trench **8 ft deep** with a **2 ft** wide bottom in **Type C** soil, sloped on both sides.

- Slope 1½ : 1 → for every 1 ft of depth, the side moves back 1.5 ft.
- Horizontal setback each side = 8 × 1.5 = **12 ft**
- Top width = 2 + 12 + 12 = **26 ft**

That's a lot of excavation for one conduit run, which is why **trench boxes** (shields) are common:
the box protects workers inside it, and the trench walls can be closer to vertical. Workers must
stay inside the shield, and the shield must extend high enough to protect them.

### Worked Example — Type B
Same trench in Type B soil (1 : 1): setback = 8 ft per side; top width = 2 + 8 + 8 = **18 ft**.

## Protective System Options

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Sloping** | Cut the walls back at an angle so they won't collapse |
| **Benching** | Cut the walls in steps (not permitted in Type C soil) |
| **Shoring** | Hydraulic, mechanical or timber supports hold the walls in place |
| **Shielding** | Trench boxes protect workers from collapsing soil (don't prevent collapse) |

## Warning Signs of Collapse

- Cracks or fissures in the ground parallel to the trench
- Bulging, sloughing or spalling walls
- Water seeping into the trench or pooling at the bottom
- Soil falling from the face
- Heavy equipment, vehicles or spoil piles close to the edge

> **Safety:** Never enter an unprotected trench 5 ft or deeper, and never enter any trench the
> competent person hasn't inspected that day. Never jump into or climb the walls of a trench —
> use the ladder. If you see warning signs, get out and notify the competent person. Do not
> attempt to rescue a buried co-worker by entering an unprotected trench; call 911 and the
> competent person. Before placing conductors in a trench, confirm any existing conductors you
> uncover are identified and, if they must be worked on, locked out and verified de-energized.

## Underground Electrical Work Reminders

- Burial depths are set by NEC 300.5 and Table 300.5 (EA1-C02 lookup practice).
- Warning ribbon is required above underground service conductors not encased in concrete
  (300.5(D)(3)) and is good practice above all buried conductors.
- Backfill must not contain large rocks or debris that could damage raceways or cables (300.5(F)).

## Key Takeaways
- Call 811 before digging; private lines are not marked by 811.
- Protective systems are required at 5 ft or deeper (unless stable rock); PE design beyond 20 ft.
- Ladder or other exit within 25 ft of lateral travel in trenches 4 ft or deeper.
- Spoils at least 2 ft back from the edge; daily competent-person inspection.
- Max slopes: Type A ¾:1, Type B 1:1, Type C 1½:1.
- Never enter an unprotected or uninspected trench.
