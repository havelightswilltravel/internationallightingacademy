## Why Device Work Matters

Devices are what customers touch. A receptacle that is reversed, loose or wrongly rated is a
shock or fire hazard — and it's the first thing an inspector checks at final. In the lighting
track you replaced devices occasionally; as an apprentice you will install hundreds, so they need
to be right every time.

> **Safety:** Before installing or replacing any device, de-energize the circuit, apply LOTO per
> your company procedure, and verify absence of voltage at the device with a tested meter
> (live-dead-live). Remember that a box may contain more than one circuit — test every conductor
> in the box, not just the one you plan to touch. Non-contact testers are for screening only.

## NEMA Configurations

Receptacles and plugs are standardized by NEMA so that equipment can only be plugged into a
matching voltage and current rating.

| NEMA | Rating | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 5-15R | 125 V, 15 A, 2-pole 3-wire grounding | General-purpose receptacles |
| 5-20R | 125 V, 20 A (T-shaped neutral slot) | Kitchens, bathrooms, garages, commercial |
| 6-15R / 6-20R | 250 V, 15/20 A | Window AC units, shop tools |
| 14-30R | 125/250 V, 30 A, 3-pole 4-wire | Electric dryers |
| 14-50R | 125/250 V, 50 A | Ranges, EV chargers |
| L5-20R / L6-30R | Locking 125 V 20 A / 250 V 30 A | Commercial equipment, generators |

Learn to recognize these by sight. The "L" prefix means a locking (twist-lock) configuration.

## Receptacle Ratings and Branch Circuits

The NEC (210.21(B)) coordinates receptacle ratings with the branch circuit:

- A **single receptacle** on an individual branch circuit must have a rating not less than the
  branch circuit. A single 15 A receptacle may not be the only outlet on a 20 A individual
  branch circuit.
- Where a branch circuit supplies **two or more** receptacles or outlets, the receptacle ratings
  must match Table 210.21(B)(3): on a **20 A** circuit, receptacles may be rated **15 A or 20 A**;
  on a **15 A** circuit, receptacles must be rated 15 A.
- A **duplex** receptacle counts as two receptacles.

## Terminal Identification

| Terminal | Color | Connect to |
|---|---|---|
| Brass (darker) screw | — | Ungrounded ("hot") conductor |
| Silver (light) screw | — | Grounded (neutral) conductor — white or gray |
| Green hex screw | Green | Equipment grounding conductor |

On a 5-15R, the **longer (wider) slot** is the neutral and the **shorter slot** is the hot.
Reversed polarity doesn't stop most equipment from working, but it can leave a lamp's screw
shell or an appliance's chassis-side switch energized — a shock hazard.

## Termination Methods

1. **Side-wire (screw terminal):** strip about 3/4 in, form a hook, and wrap it **clockwise**
   around the screw (the direction it tightens) at least two-thirds of the way around. One
   conductor per screw unless the device is marked for more.
2. **Back-wire / screw-clamp (pressure plate):** strip to the strip gauge, insert straight, and
   tighten the screw. Usually accepts 14 or 12 AWG and often two conductors per terminal —
   check the device markings.
3. **Push-in ("backstab"):** where provided, push-in terminals are commonly listed only for
   **14 AWG solid copper**. They depend on a small spring contact and have a long field history of
   loosening and overheating. Many contractors prohibit them; this program recommends side-wire or
   screw-clamp terminations.

**Torque matters.** 110.14(D) in the 2023 NEC requires that where a tightening torque is
indicated (on the equipment or in its instructions), a calibrated torque tool be used to achieve
it, unless the manufacturer provides another method. Many receptacles specify a torque in
pound-inches on the device or packaging.

**Free conductor.** Leave at least 6 in of free conductor at each box (300.14), and fold
conductors neatly into the box so the device mounts without forcing.

## Grounding the Receptacle

The receptacle's grounding terminal must be connected to the equipment grounding conductor.
- In a **metal box**, the EGC is connected to the box with a grounding screw or listed device, and
  a bonding jumper connects box to receptacle — unless the receptacle is a listed
  **self-grounding** type used in a surface-mounted box or meeting the conditions of 250.146.
- In a **nonmetallic box**, the EGC connects directly to the receptacle's green screw.
- Multiple EGCs in a box are spliced together with a listed connector, with a pigtail to the
  device, so removing a device doesn't interrupt the ground path downstream.

## Special Receptacle Types

| Type | Requirement (paraphrased) | Section |
|---|---|---|
| **Tamper-resistant (TR)** | Required in dwelling units (and several other occupancies such as child care facilities) for most 15 and 20 A receptacles; the 2023 edition extended the dwelling requirement to include 250 V receptacles in that range | 406.12 |
| **Weather-resistant (WR)** | 15 and 20 A, 125 and 250 V nonlocking receptacles in damp and wet locations must be listed weather-resistant | 406.9 |
| **In-use ("bubble") covers** | 15 and 20 A receptacles in wet locations need an enclosure that is weatherproof whether or not a plug is inserted; outlet box hoods must be listed and identified "extra-duty" | 406.9(B)(1) |
| **Isolated ground (IG)** | Orange triangle marking; grounding terminal insulated from the yoke | 406.3(D) |

## Replacing Receptacles in Existing Buildings

406.4(D) governs replacements — a frequent service-call situation:
- If the location now requires **GFCI** protection, the replacement must be GFCI protected.
- If the location now requires **AFCI** protection, the replacement must provide it (by one of the
  methods permitted), with limited exceptions.
- Where no EGC exists in the box, a **non-grounding** receptacle may replace a non-grounding
  receptacle, or a **GFCI** receptacle (or GFCI-protected grounding-type receptacles) may be used,
  marked **"No Equipment Ground"** (and downstream receptacles marked "GFCI Protected" and "No
  Equipment Ground"). Do not connect the grounding terminal to the neutral — a "bootleg ground" is
  dangerous and illegal.
- Replacements must be **tamper-resistant** and **weather-resistant** where those rules apply.

## Key Takeaways
- Match NEMA configuration and receptacle rating to the circuit (210.21(B)).
- Brass = hot, silver = neutral, green = EGC; the wide slot is neutral.
- Prefer side-wire or screw-clamp terminations; torque to the marked value (110.14(D)).
- Pigtail grounds so the ground path stays continuous.
- Know TR (406.12), WR and in-use covers (406.9), and replacement rules (406.4(D)).
- Never create a bootleg ground; use GFCI and "No Equipment Ground" labeling where permitted.
- Always LOTO and verify every conductor in the box before work.
