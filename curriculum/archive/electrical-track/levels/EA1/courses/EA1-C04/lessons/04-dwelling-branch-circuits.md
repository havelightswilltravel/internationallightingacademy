## Required Dwelling Branch Circuits

Beyond general lighting and receptacle circuits, 210.11(C) requires specific 20 A branch circuits
in dwelling units (2023 NEC; paraphrased):

| Circuit | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Small-appliance** | Two or more 20 A circuits for kitchen, pantry, breakfast room, dining room and similar area receptacles covered by 210.52(B) |
| **Laundry** | At least one 20 A circuit for laundry receptacle outlets |
| **Bathroom** | At least one 20 A circuit for bathroom receptacles (it may serve all bathrooms' receptacles, or all outlets in one bathroom) |
| **Garage** | At least one 120 V, 20 A circuit for receptacle outlets in attached garages and in detached garages with electric power |

Small-appliance circuits generally may not serve other outlets (with exceptions such as a
receptacle for an electric clock or for gas-range ignition/supplemental equipment) — see
210.52(B).

### Conductor and Breaker Pairing

| Branch circuit | Minimum copper conductor | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 15 A | 14 AWG | 240.4(D) |
| 20 A | 12 AWG | 240.4(D) |
| 30 A | 10 AWG | 240.4(D) |

Upsize for long runs (voltage drop) as needed — 12 AWG on a 15 A circuit is fine; 14 AWG on a 20 A
breaker is a violation.

### Continuous Loads

A **continuous load** runs at maximum current for 3 hours or more. The branch-circuit overcurrent
device must be rated at least **125% of the continuous load** plus 100% of non-continuous load
(210.20(A)). Turned around, a continuous load on a 20 A circuit should not exceed 16 A:

- 20 A × 0.80 = 16 A; at 120 V, 16 A × 120 V = **1,920 VA**

## General Receptacle Placement — 210.52(A)

In habitable rooms such as bedrooms, living rooms, dens and dining rooms:

- Receptacles must be placed so that **no point along the floor line of any wall space is more than
  6 ft** from a receptacle — which works out to a maximum of **12 ft between receptacles**.
- A **wall space** is any wall **2 ft or more** wide, including space around corners. Fixed panels of
  sliding glass doors count as wall space; doorways, fireplaces and fixed cabinets break the wall
  line.
- Receptacles more than 5½ ft above the floor, or part of a luminaire or appliance, don't count.

### Worked Example — Bedroom Layout
A 12 ft × 14 ft bedroom has a single 3 ft entry door at a corner. Wall line from one side of the
door, around the room, to the other side: 12 + 14 + 12 + 14 − 3 = **49 ft**.

- First receptacle: within **6 ft** of the door edge.
- Each receptacle covers 6 ft on each side → each covers up to 12 ft of wall line.
- 49 ÷ 12 = 4.08 → **5 receptacles** minimum.
- Practical layout: at 6, 18, 30, 42 ft, and one more within 6 ft of the far side of the door
  (for example at 45 ft) — or spread evenly at about 10 ft spacing.

## Kitchen Countertop Receptacles — 210.52(C)

- Every **wall countertop space 12 in or wider** needs a receptacle.
- Receptacles must be placed so **no point along the wall line is more than 24 in** from a
  receptacle — i.e., a maximum of **48 in between** receptacles, and no more than 24 in from the ends.
- Receptacles must be located within a limited height above the countertop (the 2023 edition sets
  a maximum of 20 in above the countertop) and not face-up in the work surface unless listed for
  that use.
- The **island and peninsula** rules were significantly revised in the 2023 edition — read
  210.52(C)(2) in your adopted edition carefully.
- All kitchen countertop receptacles require GFCI protection (210.8(A)) and are supplied by the
  small-appliance circuits.

### Worked Example — Counter Run
A straight wall counter is **10 ft** (120 in) long between the end wall and the refrigerator, with no
sink or range break.

- Each receptacle covers up to 24 in on each side → 48 in of counter.
- 120 ÷ 48 = 2.5 → **3 receptacles** minimum (for example at 20 in, 60 in and 100 in from one end).

## Other Required Receptacle Outlets (2023 NEC, paraphrased)

| Location | Requirement | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Bathrooms | At least one receptacle within 3 ft of the outside edge of each basin | 210.52(D) |
| Outdoors (one- and two-family) | At least one at the **front** and one at the **back**, readily accessible from grade and not more than 6½ ft above grade | 210.52(E) |
| Laundry | At least one receptacle for the laundry area | 210.52(F) |
| Basements, garages, accessory buildings | Receptacles required in unfinished basement portions and in garages/accessory buildings with power (check the edition's per-space rules) | 210.52(G) |
| Hallways | At least one receptacle in hallways **10 ft or more** long | 210.52(H) |
| Foyers | Foyers over 60 ft² need receptacles on qualifying wall spaces | 210.52(I) |

## Lighting Outlets — 210.70

- At least one **wall-switch-controlled lighting outlet** in every habitable room, kitchen and
  bathroom (in some rooms a switched receptacle is permitted instead, but not in kitchens or
  bathrooms).
- Hallways, stairways, attached garages and detached garages with power need switched lighting.
- **Stairways with six or more risers** between floor levels need a switch at **each floor level**
  (3-way switching).
- Attics, crawl spaces, utility rooms and basements used for storage or containing equipment need
  a lighting outlet with a switch at the point of entry.

> **Safety:** Rough-in layout happens in active construction. Watch for open floor penetrations,
> nails in framing, and other trades overhead. When adding to an existing panel or circuit,
> de-energize, apply LOTO and verify absence of voltage before landing any conductor.

## Putting It Together — A Simple Panel Directory
A small house might include: 2 × 20 A small-appliance (kitchen/dining), 1 × 20 A laundry, 1 × 20 A
bathrooms, 1 × 20 A garage, several 15 A or 20 A AFCI-protected bedroom/living circuits, plus
dedicated circuits for the dishwasher, disposal, microwave, range, dryer, water heater and HVAC.
Each must be identified on the panel directory clearly and specifically (408.4(A)).

## Key Takeaways
- 210.11(C): two+ small-appliance, one laundry, one bathroom and one garage 20 A circuit.
- 14 AWG = 15 A, 12 AWG = 20 A, 10 AWG = 30 A for copper (240.4(D)).
- Continuous loads: OCPD at 125%, or load at no more than 80% of the breaker.
- General rooms: no point more than 6 ft from a receptacle; wall spaces 2 ft or wider count.
- Countertops: no point more than 24 in from a receptacle; spaces 12 in or wider need one.
- Know bathroom, outdoor, laundry, hallway and lighting-outlet rules; verify edition-specific changes.
