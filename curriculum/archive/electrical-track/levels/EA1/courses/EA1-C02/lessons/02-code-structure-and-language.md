## How the Book Is Organized

The NEC is divided into an introduction, nine chapters and informative annexes. Article 90
(Introduction) explains how the chapters relate to each other — this is one of the most
important concepts in navigating the Code.

| Chapter | Title | How it applies |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | General | Applies generally to all installations |
| 2 | Wiring and Protection | Applies generally |
| 3 | Wiring Methods and Materials | Applies generally |
| 4 | Equipment for General Use | Applies generally |
| 5 | Special Occupancies | Supplements or modifies Chapters 1–4 |
| 6 | Special Equipment | Supplements or modifies Chapters 1–4 |
| 7 | Special Conditions | Supplements or modifies Chapters 1–7 as applicable |
| 8 | Communications Systems | Stands on its own; Chapters 1–7 apply only where specifically referenced |
| 9 | Tables | Apply as referenced elsewhere in the Code |
| Annexes | Informative material | Not part of the enforceable requirements |

The practical rule: **start in Chapters 1–4 for every job, then check whether Chapters 5, 6 or 7
change anything.** For example, a receptacle in a commercial kitchen follows Chapter 2 and 4
rules; a receptacle near a swimming pool follows those rules *plus* the modifications in
Article 680 (Chapter 6).

## Reading a Section Number

Take **210.8(A)(1)** as an example:

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 210 | Article 210 — Branch Circuits (Chapter **2**) |
| .8 | Section 8 — Ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection for personnel |
| (A) | First-level subdivision — Dwelling units |
| (1) | Second-level subdivision — the first listed location |

The first digit of the article number tells you the chapter: Article 314 is in Chapter 3, Article
406 is in Chapter 4. Articles are often divided into **Parts** (Part I, Part II…) that group
related sections, such as Part I General, Part II Installation and Part III Construction
Specifications in most wiring-method articles.

## Code Language — Mandatory, Permissive and Explanatory

Article 90 defines how to read the rules. Getting this right matters on exams and with
inspectors.

| Wording | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| "shall" / "shall not" | **Mandatory** | Required or prohibited |
| "shall be permitted" / "shall not be required" | **Permissive** | Allowed, but optional |
| Informational Note | **Explanatory** | Background, references, guidance — **not enforceable** |
| Exception | **Modifies a rule** | Applies only to the rule it follows, under its stated conditions |

**Exceptions** come in two kinds: mandatory exceptions use "shall," and permissive exceptions
use "shall be permitted." An exception applies only to the specific rule it is placed under —
don't stretch it to cover other rules.

**Informational notes** often point to useful standards or practices (such as voltage-drop
recommendations), but an inspector cannot fail an installation for not following an
informational note. However, project specifications may make that guidance contractual.

## Article 100 — Definitions

Words in the NEC frequently have narrower meanings than in everyday speech. Many exam questions
and field disputes turn on a definition. In the **2023 edition, definitions were consolidated
into Article 100**, with a bracketed reference after many definitions showing the article(s)
where the term is used. If a question hinges on a word, look it up in Article 100 first.

Key definitions to know cold (paraphrased — read the exact wording in your code book):

| Term | Paraphrased meaning |
|---|---|
| **Approved** | Acceptable to the AHJ |
| **Listed** | Included on a list by an organization acceptable to the AHJ that evaluates products, indicating they meet standards |
| **Labeled** | Bearing a label/mark of such an organization |
| **Identified** (equipment) | Recognizable as suitable for a specific purpose, function or environment |
| **Accessible** (equipment) | Not guarded by locked doors, elevation or other means — can be reached |
| **Readily accessible** | Can be reached quickly for operation or inspection without climbing over obstacles, removing obstacles, or using portable ladders |
| **Branch circuit** | The circuit conductors between the final overcurrent device and the outlet(s) |
| **Feeder** | Conductors between the service equipment (or separately derived system/other source) and the final branch-circuit overcurrent device |
| **Continuous load** | A load where maximum current is expected to continue for 3 hours or more |
| **Grounded conductor** | A system or circuit conductor that is intentionally grounded (often, but not always, the neutral) |
| **Equipment grounding conductor (EGC)** | Conductive path that connects normally non-current-carrying metal parts of equipment together and to the system grounded conductor and/or grounding electrode conductor |
| **Dwelling unit** | One or more rooms for one or more persons, with permanent provisions for living, sleeping, cooking and sanitation |
| **Qualified person** | One with skills and knowledge related to the equipment and installation, who has received safety training to recognize and avoid the hazards involved |

Notice the difference between *accessible* and *readily accessible*: a disconnect behind a
locked door or above a ceiling that requires a ladder may be accessible but is **not readily
accessible**. Several rules (for example, many disconnecting-means rules) require readily
accessible locations.

## Using the Index and Table of Contents

- **Index** (back of the book): alphabetical by keyword, with sub-entries. Think of the *most
  specific noun* — "Receptacles, ground-fault circuit-interrupter protection" rather than "plugs."
- **Table of contents** (front): fastest once you know which article covers a topic.
- **Article scopes**: the first section of most articles (xxx.1) states its scope. Reading the
  scope confirms you're in the right place.
- **Tabs**: most electricians tab their book by article. Many licensing exams allow
  permanently attached tabs but prohibit handwritten notes — check your exam's rules.

## Key Takeaways
- Chapters 1–4 apply generally; 5–7 supplement or modify them; Chapter 8 stands alone; Chapter 9 is tables.
- The first digit of an article number tells you the chapter.
- "Shall" is mandatory, "shall be permitted" is permissive, informational notes are not enforceable.
- Exceptions apply only to the rule they follow.
- In 2023, definitions were consolidated in Article 100 — check it whenever a word matters.
- Know the difference between accessible and readily accessible.
