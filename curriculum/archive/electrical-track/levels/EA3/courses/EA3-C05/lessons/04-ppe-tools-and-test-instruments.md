## Shock Protection: Rubber Insulating Gloves

Voltage-rated rubber insulating gloves are the most important shock PPE for an electrician.

| Class | Maximum use voltage (AC) | Common label color |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | 500 V | Beige |
| 0 | 1,000 V | Red |
| 1 | 7,500 V | White |
| 2 | 17,000 V | Yellow |

For building systems up to 480 V, Class 00 (where the voltage is 500 V or less) or Class 0 gloves
are typical. Many employers standardize on Class 0 for 480 V work.

**Rules for gloves (based on OSHA 29 CFR 1910.137 and ASTM standards):**
- Wear **leather protectors** over rubber gloves. (Protectors may be omitted only in limited
  cases allowed by the standards, such as Class 0 gloves where high finger dexterity is needed
  for small equipment and parts — check your program.)
- **Inspect and air-test** before each day's use and whenever damage is suspected: roll or
  inflate the glove and look and listen for leaks, cuts, punctures, ozone cracking, swelling or
  embedded objects.
- Check the **test date**. Gloves are electrically retested at intervals not exceeding
  **6 months** in service; new gloves may not be issued if more than 12 months have passed since
  their last test.
- Store gloves in a glove bag, not folded or crushed in a toolbox, away from heat, sunlight and
  ozone sources.

## Arc-Flash PPE

Arc-rated (AR) clothing is labeled with an **arc rating** (ATPV or EBT) in cal/cm². The arc
rating of the complete system must be at least the incident energy on the label (or the minimum
for the PPE category).

| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| AR shirt and pants or coverall | Long sleeves, sleeves rolled down and buttoned; tuck or close as designed |
| AR face shield + balaclava, or AR hood | Face shield must have wrap-around guarding; hood required at higher categories |
| Safety glasses or goggles | Always worn under face shields and hoods |
| Hearing protection (ear canal inserts) | Arc blasts are extremely loud |
| Hard hat (Class E) | Worn under the hood where required |
| Leather or rubber insulating gloves with protectors | Leather protectors provide arc protection for the hands |
| Leather footwear | As required by the PPE category or incident energy |

**Undergarments:** Must be non-melting natural fiber (cotton, wool) or arc-rated. Synthetic
undergarments like polyester or nylon can melt into skin during an arc.

**Layering:** The arc rating of layered AR garments is determined by testing the system, not
by adding the individual ratings.

Inspect AR clothing before each use. Remove from service if it is contaminated with flammable
materials (grease, oil) or damaged (holes, tears). Follow the manufacturer's laundering
instructions — no bleach or fabric softener unless allowed.

## Insulated and Insulating Tools

- **Insulated hand tools** rated for 1,000 V (marked with the double-triangle symbol and the
  rating, tested to ASTM/IEC standards) are used within the restricted approach boundary.
- Inspect before use: no cracks, cuts, nicks or exposed metal beneath the insulation layer.
- Fiberglass hot sticks, insulated ladders (fiberglass, not aluminum), and insulating blankets or
  covers are used where the task calls for them.

## Test Instruments

Your meter is safety equipment. A failure inside a meter during a measurement on a high-energy
circuit can cause an arc flash in your hands.

### Measurement categories
| Category | Location | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| CAT II | Receptacle-connected loads | Plug-in appliances, tools |
| CAT III | Building distribution inside the service | Panelboards, feeders, motors, fixed equipment, lighting |
| CAT IV | Origin of the installation | Service entrance, meter, utility connection, outdoor overhead/underground service conductors |

Use a meter rated for the **highest category and voltage** you might measure — for commercial
work, CAT III 1000 V / CAT IV 600 V is a common choice. Leads and probes must have equal or
higher ratings; use probe tip covers where required to reduce exposed metal.

### Inspection and use
- Inspect the meter, leads and probes for cracked cases, damaged insulation, or exposed metal.
- Use the correct jacks and function; never measure voltage with leads in the current (A) jacks.
- Verify fuses with the meter's own test function where available.
- Apply the **live-dead-live** method when verifying absence of voltage: test on a known live
  source, test the circuit, then test the known source again.
- **Non-contact voltage testers** are useful for screening but are **not** acceptable for
  verifying absence of voltage.

## Emergency Response

Every energized task should have a plan:
- Who calls 911 and where the nearest AED is
- How to de-energize the equipment quickly (location of upstream disconnect)
- **Contact release**: never touch a victim still in contact with an energized conductor — open
  the source or use an insulated hook/non-conductive object
- Trained responders for CPR/AED and first aid as the employer requires; training refreshed
  annually

After any shock, the worker must be evaluated by medical personnel even if they feel fine — heart
rhythm problems can appear later.

> **Safety:** PPE is the **last** line of defense. It reduces injury severity; it does not
> prevent the incident. If PPE is damaged, out of test date, or not rated for the hazard on the
> label, the task does not proceed until correct PPE is available.

## Key Takeaways
- Class 00 (500 V) and Class 0 (1,000 V) gloves cover building systems up to 480 V; use leather protectors.
- Air-test gloves before each use; electrical retest at intervals not exceeding 6 months.
- Arc-rated clothing must meet or exceed the label's incident energy or PPE category minimum; no melting undergarments.
- Use 1,000 V insulated tools within the restricted approach boundary.
- Meters must be rated for the category and voltage; verify live-dead-live; NCVTs are screening tools only.
- Plan emergency response before energized work; any shock victim needs medical evaluation.
