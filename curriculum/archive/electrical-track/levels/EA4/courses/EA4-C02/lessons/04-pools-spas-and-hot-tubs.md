## Why Water and Electricity Demand Extra Rules
A swimmer's wet skin has very low resistance, and the whole body is immersed in a conductive
medium. Voltage gradients in the water or between the deck and a metal ladder of only a few
volts can cause muscle paralysis and drowning — often with no visible injury. Article 680
(2023 NEC; AHJ edition governs) attacks this hazard in two ways: **equipotential bonding** to
eliminate voltage differences, and **GFCI protection** to clear faults quickly.

## Equipotential Bonding (680.26)
The purpose of equipotential bonding is to put everything a swimmer can touch at the same
potential. It is **not** intended to carry fault current and does not need to connect to a
grounding electrode.

**Bonding conductor:** solid copper, insulated, covered, or bare, **not smaller than 8 AWG**
(or rigid metal conduit of brass or other identified corrosion-resistant metal).

**Parts that must be bonded:**
| Item | Notes |
|---|---|
| Conductive pool shells | Structural reinforcing steel bonded with tie wires or the approved method |
| Perimeter surfaces | Extend **3 ft (1 m)** horizontally beyond the inside walls of the pool, including unpaved surfaces; attached to the pool reinforcing at a minimum of four points uniformly spaced; if no reinforcing steel is present, an 8 AWG copper ring 18–24 in from the inside wall and 4–6 in below subgrade is an option |
| Metallic components | Ladders, handrails, diving board supports, forming shells |
| Underwater luminaire forming shells | Bonded unless listed low-voltage nonmetallic |
| Metal fittings | Over 4 in in any dimension and penetrating the pool structure more than 1 in |
| Electrical equipment | Pump motors (with exceptions for listed double-insulated motors, which still get a bonding conductor left accessible), heaters, and associated metal |
| Fixed metal parts | Fences, railings, and similar within 5 ft horizontally of the inside walls and less than 12 ft above the maximum water level, unless separated by a permanent barrier |
| Pool water | An intentional bond of at least **9 in²** of conductive surface area in contact with the water |

Connections must be made with **listed pressure connectors** or exothermic welding, suitable
for the environment (direct burial, concrete encased).

## GFCI Protection
| Equipment | Protection |
|---|---|
| Pool pump motors (single-phase, 120 V through 240 V, and other outlets as specified) | GFCI required (680.21) |
| Receptacles within 20 ft of the inside wall of the pool | GFCI required (680.22(A)) |
| Underwater luminaires operating above the low-voltage contact limit | GFCI on the branch circuit (680.23) |
| Luminaires 5–10 ft horizontally from the pool | GFCI unless installed at least 5 ft above the maximum water level |
| Spa/hot tub equipment | GFCI (680.44), unless an exception for listed self-contained units with integral GFCI applies |

Recent editions have expanded GFCI requirements around pools; read the current section of the
adopted edition before quoting a rule from memory.

## Clearances (Permanently Installed Pools)
| Item | Requirement (2023 NEC) |
|---|---|
| Receptacles | Not within **6 ft** of the inside wall; a receptacle for circulation/sanitation motors is permitted not less than 6 ft away with GFCI protection, per 680.22(A)(2) |
| Dwelling convenience receptacle | At least one 125-V, 15- or 20-A receptacle on a general-purpose circuit **6–20 ft** from the inside wall |
| Luminaires over the pool (new outdoor) | At least **12 ft** above the maximum water level when over the pool or within 5 ft horizontally |
| Switches | At least **5 ft** horizontally from the inside wall unless separated by a solid fence, wall, or permanent barrier |
| Wet-niche luminaire | Top of the lens at least **18 in** below normal water level unless listed for a lesser depth (not less than 4 in) |

*Note:* Measure distances along the shortest path a supply cord of an appliance would follow
without piercing a floor, wall, ceiling, doorway, or other effective barrier.

## Underwater Luminaires
- **Wet-niche:** installed in a forming shell, surrounded by water; the cord must be long enough
  to allow the luminaire to be removed and placed on the deck for relamping. The forming shell
  is bonded.
- **Dry-niche:** installed in the pool wall in a niche sealed against water.
- **No-niche:** mounted on the pool wall surface.
- The junction box serving a wet-niche luminaire must meet height and distance requirements
  from the pool and deck (680.24). Many new pools use **low-voltage LED** luminaires fed through
  a listed pool and spa transformer or power supply.

## Spas and Hot Tubs
- Outdoor spas and hot tubs follow Part IV of Article 680; indoor installations have additional
  rules for receptacles, luminaires, and bonding.
- **Emergency shutoff (680.41):** for spas and hot tubs other than at one-family dwellings, a
  clearly labeled emergency switch that disconnects power to recirculation and jet pumps must be
  readily accessible, within sight of the spa, and at least **5 ft** from its inside walls.
- **Hydromassage bathtubs** (Part VII) require an individual GFCI-protected branch circuit and
  bonding of metal piping and motors with 8 AWG solid copper.

> **Safety:** When troubleshooting pool equipment, clear all swimmers from the water, lock out
> the pool equipment circuit and any pool lighting circuits, and verify absence of voltage. Never
> test or adjust pool equipment with anyone in the water. If a swimmer reports a "tingle," close
> the pool immediately and investigate the bonding and GFCI protection before reopening.

## Key Takeaways
- Equipotential bonding (8 AWG solid copper) equalizes potential; it is not a fault-current path.
- Perimeter bonding extends 3 ft from the inside wall; the water needs a 9 in² bond.
- GFCI protection covers pool pumps, receptacles within 20 ft, and many luminaires.
- Receptacles not within 6 ft of the pool; switches at least 5 ft away; luminaires over the pool
  at least 12 ft above the water (new outdoor).
- Commercial spas need an emergency shutoff at least 5 ft away, within sight.
