## What happened

A crew working several hours from the shop had their truck break down. Our records do not say
exactly what failed. The techs called our mechanic, and with his guidance made a field repair
and finished the trip. The meeting called out two lucky breaks: the breakdown happened at the
hotel, not on the highway, and the truck held when it was put in park, because **its parking brake
did not work.**

## Why it happened

The breakdown itself may not have been preventable. The parking brake was: it was a known
problem that had not been fixed before the truck left on an out-of-town job.

## What we changed

- A parking brake that does not hold is a **DVIR defect**. Report it before the truck is used; it
  is fixed before the truck goes out of town (49 CFR 396.7, 396.11).
- Inspect a truck **days before** an out-of-town trip so defects can be repaired in time.
- Field repairs happen only with our mechanic's or supervisor's approval, off the road, on level
  ground, with the wheels chocked.
- We do recognize the crew's good call: they phoned the mechanic first instead of improvising.

## What you would do

1. How do you test the parking brake during your DVIR?
2. Your truck's parking brake is weak and you leave tomorrow for a three-day job. Who do you tell, and when?
3. What would have been different if the breakdown had happened on the highway?
