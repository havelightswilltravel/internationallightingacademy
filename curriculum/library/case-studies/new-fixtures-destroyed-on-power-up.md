## What happened

Our company has destroyed many brand-new LED fixtures on power-up, and paid for the second trip to replace them.

## Why it happened

The installer assumed the supply voltage was compatible instead of measuring it. A 120-277 V fixture on a 480 V or 347 V circuit is ruined the instant power comes on.

## What we changed

- On every exterior project, and any replacement where you did not install the original, measure the voltage with your meter before you connect the new fixture.
- Measure L-N and L-L (and L-G); compare with the new fixture's label.
- A non-contact tester is a warning device, not a measurement.
- Write the reading on the work order.

## What you would do

1. How long does the reading take compared with a second trip?
2. What do you do if the reading does not match the new fixture's label?
3. Why does the office want the reading on the work order?
