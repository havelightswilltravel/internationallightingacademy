## System components

| Component | What it does | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Fixture | Holds the socket, lamp, wiring and trim; mounts to the box | Loose mounting, heat damage, wrong trim for the lamp |
| Lamp | Filament heats and glows when current passes through it | Open (burned-out) filament; loose base |
| Socket | Connects lamp base to the circuit (shell = neutral, center eye = hot) | Flattened center eye, burned/scorched contacts, cracked shell |
| Wire | Feeds the socket from the box | Brittle insulation from heat, loose splice, burned wire nut |
| Switch | Opens and closes the hot conductor | Worn contacts, loose terminal, broken toggle |
| Thermal protector | Opens the circuit in recessed cans when the fixture overheats | Trips from over-lamping or insulation against the can; light cycles on/off; fails open |

## Safety first

- Turn the switch off, then **lock out/tag out** the circuit at the breaker per the company LOTO program. Use the **live-dead-live** check (test meter on a known source, test the fixture, retest on the known source) before touching sockets or wiring.
- Lamps and trims can be hot enough to burn - let them cool, wear gloves. Wear eye protection when removing broken lamps; use pliers or a lamp extractor on broken bases, never fingers, and only after LOTO.
- Use a ladder rated for the job; never stand on the top cap.
- Breakers: reset or check them **only if qualified and authorized; otherwise write it up.**

## Company troubleshooting procedure

1. **Check the lamp.** De-energized, measure lamp continuity across the base (center contact to shell). Expected: low resistance (a few to a few tens of ohms cold). OL = open filament. Or simply install a **known-good lamp** of the same type and wattage.
2. **Check power.** If a good lamp does not light, verify voltage at the fixture (qualified persons only for energized testing; otherwise confirm the circuit is on and the switch is on). Expected: about 120 V hot to neutral (or the voltage on the label).
3. **Check the light switch.** De-energized and locked out, check the switch for continuity: near 0 ohms in ON, OL in OFF. Look for loose or burned terminals.
4. **Check the socket and socket eye.** After LOTO, look for scorching or a flattened center eye. A flattened eye can be gently lifted (about 1/8 in.) with an insulated tool *only while de-energized*. Replace scorched or cracked sockets.
5. **Check for the thermal protector.** In recessed cans, a light that goes out after running and comes back after cooling is a tripped thermal protector. Check the lamp wattage against the fixture's maximum label and look for insulation packed against a non-IC can. Correct the cause; replace the protector only with the manufacturer's part.

## Escalate / write it up when

- No voltage at the fixture and the breaker is on or trips again - write it up for an electrician.
- Damaged branch wiring, burned splices in the box, or heat-damaged insulation beyond the fixture whip.
- Insulation contact with a non-IC can (fire hazard) - tell the manager the same day.

## Parts & information

- Record lamp base (E26, E12, etc.), wattage, voltage and shape; check the fixture's maximum-wattage label before relamping.
- Photograph the fixture label and the socket. For sockets and thermal protectors, order by fixture manufacturer and catalog number or bring a sample.
- If the customer is converting to LED lamps, confirm the lamp is rated for enclosed or recessed fixtures where required.

## Document on the work order

Fixture location/ID, what you tested and the readings, parts replaced (with wattage and base), any over-lamping or insulation issue found, and anything written up for an electrician.
