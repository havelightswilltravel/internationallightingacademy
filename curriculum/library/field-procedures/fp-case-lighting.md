## System components

| Component | What it does | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Case | Refrigerated or dry display case | Defrost cycle turns lights off; condensation |
| Lamps (fluorescent or LED) | Light the product | End of life, cold-temperature dimming |
| Sockets | Hold lamps; often sealed/moisture-resistant | Cracks, burns, corrosion from condensation |
| Ballasts / drivers | Run the lamps; often **remote-mounted** away from the canopy or door | Failure; wrong (non-remote) replacement |
| Wire harness | Runs from ballast to sockets, often through sealed case walls and door frames | Breaks at door hinges, corrosion, chafing |
| Reflectors | Direct light onto product | Loose, rattling, dirty |
| Tube guards | Protect lamps and contain glass | Missing, cracked |
| Lenses | Diffuse light; protect lamps | Cracked, yellowed |
| Distribution block | Splits power to several lamp runs | Loose or burned terminals |

## Safety first

- **LOTO and verify absence of voltage (live-dead-live)** before opening raceways or touching sockets and harnesses. Confirm with the store which breaker feeds the lighting - case lights may be on a different circuit than the refrigeration.
- Do not disturb refrigeration lines, defrost heaters or case seals. Wet, cold surfaces increase shock risk; keep tools and hands dry.
- Protect product: coordinate with the manager before opening a case.
- Check breakers **only if qualified; otherwise write it up.**

## Company troubleshooting procedure

1. **Check for switches** (case light switch, often at the end of the case or behind a kick plate).
2. **If the case may be defrosting, mark the location and come back in ½ hour.** Many cases turn lights off during defrost.
3. **Check the lamps** - try a known-good lamp.
4. **If not the lamps, check the sockets** for breaks and burns (locked out).
5. **If the sockets look good, open the case to locate the ballast and test for proper line voltage** (qualified persons for energized testing). Expected: rated line voltage at the ballast input.
6. **If line voltage is correct and the ballast is not located in the hood or door, verify it is a remote ballast.**
7. **Replace the ballast with the correct remote ballast for the lamp application.** A standard ballast may not start lamps over the longer remote lead lengths or at cold temperatures.
8. **If the ballast does not solve the problem, check the wiring harness continuity** (de-energized). Expected: near 0 ohms on each conductor end to end; OL = break.
9. **If the harness can be repaired without breaking the seals, make the repair.** If the seals must be broken to replace the harness, **turn the service over to the manager to call a case specialist or refrigeration company.**
10. **If there is not proper line voltage, verify the unit is not in defrost mode, then check the breaker (if qualified) or write it up for an electrician.**

## Escalate / write it up when

- Harness replacement requires breaking case seals - case specialist/refrigeration company via the manager.
- No line voltage and not in defrost - electrician.
- Burned distribution blocks or water in electrical raceways.

## Parts & information

- Record case manufacturer, model and serial (usually on a plate inside the case or on the end panel); photograph it.
- Ballast label: remote-mount rating, lamp type/quantity, input voltage, maximum lead length, cold-start rating.
- Lamps: length, type, and whether cold-temperature or sleeved lamps are required. Order sockets and harnesses by case model.

## Document on the work order

Case location/number, defrost status and return time, readings, parts replaced, seal issues, and what was turned over to the manager or written up.
