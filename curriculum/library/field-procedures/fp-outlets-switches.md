## System components

| Component | What it does | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Outlet (receptacle) | Provides power to plug-in loads | Loose contacts (plugs fall out), burned slots, cracked face, tripped GFCI |
| Switch | Opens and closes the hot to a load | Worn mechanism, loose terminals, burned contacts |
| Wire | Feeds the device; may feed devices downstream | Loose backstab, burned insulation, broken conductor |
| Fixture | Load controlled by the switch | Fixture fault mistaken for a switch fault |
| Electrical box | Encloses connections and supports the device | Overfilled, damaged, missing cover, loose mounting |

## Safety first

- **LOTO and verify absence of voltage (live-dead-live)** before removing any cover plate. Boxes may contain more than one circuit - test every conductor.
- Energized line-voltage testing is **qualified persons only**. Check breakers **only if qualified; otherwise write it up.**
- If a device is burned or hot, do not operate it; lock it out and investigate.

## Company troubleshooting procedure

1. **Look for damaged, burned or loose parts** - scorch marks, melted plates, loose devices, plugs that fall out.
2. **Check for line voltage** at the device (qualified) or with a plug-in tester on receptacles. Expected: about 120 V hot to neutral, about 120 V hot to ground, near 0 V neutral to ground. Check for a tripped GFCI upstream first.
3. **Lock out, then check the connections** - tighten screws, move backstabbed wires to screw terminals, look for burned conductors.
4. **If line voltage is good and the device is damaged or fails, replace it** (locked out). Re-test after restoring power.

**Tips:** when replacing a receptacle, provide GFCI or AFCI protection where the current code now requires it (2023 NEC 406.4(D) covers replacements). A receptacle that "feeds through" may be protecting others downstream - test them after replacement.

## Escalate / write it up when

- No line voltage at the device, or the breaker trips.
- Burned conductors in the box, aluminum branch wiring, or an overfilled/damaged box.
- Ungrounded wiring where a grounding receptacle is being installed.

## Parts & information

- Record device type, amp rating (15 or 20 A), voltage, configuration (e.g., NEMA 5-15R, 5-20R), color, and any GFCI, AFCI, tamper-resistant or weather-resistant rating. Photograph wiring before disconnecting.
- Switches: single-pole, 3-way, 4-way, dimmer type and load rating.

## Document on the work order

Device location, condition found, readings, parts replaced (with rating), GFCI/AFCI provided, and anything written up.
