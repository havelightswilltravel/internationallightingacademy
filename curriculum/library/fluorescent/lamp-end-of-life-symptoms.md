## Symptoms

- **Fluorescent:** blackened or dark-gray ends, flicker or blinking, slow start, orange/pink
  glow at the ends with the center dark, swirling light that doesn't settle (some swirling
  in brand-new lamps during the first hours is normal)
- **Metal halide:** color shift (pink, green or blue cast), lower output, cycling on/off,
  slow or failed starts
- **High-pressure sodium:** cycling on/off every few minutes, deeper orange/red color, long
  warm-up
- **LED:** gradual dimming (lumen depreciation), color shift, individual dark LEDs, flicker
  (often a driver issue rather than the LEDs)

## Safety first

- **De-energize, LOTO and verify absence of voltage before relamping**, especially Type B
  TLED fixtures (line voltage at sockets) and HID fixtures (high ignitor voltage).
- Let HID lamps cool before removal - they run extremely hot.
- Wear eye protection and gloves. Fluorescent and HID lamps contain mercury: handle
  spent lamps as universal waste; use the broken-lamp cleanup procedure if one breaks.
- **Metal halide lamps can rupture at end of life.** Use lamps rated for the fixture
  (open vs enclosed fixture), and follow the manufacturer's guidance on group relamping
  and on scheduled off-cycles for continuously operated MH lamps.

## Tools needed

- Known-good replacement lamps (correct type, wattage, base, color)
- CAT III multimeter (ohms) for cathode checks on fluorescent lamps
- Lamp carrier / universal waste container, gloves, eye protection
- Ladder or MEWP as required

## Likely causes

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Normal end of life | Hours of use near rated life; symptoms above; group of same-age lamps failing | Replace; recommend group relamp |
| Short lamp life from frequent switching (instant-start fluorescent) | Lamps on occupancy sensors failing early with instant-start ballast | Recommend programmed-start ballast or LED for frequently switched areas |
| Ballast/driver problem mimicking lamp failure | Known-good lamp also fails | Go to the ballast/driver guides |
| Wrong lamp type | Label vs lamp | Install correct lamp |
| Heat (enclosed fixture, high ambient) | Fixture runs hot; short life repeatedly | Correct environment; use rated lamps |
| Voltage problems | Measured voltage high or low | See electrical guides |

## Step-by-step diagnosis

1. Look first: compare the lamp with neighbors (color, brightness, end blackening).
2. **De-energize, LOTO, verify absence of voltage.** Remove the lamp (let HID cool first).
3. **Fluorescent:** check cathode continuity end-to-end on each end's two pins. Expected:
   **continuity (a few ohms)** on each end. Open = failed cathode.
4. Install a **known-good** lamp of the correct type. If it works normally, the old lamp was
   at end of life. If not, the problem is the ballast/driver, socket or supply.
5. Consider age: if one lamp of a group installed at the same time has failed, others will
   follow. Recommend group relamping to the customer where practical.
6. Dispose of spent lamps per universal waste rules.

## When to escalate

- Lamps failing well before rated life (look for ballast, voltage or switching problems)
- Ruptured metal halide lamp or broken arc tube - inspect the fixture and surroundings for
  hot fragments and report
- Customer interest in LED conversion (refer for retrofit scope)

## Documentation

- Fixture locations and lamp types replaced, quantities
- Symptoms observed and whether known-good lamp test confirmed lamp failure
- Group relamp recommendation given to customer
- Universal waste container/location used
