## Symptoms

- Exit sign dark, partially lit (some letters or chevrons out) or noticeably dim
- Sign fails the monthly 30-second test (goes dark on battery)
- Charge indicator off or showing a fault
- Self-luminous (tritium) or photoluminescent sign is faint or unreadable

## Safety first

- Exit signs are **life-safety equipment.** A dark or failed sign is an impairment - notify
  the owner/manager the same day.
- **De-energize, LOTO and verify absence of voltage** before opening a sign. Battery-equipped
  signs remain lit on battery power after the AC supply is off; disconnect the battery
  per the manufacturer's instructions before working on internal wiring.
- Energized testing is **qualified persons only**, with risk assessment, appropriate PPE and
  an energized-work justification per NFPA 70E.
- **Self-luminous (tritium) signs contain radioactive material.** Do not open, damage or throw
  them in the trash. They are regulated (generally licensed in the U.S.); follow the owner's
  and manufacturer's return/disposal procedure. Report a damaged sign to your supervisor.

## Tools needed

- CAT III multimeter
- Manufacturer's instructions; replacement battery and LED panel (exact type)
- Light meter (optional, for photoluminescent charging light check)
- Ladder

## Likely causes

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Battery at end of life | Sign lit on AC, fails on test | Replace battery with specified type; charge and retest |
| Battery disconnected | Connector unplugged | Connect per manufacturer |
| No AC supply | Charge LED off; no voltage at input | Restore supply; find open circuit/breaker |
| LED panel/board failure | Dark sections; good AC and battery | Replace LED board or sign |
| Wrong voltage tap connected (120/277 V input leads) | Lead used vs circuit voltage; damage | Correct wiring; replace damaged unit |
| Photoluminescent sign not charged | Required charging light level not present in the area | Restore the required lighting per the sign's listing |
| Self-luminous sign past rated life | Date on label; faint | Replace and return old sign per procedure |
| Dirty or damaged face/diffuser | Visual | Clean or replace face |

## Step-by-step diagnosis

1. Identify the sign type: AC-only, battery (self-contained), generator/central-system fed,
   photoluminescent, or self-luminous.
2. Observe on AC power: fully lit, partially lit or dark? Check the charge indicator.
3. Press the test switch (or use the remote test) for at least 30 seconds. Note when it fails.
4. If dark on AC: **(qualified, energized, with PPE)** verify input voltage at the sign.
   Expected: nominal line voltage on the lead used (120 V or 277 V). No voltage = supply problem.
5. **De-energize, LOTO, verify absence of voltage,** disconnect the battery and inspect: loose
   connectors, swollen battery, burned board. Measure battery voltage; compare with its rating.
6. Replace the failed component with the manufacturer-specified part. Reconnect the battery,
   restore power, allow charging per manufacturer and retest.
7. For photoluminescent signs, confirm the area lighting meets the level and type the sign's
   listing requires (lights cannot be switched off in that area if the sign depends on them).
8. Check that directional chevrons are correct for the egress route after any replacement.

## When to escalate

- Self-luminous sign damaged, missing or due for disposal
- Signs fed from a generator/central inverter/emergency panel with supply problems
- Questions about required sign locations or visibility (refer to the owner, designer or AHJ)
- Multiple failures in one area (circuit problem)

## Documentation

- Sign location and type, symptom, test result
- Parts replaced (battery model/date code, LED board)
- Retest result; chevron orientation verified
- Owner notified of impairment; tritium sign handling if applicable
- Update the building's emergency lighting test log
