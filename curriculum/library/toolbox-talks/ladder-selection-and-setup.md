*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Falls from ladders break bones and kill people, and a short fall can be enough. Our company
safety meetings keep coming back to the same point: **the single biggest ladder infraction
in our company is using the wrong size ladder for the job.** Every one of our vans carries
an extension ladder, a 12-ft, an 8-ft and a 6-ft ladder for exactly this reason. If the right
one is not on your truck, go get it. You are paid to take the time to be safe.

## Key points

- **Pick the ladder that lets you work without the top two steps.** Never stand on the top
  cap or the step below it unless the ladder is designed for it. You cannot keep a ladder
  stable from up there.
- **Company ladders are heavy-duty fiberglass only** (Type IA, 300 lb or higher). If you find
  a ladder of any other kind in the fleet, do not use it - tell your supervisor. Painted
  ladders are not used (paint hides cracks). Some aluminum multi-position ladders are not
  electrically rated by their maker - keep metal ladders away from exposed electrical parts.
- **Stepladders are used fully open and locked**, on a solid, level surface. Never as a
  straight ladder leaning on a wall, never on boxes, carts or uneven ground.
- **Extension ladders: 4:1.** Base out 1 ft for every 4 ft of height to the top support (a
  12-ft working length = 3 ft out). Rails extend **at least 3 ft above** a landing you are
  stepping onto. Tie off or anchor the top and bottom; use spikes on soft ground.
- **Stay centered.** Belt buckle (torso) between the rails. Move the ladder instead of
  reaching. Three points of contact, face the ladder, carry tools in a belt or pouch.
- **Doors and traffic:** never set a ladder in front of a door that is not locked or propped
  open and coned off. Cone off aisles. Nobody works under your ladder.
- **One person per ladder.** Never "walk" a ladder while standing on it.

## What happened

One of our techs climbed an 8-ft A-frame ladder to cover a photocell on a wall-mounted
fixture so the lights would come on for testing. The ladder was too short for the reach, he
over-extended, and he fell. Injuries were minor that time. What would you have done
differently? (Right height ladder, or an extension ladder set at 4:1, or a lift.)

## Discussion questions

1. What jobs on this week's schedule need more than an 8-ft ladder?
2. Why is standing on the top two steps such a common shortcut, and what does it cost?
3. Where on a typical retail job do doors, carts and customers put a ladder at risk?
