*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Lighting work removes a steady stream of lamps, ballasts, drivers, batteries and breakers.
Several of them contain mercury, PCBs, lead or lithium, and many cannot legally go in a
dumpster. Recycling is the right thing to do for the people who come after us - and recycling
is billed by type and weight, so **if it is not on the work order, the company pays for it.**

## Key points - company rules

- **All lamps are recycled**, including low-mercury lamps. **Lamps are never crushed** unless
  the company has an approved, state-allowed system.
- **Every removed magnetic ballast goes to the recycler**, kept **separate from suspect PCB
  ballasts**, and counted. (No "No PCBs" label on an older ballast? Treat it as suspect.)
- **Emergency batteries and emergency ballasts/drivers never go in the trash.** Nickel-cadmium,
  lithium-ion, lithium-polymer, lead-acid - and AA/AAA cells too. **Write the battery type and
  weight on the work order** (for example, "3.5 lb lithium-ion") because recycling is charged by
  chemistry and weight. Tape the terminals of lithium batteries so they cannot short.
- **Breakers:** a scrap breaker recycler near the shop takes all breakers for recycling (they buy
  some and take the rest free). Put removed breakers in the shop's breaker recycling bin, not the
  trash.
- **Bring it back and log it.** When you put spent material in the recycle bins at the shop,
  **mark it on that job's work order** - type, quantity or weight.
- **Charge recycling on every job** where we remove material, per company pricing (the office
  sets the rate - don't guess it on site).

## What happened

At one of our meetings, a tech was recognized as the example to follow: every job ticket he
turned in listed the spent material he brought back for the recycle bins, so the cost
reached the customer. Jobs without that information are recycling costs we absorb.

## Discussion questions

1. How do you tell a PCB ballast from a non-PCB ballast?
2. Why must lithium battery terminals be taped?
3. Where do the different recycle bins in our shop sit, and what goes in each?
