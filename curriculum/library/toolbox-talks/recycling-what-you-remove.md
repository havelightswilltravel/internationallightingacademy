*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Lighting work removes lamps, ballasts, drivers, batteries and breakers. Several contain mercury,
PCBs, lead or lithium, and many cannot legally go in a dumpster. A lithium battery thrown loose in
a bin can short and start a fire. Recycling is also billed by type and weight, so **if it is not
on the work order, the company pays for it.**

## Key points - the safety and compliance rules

- **All lamps are recycled**, including low-mercury lamps, and **never crushed** unless the
  company has an approved, state-allowed system. Broken lamps follow the cleanup steps in LT1-C06.
- **Magnetic ballasts go to the recycler, kept separate from suspect PCB ballasts.** No "No PCBs"
  label on an older ballast? Treat it as suspect, and don't handle a leaking one without gloves.
- **Emergency batteries and emergency ballasts/drivers never go in the trash**, AA and AAA cells
  included. **Tape the terminals of lithium batteries** so they cannot short.
- **Breakers** go in the shop's breaker recycling bin, not the trash.

## How we deal with it on the job

- **Bring it back, put it in the right bin, and write it on that job's work order:** type and
  quantity, and for batteries the chemistry and weight (for example, "3.5 lb lithium-ion").
- **Don't** set or quote a recycling price on site; the office prices it. Not sure which bin?
  Ask the warehouse lead or your supervisor.

## Discussion questions

1. How do you tell a PCB ballast from a non-PCB ballast?
2. Why must lithium battery terminals be taped?
3. Where do the different recycle bins in our shop sit, and what goes in each?
