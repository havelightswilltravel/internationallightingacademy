*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Even a parking-lot bump can turn into an injury claim weeks later. What you do in the first
30 minutes - who you call, what you photograph and what you say - protects you and the
company.

## Key points - the company procedure

1. **Injuries? Call 911 first.**
2. **Call your supervisor or the office.**
3. **Another person or vehicle involved (personal injury accidents):**
   - Call the police and report to our insurance company **no matter how minor** it seems.
     Follow the insurer's instructions.
   - Document everything: photos of all vehicles, license plates, the other party, the scene
     from close up and far away; witness names and statements; a diagram of the scene.
   - Fill out the company accident investigation form.
   - **Never admit fault or comment on the accident** to the other party. Answer the police's
     questions honestly and leave fault to the police and the insurance companies.
   - If the police will not come out, **call the other driver's insurance company and ours
     from the scene.**
4. **Property damage only, no one else involved:** if you know you are at fault, the company
   may prefer to handle it directly - **do not make that decision yourself.** Call your
   supervisor or the office right away. If the property owner is not available, leave your
   name, your supervisor's name and phone number and an apology. Photograph, diagram and fill
   out the form.
5. **Keep the incident form in your vehicle** (the paperwork tin), especially if you ever work
   alone or travel.
6. **Hit and run?** If no one is hurt, it is not a 911 call - use the police non-emergency line
   and call your supervisor.
7. **Need a tow?** The office keeps the fleet service and towing contacts on the tech cheat
   sheet.

## What happened

Another vehicle made contact with one of our trucks. There was no real damage to our
vehicle and the police would not come out, so the tech almost just drove away. The lesson we
took from it: the other driver could still call our insurer later claiming we hit them and
that they were hurt. Call both insurance companies from the site, photograph everything and
fill out the form.

## Discussion questions

1. Where is the accident form on your truck right now?
2. What photos would you take at a minor parking-lot accident?
3. Why should you never say "sorry, that was my fault" at the scene?
