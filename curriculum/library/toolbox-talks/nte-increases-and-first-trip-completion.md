## The issue

Many service calls carry a **not-to-exceed (NTE)** amount, the most the customer has approved for
the visit. Sometimes the job needs more than the NTE allows, or the fix is clear but we are about
to leave and "write it up for a quote."

## Why it matters

**Work done past an NTE can never be billed afterward**, whatever the customer. A return trip
that one phone call could have avoided costs the customer time and costs us a second visit.
Customers also respond faster to a tech calling from the job than to the office calling later.
See case study *Two NTE Overruns in Two Days*.

## How we deal with it

- **Know the NTE before you start** and keep it in mind as the job goes.
- **About to go over, and you can finish today?** Stop and call from the site before you go past
  it. Call the office first if you need pricing.
- **Document the answer either way:** the name of the person who approved or denied it, and ask
  them to email the approval. Put both in the work-order notes.
- **Really need a return?** Write a clear return sheet so the office can quote it.
- **Don't** go past an NTE "because it was only a little more," and don't promise the customer a
  price yourself. Running late for an appointment? Call before the time passes.

## Discussion questions

1. What would you say on the phone to request an increase?
2. Which returns this month could have been finished with one call?
