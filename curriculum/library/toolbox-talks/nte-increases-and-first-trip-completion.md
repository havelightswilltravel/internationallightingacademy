*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Many service calls come with a **not-to-exceed (NTE)** amount - the most the customer has
approved for the visit. **Once you blow past an NTE, we can never go back and bill it**, no
matter the customer. And every job that ends with "write it up, we'll quote it" means a second
trip, a longer wait for the customer and more office work. Our company wants more jobs
**finished on the first trip.**

## Key points

- **Know the NTE before you start** and keep track of where you are against it (labor,
  materials, equipment).
- **Quote vs. NTE increase:**
  - A **quote** is for work that requires a return trip.
  - An **NTE increase** is what you ask for **while on site** so you can finish now, because
    you already have the parts, equipment and time to do it.
- **Call from the job site.** Customers treat a call from the tech on site differently from a
  call from the office - they know the tech is waiting. Call the office for pricing if you need
  it, then call the customer before you leave.
- **Get it in writing.** Approved or denied, **get the name of the person you spoke with and ask
  them to email the approval.** Note it on the work order.
- **Don't give up on the job.** Before you write a return, ask: could I finish today with one
  phone call?
- **Help the office with quotes.** When a return is truly needed, write the return sheet so it
  can become a quote quickly - parts and codes, quantities, equipment, people and time.
- **Appointments are not suggestions.** The office will contact you the day before an
  appointment so you have time to review the paperwork and ask questions. **Arrive before the
  customer** - our company should never be associated with being late. If you are going to
  miss a time, call before it passes.
- **Don't take on more than you can finish.** Take one job at a time unless directed
  otherwise. If you are dispatched work you are sure you cannot complete safely or well, talk
  to your dispatcher. Neglecting quality is never the solution to being busy.

## What happened

On two jobs in two days for the same customer, our crew went past the NTE. The
overage could not be billed afterward - a loss a small company cannot absorb. On another
occasion an office manager was turned down when he asked a customer for an NTE increase by
phone after the tech had left. Our experience is that customers respond faster to a request
made while the tech is standing at the job, ready to finish.

## Discussion questions

1. How do you track time and materials against an NTE during the job?
2. What would you say on the phone to request an increase?
3. Which jobs are you writing up for return that you could finish with one call?
