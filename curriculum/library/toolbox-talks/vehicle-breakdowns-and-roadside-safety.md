*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Our trucks have stalled in traffic, broken down out of town, lost a serpentine belt and been
stuck in the mud. The breakdown itself rarely hurts anyone. What hurts people is standing in a
traffic lane, crawling under a truck that can roll, a tow strap that snaps, or a roadside repair
that should have waited for the shop.

## Key points

- **Get out of traffic.** Signal and coast as far off the road as you safely can. If the engine
  dies while moving, **press hard and hold the brake**: some of our trucks have a backup electric
  brake-boost motor that takes over. Steering will be heavy.
- **Flashers on at once. Triangles out within 10 minutes** (49 CFR 392.22, which our company
  follows on every trip):
  - Two-way road: about **10 ft behind** on the traffic side, **100 ft behind** and **100 ft
    ahead**.
  - Divided or one-way road: about **10, 100 and 200 ft behind**.
  - Hill or curve hides the truck: move the rear triangle back as far as **500 ft**.
  - Wear a high-visibility vest and walk facing traffic.
- **Wait clear of traffic.** On a busy road the safest place is usually off the shoulder and
  behind a barrier. If you stay in the cab, keep your seat belt on.
- **Call your supervisor.** The office arranges towing through our fleet service (the contact is
  in the tech cheat sheets). Don't idle an engine that has a battery light or a broken belt:
  without the belt, coolant does not circulate.
- **No roadside repairs without approval.** If our mechanic or your supervisor approves a field
  fix, do it only off the road, on level ground, parking brake set and wheels chocked. Never
  get under a vehicle held up only by a jack.
- **Stuck?** Don't spin the wheels. Rock gently and add traction material. If that fails, call for
  a proper tow. Don't accept a yank from a passer-by with a strap tied to a bumper; a strap or
  hook that lets go becomes a missile. Company vehicles are not driven off road, and spring mud
  is the worst.
- **Boom won't come down on a lift truck?** Stay in the basket, use the manufacturer's emergency
  lowering, then call. Never climb out of an elevated basket.
- **A bad parking brake is a defect, not a quirk.** Put it on the DVIR and tell your supervisor
  before the truck goes out, especially before an out-of-town trip.
- **Winter:** keep diesels at least half full, and follow the stranded-vehicle rules in
  *Winter Driving*.

## Discussion questions

1. Where are the triangles and the vest on your truck right now?
2. When would you wait outside the truck rather than in the cab?
3. Your mechanic talks you through a fix on the phone. What has to be true about where the truck
   is parked before you start?
