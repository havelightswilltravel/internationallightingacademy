*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Metal halide lamps and ballasts are going away. Common wattages of MH ballasts are already
gone from our stock, and the remaining lamps are being discontinued. Our company is working
through its HID stock and will move to **LED replacement lamps or new LED fixtures** instead.
How you handle an HID call today decides whether the customer gets a good repair or a second
trip.

## Key points - HID calls

- **First choice for a failed MH ballast: an LED HID-replacement lamp** (ballast-bypass "corn"
  or similar), wired per the manufacturer and LT2-C05, with the fixture labeled as converted.
- **Check the envelope size.** Replacement lamps come in different body sizes. Look at the
  existing lamp (for example a larger BT-shape vs. a smaller ED-shape) and measure the room in
  the fixture before choosing - a lamp that does not fit, or blocks the optic, is the wrong
  lamp.
- **Color-selectable lamps:** set the color temperature to match the site, and record it.
- **Customer preferences rule.** Some customers do not yet allow bypass lamps in their exterior
  fixtures, or limit them to small fixtures. Know the customer's standard; if in doubt, write it
  up instead.
- **Can't solve it from the truck?** Write it up with the information the office needs to
  choose between a lamp, a ballast or a new fixture: measured supply voltage, fixture location
  on the property (edge or middle - it drives the light distribution), pole type (round or
  square), whether a photocell is needed, and photos.

## Key points - lenses

- **Flat prismatic lenses:** our vendor cuts them to size for little more than an uncut lens -
  so **always take a precise measurement along with the count.** If you already have a lens and
  can cut it on site to finish the job, do it.
- **Wrap lenses are not guaranteed to be available.** Trace the lens, bring back a sample, and
  **photograph the fixture and count the matching fixtures in the area.** If the lens cannot be
  found, the fix may be a new fixture - and one new fixture among old ones may look wrong, so
  the office may need to quote the whole area.
- **Sign and display fixtures:** spare lenses for older fixtures run out. Photograph the fixture
  type and count all of them on the structure, in case replacing them is the answer.

## Discussion questions

1. A 400 W MH wall pack is out. What do you check before installing an LED replacement lamp?
2. What photos help the office price a new pole fixture without a second visit?
3. Why count the fixtures in the area when you only need one lens?
