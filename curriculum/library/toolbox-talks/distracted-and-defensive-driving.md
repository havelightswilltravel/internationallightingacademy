*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Distracted driving is a leading factor in fatal and serious crashes. Drivers talking on a
phone are several times more likely to crash, and texting drivers far more. In under a year
our company had **two company vehicles totaled** - and every crash raises insurance costs that
come out of the same budget as raises and equipment.

## Key points

- **Flip the switch.** Before you get into a company vehicle - during your pre-trip
  inspection - switch into "professional, defensive driver" mode. You are paid to drive that
  way.
- **Phones distract three ways:** eyes off the road, hands off the wheel, mind off driving.
  Hands-free does not fix the third one; your brain switches between tasks, it does not do
  both.
- **Prevention:** let voicemail take calls or pull over safely to respond. Tell coworkers and
  family you will not answer while driving. Set navigation and audio before you roll; use
  voice navigation. No eating or grooming behind the wheel. Don't have heated conversations
  while driving.
- **Never be in a hurry.** If you feel pressure to get somewhere fast, stop and do a reality
  check - and tell your dispatcher. No job is worth a crash.
- **Keep your space:** at least 3 seconds of following distance (more for heavy trucks and bad
  weather). If someone cuts in, back off and rebuild the gap. Use your mirrors constantly.
- **Slow way down when passing stopped traffic** in the next lane - someone may pull out or
  step out.
- **Bucket and lift trucks:** the bucket and step platform hang off the front. They grab
  things **when you turn the wheel** and when backing. Watch your swing. If you do not drive
  these trucks often, be extra careful. Use a spotter to back whenever you can.
- **Report moving violations** - on or off the clock - to your supervisor right away
  (company policy).
- **Idling:** shut the engine off if you will sit more than a minute, except to cool the cab
  or run the PTO.

## What happened

One of our lift trucks was totaled when its front-mounted bucket caught a structure at a
restaurant site while the driver was turning. In another crash we reviewed from the accident
diagram, the lesson was simple: slow way down when you pass a lane of stopped traffic. Both
were preventable with more time and more space.

## Discussion questions

1. What distracts you most in the truck?
2. What do you do when someone takes away your 3-second gap?
3. What are your side mirrors for in a bucket truck, besides lane changes?
