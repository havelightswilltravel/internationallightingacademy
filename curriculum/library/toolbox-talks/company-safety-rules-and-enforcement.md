*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's safety manual reviews at monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Every safety meeting opens with a section of our company safety manual. These are the rules
underneath everything else we teach. Each time a rule is broken, people, customers or
property are put at risk.

## The ten commandments of safety (company manual)

1. Learn the safe way to do your job **before** you start.
2. Always think safely and act safely.
3. Obey safety rules and regulations - they are there for your protection.
4. Wear proper clothing and protective equipment.
5. Conduct yourself properly - **horseplay is prohibited.**
6. Operate equipment correctly, according to your training, and only for its intended use.
7. Inspect tools and equipment **before** starting work.
8. Tell your supervisor **immediately** about unsafe conditions or practices.
9. Report any injury **immediately**.
10. Support the safety program and take an active part.

## Other everyday rules

- Dress for the work: work clothes, gloves, proper boots; no loose clothing or jewelry. Come
  prepared for any environment.
- Be trained on equipment before you run it unsupervised.
- Know the hazards and emergency exits at every site.
- **Housekeeping is the foundation of a safe site:** stack material so it cannot fall, remove
  scrap and trash often, and keep stairways, walkways, exits, **electrical panels and fire
  equipment clear**. Never leave material in aisles or egress paths.
- Report to work fit for duty.

## How enforcement works (company policy)

- **First violation** of a rule you were trained on: a written warning, a copy to you and your
  supervisor, and refresher training if needed. It goes in your file.
- **Second** same or similar violation within a year: suspension.
- **Third** within a year: termination, regardless of how serious it was.
- **Willful neglect or blatant disregard**, or creating a life-threatening condition, can mean
  immediate termination without a warning.

The point of the policy is not punishment. It is that a known, trained-on rule is not
optional - and that the company takes your safety as seriously as you should.

## Discussion questions

1. Which commandment do you think gets broken most often on our jobs?
2. What counts as "blatant disregard"? Give an example.
3. What does good housekeeping look like at the end of a troffer retrofit?
