*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Ladders, fixtures, lift parts and boxes of material are heavy and awkward. A back injury can
end a career in this trade. Our company reviews material handling several times a year, and
the manager who presents it once admitted to being caught **carrying the large multi-position
ladder alone** - a violation of our own team-lift rule. Don't follow that example.

## Key points

- **Size up the load.** Look for a weight label, sharp corners, nails or projections. Get help
  if the load is **more than 50 lb** or the lifting is repetitive, or use a cart, dolly, hoist
  or lift - and only if you know how to operate it.
- **Team lifts are required (company policy)** for the large multi-position ladder and for
  extension ladders over 30 ft (so every 32-ft and longer ladder) - carrying, raising and
  positioning. Agree on the route and the signals (stop, turn, set down) before you lift, and
  keep in step.
- **The squat lift.** Clear the path. Feet firm, one beside the load and one slightly behind.
  Get close, firm grip with fingers under the load, tighten your stomach muscles, and lift with
  your legs, smoothly. **Don't twist - step.**
- **Treat ladders like material.** They are heavy, long and awkward. Carry them balanced and
  watch the ends around people, doors and vehicles.
- **Store heavy items low and light items high**, in the warehouse and on the trucks.
- **Warm up.** Back strains happen most at the start of a shift, when muscles are cold, and
  right after lunch. A few minutes of stretching helps. Strong abdominal muscles support your
  spine; stress tightens back muscles too.
- **Tell your supervisor before your shift if you are sore or stiff.** We would rather change
  your dispatch than send you to a lifting job hurt.
- **Hoists and slings:** use only well-maintained slings and chains rated for the load; check
  them for defects first and report any problems.

## What happened

Two techs watched a manager pull the big multi-position ladder off the rack by himself to
save a minute. He got it down without injury, and he put the violation on the next safety
meeting agenda himself so everyone would learn from it. Anyone can stop an unsafe lift, whatever their title.

## Discussion questions

1. Which items on your truck weigh more than 50 lb?
2. How do you carry a 32-ft extension ladder through a parking lot with two people?
3. What would make it easier to speak up when you see a coworker lifting alone?
