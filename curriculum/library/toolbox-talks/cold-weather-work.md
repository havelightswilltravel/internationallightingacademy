*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's November and December safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Winter brings cold hands in a bucket, ice on ladders and lifts, and long days in freezers.
Cold slows your hands and your thinking - and a tech with numb fingers on a ladder is a fall
waiting to happen. Our safety rules also say: **come prepared to work in any environment.**
You may need a jacket even in summer if your schedule includes an all-day freezer job.

## Key points

- **Dress in layers.** A thin wicking layer next to the skin (polyester or polypropylene, not
  cotton), a warm middle layer such as wool or fleece, and an outer jacket that blocks wind and
  water. Several layers beat one heavy coat.
- **Cover up:** a warm hat or liner under your hard hat that covers your ears, gloves, dry
  socks and waterproof boots with good traction. A face mask or gaiter in wind; take it off
  indoors so you do not overheat and sweat.
- **Stay dry.** Wet skin freezes faster and wet clothing loses its insulation. Carry spare
  dry gloves and socks.
- **Drink water** - you still dehydrate in the cold. Limit alcohol and nicotine; alcohol makes
  you lose heat faster and smoking reduces circulation.
- **Avoid bare metal** and metal jewelry in the cold.
- **Plan the day:** outdoor work in the warmest part of the day when you can, take warm-up
  breaks, and do not sit still outside for long periods.
- **Ice on equipment:** knock ice and snow off ladders, lift platforms and steps before you
  climb. Do not carry loads across icy surfaces. Our company covers the scissor lifts through
  the winter months for this reason.
- **Know the signs:** frostbite (numb, white or waxy skin) and hypothermia (shivering,
  confusion, slurred speech, clumsiness). Get the person warm and dry and get medical help.
- **Diesel trucks in winter:** keep at least a half tank, especially overnight. Running a
  diesel out of fuel - especially with the boom up - means gelled lines and filters, a cold
  wait and an expensive repair.

## Scenario (for discussion)

A tech is working a parking-lot pole in freezing wind at the end of a long day. His fingers
are numb and he is dropping wire nuts from the basket. What should he do? (Lower the basket,
warm up in the cab, change into dry gloves, and finish when he can work safely.)

## Discussion questions

1. What is in your winter kit on the truck?
2. How do you inspect a lift or ladder that has been sitting in freezing rain?
3. What makes a long freezer job harder than outdoor cold?
