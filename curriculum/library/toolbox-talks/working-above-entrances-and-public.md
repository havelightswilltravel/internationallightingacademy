*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Most of our work is done in places that are open for business - stores, offices, parking
lots. The public does not know what a bucket truck or a scissor lift can do, and they will
walk right under you. **Their safety is part of your job.** Our company calls the area under
and around you the **controlled access zone**: whenever you are elevated more than 6 ft, it
is the area you control at all times.

## Key points

- **Talk to the site manager first.** Before you start, discuss what you will be doing,
  where, and the plan for keeping people clear - especially near entrances and checkouts.
- **Cone and restrict the area** around bucket trucks and lifts so nobody can be hit by a
  falling tool, lens or fixture. Cones at the back of the truck alone do not protect the
  front door.
- **Working above an entrance?** Close or redirect that entrance with the manager's approval,
  schedule the work outside business hours, or post a person to keep traffic out. Never
  work directly over people.
- **A spotter is required** while using lift equipment on most interior services, and
  especially during business hours. The spotter watches the floor, the doors and the people
  - not their phone.
- **Tools up high:** take only what you need, keep it in a bag or tethered, and clear loose
  parts off the platform.
- **Slips and trips:** set up wet-floor signs if you clean, keep cords out of walkways, check
  your lift for leaking fluid, and keep exits, panels and fire equipment clear.

## What happened

A photo from one of our jobs showed a bucket truck set up at a storefront with the boom over
the customer entrance and cones only at the rear bumper. Nobody was hurt, but customers were
walking in and out beneath the basket. Ask the crew: what would you have done? (Talk to the
manager, use the other entrance, barricade the walkway, or come back after hours.)

## Discussion questions

1. How do you set up a controlled access zone in a parking lot? In a store aisle?
2. What do you do if a customer walks through your cones?
3. When is it better to reschedule than to work during business hours?
