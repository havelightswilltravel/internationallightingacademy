*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings and safety manual.*

## Why it matters

Working at height is not a circus act. Most of our work happens above the floor - on
ladders, in bucket trucks, boom lifts and scissor lifts - so fall protection is reviewed at
our safety meetings every year, along with a hands-on PPE inspection.

## Key points

- **When it is required.** Our company manual requires a harness system or a standard
  guardrail **any time there is an unguarded fall potential of more than 4 ft** (the OSHA
  general-industry trigger). On construction work the OSHA trigger is 6 ft. When in doubt,
  use the stricter number.
- **Boom lifts and bucket trucks: harness on and tied off at all times** in the basket, to
  the manufacturer's anchor. Scissor lifts and personnel lifts: stay inside the guardrails,
  and follow the manufacturer's manual on harness use.
- **Inspect before every use.** Harness webbing, stitching, D-ring, buckles, lanyard and
  shock pack. Any fraying, cuts, burns or a deployed shock pack = out of service.
- **Trauma (suspension relief) straps are installed on every harness.** A worker hanging in
  a harness can lose consciousness from blood pooling in the legs (orthostatic intolerance)
  in minutes. Know your rescue plan before you go up.
- **100% tie-off.** When moving between anchors with no guardrail or cage, use a double
  (twin-leg) lanyard so one leg is always attached. On fixed ladders with a vertical
  lifeline, use the cable-grab system, one worker per lifeline.
- **Anchors:** capable of supporting **5,000 lb** per worker, or designed by a qualified
  person.
- **Clearance:** with a 6-ft shock-absorbing lanyard, plan on about **18 1/2 ft of clear space
  below the anchor** (lanyard length + deceleration + your height + a safety margin). If you
  do not have that, a lanyard will not stop you before you hit the level below - use a
  self-retracting lifeline or a restraint setup instead.
- **Restraint at open edges.** Where you would be exposed to an unprotected platform edge, set
  the system up so you cannot reach the edge at all.
- **Housekeeping up high.** Take only the tools you need. Clear scrap, old parts and loose
  objects from the platform as you go.

## Scenario (for discussion)

At an outdoor sign structure, a tech climbs onto a service catwalk to reach a fixture and
wants to skip clipping in "for just one minute." What do you say? Our rule for elevated
structures: service from the ladder or lift, or wear the harness and stay tied off on the
platform - and if the ladder cannot reach, write it up for the right size lift.

## Discussion questions

1. Where would you anchor in a bucket truck basket? Where would you never anchor?
2. You are 14 ft up with a 6-ft lanyard. Does it protect you? Why not?
3. Who on the crew could perform a rescue if you were hanging in your harness?
