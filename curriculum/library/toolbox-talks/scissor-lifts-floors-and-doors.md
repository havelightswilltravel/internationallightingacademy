*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

A scissor lift is heavy, quiet and easy to drive - and that is how it cracks tile, gouges a
door frame or rolls into a customer. Most of our scissor-lift problems are about the
**building**, not the lift.

## Key points

- **Know the weight.** Our standard 19-ft slab scissor weighs about **3,300 lb** on four small
  wheels. A one-person man lift weighs about 1,100 lb.
- **Tile floors and heavy lifts don't mix.** Point loads can crack tile. When you write up the
  return: specify the man lift if it can do the job, or write up **plywood to go out with the
  scissor** to spread the load. Some customers require floor protection on every service.
- **Single doors:** before you bring a lift through a door, **inspect the door and frame for
  existing damage, photograph it and tell the manager.** Otherwise we will be blamed for it
  the day after we leave. Go slow and **use a spotter** any time you enter or exit with a
  lift.
- **Spotter on interior jobs**, especially during business hours.
- **Inspect before use:** fluid leaks (a slip hazard on a customer's floor), guardrails and
  gate, controls, emergency lowering, tires, decals.
- **Straddle attachments:** when sent out with a man lift and straddle, follow the
  instructions on the label exactly. Best practice is to set it up once in the shop, with
  someone experienced, before you load it.
- **Charging:**
  - Unplug by **holding the plug**, not yanking the cord - we keep ruining chargers.
  - **Check for the charge light.** No light? Check and reset the GFCI outlet you are plugged
    into.
- **Winter:** lifts are kept covered through the winter months; clear ice and snow before use.
- **Nobody rides or moves a rolling scaffold with anyone on it** (company policy) - and the
  same thinking applies to lifts: drive elevated only as the manufacturer allows.

## What happened

Our crews have left cracked floor tile behind a scissor lift, and we have been blamed for damaged doors we did not damage because nobody photographed them
first. Both are avoidable with a few minutes of planning and a phone camera.

## Discussion questions

1. How can you tell if a floor will crack under a lift?
2. When should you write up plywood or a lighter lift?
3. What photos do you take before rolling a lift into a store?
