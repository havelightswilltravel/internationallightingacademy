*Toolbox talk (about 15 minutes including the inspection) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

A Bureau of Labor Statistics study of ladder accidents found that about two-thirds of the
injured workers had **never been trained to inspect a ladder for defects**, and more than half
of the straight ladders involved had not been secured at the top or bottom. Inspection is the
cheapest fall protection we have. That is why our company runs a **formal ladder, ladder-rack
and extension-cord inspection several times a year**, right after the safety meeting.

## Key points

- **Two sets of eyes on every ladder and cord.** Pair up with another tech. If you have never
  used the inspection form, do not pair with someone else who has never used it.
- **Read the reminders on the form** before you start. Turn the completed form in to your
  supervisor before you leave for your jobs. The inspection is part of the meeting time and
  should take about 15 minutes; if you need longer, get approval.
- **Ladder checklist** (also every time you use it): non-slip feet in place; rungs and steps
  free of oil, grease and mud; joints and fittings tight; spreaders straight and locking;
  rails and steps not cracked, crushed or bent; no sharp edges; locks and rope on extension
  ladders working; all labels in place and readable.
- **Red tag, don't repair.** A ladder with a structural defect is red tagged "DO NOT USE",
  taken off the truck and a replacement is requested. Most ladders cannot be repaired to
  manufacturer specifications, and side rails are never repaired. Only manufacturer parts
  are used for things like feet.
- **Ladder racks count too.** Report a bad rack or a missing lock on the pull sheet. The large
  multi-position ladder is always locked to the rack, in the shop and on the truck - unlocked,
  it falls off or walks away.
- **Extension cords:** check the jacket for cuts, burns, cracks and crushing, the plug and
  ground pin, and the strain relief. A failed cord is red tagged, **the ends are cut off** so
  nobody uses it again, and you tell the shop so it is replaced.
- **Store ladders right:** in racks or on hooks where they cannot be damaged or warp, with
  nothing hung on them.

## Scenario (for discussion)

During an inspection a pair of techs found a stepladder with a cracked rail that had been
riding on a van for weeks. The tech who normally used it had not noticed - he looked at the
steps, not the rails. Two people catch what one person misses.

## Discussion questions

1. Which item on the form do people skip most often?
2. What do you do if you find a damaged ladder on a job, far from the shop?
3. Why do we cut the ends off a failed cord instead of taping it?
