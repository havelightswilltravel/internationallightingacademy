*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings and safety manual.*

## Why it matters

We do not use many chemicals, but we use some every day - cleaners, wasp spray, lubricants,
caulk, spray paint, fuel - and we work above ceilings full of insulation and old materials.
Our safety manual covers hazard communication, industrial hygiene and fire prevention, and
they all come down to: **know what you are working with.**

## Key points - chemicals

- **Safety Data Sheets (SDSs)** - the older term is MSDS - come from the manufacturer. The
  company keeps a master file in the warehouse that you can reach at all times. **If there is
  no SDS for a product, don't use it.**
- **Label secondary containers.** If you pour a product into a spray bottle or other container,
  label it with the same information as the original, including the product name, so anyone
  dealing with a spill knows what it is.
- Consumer products used the way a consumer would use them are exempt from some HazCom rules,
  but the labeling rule above still applies to anything you transfer.

## Key points - health hazards on site

- **Stay reachable.** Keep cell contact with the office at every job site so help can be sent
  in an emergency.
- **Masks around insulation** and other airborne dust - above ceilings, in attics, when cutting
  or drilling.
- **Lead and asbestos:** any demolition or remodeling (drywall, painted surfaces, grinding) is
  assessed for lead and asbestos **before** work starts. Old ceiling tile, pipe insulation and
  some fixture parts can contain asbestos - if you suspect it, stop and call your supervisor.
- **Noise:** use hearing protection around loud equipment and when using hammer drills or saws.
- **Equipment you do not recognize:** stay clear. Our crews have come across industrial
  equipment with radiation warning labels on a job.

## Key points - fire prevention

- Fire extinguishers in every vehicle and in the shop are **visible, accessible, inspected and
  charged**.
- Flammable liquids over one gallon go in **approved safety cans**; a gallon or less stays in
  its original container or a safety can.
- Keep the area clear of combustibles when using torches or other heat-producing tools, and
  keep an extinguisher right there. No open-flame heaters in enclosed spaces without
  ventilation. No propane (LPG) storage inside buildings.

## Discussion questions

1. Where is the SDS binder? How would you find the SDS for the wasp spray on your truck?
2. What tells you a ceiling space might contain asbestos?
3. When did you last check the gauge on your truck's extinguisher?
