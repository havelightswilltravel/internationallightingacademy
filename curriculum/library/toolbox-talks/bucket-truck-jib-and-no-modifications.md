*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Our bucket trucks and lift trucks are the most expensive and most dangerous tools we own.
Most of the problems discussed at our safety meetings were not mechanical failures - they were
small choices: a jib left up, a bracket screwed to a basket, a hole drilled to drain water.

## Key points

- **Know the machine before you run it.** Inspect every lift before each use (fluid leaks,
  controls, emergency lowering, platform, decals). Harness and lanyard on in the basket at all
  times. Cone off the area around the truck.
- **No modifications - ever - without the manufacturer's written approval.** That includes:
  - drilling **drain holes** in a bucket (it weakens the structure and can fail the
    dielectric/structural inspection);
  - screwing a **bracket or hook to a basket** to hang a drill - even small screw holes can
    fail the annual inspection;
  - improvised mounts on trucks or trailers.
- **Jib and winch line:**
  - **Inspect the rope before use.** Like your harness, any fraying means it is replaced.
  - **When you coil the line, keep tension on it** - even hand pressure works. A tight coil
    followed by a loose coil can let the load drop suddenly.
  - **Stow the jib before you drive.** Left up, it is the highest point on the truck and will
    eventually be ripped off by a bridge, sign or wire.
  - Not trained on the jib? Do not use it.
- **PTO won't engage?** On some of our trucks a 12-V circuit breaker protects the PTO circuit.
  The red button is **test only**; you reset it with the lever. Check it before calling for
  help.
- **Boom works but will not raise or lower?** On trucks with a 24-V boom battery bank, low
  battery voltage is a likely cause. Call the equipment service line and check fuses and
  relays first; then measure the bank (24 V minimum) and each battery. Charge or replace
  batteries only as the manufacturer allows.
- **Fuel:** never let a diesel lift truck run low while you are up - and keep at least a half
  tank in winter.

## What happened

Someone screwed a small bracket to one of our lift-truck baskets, most likely to hang a drill.
On another truck, holes had been drilled in the bottom of the bucket to let rain drain out.
Either "improvement" can fail the annual inspection and take the truck out of service until
it is repaired the manufacturer's way.

## Discussion questions

1. What should you do with water that collects in a bucket?
2. Where do you hang your tools in the basket, if not on a homemade hook?
3. What is the first thing you check if the boom will not move but the truck runs?
