*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's October-December safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

Counting how often we do it, **driving is the most dangerous thing we do**. Winter roads
multiply that risk, and our trucks are heavy - a loaded bucket truck can weigh 17,000 lb.

## Key points - icy roads

- **Slow down and triple your following distance.** Brake gently; if the wheels lock, ease
  off. With ABS, press and hold - the pulsing is normal.
- **Headlights on**, lights and windshield clean. Use lower gears for traction, especially on
  hills. **No cruise control or overdrive** on ice.
- **Bridges, overpasses, shaded spots and back roads freeze first** - even above freezing.
- **Don't pass snowplows or sanders.**
- **Rear-wheel skid:** off the gas and steer where you want the front to go; be ready to
  correct back the other way.
- **Stuck?** Don't spin the wheels. Rock the steering side to side, use a light foot, shovel
  out, and put sand, kitty litter or gravel under the drive wheels.

## Key points - company rules

- **Mountain and canyon roads in snow:** our company prefers that you **not travel them at all
  when there is snow on the road**. Some state laws require chains or approved traction tires
  on those roads in winter conditions. Not every company vehicle has the required tires. If you
  are dispatched to a canyon job with snow on the road or in the forecast for your return,
  **check the weather and talk to your supervisor before you go** - dispatchers can misjudge
  weather.
- **No off-road driving in a company vehicle**, especially in spring mud season. Every tow
  out of the mud costs real money and a lost day.

## If you get stranded

- **Stay with the vehicle** - it is shelter and easier for rescuers to find. Call for help.
- **Be visible:** bright cloth on the antenna or window; raise the hood when the snow stops.
- **Run the engine about 10 minutes each hour** for heat, with the dome light on. **Keep the
  exhaust pipe clear of snow** and crack a downwind window to prevent carbon monoxide
  poisoning.
- Move arms and legs to keep blood moving, but do not work up a sweat. Huddle together and
  sleep in shifts.

## Discussion questions

1. Which of our vehicles have traction-rated tires? How do you find out?
2. What winter items should be on every truck (scraper, shovel, blanket, traction material)?
3. What would you say to a dispatcher who sends you up a canyon in a storm warning?
