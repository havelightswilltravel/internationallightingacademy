*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

"The lights are flashing" is one of our most common service calls - and one of the easiest to
leave unfinished. Intermittent problems may not show up for hours, and if we do not pin down
which fixture it is, we replace the wrong part or leave without fixing anything.

## Key points - company procedure

- **Step 1 is still the meter.** Measure supply voltage at the fixture (hot to neutral) before
  changing anything. Flashing across a whole area points to supply or controls, not to every
  driver at once.
- **Read the symptom:**
  - **Strobing on and off at full brightness** is usually a **failing driver** (or a driver
    cycling on thermal or over-voltage protection).
  - **Flicker in one LED or one section** of a fixture - for example a single-diode or single-
    board fixture - points to the **LED board or the fixture itself**, not the driver.
  - **Several fixtures on the same circuit** flashing together: look upstream - loose
    connections, a failing control (sensor, photocell, relay) or dimming compatibility.
- **Pin down the fixture.** If the customer reports flashing but cannot show you exactly which
  fixtures, **ask them to mark or identify the exact fixtures and call in a new ticket** when
  they have. Don't guess and don't shotgun parts.
- **"A fixture" includes the small stuff.** A single LED light stick in a refrigerated case door
  counts as a fixture for this procedure.
- **Never leave a fixture strobing.** A strobing fixture is a hazard (it can trigger seizures and
  disorients people) and it makes us look like we did not finish. If you cannot repair it on
  this trip, lock out and disconnect that fixture safely, label it, and write up the return.
- **Make good notes:** which fixtures, what the symptom looked like, how long you watched,
  readings taken, what you replaced or disconnected.

## Scenario (for discussion)

A customer complains of flashing fixtures but cannot say which ones, and nothing flashes while
you are on site. What do you do before you leave? (Ask the customer to identify the exact
fixtures and call in a new ticket, write down everything you checked and observed, and make
sure nothing is left strobing.)

## Discussion questions

1. How long would you watch an intermittent fixture before deciding it is not acting up?
2. What notes would let the next tech pick up where you left off?
3. A single LED stick in a case door flickers. Driver or stick? How do you tell?
