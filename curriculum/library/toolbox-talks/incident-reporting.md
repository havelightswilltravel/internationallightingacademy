*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings and safety manual.*

## Why it matters

We cannot fix what we never hear about. Our safety committee - the company leadership plus
employee representatives - meets monthly and at least once a year reviews injury trends and
the **root causes** of serious and near-serious incidents. That review is only as good as the
reports you turn in.

## Key points - company rules

- **Report unsafe conditions immediately** to your supervisor, and **report every accident,
  injury and incident promptly** - including near misses.
- **Emergency?** Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. Notify your supervisor as soon
  as you are able.
- **Non-emergency work injury?** Notify your supervisor first, then go to the company's
  designated occupational clinic.
- **Anything beyond first aid requires the incident investigation form.** Temporary or agency
  workers must also notify their own employer right away.
- **Carry the form.** A copy lives in the tech cheat sheets, and it must travel with you -
  keep one in your vehicle paperwork tin, especially if you ever work alone or travel. Incidents
  on and off the clock while traveling are reported.
- **Root cause, not blame.** Investigations of serious or potentially serious events look past
  the symptom ("he slipped") to the underlying causes - methods, equipment, training,
  scheduling pressure, culture - so the same thing does not happen to the next person.
- **Discipline is for unsafe acts, not for reporting.** Our enforcement policy targets people
  who knowingly break safety rules they were trained on. Reporting a near miss or your own
  mistake is what we want.

## What happened

A tech dispatched to cover a photocell climbed a ladder that was too short and fell. He had
only minor injuries, but because it was reported and brought to the safety meeting, the whole
crew talked through the right ladder choice. A near miss that is never reported teaches no one.

## Discussion questions

1. Where is your incident form right now?
2. What near misses have you seen this month that we never talked about?
3. What makes people hesitate to report, and how do we fix that?
