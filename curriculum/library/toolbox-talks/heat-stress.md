*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's May safety meetings.*

## Why it matters

When it is hot, your body works overtime to cool itself by sweating and moving blood to the
skin. Hot weather, a black parking lot, a bucket in full sun, or an attic can overwhelm that
system. Heat illness is preventable if we watch for it - in ourselves and each other.

## Key points

- **Know the early signs:** thirst and less urination (dehydration), heat rash, muscle
  cramps, swollen hands and feet, and **heat exhaustion** - clammy, pale skin, dizziness,
  nausea, headache, weakness.
- **Heat stroke is a medical emergency - call 911.** Signs: confusion, irritability or
  strange behavior, sluggishness, rapid heart rate and breathing, very high body temperature,
  fainting, seizures, loss of consciousness.
- **While waiting for help:** move the person to shade or air conditioning, remove extra
  clothing, and **cool them fast** - soak them with cool water, use ice packs or cold wet
  towels on the neck, armpits and groin, and fan them. Stay with them.
- **Who is at higher risk:** anyone ill, with heart disease or high blood pressure, on certain
  medications (ask your pharmacist), new to the heat, or wearing heavy PPE such as arc-rated
  clothing, respirators and hard hats.
- **Prevention:**
  - Drink water early and often - do not wait to be thirsty. Go easy on energy drinks and
    sugary drinks.
  - Wear light-colored, loose, breathable clothing.
  - Fans do little once temperatures are in the 90s.
  - **Use the company pop-up canopies** when working in unshaded areas - very useful when
    digging or working a pole base.
  - Schedule the hardest outdoor work early in the day and take breaks in the shade or a
    cooled cab.
  - It is OK to idle your truck to run the A/C for a cool-down break.

## Scenario (for discussion)

On a summer parking-lot job, a tech kept working through a headache and nausea to finish a
pole. His partner noticed he had stopped sweating and was confused about which fixture they
were on, sat him in the air-conditioned cab, and called the supervisor. He recovered - because
his partner was watching.

## Discussion questions

1. What jobs this week put us in full sun or hot attics?
2. How much water is on your truck right now?
3. What would make you stop a coworker who says "I'm fine"?
