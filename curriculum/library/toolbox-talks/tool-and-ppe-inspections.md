*Toolbox talk (about 15 minutes including the inspection) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings and safety manual.*

## Why it matters

A cracked screwdriver handle, a frayed harness or a meter lead with a nick in it fails at
the worst moment. Our company runs **PPE and hand-tool inspections during the safety meeting**
several times a year, and every tech inspects their own gear before each use.

## Key points - company rules

- **A tool that fails inspection is not used again.** You have **30 days to repair or replace
  it**, and you show the replacement to your supervisor. Failed tools are signed off with your
  supervisor.
- **Failed PPE is replaced immediately** - you do not work without it.
- **Your own tools must meet OSHA and ANSI requirements** just like company tools.
- **PPE you are expected to have and use:** safety glasses; a **non-conductive (Class E) hard
  hat** where objects could fall or you could strike your head - including whenever you are
  being lifted in a bucket or lift; hearing protection; insulated tools; gloves; your
  lock and tags; and the harness and trauma straps for lift work. Rated rubber insulating
  gloves are used for energized work by qualified workers only.
- **Gloves:** keep them dry and with your tools, not stuffed in the van. Use work gloves until
  they are actually worn out and turn in old ones when you get new ones - they are not
  disposable.
- **Power tools (safety manual):**
  - Turn the tool off and let it stop before you set it down. Never leave a running tool.
  - Unplug it or remove the battery before changing bits or blades or making adjustments.
  - Guards stay on saws and grinders; never run a grinder wheel above its rated speed.
  - Compressed air for cleaning only below 30 psi, with chip guarding and eye protection.
  - Avoid striking metal on metal (chipping and mushroomed heads send fragments flying).
- **Equipment is used only for its intended purpose, with all guards and safety devices in
  place.** No one modifies company equipment without written approval from the manufacturer.
- **Need a tool you don't have? Ask.** The shop keeps a list of the tools we own in the tech
  cheat sheets. Better to come back with the right tool than to fight the job with the wrong
  one.

## Scenario (for discussion)

During an inspection, a tech's favorite insulated screwdriver has a crack in the handle
insulation. It still works fine as a screwdriver. What happens next? (It fails, it is not
used on anything electrical again, and it is replaced within 30 days.)

## Discussion questions

1. What does a failed insulated tool look like?
2. Why do bucket and lift operators need a hard hat even outdoors in open air?
3. What gloves are right for wire pulling? For handling broken lamps? For energized work?
