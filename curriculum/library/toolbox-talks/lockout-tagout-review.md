*Toolbox talk (about 10 minutes) - adapted from our company's monthly safety meetings and safety manual.*

## Why it matters

If you turn off a circuit to work on it, you are exposed the moment someone else turns it
back on. That is the whole reason for lockout/tagout: **lockout whenever possible, tagout only
when lockout is not possible**, and the company provides the locks.

## Key points - the company method

1. **Identify the equipment** involved in the work.
2. **Determine availability.** Get approval from site management before you shut down
   lighting, electrical or equipment, and notify everyone affected.
3. **De-energize and tag.** Put the disconnects for the circuits you will work on in the OFF
   position and tag them.
4. **Lock out.** The person doing the work places their own lock at the breaker or
   disconnect. A wall switch or a lighting control is **not** a lockout point.
5. **Safety review / verify.** Make sure no one is exposed, **try to start it** (operate the
   switch), return the switch to off, then test for absence of voltage live-dead-live.
6. **More than one worker or crew?** Each person places their own lock and tag.
7. **Removal.** When work is complete and everyone is clear, notify affected personnel, do a
   safety check, and **each worker removes their own lock**. Restore power and verify the
   system works.

**Your key stays with you** the whole time your lock is on. Only the person exposed to the
hazard places or removes that lock.

## When the lights stay on

Our manual recognizes two situations where lighting may stay energized: occupied sites
during business hours where the lights are needed for safety and shutting them off would
create a greater hazard, and troubleshooting that can only be done with power on. In both,
**only trained, qualified people do the work**, with the PPE and plan the company program
requires - and the first choice is still to schedule the work so it can be done dead.

## Ask yourself

- Can everyone tell, from the locks and tags, who is working on this equipment?
- Do I have enough locks and tags for this job?
- If this cannot be locked out, is a safe procedure written down and being followed?

## Discussion questions

1. Why is turning off the wall switch not lockout?
2. What do you do if you find someone else's lock still on after they have left the site?
3. How do you lock out a fixture fed from a panel with no lockable breaker?
