*Company job-type playbook. It covers both service calls on case lighting and LED cooler retrofit
projects.*

## What this job is

Repairing or retrofitting the lighting in reach-in coolers and freezers, open multi-deck cases and
walk-ins in grocery and convenience stores. You work in an open, busy store, above and around food,
often in the cold, on circuits that a defrost cycle or a case switch can bring back on at any time.

## What "done right" looks like

- You **ruled out defrost** before calling it an outage: mark the location and come back in about half
  an hour.
- Diagnosis followed the company order: switches, defrost, lamps, sockets, ballast or driver, harness,
  then line voltage.
- **Tube guards and lens covers are on every lamp over food.** Food was covered before you opened a
  canopy.
- Every LED stick or strip is **seated and secured**, wiring is trimmed and dressed with splices inside
  an enclosure, and no leftover brackets or parts are left in the case.
- No stick is left strobing. Refrigeration controls were never touched.
- Seal-breaking harness work was turned over to the manager for a case specialist.

## Before you leave the shop

- Read the service order or project sheet: store, case lineup, door numbers, case make and model if
  known, lamp or LED type.
- Pack a jacket and dry gloves. A freezer job can last all day, even in summer.
- Bring the right parts: moisture-rated sockets, remote-rated ballasts or the correct LED drivers,
  guards. For a retrofit, count kits and mounting clips against the door count.
- If a product line is discontinued, plan to bring back photos of the case, plug and receptacle and
  ballast label, measurements, color temperature, and lamp type and quantity per ballast.

## On site

1. Check in with the manager: which cases, any known defrost schedule or recent refrigeration work,
   who moves product (store staff usually do), food-safety and glass policies, where the panels are.
2. Block your work area with cones or a cart; keep the aisle passable; never leave a ladder unattended.
3. Check switches and schedules. If defrost is possible, **mark it and come back**.
4. A case switch, defrost or controller is **not a lockout**. Lock out the case lighting breaker,
   tell the manager what else it may feed, and **verify live-dead-live**.
5. Cover food, keep doors closed as much as possible, and do not leave a case open for long.
6. If a lamp breaks over food, stop and tell the manager; the manager decides about product.
7. Restore power, check every door or section, and clean up all debris and old lamps.
8. Be polite to shoppers and keep language and calls professional.

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **LED cooler strips hanging by their wires.** On an early LED cooler retrofit, some light bars were not
  seated and ended up hanging by their wires, and the customer pointed out loose slack, exposed
  connectors and a leftover bracket. A tech had to go back (case study *LED Cooler Strips Hanging by
  Their Wires*).
- **Flashing door sticks nobody can point to.** A single LED stick counts as a fixture. Ask the manager to
  mark the exact doors and call in a new ticket; never leave a stick strobing (LT3-C08 Lesson 3).
- **"Outage" that was a defrost cycle.** Mark it and come back in about 30 minutes before replacing parts.
- **Discontinued case ballasts.** Whole product lines have disappeared. Collect what the office needs to
  find an LED option (toolbox talk *Driver Replacement or New Fixture? Writing It Up Right*).

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- Store, aisle, case lineup and door or section numbers.
- Case manufacturer, model and serial; complaint and what you checked, in order.
- Parts replaced with full part numbers; parts needed and why.
- Anything turned over to the manager for a case specialist, with the manager's name.
- Any product or glass incident and what the manager decided.
- Photos of the data plate, failed parts and finished work. For retrofits: counts, skipped doors and
  after photos.
