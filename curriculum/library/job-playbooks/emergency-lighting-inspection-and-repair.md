## What this job is

The annual **90-minute** inspection of a site's exit signs, bug eyes, combo units and battery-backup
fixtures using the company inspection spreadsheet, plus repair of the failures we find (batteries,
lamps or heads, charging boards, whole units). The customer is buying a **record** to show the fire
marshal or insurance inspector, so the paperwork is the product.

## What "done right" looks like

- Every unit is on the floor plan and on the spreadsheet, with a location the next tech can find.
- Charge indicators were checked **before** anything was turned off.
- Only **labeled** emergency breakers were operated, by someone qualified and authorized to operate
  them. If they were not labeled, the inspection stopped and it was written up.
- Failures are diagnosed (battery, lamp/head, charging unit), tagged on the unit, and photographed with
  a full elevation photo and a label close-up.
- Every breaker was restored and every indicator confirmed charging before you left.
- The record shows start and end times, results, and later the corrective action and retest date.

## Before you leave the shop

- Read the service order: site, number of units expected, whether repairs are approved on this trip.
- Print or load the inspection spreadsheet and ask the site for a floor plan.
- Agree on timing with the site: batteries need up to 24 hours to recharge afterward, so the building
  should not need emergency lighting right after the test.
- Load common batteries and heads if repairs are approved; bring a flashlight and the right ladder.
- Know the company PPE requirements for operating breakers.

## On site

1. Check in with the site manager, get the layout and mark each unit on the map as you go.
2. Walk every unit and check the charge indicator. Dark indicator with good power means a bad unit;
   no power is a circuit problem to write up.
3. With permission, warn occupants, turn off the labeled emergency breakers and **start the 90-minute
   timer**. Write the start time.
4. Walk the route quickly: every indicator should be off and every unit lit. Indicators still on mean
   you have not found all the emergency breakers.
5. While the timer runs, fill out the spreadsheet and tag failures. Do approved preventive maintenance
   (clean faces, re-aim heads) without opening anything energized.
6. At 90 minutes, walk again: units still lit pass. A unit that lit but did not last is usually the
   battery; an old unit usually gets replaced rather than re-batteried.
7. **Restore every breaker** and confirm each indicator is back on. Tell the manager the units are
   recharging.
8. Repairs: lock out and verify live-dead-live; remember battery packs keep heads live with the breaker
   off.

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **Unlabeled emergency breakers.** Company rule: do not continue the inspection. Restore what you
  turned off and write it up for an electrician; guessing can drop a cooler or register lane (LT3-C04
  Lesson 6).
- **Emergency wall packs counted as standard.** On one of our jobs, 13 of 20 wall packs sold as standard
  were emergency units. Count EM units separately (case study *Emergency Wall Packs Quoted as
  Standard*).
- **Emergency fixtures nobody planned for on retrofits.** Never cut out or add emergency equipment
  without direction (case study *Emergency Fixtures Nobody Planned For*).
- **Live parts in "dead" fixtures.** DC battery packs are one of the reasons our techs have found live
  wiring after the breaker was off (case study *Live Wiring in "Dead" Fixtures*).
- **Removed batteries in the trash.** Recycle them; write chemistry and weight on the work order.

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- Completed spreadsheet: header (site, S/O number, contact), one row per unit, required fields for every
  failed unit (model/manufacturer, housing color, letter color, failed component, battery or ballast
  code, picture number).
- Footer: panel voltage, totals by unit type, surveyed by, title and date.
- Marked floor plan; numbered photos matching the spreadsheet.
- Start and end times; units repaired on this trip and retest results.
- Battery chemistry and weight for recycling. Send everything to the office the same day.

## Load list

Based on the core kit; no company list yet for this job type. Typical items:

- Inspection spreadsheet, floor plan, clipboard and marker
- Timer and flashlight
- PPE required for operating breakers
- Tested meter and lockout kit

- Common replacement batteries and lamp heads (if repairs approved)
- Failure tags
- Rags and cleaner for sign faces
- Recycle container for removed batteries (batteries go back on the separate recycle pallet)
