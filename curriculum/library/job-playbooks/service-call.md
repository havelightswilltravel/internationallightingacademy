## What this job is

A single-site visit to find and fix a lighting problem: lights out, flashing, a dead sign face, a time
clock or photocell issue, a broken lens. Most service calls carry a **not-to-exceed (NTE)** amount, the
most the customer has approved for the visit. Our company's goal on every service call is to **finish
on the first trip.**

## What "done right" looks like

- You arrived **before the appointment time**, checked in the way the customer requires, and checked out
  the same way.
- The problem is diagnosed with a meter, not guessed at. Step 1 on a dark LED fixture is your meter
  across hot and neutral.
- The job is finished today, or you asked for an **NTE increase from the site** before you left, and the
  answer (approved or denied) is in writing with the name of the person who gave it.
- Every fixture you opened was cleaned, and the cleaning is in your notes.
- Nothing is left strobing, hanging or exposed.
- The work order, photos and return sheet are finished **on site, while you are still clocked in to the
  job.**

## Before you leave the shop

- Read the service order the day before. The office contacts you ahead of an appointment so you have
  time to ask questions; use it.
- Know the **NTE**, the site contact, the check-in method and any customer standard (for example,
  whether the customer allows ballast-bypass lamps).
- Check the truck against the **pull sheet**: standard ladders, tape, connectors, bagged and labeled
  sockets, rags and cleaner. Write anything missing on the pull sheet.
- Pick the right ladder or lift for the reach. If the reach needs a lift, confirm it before you go.
- Pull any special parts for the job, and note the color setting you need for color-selectable lamps.

## On site

1. Check in with the site contact. Ask exactly which fixtures are out or flashing and since when.
2. Look before you touch: note existing damage (tiles, lenses, poles, doors) and photograph it.
3. Set up: cones or barricade below the ladder or lift, out of entrances and walkways.
4. Diagnose: controls first (switch, sensor, clock, photocell), then **meter hot to neutral** at the
   fixture. No voltage at the fixture means an upstream problem; a new driver will not fix it.
5. **Lock out and verify live-dead-live** before you open the wiring compartment. Treat every fixture as
   live until you have tested it; our techs have found live conductors in "dead" fixtures.
6. Repair with matching parts. Program programmable drivers unpowered and mark the setting.
7. Clean the fixture while it is open. Put tiles back with clean hands.
8. Restore power and prove it works through its real control.
9. Watch your language and phone calls; you are in the customer's space.

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **Going past the NTE.** Overages cannot be billed afterward. Track labor, parts and equipment against
  the NTE as you go, and call for an increase from the site, not later (toolbox talk *NTE Increases, Quotes and
  Finishing on the First Trip*).
- **Flashing fixtures nobody can point to.** Ask the customer to identify the exact fixtures and call in a
  new ticket; record what you checked; never leave a fixture strobing (toolbox talk *Flashing, Strobing and
  Flickering LED Fixtures*).
- **Wrong or vague part on the write-up.** "24 V driver" is not a part code. Capture the full label, the
  output connector and a count of matching fixtures so the office can price repair versus a new
  fixture (toolbox talk *Driver Replacement or New Fixture? Writing It Up Right*).
- **Customer told us the fixtures were dirty.** Bugs and dust left in fixtures we maintain have drawn
  repeated complaints. Clean and note it (toolbox talk *Leave It Cleaner - Fingerprints, Bugs and Professionalism*).
- **Too-short ladder.** A tech fell from an 8-ft A-frame while covering a photocell. Use the right height
  ladder or a lift (case study *Too-Short Ladder at a Wall Fixture*).

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- Work order: what you did, with **exact part codes**, ending "see return" if more is needed.
- Return sheet: parts and codes, quantities, equipment, people, time estimate, access notes, photos.
- Photos uploaded to the company folder **and** the customer portal, set to customer-visible.
- Signatures with **printed names**. Check-out done; if the system fails, call and note it.
- Equipment beyond standard ladders and tools, and job supplies, recorded.
- Truck stock used written on the pull sheet; unneeded special parts to the parts-return shelf.

## Load list

Based on the core kit; no company list yet for this job type. Service trucks and lighting vans carry standard stock (wire nuts, common sockets, ladders); check it covers the work order. Typical items:

- Tested multimeter and proving unit, non-contact tester (warning only)
- Lockout kit with personal lock and tags
- Ladders sized for the reach (check the truck's standard ladders)
- Hand tools, insulated screwdrivers and strippers
- Wire nuts, lever connectors, tape, bagged and labeled sockets
- Common replacement drivers or lamps from the pull sheet
- Rags, approved cleaner, clean gloves
- Cones and caution tape
- Phone or camera, work orders and return sheets
