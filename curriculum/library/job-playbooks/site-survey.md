## What this job is

Walking a site to record what is there, so the office can quote a retrofit or replacement, set up a
service agreement or report outages **without sending someone back.** Every project crew later works
from your survey, so a missing field becomes their problem.

## What "done right" looks like

- Every area and pole is numbered on a map, and those numbers match the sheet and the photos.
- One row per **group** (same type, source and mounting in the same area) with quantity, source and
  watts, height, mounting, pole shape and size, and voltage, or "not verified."
- Emergency units, including exterior emergency wall packs, are counted separately.
- Hazards (leaning pole, open hand hole, exposed wire) were reported the same day and made safe.
- Photos are numbered and show the fixture, mounting, label and any damage.
- Comments cover outages, controls, access limits, ceiling types and anything a crew would need to know.

## Before you leave the shop

- Confirm the scope: inspection only, retrofit quote, or baseline for a service agreement. The scope
  decides whether you need light readings and lift access.
- Get a map: site plan or aerial for exteriors, floor plan for interiors.
- Pick the time: exterior outages show at night; labels and mounting are easier in daylight. A dusk
  start gets both.
- Ask where the lighting panels, time clocks, photocells and contactors are.
- Print the survey worksheet (and the emergency inspection spreadsheet if egress lighting is included).

## On site

1. Check in. Ask about access limits, hours areas can be worked, and special areas (coolers, food
   prep, data rooms).
2. Wear high-visibility clothing in lots; cone around a parked truck or lift.
3. Stay away from damaged or leaning poles and open hand holes until made safe; report them.
4. Work group by group, numbering on the map as you go.
5. Reading a label inside a fixture or hand hole means **lockout and live-dead-live verification**
   first; otherwise mark the field "not verified."
6. Record controls: switches, sensors, clocks, photocells, relays, dimming.
7. Interior: ceiling type (grid, hard lid, open structure), lenses, guards, flicker, mismatched color,
   access limits such as racks and cases.
8. Look for what would trip up a crew: kits that may not fit, fixtures over partitions, fixtures with
   emergency ballasts, floors that need protection under a lift.

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **Survey count did not match the site.** An office retrofit was bid for two-lamp fixtures but many had
  four lamps, and the crew found out on site (case study *Survey Said Two-Lamp Fixtures*). Open a sample
  of fixtures in each area; do not count from the floor alone.
- **Emergency wall packs quoted as standard.** 13 of 20 on one of our jobs (case study *Emergency Wall
  Packs Quoted as Standard*). Check every label or test button.
- **Missing tenon or mount size.** The most common reason a replacement head arrives and does not fit
  (LT5-C06 Lesson 7).
- **Unknown voltage.** New fixtures are destroyed when voltage is assumed (case study *New Fixtures
  Destroyed on Power-Up*). Measure it where you are qualified to, or write "not verified."
- **Canopy housings that did not match the kit.** Note can sizes and housing condition (case study
  *Canopy Kits That Did Not Match the Housings*).

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- Completed worksheet with general comments (access, traffic hours, trees, where controls are) and the
  totals block, including the number out when surveyed.
- Marked map; numbered photos matching the rows.
- Emergency inspection spreadsheet if included.
- Hazards reported, to whom and when.
- Send it all the same day so the office can enter it into the asset inventory.
