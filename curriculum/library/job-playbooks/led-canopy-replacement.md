## What this job is

Replacing or retrofitting the recessed or surface canopy fixtures over fuel islands, drive-through
lanes and similar covered areas, usually with LED kits or new fixtures, working from a lift or bucket
over an area where the public drives and walks. The site usually stays open.

## What "done right" looks like

- Every kit or fixture is **secured per the manufacturer's instructions**. Where a housing does not
  match the kit, the closest approved bracket fit is used with an **approved secondary restraint**, and
  nothing is left that you are unsure will stay secure.
- Loose or rusted housings were documented and secured, and the time spent is kept separate on the
  service order so it can be billed.
- A neat **canopy diagram** (north arrow, the building, every fixture and every problem fixture) goes in
  with the paperwork.
- Supply voltage was measured, factory photocells handled per company rule, and function tested.
- Traffic and pedestrians were kept out from under the lift the whole time.

## Before you leave the shop

- Read the sheet and survey: fixture counts per canopy, can or housing sizes, kit type, mounting
  instructions, voltage.
- Ask the questions the sheet does not answer: which lanes or pumps can be closed and when, who on site
  approves closures, and work hours (peak hours and blackout times).
- Confirm the lift fits under the canopy and how it gets to site. Inspect the lift and harness.
- Open any damaged kit cartons at the shop and report damage.
- Bring graph paper for the canopy diagram, and extra approved securing hardware.

## On site

1. Check in with the site manager. Agree on which lanes or islands are closed, in what order, and how
   customers are redirected.
2. Barricade the lane under the lift; keep vehicles and people out of the zone. Stop work if people
   keep walking in.
3. Before work, photograph each canopy and any existing damage.
4. **Meter first**, then **lock out and verify live-dead-live** on each circuit.
5. Do the sample install first and confirm the fit and the time per fixture.
6. Check every housing for rust and looseness before installing into it.
7. Install, dress wiring inside the enclosure, seal, and secure. If you cannot secure it, **stop,
   make it safe and call your supervisor.**
8. Restore power and test the lights through the site's control (or note "unable to test").
9. Mark the diagram as you go.

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **Canopy kits that did not match the housings.** On a gas station canopy retrofit, some housings did
  not match the kit's sizes and some were loose or rusted. The crew used the closest fit plus an approved
  secondary restraint, did not install kits they were unsure of, called the supervisor, drew a diagram
  of each canopy and kept securing time separate (case study *Canopy Kits That Did Not Match the
  Housings*).
- **New fixtures destroyed on power-up.** Meter first on every exterior project (case study *New
  Fixtures Destroyed on Power-Up*).
- **Working over the public.** Customers walked under a basket at a storefront set up with cones only at
  the rear bumper. Cone the whole area and agree on closures with the manager (case study *Bucket Over a
  Store Entrance*).
- **Loose fixtures left behind.** A loose fixture over a fuel island can fall long after you leave. If
  you are not certain it is secure, do not leave it (LT2-C12 Lesson 5).

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- Canopy diagram(s) with every problem fixture marked.
- Counts: kits or fixtures installed, secondary restraints used, housings repaired or secured (time kept
  separate), skipped fixtures with reasons.
- Measured voltage; photocell handling; function tested or "unable to test" with reason.
- Before and after photos from the same spot; photos of rusted or loose housings.
- Recycling and removed material recorded; service order signed.

## Load list

**Core kit** (every project): 6 ft and 8 ft ladders, wire nuts (BWN) and lever connectors, spray bottles, cleaner concentrate and the 5-gallon cleaner container, clean rags, drop cloths, vacuum (or the site's), red cart, trash cans with wheels, large garbage bags, tape gun and tape, recycle boxes (project lamps / 50 = boxes), black marker, shrink wrap, cones and caution tape, and a tablet with recaps and timecards loaded. Your own drill, bits, meter and lockout kit for every person.

**LED canopy add-ons** (from the company's LED canopy retrofit list). Canopy fixtures are expensive: count them when loading and on site, and handle them with care.

- Canopy fixtures, counted against the survey
- 2 cordless drills with bits (your own; ask before using the shop's)
- 12 ft ladders or the lift or bucket truck on the sheet; harness and lanyard
- Putty knives and adhesive remover for old gaskets and residue
- Tan wire nuts; wet-location connectors and sealant
- Tek screws in the sizes the fixture needs; a box of jack chain; approved secondary restraints
- Extension cords (to charge batteries at the hotel or on site)
- Clean rags, a booklet of graph paper for canopy layouts
- Cones, barricade tape and lane-closure signs
