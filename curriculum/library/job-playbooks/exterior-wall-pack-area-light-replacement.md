## What this job is

Replacing building-mounted wall packs, flood lights, and area or shoebox heads on existing poles,
usually with new LED fixtures, often from a bucket truck. It can be one fixture on a service call or a
whole lot on a project.

## What "done right" looks like

- **Supply voltage was measured with a meter** before anything new was connected, and the reading is on
  the work order ("277 V L-N, pole 4").
- The new fixture's label matches the measured voltage.
- **Redundant factory photocells are disconnected** unless you were told to keep them, and that is noted.
- **Function was tested** through the real control, or the work order says "unable to test" and why.
- Existing damage (pole base, hand hole, wall) was photographed and reported **before** the work.
- Emergency (battery) wall packs were counted separately and replaced with emergency-rated units.
- The work zone under the truck was coned off, including entrances and walkways.

## Before you leave the shop

- Read the service order or project specifics sheet. Confirm fixture types, quantities, mounting kits
  and whether any units are emergency-rated.
- Confirm the bucket truck or lift: daily inspection done, harness and lanyard inspected, jib stowed.
- Check that new fixtures are rated for the site voltage. If the site voltage was never measured, plan to
  measure first and call before installing.
- Know how the lot is controlled (contactor, time clock, building photocell) and where the controls are.
- Plan around trees and shrubs: if access needs trimming, it needs the right tool, training and the
  customer's permission.

## On site

1. Check in. Agree on where the truck sets up; avoid working over entrances, or close them with the
   manager's agreement.
2. **Look up** for overhead lines and keep company clearances. Cone off the whole area around the truck.
3. Inspect and photograph the pole, base, anchor bolts, hand hole and wall before you start.
4. **Meter first:** measure line-to-neutral and line-to-line. Never rely on a non-contact tester.
5. **Lock out and verify live-dead-live** before touching conductors.
6. Install per the manufacturer's instructions with wet-location connectors; seal entries.
7. Disconnect the redundant factory photocell where the site has its own control.
8. Restore power and **test function**: cover the photocell, use the clock override or HOA if you are
   qualified and the site allows it, or come back after dark if scheduled.
9. Clean up, remove old fixtures and hardware, and restore the area.

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **New fixtures destroyed on power-up.** Our company has destroyed many new LED fixtures by assuming
  the voltage. Meter first, every exterior project (case study *New Fixtures Destroyed on Power-Up*).
- **Emergency wall packs quoted as standard.** On one of our jobs, 13 of 20 wall packs sold as standard
  were emergency units. Check labels and test buttons and count EM units separately (case study
  *Emergency Wall Packs Quoted as Standard*).
- **Double controls.** A factory photocell left in series with the site's clock makes lights fail
  "randomly." Disconnect it unless told otherwise.
- **Damaged pole base.** One of our techs reported a damaged base to the manager and the office before
  starting, which protected everyone (case study *A Damaged Pole Base Reported First*).
- **Bucket over the entrance.** A safety-meeting photo showed customers walking under a basket with
  cones only at the rear bumper (case study *Bucket Over a Store Entrance*).
- **Front-end swing.** One of our lift trucks was totaled when its bucket caught a structure during a
  turn. Watch your swing and use an observer to back (case study *Lift Truck Totaled on a Turn*).

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- Measured voltage and location for every fixture; photocell disconnected (yes/no); function tested or
  "unable to test" with the reason.
- For any fixture still needing replacement: measured primary voltage, location on the property
  (property line or mid-lot), pole shape and size, mounting, photocell need, count of matching fixtures,
  and photos (head, label, pole and base, wide shot).
- Before and after photos from the same spot; existing damage photos.
- Removed fixtures and lamps recorded for recycling.
- Equipment used (bucket truck, large ladder) recorded on the work order.

## Load list

**Core kit** (every project): 6 ft and 8 ft ladders, wire nuts and lever-nut connectors / in-line disconnects, clean rags, drop cloths, vacuum (or the site's), a cart, trash cans with wheels, large garbage bags, tape gun and tape, recycle containers sized to the lamp count (check what each container holds), black marker, shrink wrap, cones and caution tape, and a tablet with recaps and timecards loaded; cleaner and spray bottles if cleaning is in scope. Your own drill, bits, meter and lockout kit for every person. Plan the rest from the project size, the job type and the project specifics, and arrange a project-style vehicle or trailer if the ladders, lifts, material and recycle need it.

**Exterior add-ons** (scale to the survey):

- Ladders by height (8 ft, 12 ft, an articulating ladder, 24 ft extension) or the lift or bucket truck on the sheet
- YWN (yellow) wire nuts and wet-location connectors, sealant and tape
- Cones and caution tape for the full work zone; harness and lanyard, hard hat, high-visibility vest
- Tested meter and proving unit, lockout kit, personal lock and tags
- Mounting kits, slip-fitters or arm adapters for the poles on site; photocells or shorting caps per the fixture instructions
- Plenty of rags and garbage bags; cleaner if cleaning is in scope
- HID lampholders, ballast kits and capacitors, only if the project specifics call for a retrofit instead of new fixtures
