## What this job is

Service on building signs, pole signs and channel letters lit by LED modules or neon, and the small LED
message signs found on exterior inspections. Signs are usually at height, often over walkways, and
controlled by time clocks and photocells that make a sign look "out" when it is simply off.

## What "done right" looks like

- Controls were checked first: time clock, photocell, switches, sign disconnect.
- The **sign disconnect** was locked out and absence of voltage verified on the primary before opening
  letters, raceways or supply compartments. A time clock is not a lockout.
- Neon was diagnosed energized by **observing and listening only**, from a safe distance.
- Connections were tested and remade before any power supply or transformer was condemned.
- Replacement modules match on voltage, color temperature, brightness and series, so the letter looks
  even.
- The whole sign lights evenly before you leave, and sign faces were cleaned if dirty.

## Before you leave the shop

- Read the service order: sign type (LED or neon), what is out, whether it is intermittent.
- Plan access: bucket truck or ladder, harness for any platform or catwalk, barricades for below.
- Load common modules, power supplies, connectors and GTO as the service order suggests.
- Neon: only techs trained on high-voltage sign work handle transformers, GTO and glass; secondary
  measurements need high-voltage-rated test equipment and qualified persons.

## On site

1. Check in. Ask what is out and when it happens (rain, cold, time of day).
2. Barricade below the work; do not work over entrances without the manager's agreement.
3. Check clock, photocell, switches and breaker position. A breaker that trips again goes to an
   electrician; do not keep resetting it.
4. **LED:** line voltage at the supply input (qualified person), then secondary with modules connected,
   then the low-voltage connections. Swap dark modules with known-good ones.
5. **Neon:** observe and listen energized; then **lock out and verify** before handling glass, GTO or
   transformers. Start with the first piece off the transformer; split-half channel signs per company
   method.
6. Look for the cause: water in the can, blocked drain holes, fallen modules, chafed wire, GTO burns.
7. Restore power, verify the whole sign, clean the faces.

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **Connections, not parts.** The company guide says secondary connections are "more often than not the
  issue." Check and recheck before replacing a supply (LT3-C10 Lesson 4).
- **Some supplies read nothing with no load.** Test with modules connected.
- **Small LED signs under warranty.** Treat even one LED out as a failure, photograph the face and label
  so the office can claim the warranty.
- **Elevated sign structures.** Company rule: service from the ladder or lift, or wear the harness and
  stay tied off on the platform; if the ladder cannot reach, write it up for the right size lift
  (toolbox talk *Fall Protection Rules We Live By*).
- **Working above the public.** Cone the whole area below, not just the truck (case study *Bucket Over a
  Store Entrance*).

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- What was out, what you checked in order, what you found and replaced.
- LED: module make, series, part number, voltage, color temperature, count; supply output, wattage,
  class and location rating; connector types.
- Neon: transformer rating, GTO rating and length, glass needing a neon shop (with pattern or photo).
- Photos of the sign face (dark sections), labels and finished work.
- Branch-circuit, structural or water problems written up for the right trade.

## Load list

Based on the core kit; no company list yet for this job type. Typical items:

- Bucket truck or ladder; harness and lanyard
- Cones and caution tape for below the sign
- Tested multimeter, proving unit and lockout kit
- Known-good LED modules and common power supplies
- Connectors suited to the location, sealant, wire
- GTO and neon parts (trained techs only)
- Rags and cleaner for sign faces
- Camera or phone for labels and sign face photos
