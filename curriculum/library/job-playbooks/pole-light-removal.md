## What this job is

Taking down a pole light that has been hit by a vehicle, is leaning or cracked, or has snapped off at
the base plate. The goals: **nobody gets hurt, the rest of the lot keeps working, and we leave with
everything needed to order the replacement.**

## What "done right" looks like

- The pole was treated as **unstable** and every exposed conductor as **live** until tested.
- The circuit was locked out and absence of voltage verified at the hand hole.
- Fixture make, model and condition were recorded before anything came apart.
- The pole was rigged above its center of gravity, a light strain taken before the last nut came off,
  and lifted **straight up**.
- Every nut and washer was bagged and taken with us.
- The base is safe: feed-through splices remade with wet-location connectors, dead ends capped
  individually, then **tape, cup, tape**; bolt threads protected and a cone on the base.
- **Every other light in the lot was checked** after power was restored, and anything dark reported.

## Before you leave the shop

- Get photos and the location from the service order. Estimate pole height and weight and pick the
  lifting machine.
- Load the company pole-removal tools (see load list) and inspect the sling and shackle.
- Confirm you have trained people for the jib, rigging and signals. Not trained on the jib? Don't use it.
- Know where the lot's lighting circuit is controlled (panel, contactor or time clock) and who can give
  access.
- Plan the haul: how the pole is secured and flagged on the truck or trailer.

## On site

1. Survey and photograph the pole, heads, base and damage. **Look up** for overhead lines.
2. Report existing damage to the site manager and tell them other poles on the circuit will go dark.
3. Barricade a fall zone larger than the pole is tall; move vehicles out.
4. Lock out, tag and **verify live-dead-live at the hand hole.**
5. Record the fixtures; remove good heads from the lift (tied off) if the plan calls for it.
6. Disconnect the pole leads from the branch splices and fuse holders.
7. Rig: choke high on the shaft above the center of gravity, protect the sling from corners, take a
   light strain.
8. Break the anchor nuts loose from the side away from any lean; bag every nut and washer.
9. Lift straight up on the signal person's command, control swing with a tag line, lay down on blocking.
10. Make the base safe, restore power, and **check the rest of the lot.**

If the plan stops matching what you see, **stop, set the load down and call your supervisor.**

## Common problems we see and how we handle them

- **Nuts and washers disappear.** They go missing if left on the pole; bag them (LT3-C12 Lesson 4).
- **Other poles go dark afterward.** The stress of the hit or the pull can break splices elsewhere;
  check every light before you leave.
- **Damaged base found during a replacement.** One of our techs reported a damaged pole base before
  starting, which protected the company and the next person (case study *A Damaged Pole Base Reported
  First*).
- **Front-end swing on lift trucks.** A company lift truck was totaled when its bucket caught a structure
  while turning (case study *Lift Truck Totaled on a Turn*).
- **Modified or improvised equipment.** Never add brackets or homemade mounts to lifts or trailers (case
  studies *Brackets and Drain Holes in a Lift Basket* and *Improvised Trailer Repairs*).

## Paperwork and photos at close-out

- Fixture make, model and manufacturer; one or both fixtures to replace.
- Pole dimensions and shape, base and fixture-mount bolt patterns, leveling nuts (LT3-C12 Lesson 5).
- Photos before, during and after; damage photos.
- What is still energized at the base and what is on order, told to the customer.
- Lot check results; equipment used (lift truck, jib) recorded.

## Load list

> **Company load list pending.** The company's load list for this job type will be added here. Until then, use your supervisor's list.

Typical items (generic example, based on the tools named in LT3-C12 Lesson 4):

- Very large adjustable wrench and a breaker bar (or conduit sleeve for leverage)
- 6, 8 or 10 ft web sling and a rated 3/4 in screw-pin anchor shackle
- Tag line and blocking (dunnage)
- Lockout kit, tested meter and proving unit, insulated hand tools
- Wet-location connectors, insulating caps, lots of electrical tape and a plastic cup
- Penetrating oil and a wire brush
- Tape measure, bags for hardware, camera or phone
- Cones and caution tape for the fall zone
- Hard hat, safety glasses, gloves, high-visibility vest
