## Lighting terms

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Voltage (V)** | Electrical "pressure" supplied to the lamp or fixture: 12, 24, 120, 208, 240, 277, 347, 480 V. The lamp, ballast or driver must be rated for it |
| **Watts (W)** | The **power the lamp or fixture uses**. Not brightness |
| **Lumens (lm)** | Total visible light a lamp or fixture produces |
| **Efficacy (lm/W)** | Lumens divided by watts; how efficiently a source makes light |
| **Foot-candles (fc)** | Light *arriving* on a surface: 1 fc = 1 lumen per square foot (about 10.8 lux). Measured with a light meter at the floor, desk or pavement |
| **Fixture efficiency** | Share of the lamps' lumens that leaves the fixture. Reflectors, lenses and dirt absorb some light; never 100% |
| **Correlated color temperature (CCT)** | Whether white light looks warm or cool, in kelvin (K). For fluorescent, HID and LED it is "correlated" because these sources only approximate the color of a heated reference |
| **Color rendering index (CRI, Ra)** | A score up to 100 for how accurately colors look compared with a reference source. **Not a percentage** |
| **Average rated life** | Hours at which **half of a large test batch has failed**. Some lamps fail sooner |
| **L70** | For LEDs: hours until output drops to 70% of the original lumens. LEDs dim rather than burn out |

> **Service tip:** LED fixtures installed years ago may be well into their lumen depreciation.
> Customers forget how bright the space was. Note dim aging fixtures on the work order; it is a
> service opportunity for our company.

## Incandescent and halogen codes

Read as **wattage + shape and size + options**:

- `100A19` = 100 W, A shape, 19/8 in (about 2-3/8 in) diameter
- `65BR30` = 65 W, bulged reflector, 30/8 in (3-3/4 in)
- `90PAR38/HAL/FL40/120V` = 90 W halogen PAR38, 40-degree flood, 120 V
- `300T3/CL/118mm` = 300 W clear double-ended T3 halogen, 118 mm, R7s base

R30 and PAR30 are the same diameter but different lamps: an R is a soft-beam reflector, a PAR
has a pressed lens and a tight beam. They also sit differently in a can; the fixture housing and
trim decide which fits. General-service incandescent A-lamps have been phased out in the U.S.;
halogen and specialty incandescent lamps are still sold.

## Fluorescent codes

| Code piece | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| F | Fluorescent | F32T8 |
| Number after F | **Watts** for tubes 4 ft and shorter; **length in inches** for longer tubes | F32T8 = 32 W 4 ft; F96T12 = 96 in (8 ft) |
| T + number | Tubular, diameter in eighths of an inch | T8 = 1 in |
| /8xx | First digit CRI range (7 = 70s, 8 = 80s, 9 = 90s); last two digits CCT | /841 = 80s CRI, 4100 K |
| HO, VHO | High output, very high output | F96T12/HO |
| Brand letters | Manufacturer's own prefix or suffix | "F32T8/XYZ841" is still an 841 lamp |

**Diameters:** T12 = 1-1/2 in; T8 = 1 in; T5 = 5/8 in (metric lengths, about 46 in for a "4 ft"
T5; not interchangeable with T8).

**U-bent:** codes such as `FB31T8/841/6` or `FB32T8/6`: FB = bent, the last number is the spacing
between the legs in inches (often 6 in or 1-5/8 in). Measure leg spacing on the old lamp.

**Circline:** codes such as `FC12T9/CW`: FC = circline, 12 = outside diameter in inches, T9 tube,
4-pin connector (G10q).

**CFL (compact fluorescent):**

| Type | Pins | Started by | Common bases |
|---|---|---|---|
| Screw-in (self-ballasted) | None (screw base) | Built-in ballast | E26, E12, E39 |
| Plug-in 2-pin | 2 | Starter inside the lamp base; magnetic ballast in fixture | G23, G24d |
| Plug-in 4-pin | 4 | Electronic ballast in fixture; often dimmable | G24q, GX24q, 2G11, GX23-2 |

Order CFLs by watts, shape (twin, quad, triple), pin count, base and CCT. CFLs often fall short of
their rated life in hot or enclosed fixtures because heat damages their electronics.

## HID codes

| Family | Example | Reads as | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal halide (MH) | `MH175/U` | Metal halide, 175 W, universal burning position | /BU base up, /BD base down, /H horizontal. 3000 K or 4000 K options. About 10 minutes to full output |
| Pulse-start MH | `MS400/PS` or `MH400/U/PS` | 400 W pulse start | Needs a pulse-start ballast |
| Ceramic MH | `CMH70/T6/G12` (typical form) | 70 W ceramic metal halide, T6, G12 bi-pin | Better color |
| High-pressure sodium (HPS) | `LU150/55` or `HPS150` | 150 W HPS, 55 V lamp | Yellow-orange, CRI about 22, very efficient, life about 24,000 h |
| Mercury vapor (MV) | `H39KC-175` (typical form) | ANSI H39, 175 W MV | Legacy only; MV ballasts banned for sale in the U.S. since 2008 |

**Metal halide CRI:** about 65 for probe-start lamps; pulse-start and ceramic MH reach 80–90+.

**ANSI codes** describe the lamp's electrical type, and the lamp and ballast codes must match:
for example M59 (400 W probe-start MH), M57 (175 W probe-start MH), S55 (150 W 55 V HPS), H39
(175 W MV). Confirm on the lamp sleeve and ballast label; a lamp on the wrong ballast can fail
early or rupture.

## LED replacement lamps

| Type | How it works | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **TLED Type A** ("plug and play") | Runs on the existing fluorescent ballast | Must be listed for that ballast; fails when the ballast fails |
| **TLED Type B** ("ballast bypass") | Ballast removed; line voltage wired to the sockets (single-ended or double-ended) | Rewiring required; label the fixture; single-ended needs non-shunted sockets |
| **TLED Type C** | Low-voltage tubes powered by a separate LED driver | Driver and tubes must be the same system |
| Type A/B (hybrid) | Works on a ballast or bypassed | Know which way it is wired before relamping |
| **Corn lamp / LED HID replacement** | Screw-in (E26/E39) LED lamp replacing an HID lamp, usually ballast bypass | Must physically fit and be rated for enclosed fixtures if the fixture is enclosed; check weight, burn position and the fixture's label |

Integrated LED fixtures have no lamp code; record the fixture's full catalog number and the
driver label (LT1-C10).

## Color temperature

| CCT | Looks like | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 2700 K | Warm white, like incandescent | Hospitality, restaurants, homes |
| 3000 K | Soft warm white | Retail, hotels, lobbies |
| 3500 K | Neutral white | Offices, schools |
| 4000–4100 K | Cool white | Offices, schools, healthcare, warehouses |
| 5000 K | Daylight, crisp | Warehouses, manufacturing, parking, display |
| 6500 K | Very cool, bluish daylight | Some industrial and task areas; can look harsh |

HPS is about 2000–2100 K (orange-gold). Never mix CCTs in one space unless the customer asks.

## CRI bands

| CRI | Quality | Typical sources |
|---|---|---|
| 90+ | Excellent | Incandescent, halogen, high-CRI LED, some ceramic MH |
| 80–89 | Good | Standard LED, 800-series fluorescent, pulse-start and ceramic MH |
| 70–79 | Fair | Older fluorescent, low-cost LED |
| Below 70 | Poor | Probe-start MH (about 65), mercury vapor, HPS (about 22) |

## Cross-referencing a lamp between manufacturers

The same lamp is sold under different codes. A generic example for one 4-ft T8 lamp:

| Source | Code on the sleeve |
|---|---|
| Manufacturer A | F32T8/AB841 |
| Manufacturer B | F32T8/841/CD |
| Manufacturer C | XX32/841 |

All three are a 32 W, 4-ft T8, 80s CRI, 4100 K lamp. To cross a lamp:

1. Decode the core: wattage or length, shape and diameter, base, CRI and CCT.
2. For HID, add ANSI code, probe or pulse start, burn position and enclosed/open rating.
3. Match those fields, not the brand letters. Check reduced-wattage and TLED lamps against the
   ballast label.
4. Use the manufacturer's cross-reference tool or catalog, then compare the fields yourself.

**Call the supply house** when the code is unreadable, the cross does not match on every field,
the lamp is discontinued, the fixture needs a special lamp (enclosed-only MH, SN/LN PAR, odd U-bent
leg spacing), or you need LED options for a legacy lamp. Bring the old lamp or a clear photo of
the sleeve, the lamp and the fixture label.
