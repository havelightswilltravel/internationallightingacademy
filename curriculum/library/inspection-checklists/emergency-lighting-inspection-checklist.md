*Company checklist — mirrors the company 90-minute emergency lighting inspection spreadsheet and
the company Emergency Lighting 90-Minute Inspection Procedure. Full lesson: LT3-C04, "Our
Emergency Lighting Inspection Procedure."*

## When to use it

The annual 90-minute (1.5-hour) test of battery-powered emergency lighting required by NFPA 101
(7.9.3), and any customer-requested emergency lighting survey. The monthly 30-second test uses
the same unit list but only the indicator, transfer and quick-look items.

## Safety first

- Get the site manager's permission and agree on timing. Warn occupants before turning off
  breakers; keep people off ladders and stairs in darkened areas. Carry a flashlight.
- Operating breakers at an open panel exposes you to energized parts: follow the company NFPA 70E
  procedures and PPE, and operate only breakers you are qualified and authorized to operate.
- **Turn off only breakers labeled for emergency lighting.** Unlabeled = stop and write it up.
- Do not open any unit during the test. Repairs happen later, under **LOTO with verification of
  absence of voltage (live-dead-live)** — and remember a battery unit stays energized from its
  battery after the breaker is off; disconnect the battery per the manufacturer.

## Tools needed

Company 90-minute inspection spreadsheet (paper or tablet), site floor plan, timer, flashlight,
ladder or remote test wand for high units, camera/phone, failure tags, non-contact tester and
meter (qualified tech for supply checks), PPE for operating breakers.

## Before the test

- [ ] Checked in with the manager; floor plan/layout obtained
- [ ] Header filled in: site name and number, S/O (service order) #, address, city, state, zip, contact, phone
- [ ] Every unit walked and marked on the map
- [ ] Charge (LED) indicator checked on every unit; dark indicators noted
- [ ] For each dark indicator: power verified by a qualified tech (power good = bad unit; no power = circuit write-up)
- [ ] Emergency breakers identified and labeled (if not: **stop**, write up for an electrician)

## Starting the test

- [ ] Emergency breakers turned off; **90-minute timer started**; start time written down
- [ ] Every charge indicator went out (if not, a breaker was missed)
- [ ] Units that did not light immediately written up and tagged

## Spreadsheet fields (one row per unit)

| Field | What to look for and record |
|---|---|
| Fixture Location | Location matching the number on the map |
| Fixture Description | Exit, bug eye, fluorescent (battery-backup fixture), combo, etc. |
| Fixture Height | Mounting height (tells the office what access the repair needs) |
| Model/MFG* | Manufacturer and model from the label |
| Housing Color* | White, black, other — replacement must match |
| Exit Sign Letter Color* | Red or green — match the local requirement and other signs |
| LED? | Yes/No; incandescent or fluorescent signs are retrofit/replace candidates |
| 90min Pass/Fail | Pass only if lit for the full 90 minutes |
| Failed Component* | Battery, lamp/head, charging unit, etc. |
| Battery or Ballast Code* | Battery part number/voltage, or emergency ballast / LED emergency driver model |
| Picture #* | Photo number for the failure |
| Notes | Damage, aiming, blocked or illegible signs, missing chevrons, suspected switched feed |

\*Required for every failed unit.

## While the timer runs

- [ ] Every row completed; defective fixtures tagged with the problem (battery, lamp, charging board)
- [ ] Approved PM only (clean faces, re-aim heads, replace missing covers) — no energized work

## At 90 minutes

- [ ] Every unit checked again: still lit = **Pass**; dark or badly dimmed = **Fail**
- [ ] Units that lit at the start but did not last recorded as **battery** (confirm the indicator was lit beforehand; old units get a new unit)
- [ ] Model/MFG and battery information recorded for every failure
- [ ] Photos of each failure: **full elevation** plus **close-up of model and date code label**
- [ ] All breakers restored; every charge indicator back on; manager told batteries are recharging (up to 24 hours)

## Footer

- [ ] Panel voltage circled: **120 or 277**
- [ ] Totals and voltage: EXIT signs, EMERGENCY units, Combo units, Bug-eye units
- [ ] Surveyed By, Title, Date
- [ ] Test start and end times noted (NFPA 101 record)

## Likely findings

| Finding | How to confirm | Record as |
|---|---|---|
| Indicator dark, power present | Qualified supply check | Failed unit / charging unit |
| Indicator dark, no power | Qualified supply check | Circuit write-up (not a unit failure) |
| Did not light when breakers went off | Indicator was lit, no light on transfer | Lamp/head or transfer failure; often a new unit |
| Lit, but dark before 90 minutes | Watch at mid-test and at 90 min | Battery |
| Indicator stays on with breakers off | — | Missed breaker or unit on another circuit; note it |

## When to escalate

- Breakers not labeled for emergency lighting
- No power at a unit, or a unit that goes to battery whenever the room lights are switched off
- Generator- or inverter-fed emergency systems (tested under NFPA 110 / manufacturer program — supervisor coordinates)
- More than a few failures in one area (possible circuit problem)

## Documentation

Turn in the completed spreadsheet, the marked-up map and all photos the same day. The record must
show date, start/end times, each unit's result, and who tested; after repairs, add the corrective
action and retest date (NFPA 101 written-record requirement). **Self-diagnosing units:** record
indicator status; they skip our manual timed test only if they automatically perform the monthly
and annual-duration tests — indicators still get checked every 30 days and the AHJ has the final say.
