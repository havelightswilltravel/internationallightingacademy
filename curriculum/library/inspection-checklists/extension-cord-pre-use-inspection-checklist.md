*Company checklist: adapted from the company Ladder and Extension Cord Inspection Form.*

## When to use

- **Before using any extension cord, every shift.**
- On the company inspection form for your vehicle: **list the number of each cord on the
  van and initial** that it was inspected.
- After a cord has been run over, pinched in a door, soaked, or overheated.

## Safety first

- OSHA requires portable cord-and-plug equipment, including extension cords, to be
  **visually inspected before use on any shift** and any defective cord to be removed from
  service until repaired or replaced (29 CFR 1910.334(a)(2)).
- Worn or frayed cords are not used (29 CFR 1926.416(e)(1)).
- **Never remove or bend the ground pin**, and never use an adapter that defeats it. The
  ground pin carries fault current to trip the breaker instead of through you
  (29 CFR 1910.334(a)(3)).
- Use **GFCI protection** for cords and tools on construction and outdoor work
  (29 CFR 1926.404(b)(1)), and test the GFCI before use.
- Inspect cords **unplugged.** Never handle a damaged cord that is still plugged in; unplug
  it at the plug, with dry hands and dry gloves.

## Tools needed

- Company Ladder and Extension Cord Inspection Form (or the app checklist)
- "DANGER - DO NOT USE" tags and ties
- Plug-in GFCI tester or receptacle tester (for checking the GFCI you plug into)
- Dry work gloves

## Checklist

Uncoil the cord completely and run it hand over hand from plug to connector.

| # | Item | Pass when | Fail when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Insulation (outer jacket)** | Jacket whole along the full length | Any **break, burn, cut, or crack**; nicks showing inner wires; melted, crushed, or bulging spots |
| 2 | **Repairs** | No tape, no splices | Any tape-over or field splice |
| 3 | **Plug (male end)** | Body whole; blades straight and tight; **ground pin present** | Missing or bent ground pin, loose or bent blades, cracks, burn marks |
| 4 | **Connector (female end)** | Housing whole; grips a plug firmly | Cracked, loose, burned, or discolored |
| 5 | **Strain relief** | Jacket firmly held inside both ends | Jacket pulled back so inner conductors show |
| 6 | **Rating and label** | Gauge/amp rating readable and right for the load; hard or extra-hard usage type; "W" (outdoor) rating for outdoor use | Unreadable, light-duty indoor cord on a job site, or under-rated for the tool |
| 7 | **Heat and odor** | Cool, no smell | Warm spots or burnt smell after use |

### Using cords on the job

- One cord per run; do not daisy-chain cords or plug a power strip into a cord.
- Route cords away from walkways, or tape and cover them. A cord across an aisle is a trip
  hazard for customers and coworkers.
- Do not run cords through doorways, windows, or ceilings where they can be pinched, and
  keep connections out of water and snow.
- Unplug by the plug, not by pulling the cord. Coil loosely; keep cords off sharp ladder
  rack edges.

## If an item fails

1. **Stop using it.** Unplug it at the plug.
2. **Tag it "DANGER - DO NOT USE"** and **remove it from service** (out of the van's cord
   bin).
3. If your supervisor directs, **cut off the plug** so the cord cannot be used by mistake.
   Field-taped or spliced cords are not returned to service.
4. **Report it to your supervisor** and **write the replacement on your pull sheet.**
5. Record the cord number as failed on the inspection form.

## When to escalate

- A cord or tool trips the GFCI repeatedly: stop and report it; do not move to an
  unprotected outlet.
- Shock, tingle, smoke, or burn marks at a receptacle: stop, keep people away, and call
  your supervisor; the receptacle may also be damaged.
- No good cord on the van for the job: call your supervisor; do not borrow an uninspected
  cord.

## Documentation

- Cord numbers on the van, initials for each cord inspected, date, and both technicians'
  signatures on the form.
- Any cord removed from service: number, defect, and who it was reported to.
- Replacement written on your pull sheet.
