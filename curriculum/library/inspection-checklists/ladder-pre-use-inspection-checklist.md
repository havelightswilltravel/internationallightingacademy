*Company checklist: adapted from the company Ladder and Extension Cord Inspection Form and
the ladder section of the company safety handbook.*

## When to use

- **Before every use of every ladder, every shift.** It takes about two minutes per ladder.
- On the company inspection form for your **vehicle or location**, on the schedule your
  supervisor sets.
- **Again** after a ladder is dropped, falls over, slides, is struck, or comes loose on the
  rack, before anyone climbs it.

## Safety first

- OSHA requires ladders to be inspected before first use in each work shift and any ladder
  with structural or other defects to be tagged and removed from service until repaired or
  replaced (29 CFR 1910.23(b)(9)-(10); construction: 1926.1053(b)(15)-(16)).
- **Company rules:** painted ladders are not used (paint hides defects); metal ladders are
  not used around exposed electrical equipment; the large multi-position ladder and
  extension ladders 32 ft and longer are **two-person work** to carry and set up.
- Never repair a ladder in the field. Repairs, if allowed at all, are done the
  manufacturer's way by the people your supervisor assigns.
- Before working on or near any fixture from a ladder, the circuit is **locked out and
  verified dead** (see *Lockout/Tagout Quick Card*). A ladder does not make live work safe.

## Tools needed

- Company Ladder and Extension Cord Inspection Form (or the app checklist)
- Red **"DANGER - DO NOT USE"** tags and ties
- Rags and cleaner to remove oil, grease, mud, snow, or ice
- Flashlight for checking rivets, rung locks, and the inside of rails

## Checklist

Mark each line **pass** or **fail** for each ladder. Write the ladder size at the top of the
column (for example: 6-ft step, 8-ft step, 24-ft extension, multi-position).

| # | Item | Pass when | Fail when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Non-slip material on bottoms of all four rails** | Feet/shoes present, tread visible, secure | Missing, worn through, loose, or split feet |
| 2 | **All surfaces free of oil, grease, or other slippery materials** | Clean and dry rails and steps | Any slippery contamination (clean it, then re-check) |
| 3 | **Joints between steps and rails tight** | No movement when you twist a step | Steps shift, rack, or rattle |
| 4 | **All hardware and fittings securely attached** | Rivets, bolts, nuts, and brackets tight | Loose, missing, or sheared rivets or bolts |
| 5 | **Moving parts operate without binding or too much play** | Hinges, rung locks, pulley, and swivel feet move freely and lock | Binding, sticking, sloppy, or does not lock |
| 6 | **Spreader bars straight and lock securely** | Straight, lock fully open | Bent, cracked, or will not lock |
| 7 | **Rails and steps without cracks, crushing, etc.** | No damage | Any crack, dent, bend, crush, split, burn, or fiberglass "blooming" |
| 8 | **Metal ladders: insulator pads intact on bottoms of all rails** | Pads complete | Missing or worn-through pads |
| 9 | **All parts free from sharp edges** | Smooth | Burrs or sharp edges that cut hands, cords, or harness webbing |
| 10 | **All stickers in place and legible** | Duty rating, type, and warnings readable | Missing or unreadable labels |
| 11 | **All steps have non-skid surfaces** | Tread intact | Smooth, worn, or painted-over treads |

**Extension ladders, also check:** both rung locks engage fully and are not cracked; rope
not frayed and pulley turns freely; guide brackets hold the fly straight; spur spikes and
swivel feet work.

**Vehicle ladder rack:** mounts tight, no cracked welds or bent arms, rollers and locks
working, straps and tie-downs in good condition. Note any damage on the form.

## If an item fails

1. **Do not climb it.** Not "just this once."
2. **Red tag it "DANGER - DO NOT USE"** at the damaged area.
3. **Remove it from service:** take it off the truck or set it aside where nobody will grab
   it by mistake.
4. **Report it to your supervisor** the same day and **write it on your pull sheet** so a
   replacement is ordered.
5. If the only problem is dirt, grease, snow, or ice, **clean it, then re-inspect.**
6. A ladder that cannot be repaired the manufacturer's way is destroyed so it cannot be
   reused.

A damaged **ladder rack** is reported the same way. Do not load ladders on a rack that
cannot hold them.

## When to escalate

- Any structural defect, or any doubt: call your supervisor. You may retire a ladder at
  any time you feel it is no longer safe.
- No safe ladder on the truck for the job: stop and call. Do not borrow the customer's
  ladder unless your supervisor approves it and you inspect it first.
- The job needs more reach than your ladders give: a different method is needed (lift or
  other plan), not a taller stepladder. Stepladders longer than 20 ft are not used.

## Documentation

- Date, vehicle or location, ladder sizes, pass/fail for each line, and both technicians'
  signatures on the inspection form.
- Ladder rack condition.
- Any ladder red-tagged: what failed, where it is now, and who you reported it to.
- Replacement written on your pull sheet.
