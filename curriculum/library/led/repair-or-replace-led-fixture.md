> **DRAFT POLICY: pending company decision (Discussion 002).** Use this card to frame the conversation with your supervisor and the customer until the company sets its final rule.

## When to use this card
An LED fixture has failed and you have **diagnosed the cause**: the driver, the LED board, the supply or the controls. You still need to decide whether to repair it (replace the driver or board) or replace the whole fixture.

> **Safety:** Lock out and verify absence of voltage before opening any fixture or driver compartment. Some drivers hold a charge; follow the manufacturer's instructions.

## Step 1: Diagnose first
Never swap a fixture without knowing why it failed. If the supply, a photocell, a sensor or the wiring is the problem, a new fixture will fail too.

## Step 2: Work through the questions in order

| # | Question | If yes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the fixture under manufacturer warranty? | Follow the warranty process (record the model, date code and photos) |
| 2 | Does the property have an **approved standard replacement** for this fixture type? | Install the standard model if the driver isn't on the truck or the fixture is past mid-life |
| 3 | Is the right driver on the truck, the fixture in good condition, and matching the look important? | Replace the driver |
| 4 | Would the driver cost more than about half of a standard replacement fixture, or does it need to be ordered (a return trip)? | Recommend replacing the fixture with the standard model, set to match output and color |
| 5 | Still unsure? | Give the customer the choice on the spot, with both options and the return-trip impact. Record their answer as the property's standing preference |

*The "about half" threshold is a made-up starting point for discussion, not a company figure.*

## Step 3: Set and record the replacement
- **Field-selectable fixtures:** set wattage or lumens and color temperature (CCT) to match the neighboring fixtures as closely as possible. Note the settings.
- **Mounting:** confirm the arm, slip-fitter or bolt pattern matches. Measure first (see the bolt-pattern lesson in LT3-C12).
- **Record it:** fixture type, old model, new model, settings, location and photo, in the work order and the site's asset inventory (LT5-C06).

## Talking to the customer
- Lead with **total cost and time**: "I can fix it today with a new fixture, or order the driver and come back."
- Be honest about **appearance**: the new fixture may look different. Offer to plan a matching row or façade conversion.
- Never make the swap without approval when the customer has brand or appearance standards.

## Escalate when
- The customer has brand standards, or the fixture is part of a matched design (lobbies, storefronts, signage).
- The job would change controls, emergency function or circuit loading.
- You're not sure the standard fixture is listed for the location (wet, damp, enclosed, high-temperature).

## Documentation checklist
- [ ] Failure cause and how it was confirmed
- [ ] Option chosen and who approved it
- [ ] Old and new model, settings and photos
- [ ] Asset inventory updated with the property standard
